- ## Netflix Q2 2026 Earnings
	- **Quarter covered**: Q2 2026
	- **Sources**: Netflix Q2 2026 shareholder letter and July 16, 2026 earnings call transcript
	- **Thesis**: Netflix is sustaining low-teens revenue growth while expanding full-year margin, with pricing, membership growth, and a roughly doubling ads business providing multiple monetization levers. The more important strategic shift is management's move away from treating raw view hours as the sole measure of content value: live events, advertising, AI-assisted production, third-party integrations, podcasts, and games are being evaluated by acquisition, retention, monetization, and incremental engagement rather than hours alone.

- ## Financial Trajectory and Company Outlook
	- | Metric | Q2 2026 or current result | Forward view |
	  |---|---:|---|
	  | Revenue | **\$12.56B; +13.4% YoY** | Q3: **\$12.86B; +11.7% YoY** |
	  | Operating income | **\$4.19B; +11% YoY** | Q3: **\$4.27B** |
	  | Operating margin | **33.4%**, versus 34.1% a year ago | Q3: **33.2%**; FY2026: **31.5%**, versus 29.5% in 2025 |
	  | FY2026 revenue | — | **\$51.0-\$51.4B; +13%-14% YoY**, or approximately 12% FX-neutral |
	  | Advertising revenue | Growing from a small base | Approximately **\$3B in 2026**, roughly double 2025 |
	  | Free cash flow | **\$1.53B**, versus \$2.27B a year ago | Approximately **\$12.5B** for 2026 |
	  | Content amortization | Faster growth in the first half | Approximately **10% growth** for 2026, with growth slowing in the second half |
	- Q2 operating income grew more slowly than revenue because content amortization was front-loaded. The full-year margin forecast nevertheless implies **20%+ operating-income growth**, so the annual earnings algorithm remains revenue growth plus margin expansion.
	- Q3's slower FX-neutral growth reflects a more back-half-weighted 2025 comparison rather than a stated change in acquisition, retention, pricing, or advertising demand. Management described acquisition and retention as healthy and recent price changes as performing in line with prior cycles.
	- Netflix remains under **45% penetrated** across approximately **800M addressable households**, captures an estimated **7%** of a roughly **\$670B addressable revenue market**, and represents approximately **5% of global TV viewing**. These company estimates frame the runway beyond the current approximately 330M subscription households.

- ## Engagement and Content Economics
	- Members watched more than **97B hours** in the first half, up **2% YoY** or approximately **1.5B incremental hours**. Growth accelerated modestly from **1.5% in 2025** despite competition from the Winter Olympics and World Cup.
	- Non-English content again produced more than one-third of viewing, reinforcing Netflix's ability to amortize local productions across a global audience.
	- Management argued that content categories with similar cost can create very different business outcomes:
		- Live programming is expected to consume just over **5% of 2026 content spend** but generate only approximately **1% of view hours**.
		- Live events nevertheless produced **six of Netflix's ten largest new-member signup days** over the last five years.
		- Animation series and kids/family TV also consume approximately **5% of content spend** but are expected to generate **8% of view hours**.
		- The comparison supports management's claim that acquisition, advertising, promotion, retention, and fandom can make a low-hours category as valuable as a high-hours category.
	- Content expense is expected to grow approximately **10% in 2026**, above the **8% five-year average** but below the **14% average over the past decade**. Management continues to target content-spend growth below revenue growth.
	- Second-season viewing declines are slightly better in 2026 than in 2025 and remain within management's expected ranges; Netflix therefore does not plan to change its release strategy.
	- Netflix will move its What We Watched report from semiannual publication to annual publication beginning in Q1 2027. Title-level weekly rankings remain, but investors will receive the comprehensive hours dataset less frequently.

- ## Advertising and Pricing
	- Ads revenue is expected to roughly double to approximately **\$3B in 2026**, equal to about 6% of the midpoint of total revenue guidance.
	- Ad-tier average revenue per membership remains below the standard ad-free plan, but the gap is narrowing. Management described this difference as under-realized revenue that can be captured through better demand access, fill rates, formats, measurement, and transaction workflows.
	- Netflix expanded AI-powered tools across planning, creative production, campaign management, optimization, and reporting. Programmatic access is being extended to Pause Ads and live inventory, reducing manual work that previously limited smaller advertisers.
	- First-half price increases in the US, Mexico, and Spain performed in line with prior price changes. Management continues to price after improvements in perceived member value rather than follow a fixed calendar.
	- Management cited the **\$8.99 US ad plan** as the accessible entry tier and said US Netflix members pay less per viewing hour than comparable streaming services, in some cases by roughly half.

- ## AI Across Production, Discovery, and Ads
	- Generative-AI workflows have been used in approximately **300 titles during 2026**, primarily in post-production but increasingly across concept development, pre-visualization, VFX, shot planning, and delivery.
	- The documentary series American Experiment included **17 minutes of AI-enhanced footage** that management said was produced **twice as fast and at half the cost** of previous production options.
	- Management expects efficiency savings to be reinvested into additional or higher-quality content rather than used primarily to reduce the content budget.
	- Netflix is using LLMs to improve title discovery and member-preference understanding, alongside voice search and AI-powered natural-language search.
	- The economic case is broader than labor savings: management said some complex shots would have been omitted without AI because conventional production methods were too expensive or slow.

- ## New Formats, Games, and Distribution
	- Video podcasts are over-indexing during daytime and on mobile, when Netflix historically has less engagement. Management views this viewing as incremental rather than displacement of prime-time series and film consumption.
	- The TF1 integration in France combines TF1 linear channels and on-demand programming inside Netflix at no added member cost. Four weeks after launch, TF1 viewing was growing weekly and early engagement was described as promising.
	- Netflix is open to more third-party content integrations when member, partner, and Netflix economics align, but management did not endorse becoming a general channel store.
	- A free ad-supported service could make sense in selected markets with a sufficiently scaled ads business, but Netflix has **no near-term launch plans** because of paid-tier cannibalization risk.
	- Cloud games are producing stronger early adoption signals:
		- Monthly active cloud-game players increased **11x in eight months**, with adoption and retention running ahead of the earlier mobile-games curve.
		- Netflix Playground daily players increased **3x** after launch, contributing to **600% YoY growth** in kids mobile-game engagement.
		- Management will continue scaling investment only when demonstrated member value and business returns justify it; games remain a small share of content spending.

- ## Capital Allocation and Strategic Boundaries
	- Q2 free cash flow declined to **\$1.53B** from **\$2.27B** because of higher cash taxes, partly related to the Warner Bros. termination fee. Full-year free-cash-flow guidance remains approximately **\$12.5B**.
	- Netflix repurchased **\$4.7B** of shares in Q2, its largest quarterly buyback, and ended the quarter with **\$27.1B** of remaining authorization.
	- The quarter ended with **\$9.1B of cash** and **\$14.4B of gross debt**; Netflix plans to refinance \$1B of debt maturing later in 2026.
	- Management reiterated that Netflix is primarily a builder rather than a buyer. The company remains open to selective intellectual-property, library, or capability acquisitions but maintains a high bar for transformative M&A.
