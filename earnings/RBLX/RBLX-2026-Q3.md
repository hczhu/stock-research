- ## Roblox 2026 Q3 earnings
	- Quarter ended September 30, 2026. Roblox reported on October 29.
	- **Source**: Roblox Q3 2026 press release, shareholder letter, supplemental materials, investor data sheet, and earnings call with David Baszucki and Naveen Chopra. Page references use the supplied PDFs.
	- **Thesis**: Discovery changes improved retention and helped bookings beat guidance, while younger US users still faced tough monetization comparisons. Baszucki's next bet is that AI lowers the cost of making and porting games, increasing the value of Roblox's distribution, discovery, and payments infrastructure.

- ## Bookings beat guidance while payer counts fell
	- | Metric | Q3 2026 | Comparison |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Bookings | \$1.697B | Down 12% YoY, up 9% QoQ and up 50% over two years. Beat the prior guide's high end by \$47M |
	  | GAAP revenue | $1.572B | Up 16% YoY. Prior-period bookings continue to flow through revenue |
	  | Daily active users | 138M | Down 9% YoY, up 12% QoQ and up 55% over two years |
	  | Hours engaged | 33.8B | Down 15% YoY, up 17% QoQ and up 63% over two years |
	  | Average bookings per DAU | $12.29 | Down 3% YoY |
	  | Average monthly unique payers | 30.3M | Down 16% YoY and up 12% QoQ |
	  | Average bookings per monthly unique payer | $18.69 | Up 5% YoY |
	  | Consolidated net loss | \$230M loss | Improved from a \$258M loss a year earlier |
	  | Adjusted EBITDA | \$138M | Up from \$46M. Net deferrals fell to \$136M from \$458M |
	  | Operating cash flow and free cash flow | \$395M operating cash flow and \$364M FCF | Down 28% and 18% YoY, respectively |
	- **Revenue recognition**. Durable items were 90% of virtual item revenue in Q3. Roblox generally recognizes the associated bookings and payment processing costs over its estimated **27-month payer lifetime**. DevEx, personnel, and certain infrastructure and safety costs are recognized in the current period.
	- **Where bookings fell**. US and Canada bookings declined 13% YoY and represented about 53% of total bookings. Europe fell 12%, APAC 7%, and the rest of the world 8%. India and Japan had DAU growth of 37% and 33%, respectively, even as APAC overall declined.
	- Sources: letter PDF pp. 5, 7, and 13–15, data sheet pp. 1–5, supplemental slides 13 and 33–35, call p. 6.

- ## Discovery, retention, and monetization
	- **Recommended for You**. Q2's shift toward long-term retention initially pressured monetization. In Q3, Roblox incorporated age cohorts and richer play data into the recommender. Chopra attributed the bookings beat primarily to retention and engagement, with seasonally adjusted retention at its highest since 2021. Bookings per hour rose **about 4% globally and 2% in the US and Canada** YoY. Monetization remained challenged among younger US cohorts.
	- **New games are surfacing faster**. By the end of Q3, approximately 40% of weekly top-100 Recommended for You titles by impressions were less than three months old. This matters as AI increases the volume of games competing for attention.
	- **Moments**. Live in most of the US, with videos linking directly into games. Roblox added avatar item purchases from videos and comments, and plans to make Build games playable directly from the feed.
	- **Roblox Plus and ads**. Plus reached millions of active subscribers. They averaged 2× the playtime and 7× the Robux spend of other users, a comparison between groups. Over 70% of the top 100 games by Robux spent ran Sponsored Ads. Among games using rewarded video, more than 20% earned over 10% of their in-game earnings from ads. Roblox plans skippable pre-roll to grow first-party inventory.
	- Sources: letter PDF pp. 8–9 and 13, call pp. 4–8.

- ## Adult audience and age checks
	- | US age cohort | DAU growth YoY | Hours growth YoY | Growth over two years |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | Age 18–34 | Up 26% | Up 23% | DAUs up 114% and hours up 126% |
	  | Age 18 and up | Up 18% | Up 13% | DAUs up 89% and hours up 95% |
	- **Monetization and acquisition**. Management estimates US users aged 18 and up monetize at least 50% above under-18 users. Marketing spend targeting adults was nearly 3× last summer's level. Roblox reported stronger retention, engagement, and monetization for paid sign-ups than organic sign-ups in this cohort. The targeted US adult DevEx premium gives creators an incentive to build for the same audience.
	- **Age-check coverage**. 64% of global DAUs had completed an age check at quarter-end, up from 57% in Q2. Coverage reached 75% in the US and UK and 80% in Australia. Adults made up 29% of global and 37% of US age-checked DAUs, up from 27% and 33% in Q2. The mix figures use age-checked DAUs as the denominator. Roblox extrapolates some US growth and monetization figures from age-checked users under the methodology introduced in Q1 2026.
	- **Communication and safety**. DAUs sending a chat message or connecting to voice grew 24% QoQ. Roblox paired age checks and age-banded chat with new in-game communication tools and expanded multimodal moderation to evaluate full scenes and combinations of avatar items.
	- Sources: letter PDF pp. 6–8, 10 and 21, call pp. 4–5 and 10.

- ## Baszucki's AI roadmap for creating, publishing, and importing games
	- Baszucki reiterated a goal of capturing **10% of the global gaming market** and a greater share in the US. He estimates Roblox already captures approximately 20% of US under-18 gaming revenue. To expand into adult audiences, Roblox plans to broaden the types of games it can run and the places creators can reach players.
	- | Initiative | Product and rollout |
	  |---|---|
	  | Roblox App, Build, and Studio Assistant | Build lets users create and publish games from natural-language prompts, including on mobile. A broader US release was planned for December 2026, with global rollout in 2027. Roblox intends to offer a free base tier and charge for additional prompt capacity |
	  | Roblox Everywhere | Roblox plans to publish selected native titles as standalone apps in the first half of 2027. A later phase would let creators submit their own standalone builds to app stores while using Roblox's economy, social graph, communications, and safety services |
	  | Universal Player | AI-assisted import of existing console, PC, and mobile games into Roblox. Baszucki's goal is to add instant joining, low-latency multiplayer, cross-device support, and rapid updates. Roblox is working with studios and wants a self-serve process for rights holders and creators |
	  | Engine and delivery | Roblox Reality targets early access in H1 2027 and combines video world models with the engine for multiplayer photorealism. Web Play would bring instant cross-play to Chrome through WebAssembly. Server Authority shipped in Q3, and expanded 2D and offline play are in development |
	- | AI adoption signal | Reported data |
	  |---|---|
	  | Established creators | More than 80% of the top 100 games by hours used AI tools over the prior month. Time spent with AI tools among top creators tripled since Q2 and daily Roblox MCP users grew nearly 5× |
	  | Content supply | Daily asset uploads tripled month over month. Published game volume using AI grew more than 50% QoQ. Break and Steal an Egg and Huss Valley were built almost entirely with AI and reached the top 20 by concurrent players |
	  | New creators | Build alpha reached over 500,000 users and 70,000 published games across four test markets. More than 75% of Build users had never opened Studio |
	- **Roblox's infrastructure advantage, in management's view**. Build and Studio Assistant share a system that routes prompts across models for code, scenes, and other tasks. Roblox said self-hosted models handled 77% of requests and plans to use frame-level gameplay data to train NPC playtesters, scene verification, and specialized creation models. As creation gets cheaper, Baszucki expects creators to place more value on finding players, operating multiplayer games, and earning revenue.
	- Sources: letter PDF pp. 3–6 and 9–13, call pp. 3–6 and 8–12.

- ## Q4 outlook, AI spending, and capital allocation
	- | Q4 2026 metric | Guidance |
	  |---|---|
	  | Revenue | \$1.47–1.58B, up 4–12% YoY |
	  | Bookings | \$1.89–2.00B, down 10–15% YoY |
	  | Consolidated net loss | \$363–423M loss |
	  | Adjusted EBITDA | Negative \$70M to negative \$10M |
	  | Operating cash flow | \$190–275M |
	  | Capex and intangible purchases | $380M |
	  | Free cash flow | Negative \$190M to negative \$105M |
	- **Bookings assumptions**. The guide assumes normal sequential DAU seasonality, continued near-term monetization pressure, and a modest headwind from the GTA release as anticipated at the call. Roblox widened the range because the last week of December accounts for a significant share of Q4 bookings. Adding the quarterly guide to actuals implies full-year bookings growth of roughly **1–3%** versus 2025.
	- **Capex timing**. Roughly **\$140M moved from Q3 into Q4**, lifting Q3 FCF and depressing the Q4 forecast. The implied full-year capex of about \$468M was at the low end of Roblox's initial expectations.
	- **Cost mix**. DevEx fell 6% in dollars but rose to 24% of bookings, up 140 bps, following creator rate increases. Certain infrastructure and trust and safety costs rose 24% to $258M, or 15% of bookings, up 440 bps, reflecting AI training and inference. Management expects Q4 fixed-cost deleveraging and higher AI and adult acquisition spending. Chopra sees training as largely fixed cost and expects to move more of it on-premises over time.
	- **Balance sheet and buybacks**. Cash and investments were \$6.2B, or \$5.2B net of debt. Roblox bought back 4.4M shares for approximately \$200M, with \$2.4B of authorization remaining. Outstanding and potentially dilutive shares still grew 2% YoY to 755M. Management continues to prioritize funding organic investment and preserving room for acquisitions.
	- Sources: letter PDF pp. 14–16, data sheet pp. 1 and 5, supplemental slides 23, 26 and 28–31, call pp. 6–8 and 10–11.
