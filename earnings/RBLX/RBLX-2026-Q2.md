- **Source**: Roblox Q2 2026 earnings call, July 30, 2026 (David Baszucki, Naveen Chopra, Jaime Morris). S&P Global Market Intelligence transcript.
- **Theme**: **Self-inflicted monetization reset.** Engagement is fine — management explicitly rejected the "engagement headwind" framing — but bookings per hour broke down, driven by a deliberate recommendation-algorithm change that optimizes for long-term retention at the direct expense of near-term monetization. Q3 bookings are now guided to a **14–18% YoY decline** and full-year guidance was **withdrawn entirely**.

- ## The Central Mechanism: bookings per hour, not hours
	- The shortfall is **entirely a monetization-rate problem**, concentrated in the **under-13 cohort**. Chopra: *"engagement looks good. The weakness that we saw that was inconsistent with what we expected… was really around monetization."*
	- Two stacked causes, in management's own order of importance:
		- **Mix shift (the larger, exogenous cause)**: engagement rotated away from high-monetizing **2025-vintage viral hits** toward new and evergreen experiences with **lower hourly monetization**. Chopra was explicit this is *not* a structural change in young-user behavior — last year's hours were unusually **concentrated in a few very large, very high-monetizing viral games**, and those have been replaced by games with "normal levels of monetization."
		- **Discovery algorithm change (the deliberate, endogenous cause)**: the recommender now optimizes for **measured long-term retention**, giving more impressions to highly retentive games at the expense of near-term monetization. "In Q2, the bookings impact of this trade-off on younger users was greater than we anticipated."
		- A third, smaller factor: disabling the sale of **cross-experience game passes**.
	- **The A/B evidence management is relying on** — the most decision-useful disclosure on the call: in tests against the older discovery algorithm, retention improves **"very quickly"** and compounds through incremental engagement hours, but there is **"a pretty immediate hit to dollars per hour."** Based on a few weeks of data per experiment, they believe **the curves cross** — the retention benefit eventually outweighs the bookings hit. They explicitly **declined to name the crossover point or a timeline**.
	- Current engineering focus is telling: *"finding ways to improve the algorithms such that we can reduce the near-term bookings impact while retaining the retention benefit"* — i.e. mitigating their own trade-off, with "promising irons in the fire" but no quantification.
	- Stated mitigation lever: using **age-check data to target recommendations by region and age cohort**, which should reduce the monetization headwind over time.

- ## Guidance: the number that matters
	- | Metric | Q2 2026 actual | Q3 2026 guide |
	  |------------------------|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
	  | Bookings | \$1.6B, +8% (low end of guide) | **\$1.58–1.65B, −14% to −18% YoY** |
	  | Revenue | \$1.5B, +36% | — |
	  | Full-year outlook | 8–12% bookings growth (prior) | **Withdrawn — not re-guided** |
	- Q2 bookings landed **within guidance but below internal goal** — management flagged this distinction directly.
	- **Full-year guidance withdrawn** on grounds of "increasing variability and continued updates to our platform." Asked point-blank by BofA whether 2026 is still a growth year — whether 2026 bookings will exceed 2025 — Chopra **declined to answer**, saying only that it's "very early days" for Build and Moments and there's "no model we can look at."
	- **Q3 margin compression splits roughly in half**: about 50% fixed-cost deleveraging on lower bookings, 50% AI investment (Build, Roblox Reality/Morpheus, Moments). This is a clean framing for modelling the recovery path — one half reverses with bookings, the other does not.

- ## Cost Structure Change: AI infra is fixed, historical infra was variable
	- The most important structural point Chopra made: historically infrastructure expense **scaled with hours and users**. The new AI infrastructure spend is **largely fixed cost**, because it is driven by **model training** rather than usage.
	- Consequence: near-term margin pressure from incremental **cloud GPU capacity**, but operating leverage on that spend as bookings grow — a different margin shape than Roblox has had before.
	- Long-term margin drivers stated as unchanged: bookings growth → fixed-cost leverage, plus **mix shift to lower-cost platforms** helping COGS.
	- **DevEx increases remain the intent**, funded by fixed-cost leverage elsewhere — including a **50% DevEx premium for games consumed by over-18 users**, a direct supply-side incentive aimed at the age-up strategy.

- ## Age-Up Progress (the strategic offset)
	- | Age-check metric | Q2 2026 |
	  |---------------------------|-------------------------|
	  | Global age-check penetration | **57%** |
	  | Australia | approaching **80%** |
	  | US and UK | **70%** |
	  | US under-18 | **75%** |
	- **18+ now ≈ one-third of age-checked DAUs.** US 18–34 DAUs and hours growing **~40% YoY** — worth tracking against Q1's disclosed **50%** growth for the same cohort, i.e. a visible deceleration quarter over quarter.
	- TAM framing updated: 18+ is **80% of the \$200B global gaming market** (Q1 framed the same 80% against ~\$150B ex-China — check the basis before treating these as a series).
	- Roblox estimates **20% of US under-13 gaming revenue already flows through Roblox** — the proof-of-concept management cites for repeating the playbook in 18+.
	- Age-based **Roblox Kids and Select accounts** rolled out in June; no image sharing in chat; age-banded communication implemented.
	- Long-term ambition restated: **10% of the global gaming market**, and long-term growth **in excess of 20%**.

- ## Engagement and Content Diversification
	- DAUs **123M (+10%)**, hours **29B (+5%)** — hours growing slower than users, consistent with the monetization-rate story rather than an engagement collapse.
	- Operating cash flow **\$318M (+60%)**; free cash flow **\$294M (+66%)** — cash generation held up strongly despite the bookings miss.
	- **Content concentration has fallen sharply**: top 10 games were **~30% of hours three years ago, ~20% today**. Management frames this as healthy diversification — but it is also the mechanical explanation for the monetization miss, since the concentrated hits were the high-monetizing ones. **The diversification and the bookings problem are the same phenomenon.**
	- International strength: **Japan DAUs +67%, India DAUs +64%**; hours +61% and +59% respectively. **Russia reinstated** late in the quarter — a full-quarter DAU benefit lands in Q3 and is one of two named drivers of sequential DAU improvement (the other being seasonality).

- ## New Product Vectors
	- **Build** — conversational AI game creation, "everyone in the world can be a game builder." Live in **New Zealand**; management reports "a large number of creations flowing through the system already" and *"more people per day on Build than on Studio"* in NZ. Fully integrated with Roblox Studio (start in Build, finish in Studio). Monetization: generous free AI/token allowance, with additional tokens sold through the **Roblox Plus subscription**.
	- **Moments** — video discovery and creation moved to the homepage for 16+; testing in **Canada, New Zealand, Singapore**; expanding beyond game captures to **short-form influencer video**. Early tests show "substantial gains in Moments user retention." Roadmap goes to full 3D games in the Moments tab, watch-then-play-in-one-tap.
	- **Roblox Reality / Morpheus** — photorealistic multiplayer via **hybrid cloud 3D synchronization plus user-side super up-sampling**. Explicitly **subscription-funded**: "the cost of infra here will initially be offset by subscription… we will initially expect to charge subscription to access that." Games run in both regular and Morpheus modes.
	- **Comms** — Global Chat and Quick Words shipped; **In-Experience Friend Chat deploying in Q3**; roadmap to video chat and avatar streaming. Rationale is that in-platform comms travel with users between games and are safer than the off-platform tools "a significant portion" of users currently use.
	- **Removing self-imposed content limits** — doubling down on **2D** (puzzle, strategy, orthographic) alongside 3D, "expanding the bookends" of what's possible on the platform.

- ## Capital Allocation
	- Priority order stated as: aggressive organic investment (R&D, people, tokens, infrastructure) first, keeping **"a lot of dry powder"** to stay nimble; then buyback and potentially M&A.
	- The buyback is **structured largely to offset employee equity dilution**, which means it mechanically **buys more stock when the price falls and less when it rises** — a natural stabilizer, with the option to accelerate or supplement.
	- M&A historically **technical talent acquisition**, but Chopra left the door open to "bigger things in the future to the extent there's strategic or industrial logic."
