Quotes are management's as given in the review. Prior-period figures and incremental margins below are back-calculated from the stated growth rates. - **Theme**: A clean beat driven by the **old core business**. A next-generation AXON model, still only partly rolled out, pushed mobile gaming back to nearly **50% YoY** growth, and revenue came in about **\$100M above the high end of guidance**. Consumer (e-commerce) advertisers kept growing triple digits YoY. The call spent most of its time answering structural questions: what data MAX gives AXON, how take rate works, and whether consumer ads crowd out gaming. Margins stayed at 84% with ~87% incremental, and buybacks stepped up to \$1.4B.

- ## Results
	- | Metric | Q3'26 | Change | Note |

	  | Adjusted EBITDA | \$1.842B | +59% YoY | **84% margin**; 87% incremental margin QoQ |
	  | Buybacks and share withholding | 4.1M shares, \$1.4B | Up sharply QoQ | Implies an average price of roughly \$340 per share |

	- **Operating leverage is close to total**: almost every extra revenue dollar falls through to EBITDA. Revenue grew \$262M QoQ and EBITDA grew roughly \$228M. The business is a model plus an auction, with little cost that scales with revenue.
	- **The guide is strong, not conservative-looking**: +8.9% QoQ follows a +13.6% quarter, and Q4 is seasonally strong for ads. If AppLovin beats its high end by the Q3 margin again, Q4 revenue would be around \$2.5B.

- ## Gaming reacceleration from the new AXON model
	- **What drove the beat**: "Revenue grew 14% sequentially and exceeded the high end of our guidance by approximately \$100 million, primarily driven by the core mobile gaming business due to model improvements during the quarter ... Our gaming business grew nearly 50% year-over-year, re-accelerating right alongside the rollout."
	- **Scale of the change**: management compared the model's technical complexity to the AXON 2.0 step and called it "the next generation of the model." Its main value is headroom: "a lot bigger path in the future in terms of processing more data and spinning up bigger clusters and hopefully getting better output."
	- **Still in partial rollout**: "it has to roll through all of our models. There is still a lot of complex R&D work there, but it gives us a much longer roadmap than the old models would have given us." Q3 therefore reflects only part of the uplift.
	- **Biggest gains where data is sparse**: "newer advertisers, smaller advertisers, newer categories, exactly where our consumer vertical lives." A model that handles thin data better helps most with the e-commerce expansion, where AppLovin has the least history.
	- **Why it matters**: the bear case on AppLovin has been that gaming is a mature, slow-growing market and that all the growth has to come from e-commerce. A gaming business growing ~50% YoY at this scale shows that model quality, not market growth, sets the growth rate. It also means the model is the main risk: growth depends on R&D continuing to deliver steps like this one.

- ## Consumer ads and the gaming ad mix
	- **Consumer keeps scaling**: "spend up double digits quarter-over-quarter and growing triple digits from the same quarter last year." No dollar figure was given.
	- **Consumer ads don't take gaming's converting impressions**: "Instead of showing the same user 20 gaming ads in a week, we might show them 15 product ads and five gaming ads ... those same five impressions that would have driven the conversion are still going to sit there." The claim is that most of the 20 gaming impressions weren't converting anyway, so the model can target gaming ads more tightly and sell the freed inventory to product advertisers.
	- **Priority is conversion, not supply**: "We're still very low on what we think is the possible conversion rate inside this gaming ecosystem ... That's the largest needle mover in the short, medium term. Long term, for sure, supply expansion is something that we're going to be focused on."
	- **Read**: AppLovin plans to grow by earning more per impression on inventory it already reaches, not by buying new supply. That is the cheapest kind of growth and explains the incremental margins. The open question is how long conversion gains can keep coming before supply becomes the limit.

- ## Performance brand budgets moving into apps
	- Management split "performance brand" spend into two kinds:
		- **True performance**: optimizing incremental ROAS or a downstream purchase. AppLovin prices "the discovery all the way to the point of transaction," so it expects "a lot of those dollars will shift into the app space."
		- **Reach-focused performance brand**: much of mobile web and traditional open-web advertising, which AppLovin doesn't target.
	- **Size claim**: "one of the largest advertising opportunities that there are outside of social and search, maybe the largest outside of social and search."
	- **Read**: AppLovin is positioning itself against open-web display (The Trade Desk, Google's network business, Criteo-style retargeting), not against Meta's feed. Budgets that are measured on purchases go to whoever can track to the transaction. The thinner the advertiser's measurement, the weaker that argument becomes.

- ## MAX: what data AXON actually gets
	- **Limited signal**: AXON sees only "did we win the ad or did we not win the ad? What was the price for the winning auction? And then everyone who loses gets the price for the losing auction. That is it." MAX sees all bids but not what they were for.
	- **No view of rivals' ads**: "We do not have visibility into an auction cleared, someone else won it, they served exactly X ... It is an output of their models. That remains with them."
	- **Same access for all bidders**: "when we built MAX ... we said everyone is going to have the same access to data as we will as a bidder into MAX ... that fairness is what drove the price of every single impression to be the highest possible."
	- **Why management addressed it**: the self-preferencing critique (AppLovin uses its mediation platform to see competitors' bids and favor its own network) is the core of short-seller and regulatory concerns. Management's answer is that AXON gets the same win/loss and clearing-price signal as every other bidder. That can't be verified from outside, and it is a statement about data access, not about auction logic or default settings.

- ## MAX growth, inventory costs and take rate
	- **MAX keeps growing fast**: "still strong double digits," after growing double digits in a single quarter last quarter, versus "very low double digits to possibly single digits" for most ad markets.
	- **Two growth drivers**: more supply, and higher value per impression as bidders monetize better, which pays publishers more.
	- **Inventory costs rise with the market**: AppLovin must "either pay out more for publisher inventory or improve our own technology at that same rate, or ... more than that rate to grow our business even faster than that market. ... We have been improving our technology better than the market."
	- **Take rate is an outcome, not a target**: "When you have a conversion rate that's a little bit over 1%, if we were buying on 1,000 impressions, somewhere around 987 of them we're losing money on ... On the other ones, you have almost an infinite take rate."
	- **What sets take rate**: relative technology. Out-innovate the market and take rate expands. Innovate at the market's rate and it stays "pretty consistent." "If we fall way behind ... our take rate will contract."
	- **Read**: this is the clearest statement of AppLovin's economics. Its margin is a spread between how fast AXON improves and how fast the rest of the auction improves. Better rival bidders (Unity's Vector, Meta Audience Network, Google) raise inventory prices and compress that spread, even if AppLovin's model keeps improving. The ~1.3% conversion figure (13 of 1,000 impressions) also shows how much revenue depends on a small number of high-value impressions.

- ## CTV pilots
	- **Early, not in the numbers**: "We've got pilot campaigns going now. The product is actually looking very promising. It's early ... I wouldn't bank on it at all for being accretive to numbers anytime soon."
	- **The opportunity**: no one offers "a true top of funnel to bottom of funnel performance offering on connected TV," and the inventory is "under monetized ... sort of what mobile looked like to us 10, 15 years ago."
	- **Read**: CTV is an option, not part of the forecast. The hard part is measurement: tying a TV impression to a purchase needs cross-device identity, which AppLovin has in apps but not on TVs.

- ## Questions the quarter leaves open
	- **How much of the next-gen AXON uplift is still to come?** The rollout is partial, so Q4's +8.9% QoQ guide may include only part of it.
	- **Consumer scale in dollars**: triple-digit growth with no absolute number makes it hard to judge how much of revenue e-commerce now is.
	- **Take-rate pressure**: whether competing bidders' model improvements (especially Unity Vector) start to narrow AppLovin's spread, which would show first in slower revenue growth relative to MAX volume.
	- **Self-preferencing**: management's data-access description is the company's position. Any regulatory or litigation finding about MAX auction behavior would matter more than the quarter's numbers.
