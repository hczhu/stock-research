- ## SK hynix Q3 2026 earnings: industry trends and paradigm shifts
	- **Quarter covered**: Q3 2026, ended September 30. Results were reported October 28, 2026.
	- **Sources**: SK hynix FY2026 Q3 earnings deck (user-provided PDF) and the earnings-call transcript with Q&A (user-provided text). The previous quarter is in [[SKHY-2026-Q2]].
	- **USD conversion**: all won amounts are converted to USD and rounded.
		- **Q3 2026 figures** use **KRW 1,355.7 per USD**, the September 30, 2026 Seoul close per [Hana Bank's daily FX report](https://biz.kebhana.com/cont/hanafx/file/Daily260930.pdf).
		- **Q2 2026 figures** use the June 30 rate of KRW 1,549.4 from [[SKHY-2026-Q2]].
		- **Q3 2025 figures** use about KRW 1,402.9, the September 30, 2025 close.
		- **Why growth rates stay in won**: the won strengthened about 14% against the dollar in Q3, so USD growth rates differ from the reported won growth rates. Revenue was +51% QoQ in USD vs. +32% in won. The QoQ and YoY percentages below are SK hynix's won-based figures.
	- **Focus**: following the user's request, this file emphasizes structural trends over short-term financials. The numbers are condensed into one table at the end.
	- **Thesis**: SK hynix describes a memory industry whose operating model is changing, not just a cyclical peak.
		- **Contracts replace the spot cycle**: LTAs cover over 50% of 2027–28 capacity. DRAM and HBM are "effectively sold out" through 2029.
		- **Demand is broader and more durable**: agentic AI, sovereign and enterprise buyers, and KV-cache tiering that pulls NAND into the AI memory hierarchy.
		- **Supply growth is structurally limited through 2030**: HBM's resource intensity, slower bit gains per node, and fab lead times.
		- **Management's response to overbuild risk**: fab shells are built early, but cleanrooms and equipment are added in phases, gated by customers' **funding capability** as well as their forecasts.
		- CEO Kwak's closing: "the potential for a **new phase of growth that is distinct from the past** is manifesting through concrete changes in our business structure."

- ## 1. From spot cycle to contracted supply
	- | LTA term | Q3 2026 disclosure |
	  |---|---|
	  | Customers | **14 key customers**, including major global CSPs (about 10 at the Q2 call) |
	  | Tenor | Typically **five years**. Some customers are discussing **extensions** |
	  | Pricing | Varies by customer and product, generally **market-reflective within a range agreed with the customer** |
	  | Fulfillment safeguards | Includes **deposits**, so that purchase plans turn into actual orders |
	  | Coverage | **More than 50% of total capacity for 2027 and 2028**, with a plan to keep it at a similar level over the medium to long term |
	- **"Effectively sold out" through 2029**: LTA volume plus demand under discussion means DRAM and HBM volume for the next three years is "**effectively sold out**." The volumes under discussion rest on customers' concrete purchasing plans.
	- **Keeping some capacity open**: SK hynix uses non-LTA capacity to "flexibly respond to market conditions and demand for next generation products." Contracts give it a floor, and open capacity keeps it exposed to rising prices.
	- **Customers want longer terms**: management reads requests for extensions, "based on our product roadmap and supply capabilities," as LTAs evolving into longer-term business partnerships.
	- *Memo's reading, compared with Micron's FQ4-26 call* ([[MU-2026-Q3]]):
		- | Item | SK hynix | Micron |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Agreements | 14 customers | 26 SCAs |
		  | Coverage | Over 50% of **capacity** for 2027–28 | Over 35% of **revenue** through 2030. More than 75% of 2027 output committed, counting SCA and non-SCA customers |
		  | Long-run target | "Similar" to over 50% | About 50% of revenue |
		  | Pricing | Range-bound and market-reflective | Three-quarters of SCA revenue with a set framework, mostly floor and ceiling bands |
		- Both suppliers are converging on the same model: contract about half of supply, with deposits and price bands, and leave the rest open.

- ## 2. Demand: why management says AI investment is durable
	- **Market estimates SK hynix cited** (from market research firms):
		- Global CSP capex grows **60%+ in 2027**, and **secured power capacity 100%+**.
		- The global AI data-center infrastructure market grows at a **40% CAGR through 2030**.
	- **Agentic AI is the demand driver**: "as AI plans complex tasks and carries them out over extended periods using multiple tools," compute needs and the data generated, transferred and stored rise sharply. Memory demand across all segments is "significantly exceeding initial expectations."
	- **The answer to the monetization question** (Peter Lee, Citi), from Joondeok Park, Head of DRAM Marketing:
		- **The spending is paying off**:
			- Major hyperscalers report **cloud revenue up 30–80% YoY**, much of it from AI.
			- **Cloud order backlogs are up 2–5× YoY**.
			- Long-term enterprise AI contracts and paying users are growing.
			- Management calls current pacing a matter of customers checking each project's feasibility and timing.
		- **The buyer base is broadening** (Park): "Early AI investment was concentrated among the big tech, so a single company adjusting its investment could directly result in overall demand volatility." Now non-tech companies, **sovereign AI** and **on-prem** buyers add demand. Park calls that "a structural shift where investment adjustment by some would be offset by increased demand from others." For example, policy-backed sovereign spending can fill gaps when interest rates slow private projects.
		- **Workarounds for power**: customers are spreading deployments across multiple sites, using small and mid-size data centers where power is available, and upgrading on-site power generation and cooling.
	- **How demand evolves**: enterprise and personal AI drive near-term growth. **Physical AI** (autonomous driving, robotics) broadens demand in the medium to long term.
	- **Consumer devices lose out**: PC and mobile shipments are expected to **decline** because of rising memory costs and shortages. AI infrastructure more than offsets that. In Q2, management called the PC and phone constraint temporary.
	- **2027 "achievable" bit growth**, as limited by supply rather than demand:
		- DRAM **mid-20%** and NAND **high-teens %**, similar to 2026.
		- Micron, by comparison, sees industry DRAM bits growing low-20s % in 2027 and NAND mid-20s %. The two companies differ most on NAND.

- ## 3. Supply: structurally constrained through 2030
	- **Management's claim**: demand will outpace supply **through 2030**, "given the complexities involved in implementing leading-edge processes and the lead times required to construct new production facilities." Near term, SK hynix can't fully meet customers' volume requirements.
	- **"More fabs does not necessarily mean more supply"** (CFO Woo-hyun Kim):
		- HBM and complex processes need more resources per bit.
		- "Increasing bit output with finer technology is becoming more and more limited."
		- Investment builds a base for node migration and high-value products, not just raw capacity.
	- **Phased investment to manage overbuild risk** (asked by Simon Woo, BofA):
		- **Shells first, cleanrooms in phases**: SK hynix will accelerate construction of the building shells at the **Yongin cluster and M17**, but open cleanrooms in phases. Equipment move-in and production ramps will follow demand.
		- **Demand checks go beyond forecasts**: SK hynix reviews "the customer's CapEx or demand forecast" plus "**their financial health and funding capability**, as well as the readiness of key supply chains."
		- **Its own balance sheet** is also a constraint: a net-cash target and appropriate cash holdings.
		- If conditions change, it will adjust investment spending, production and resource allocation.
	- *Memo's reading*: screening customers' **funding ability** is new language. It acknowledges that some AI demand depends on customer financing (see the OpenAI and neocloud funding questions elsewhere in the repo).
	- **Capex**: 2027 capex will be **significantly higher** than 2026, covering equipment for more advanced nodes, the **Yongin fab 1 (Y1)** ramp, and faster infrastructure investment in Korea and overseas.

- ## 4. HBM: HBM4 crossover, the yield lead, custom HBM and fewer layers per stack
	- **HBM4**:
		- Full-scale mass production and shipments began in Q3.
		- In Q4, HBM4 passes HBM3E in **both shipment and revenue share** and becomes the flagship product.
		- **M15X** is being ramped faster, to reach full capacity by the end of 2026.
		- 12-high HBM4 is shipping on the schedule agreed with major customers.
	- **Yields**:
		- SK hynix claims "industry best level yield" in both its fabs and back-end processes.
		- The back-end figure covers "the entire process, not just individual steps like bump, stack, or KGSD test."
		- **HBM4 beat its planned yield, so the target was revised up.**
		- HBM3E has passed its planned mature yield.
	- **HBM4E and custom HBM**:
		- HBM4E samples shipped in **Q2**. Development and qualification follow customers' timelines.
		- Management sees "**growing importance of custom HBM**, optimized for each customer's system and workloads," and is working with major customers on such projects.
		- See [[2026-11-20-dram-commodity-vs-cpu-gpu-custom-hbm4-switching-friction]] and [[2026-11-20-tsmc-custom-hbm-base-die-strategic-leverage]].
	- **2027 contracts**: negotiations with major customers for 2027 volume and pricing are **finalized**. They "faithfully reflect the increased product value of HBM4," so "the profitability gap between HBM and conventional DRAM [is expected] to **narrow meaningfully**." That implies HBM currently earns less than conventional DRAM, the same inversion Micron described.
	- **Fewer layers per stack is a sign of shortage** (asked by Dong-hee Han, SK Securities). Kitae Kim, Head of HBM Sales and Marketing:
		- **Why customers do it**: "The recent moves by some customers to adjust HBM specifications illustrate that demand is indeed overwhelming supply." With fewer layers, customers "increase the number of cubes [stacks] that can be obtained for the same capacity," so they can build more accelerators.
		- **Capacity loss is partly offset**: going "from **12-stack HBM4 to eight-stack HBM4E**," higher capacity per die offsets a substantial part of the loss.
		- **Customers want more than double SK hynix's supply**: "Demand remains so strong that customers' volume requirement is **more than double** that of what we can supply today."
		- **Supply stays just as tight**: the same bit volume in shorter stacks needs **more stacks**, so it needs **more base dies and more back-end processing**. SK hynix has already built this into its plans.
		- **Profitability**: depends on mix, yield, back-end productivity and cost, not on layer count alone. In 2027 SK hynix expects HBM revenue and profit to "grow significantly" and profitability to rise meaningfully. Looking ahead, "far above the peak in past cycles."
		- *Memo's reading*: the reported cut in HBM stack height ([[2026-09-14-semianalysis-four-high-hbm-rubin-ultra-capacity-cut]]) shifts demand toward base dies and packaging, which favors TSMC on base dies and the packaging lines. Micron gave a similar answer on its FQ4 call.
	- **Graphics (including HBM) is a shrinking share of DRAM revenue** (memo reading of the deck's DRAM revenue-by-application chart):
		- | Share of DRAM revenue (≈, read from the chart) | Q3 2025 | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 |
		  |---|---|---|---|
		  | Server | ≈33% | ≈49% | **≈55%** |
		  | Graphics, including HBM | ≈42% | ≈18% | **≈16%** |
		  | Mobile | ≈14% | ≈18% | ≈14% |
		  | PC | ≈9% | ≈10% | ≈10% |
		  | Consumer | ≈2% | ≈5% | ≈5% |
		- *Memo calculation*: DRAM revenue was about \$13.6B in Q3 2025 and \$57.9B in Q3 2026.
			- Graphics including HBM rose from about **\$5.7B to \$9.2B (about 1.6×)**.
			- Server DRAM rose from about **\$4.5B to \$31.7B (about 7×)**.
		- In one year, server DRAM's price surge cut the HBM-containing segment from about 40% to about one-sixth of DRAM revenue. This is the most direct evidence yet for the mix shift in [[2026-11-21-micron-sk-hynix-hbm-vs-conventional-dram-revenue-mix]].
		- **Caveat**: SK hynix's application categories may not map exactly to HBM. Some HBM may be booked under server.

- ## 5. Conventional DRAM: server DDR5 and SOCAMM lead
	- **Q3 shipments**: server DDR5 grew **more than 40% QoQ**, driving DRAM's roughly 10% bit growth. DRAM prices rose by a high-20s percentage, similar to Q2, on strong conventional pricing and more HBM4 in the mix.
	- **Q4 shipments**: DRAM bit shipments grow by a **mid-20s percentage QoQ**, from the expansion of the **1c nm** node plus the M15X HBM4 ramp.
	- **Product focus**: SK hynix is quickly raising the share of 1c nm server products, and expanding **1c DDR5** and **LPDDR5 SOCAMM for AI servers**.

- ## 6. NAND joins the AI memory hierarchy through KV-cache tiering
	- **The shift** (Chang-seok Song, Head of NAND Marketing, asked by Young-ho Ryu, NH):
		- "As agentic AI and long context use cases spread, the size of KV cache is growing rapidly, making it difficult to store it all in HBM or DRAM due to capacity and cost constraints."
		- So **tiered memory** (HBM, then DRAM, then SSD, by access frequency) is becoming important.
		- SSDs already hold offloaded KV cache in some systems. Their role should expand into a **shared context-memory tier** across servers and GPUs.
	- **SK hynix's tiering technologies**:
		- | Technology | What it does |
		  |---|---|
		  | **SALT-KV** (semantic-aware lifecycle tiering for KV) | Software that places KV-cache data across HBM, DRAM and SSD based on each item's reuse value and storage cost |
		  | **CMM-Hybrid** | CXL-based hybrid memory: stores the **entire KV cache on SSD**, predicts which data will be needed, and **preloads it into DRAM** |
		  | eSSD validation | System demonstrations on real AI workloads to define eSSD requirements for inference, ready for customers' and platform partners' architectures |
		  | **HBF** (high-bandwidth flash) | Medium to long term: a new tier between HBM and SSD that combines high bandwidth with NAND's capacity and cost efficiency |
	- **NAND operations**:
		- Q3 NAND bits fell slightly as sales shifted toward **321-layer TLC enterprise SSDs**. NAND prices rose by a mid-20s percentage.
		- In Q4, 321-layer production passes **50% of domestic capacity**, and NAND bits grow mid-single digits.
	- **SSDs are taking over NAND revenue** (≈, read from the deck's NAND revenue-by-application chart, USD basis including Solidigm):
		- SSDs were about **62%** of NAND revenue in Q3 2025, about **74%** in Q2 2026 and about **78%** in Q3 2026.
		- Mobile was about 21%, 17% and 15%.
	- **Related**: [[2026-06-06-nvidia-cmx-kv-cache-flash-storage-tier]], [[2026-07-16-weka-kv-cache-nand-inference-memory-podcast]], [[2026-11-20-kv-cache-reads-llm-inference-gross-margin]], [[2026-07-04-dram-crisis-memory-tiering-semi-industry-observation]].

- ## 7. DRAM technology roadmap: extend 6F², then 4F² or 3D DRAM
	- **Plan** (Joondeok Park, asked by Young-gun Kim, Mirae Asset):
		- Push the current **6F²** cell as far as possible through process maturity and **EUV-based scaling**, then move to **4F² or 3D DRAM**.
		- SK hynix says it has "secured the technological ability to further extend our current 6F² platform," which is the most efficient path for now.
	- **Why development and commercialization are timed differently**: a new cell structure needs yield, reliability and cost competitiveness, plus product design and customer qualification. So SK hynix is following "a step-by-step roadmap covering from the extreme scaling of the existing DRAM to 4F² and 3D DRAM," applied "when it is ready technologically and economically."
	- *Memo's reading*: SK hynix is deliberately not racing to switch cell structures, unlike peers' publicized 4F² and 3D DRAM programs. See [[2026-06-07-dram-deep-dive-hbm-3d-dram-memo]] and [[2026-11-08-tsmc-3d-dram-report-embedded-oxide-memory-not-commodity]].

- ## 8. Capital allocation: returning investment gains, Solidigm IPO dropped
	- **Solidigm IPO dropped**: Solidigm, the SSD business SK hynix acquired from Intel, had explored a pre-IPO round or an IPO to fund manufacturing expansion. Because of market volatility and "the correction in semiconductor stocks," it **stopped the review**. It will fund growth in AI data-center storage by other means.
	- **Gains on investments go to shareholders**: SK hynix's principle is to return most gains on strategic investments.
		- Most of its **Kioxia** gains will be paid out as **special dividends**, part in Q3 and the rest with the FY2026 year-end dividend.
	- **The FY2026 return package**:
		- | Item | Amount |
		  |---|---|
		  | Total FY2026 shareholder return | About **\$73.8B** |
		  | Cash dividends | **\$22.1B or more** |
		  | Share buybacks with cancellation | **\$44.3B or more**, including the \$29.5B announced in August, which is completed and about to be canceled |
		  | Q3 dividend | **\$14.75 per share** (\$7.38 from earnings plus a \$7.38 Kioxia special), totaling \$10.3B |
		  | Q3 buyback | \$14.8B, including about \$7.4B for employee compensation, which doesn't count toward the return total |
	- **The underlying policy** (FY2025–27): a fixed dividend of \$1.11 per share, plus additional returns of **over 50% of three-year cumulative free cash flow**.
	- **Buybacks continue every quarter**, sized by the board on share price, market conditions and cash flow.
	- The CFO said the shares are **undervalued**.
	- Dividends are set at a sustainable level by benchmarking historical and peer dividend yields.
