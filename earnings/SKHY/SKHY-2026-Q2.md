- ## SK hynix Q2 2026 Earnings
	- **Quarter covered**: Q2 2026
	- **Sources**: SK hynix Q2 2026 earnings deck; SK hynix Newsroom, “SK hynix Announces 2Q26 Financial Results”
	- **Thesis**: SK hynix's record quarter was driven by simultaneous volume and pricing strength in DRAM and NAND, but the more durable signal is the company's shift from spot-cycle exposure toward scarce, technically differentiated memory secured through multi-year customer agreements. Management is accelerating capacity because demand exceeds supply, while HBM4, SOCAMM2, and enterprise SSDs broaden the AI-memory opportunity beyond HBM alone.

- ## Pricing and Operating Leverage
	- | Decision-useful metric | Q2 2026 result | Change and interpretation |
	  |---|---:|---|
	  | Revenue | **KRW 79.3T** | **+51% QoQ; +257% YoY** as both bit shipments and ASPs increased |
	  | Operating profit | **KRW 60.5T** | **+61% QoQ; +557% YoY**; fifth consecutive quarterly record |
	  | Operating margin | **76%** | Up from **72%** in Q1 and **41%** a year earlier, showing extreme scarcity-driven operating leverage |
	  | Gross margin | **83%** | Up **4pp QoQ**, despite continued investment in new products and capacity |
	  | DRAM shipments / ASP | **High-single-digit bit growth; ~30% ASP increase QoQ** | Pricing, rather than volume alone, remained the principal earnings driver |
	  | NAND shipments / ASP | **Mid-teens bit growth; mid-50% ASP increase QoQ** | NAND pricing accelerated faster than DRAM and lifted NAND to **27%** of revenue from **21%** in Q1 |
	- The quarter was not simply an HBM result. Conventional server DRAM and NAND benefited from the same supply scarcity, while AI infrastructure increased demand across HBM, server DRAM, and enterprise SSDs.
	- Q2 product mix was **73% DRAM and 27% NAND**, versus **78% and 21%** in Q1. NAND gained mix despite DRAM's strong quarter because both NAND bit shipments and pricing rose more sharply.
	- First-half revenue exceeded **KRW 100T** for the first time. This marks a step-change in the scale of the business, although current margins should be interpreted in the context of unusually tight industry supply.

- ## Supply Scarcity Is Changing Customer Behavior
	- Management said customer demand continues to exceed the company's ability to supply. Timely volume delivery has therefore become a competitive advantage alongside performance, power efficiency, and cost.
	- SK hynix has concluded long-term agreements with approximately **10 customers**, including strategic and key partners, and is discussing additional agreements.
		- Structures vary by customer and product and can span multiple years.
		- Financial mechanisms such as deposits support contract fulfillment and improve medium- to long-term demand visibility.
		- The agreements are intended to reduce price volatility for both supplier and customer, not merely lock in current spot pricing.
	- The contracting shift is important because it can make future earnings less exposed to traditional memory-cycle volatility. The durability of that change will depend on how contracts behave when supply eventually loosens.
	- Management framed additional supply as a way to unlock latent demand rather than create excess inventory. That is credible while customers remain allocation-constrained, but the aggressive capacity response raises the importance of demand discipline beyond 2027.

- ## AI Memory Roadmap
	- **HBM4**
		- Mass shipments began in Q2, with a full ramp planned for the second half of 2026.
		- Management said the product achieved customer-required speed, industry-leading power efficiency, and cost competitiveness.
		- Beginning mass shipment before the full ramp preserves SK hynix's execution advantage as accelerator platforms move from HBM3E to HBM4.
	- **HBM4E**
		- SK hynix supplied 1c-nanometer-based samples to a major customer in the first half.
		- The company selected the 1c process for process maturity and mass-production stability, signaling that yield and reliable volume matter as much as peak specifications.
	- **SOCAMM2**
		- Supply of 1c-based SOCAMM2 commenced in earnest, and Q2 sales increased significantly.
		- Management is preparing additional samples and aligning the product lineup with customer schedules to broaden adoption.
		- SOCAMM2 extends the AI-memory opportunity into low-power, high-capacity server memory and reduces reliance on HBM as the only differentiated growth vector.
	- **321-layer NAND**
		- The node became the largest share of NAND production in Q1.
		- SK hynix targets approximately **50% of domestic NAND production capacity** on 321-layer technology by year-end 2026.
		- The transition supports higher-density enterprise SSDs and should improve NAND cost competitiveness as Solidigm and SK hynix increase SSD shipments.
	- Product development is becoming more collaborative and system-level. Management emphasized co-development, system architecture, and packaging, implying that memory suppliers are moving closer to accelerator and server platform design.

- ## Capacity Response and Capital Allocation
	- 2026 capital expenditure is expected in the **high KRW 40T range**. Management is balancing faster supply growth against investment efficiency and financial resilience.
	- The M15X production schedule is being pulled forward to respond to near-term shortages.
	- Yongin Fab 1's cleanroom is scheduled to open in early 2027, followed by rapid capacity expansion.
	- Longer-term projects include the P&T7 advanced-packaging facility, the M17 NAND production base, and additional domestic clusters. Management said investments will be phased according to customer demand and expected returns.
	- The capacity plan is broader than wafer starts: HBM growth also requires advanced packaging, while high-capacity AI SSD demand requires NAND process migration and controller/system integration.

- ## Company Outlook
	- | Market or company metric | Outlook | Read-through |
	  |---|---:|---|
	  | 2026 DRAM demand bit growth | **Mid-20% YoY** | AI servers and conventional server memory remain the primary structural drivers |
	  | 2026 NAND demand bit growth | **High-teens YoY** | Enterprise SSD demand is becoming a larger AI-infrastructure vector |
	  | Q3 DRAM company bit growth | **~10% QoQ** | Growth will focus on server products |
	  | Q3 NAND company bit growth | **Low-single-digit QoQ**, including Solidigm | Mix should shift toward 321-layer NAND and SSDs |
	- Management expects Big Tech capital expenditure and memory procurement to keep growing because AI services are producing visible returns while compute remains scarce.
	- More capable agentic AI should increase memory content across HBM, server DRAM, and SSDs. At the same time, better model and software efficiency lowers the cost of AI access and can expand the total infrastructure market rather than simply reduce hardware demand.
	- PC and smartphone production has been temporarily constrained by difficulty securing memory. Management expects momentum to recover as supply tightness eases and on-device AI adoption broadens.
	- The outlook assumes that easing supply constraints converts deferred customer demand into shipments. A weaker interpretation would be that current shortages are pulling forward procurement; the multi-year agreements and customer deposits provide some evidence against that, but do not eliminate cycle risk.

- ## Earnings Quality and Financial Capacity
	- Net profit of **KRW 93.9T** exceeded operating profit because net non-operating profit reached **KRW 62.2T**, including approximately **KRW 63.3T** of investment-asset-related gains. Operating profit and cash generation are therefore more representative of the quarter's underlying economics than net income.
	- Operating cash flow was **KRW 65.7T**, while cash increased by **KRW 33.6T QoQ** to **KRW 88.0T**.
	- Debt declined to **KRW 18.6T**, leaving net cash of approximately **KRW 69.4T** and a **-26% net-debt-to-equity ratio**.
	- Working capital absorbed **KRW 32.2T**: accounts receivable rose by **KRW 14.0T QoQ** and inventories by **KRW 2.0T**. The increase is consistent with rapid revenue growth and production expansion, but receivables and inventory should remain important checks on cash conversion.
	- The balance sheet now supports accelerated fabs and packaging capacity, continued technology migration, and potential additional shareholder returns without relying on incremental leverage.
