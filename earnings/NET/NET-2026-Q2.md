- ## Cloudflare Q2 2026: non-human traffic passes human, Workers starts to show up in revenue, and gross margin stabilizes
	- **Source**: User-provided FactSet corrected transcript, Cloudflare Q2 2026 earnings call, August 6, 2026, 20 pages. Page references are to that PDF.
		- Speakers: Matthew Prince (CEO) and Thomas Seifert (CFO). Financial measures other than revenue are non-GAAP unless stated.
		- Customer examples and competitive claims are management's accounts.
		- Related: [[NET-2026-Q1]] (the quarter before), [[NET-2026-Q3]] (the quarter after), [[2026-06-25-mad-podcast-matthew-prince-cloudflare-agentic-internet]], [[2026-11-10-circuit-cloudflare-q3-customer-surge-agentic-enterprise]].
	- **Theme**: the first quarter in which several lagging metrics turned at once.
		- Net retention, large-customer growth and gross margin all improved sequentially.
		- Prince said Workers has "tipped" from a product played for adoption to "a meaningful contributor to revenue."
		- The strategic story moved further toward Act 4: payment rails for agents, built on the claim that more than half of Cloudflare's traffic is now non-human.
- ## Metrics that turned
	- | Signal | Q2 2026 | Why it matters |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Dollar-based net retention | **120%**, up 2 points QoQ and 6 points YoY | Expansion by the largest customers drove it. It rose again to 122% in Q3 |
	  | Large customers (>\$100K a year) | **4,698**, up **27%** YoY, accelerating from 25% in Q1. 282 added in the quarter and a record **986** over 12 months | Every tier from \$100K to \$5M+ set a record for YoY net additions. Large customers were **73%** of revenue, against 71% a year earlier |
	  | Gross margin | **73.1%**, up 30bp QoQ, the first sequential rise in **eight quarters**, but down 320bp YoY | The shift of network cost from sales and marketing to cost of revenue (paid traffic growing faster than free) is "beginning to stabilize." It fell back to 72.4% in Q3 |
	  | RPO | **\$2.732B**, up 7% QoQ and **38%** YoY. Current RPO is 64% of the total and grew 35% | RPO growing faster than revenue (36%) |
	  | Paying customers | **More than 80,000 added** in the quarter, up **74%** YoY | As many as Cloudflare had in total at its 2019 IPO. Q3 then added about 158,000 |
	  | Developers on the platform | **More than 7.4M**, nearly **2M added in Q2**, against 1.5M in all of 2025 | Prince "made the team triple check" the number |
	- **Sales and pipeline** (p. 4):
		- Sales productivity rose YoY for the **10th consecutive quarter** (9th in Q1).
		- New-customer bookings grew at the fastest rate in more than five years.
		- New pipeline again grew sequentially at its fastest pace in five years.
	- **Partners** were **31%** of revenue (30% in Q1). Prince thinks the share could exceed 50%, "even maybe over 60%," though never the 90%+ of first-generation Zero Trust vendors (p. 16).
- ## Non-human traffic passed human traffic, much earlier than forecast
	- ^^**"For the first time in human history, in Q2, more than 50% of the traffic flowing across Cloudflare's network was not human."**^^ (Prince, p. 6)
	- **How the forecast moved** (p. 8):
		- In November 2025, Cloudflare's data pointed to a crossover in the second half of 2027.
		- In March 2026, the same exercise pointed to the first half of 2027.
		- In May 2026, the team told Prince it had already happened.
	- **Prince's projection**: "with the big caveat that I have called it wrong at every point along the way," non-human traffic could be up to **1,000×** human traffic in five years, making humans "a rounding error." That is because non-human traffic is growing so fast, not because human traffic falls. In Q3 he shortened this to less than five years.
	- **How the traffic is handled** (pp. 8–9):
		- Malicious traffic, including AI scraping that a media customer doesn't want, is blocked at **no extra charge**.
		- Wanted agent traffic has to be served far more efficiently.
		- More than **80% of the major AI companies** are Cloudflare customers.
- ## Act 4: building payment rails for agents
	- **Launched since the start of Q3** (Content Independence Day, then Agents Week during the call, p. 6):
		- **Monetization Gateway** lets customers sell any resource behind Cloudflare: a web page, an API, a data set or an MCP tool.
		- **Wallets** let buyers pay autonomously through their agents.
		- **cloudflare.pay** gives merchants and buyers an agent-friendly way to identify themselves and establish trust.
		- A first-of-its-kind research pilot with **OpenAI** on a sustainable model for content creators and AI companies.
	- **Prince's sizing of the problem** (p. 13), which he calls rough, unaudited and partly from memory:
		- | Quantity | Estimate |
		  |---|---|
		  | Requests through Cloudflare's network | About **0.5 billion per second** |
		  | Share that could carry a micropayment | **1%–10%**, at tiny fractions of a cent each |
		  | Payment capacity needed | About **10M** transactions per second at launch, scaling to about **100M** |
		  | Visa's holiday peak, for comparison | About **20,000** transactions per second |
		- So the system has to be about **three orders of magnitude bigger than Visa**. Cloudflare looked for a partner, but "a lot of the world that's building … next generation payments networks thinks that they're competing with Visa."
	- **Distribution**: more than 20% of the Internet already sits behind Cloudflare, much of it on free plans. Prince asks, "What if we made it less than free?" Free customers could be paid from micropayments that agents generate (pp. 13–14).
	- **The pitch**: the last 27 years of Internet business models were "really defined by Google" and advertising. The next 27 will look very different (p. 9).
	- **Competitive moat**: Prince deflects the moat question and doesn't name competitors. He gives no adoption timeline or revenue target. Q3 reported the first Act 4 revenue ([[NET-2026-Q3]]).
- ## Workers: from adoption to revenue
	- **The shift** (p. 14): "for a while, we were saying that we were playing for adoption … I think that has tipped." Workers is now "a meaningful contributor to revenue," though Cloudflare still doesn't break out product revenue.
	- **Where the developers come from** (p. 10):
		- Workers suits agents: it is lightweight, charges only for work done and spins up and down quickly.
		- Developer counts are conservative. A big company with many engineers can count as one account.
		- Vibe-coding platforms (**Lovable, Replit, Base44 at Wix**) increasingly deploy to Cloudflare. Prince's EA builds household tools and deploys them on Cloudflare.
	- **Why Workers rather than containers** (p. 17): agents need short-lived, cheap places to write code, run inference, store data and reach the network, coordinated across many locations.
		- Inference is "one piece of a more complicated puzzle."
		- Containers are too heavy. If every knowledge worker ran an agent in a container, there wouldn't be enough CPU in the world. Isolates are the lighter sandbox.
		- Cloudflare owns the "the network is the computer" trademark, which Oracle let lapse after buying Sun.
	- **Pipeline ranking** (p. 15): Workers (Act 3) is still the fastest-growing act, followed by SASE (Act 2), unchanged from Investor Day.
	- **Workers pulls in pool-of-funds deals** (pp. 14–15): developer teams ask that Workers be included in Zero Trust and reverse-proxy contracts, which drives consumption. The average size of the deals Cloudflare cites as wins has risen each quarter, and \$10M+ deals still need enterprise salespeople.
- ## Agent security is driving Zero Trust wins
	- "The number one thing that's causing our phone to ring from big companies" is wanting to do AI more securely (p. 11).
	- Prince says Cloudflare sidesteps competitors' human-seat framing by saying customers will have even more agents than humans and need a security model for them.
	- **Anecdote** (p. 11): a large UK government agency was well along with a first-generation Zero Trust vendor for a big part of the UK government. When the talk turned to agents, it became clear the vendor hadn't thought about them. The agency **canceled the RFP** and is reevaluating with an agents-first approach.
	- Prince says Cloudflare's SASE and Zero Trust platform has "gain[ed] share pretty significantly in the last six months."
	- **Cloudflare OS** (pp. 10–11, 16): the internal AI harness behind last quarter's efficiency claims has been open-sourced.
		- Prince says its edge is security: secure, auditable, sampled access to systems of record, so the whole company can use powerful AI tools.
		- Large systems integrators want to implement it for clients, which Prince expects to drive Workers adoption.

- ## Margins, capex and the business model
	- **Unit economics, not gross margin** (p. 9): Seifert expects gross margin to "stabilize around the level we have," while total unit economics expand through the second half.
	- **Network capex** was only **7%** of revenue in Q2, against a full-year expectation of **14%–15%** (p. 7). That implies a heavy second half.
	- **Restructuring** (p. 7): Q2 severance and restructuring charges were **\$151M**, of which \$99M was paid. The full-year estimate rose to **up to \$165M** (up to \$130M cash), "higher than initially anticipated" because Cloudflare prioritized speed, especially internationally. Headcount ended at **4,700**.
	- **GAAP profitability** (p. 15): the Investor Day target was "by 2028 at the latest." Seifert says Cloudflare is "pacing ahead," with a Q2 GAAP net loss of about **\$18M** excluding restructuring. Q3 then pulled the target forward to Q4 2026.
	- **Forecasting gets harder** (p. 12): revenue is moving from ratable SaaS to a mix of pool of funds, consumption and "T-shirt sizes." Customers burn through commitments faster and renew early, so results vary more from customer to customer. Seifert says that is why guidance stays "prudent."
	- **Not joining the GPU arms race** (p. 19), asked after a competitor disclosed nearly \$3B of AI infrastructure contracts that night:
		- Prince: renting commodity servers on Cloudflare's balance sheet is "bad business for us." Not all revenue is created equal.
		- Hyperscaler GPU utilization is "super low" because customers lack traffic diversity and scheduling. Cloudflare says it can get up to **10×** more utilization per capex dollar.
		- Hyperscalers buy a server and lease it back for "5 turns of revenue." Cloudflare sells "work getting done."
		- He leaves room for deeper, non-commodity work with the largest AI providers, all of which are customers.
		- *My note*: the competitor isn't named in the transcript.
	- **Growth ceiling** (pp. 12–13):
		- Seifert says the TAM grew from **\$30B** at the IPO to more than **\$300B**.
		- Maximum deal size rises with each act: about \$1M in Act 1, tens of millions in Act 2 and hundreds of millions in Act 3.
		- Larger customer cohorts grow faster.
		- Prince "won't be upset if we get back to even higher growth rates."
- ## My notes
	- **Q2 set up Q3**: DNR (120% to 122%), paying-customer adds (80K to 158K), developers (7.4M to 10M) and operating margin all improved again in [[NET-2026-Q3]]. Gross margin did not hold its uptick. Seifert's guidance that Q3 would grow only 31% proved conservative, since Q3 grew 39%, which fits his warning that consumption makes revenue harder to forecast.
	- **The Circuit's figure was off**: Goldberg's Q3 comparison of about 158K paying-customer adds against "20–30K a quarter" ([[2026-11-10-circuit-cloudflare-q3-customer-surge-agentic-enterprise]]) understates the base. Q2 had already added more than 80K. Q3 was about 2× the prior record, not 5–8× the usual pace.
	- **Small and large are both growing**: the 80K paying customers are mostly small developers, by Prince's own account. Enterprise strength shows separately in record large-customer adds in every tier and in DNR. The two signals shouldn't be merged.
	- **Act 4 is still a plan**: the Visa comparison shows how hard it is, and nothing was quantified on revenue or adoption. The tests are whether Monetization Gateway and Wallets get adopted by publishers and whether the OpenAI pilot leads to paid crawl deals.
	- **Unanswered**: Colville's question on whether rising cyber risk was already in Q2 numbers got no direct answer from Seifert in the transcript.
