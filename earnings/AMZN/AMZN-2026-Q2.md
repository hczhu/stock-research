- **Source**: Amazon Q2 2026 earnings call, July 30, 2026 (Andy Jassy, Brian Olsavsky, Dave Fildes).
- **Related memos**: [[2026-05-27-aws-bedrock-anthropic-hyperscaler-margins]] argued AWS was the only hyperscaler with a genuine rising-margin trend on Bedrock TaaS economics — the 39% AWS margin this quarter is the strongest confirmation yet. [[2026-04-09-Trainium]] and [[2026-03-20-asic-vs-gpu-trainium]] cover the silicon thesis now showing up as a \$25B+ run rate.
- **Theme**: **AWS re-accelerated for a fifth straight quarter to 36.7% — its fastest in 18 quarters at more than double the revenue base — while margin expanded to 39%.** Jassy spent unusual airtime laying out the *capital cycle* underpinning \$220B of 2026 capex, an implicit answer to the ROIC bear case. Capex was raised by \$20B for one reason: **memory cost**.

- ## AWS: the acceleration and what's behind it
	- | Metric | Q2 2026 | Note |
	  |------------------------|--------------|--------------------------------------------------|
	  | Revenue | \$42.2B | **+36.7% YoY**, 5th consecutive quarter of acceleration |
	  | QoQ revenue added | **+\$4.6B** | ~**80% more than the largest increase ever** |
	  | Annualized run rate | \$169B | Would rank **24th on the Fortune 500** standalone |
	  | Backlog | **\$496B** | Growing **triple digits** YoY |
	  | Operating income | \$16.6B | **39% margin**, +650bps YoY (**+520bps ex-derivative gain**) |
	  | AI revenue run rate | **>\$25B** | Triple-digit growth |
	  | Chips run rate | **>\$25B** | Triple-digit growth |
	- Fastest growth in **18 quarters, "back when AWS was less than half its current revenue size"** — the base-effect framing is the point, not the headline rate.
	- **The AI→core flywheel is the mechanistic claim worth testing**: post-training RL and agentic tool use run **mostly on CPUs, not accelerators**, so AI adoption pulls core consumption with it. Graviton is positioned as the beneficiary at **30–40% better price-performance**. This is Amazon's structural argument for why its AI growth is less margin-dilutive than peers'.
	- Olsavsky on margin durability: *"the profitability you're seeing from AWS isn't random"* — attributed to capacity optimization (a notable Q2 benefit), disciplined fixed-cost management, and custom silicon/network gear. He repeatedly cautioned margins **will fluctuate**.
	- **Capacity**: on track to **double power capacity by end-2027 vs 2025**. Still, *"even at [\$220B] we will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026, and I believe this dynamic will also be true in 2027."*
	- Long-term framing revised upward: AWS previously seen as "a few hundred billion" revenue business, now **"at least double that, and very possibly a trillion-dollar annual revenue business."** Anchored on **85% of global IT spend still being on-premises**.

- ## The Capital Cycle Disclosure — the most substantive new content
	- **Full treatment, derived math, and gaps in the framework**: [[2026-07-30-jassy-aws-data-center-server-capital-cycle]].
	- Jassy explicitly separated two capex components with different economics, an unusually direct rebuttal to the depreciation/ROIC bear case:
		- | Component | Lead time | Payback | Useful life |
		  |----------------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------|----------------------|
		  | **Data centers** | Spent **~2 years before** servers can be installed | Revenue "right away" once servers plug in | **30+ years** |
		  | **Servers & networking** | Purchased **a few months** before service | **<3 years** to break even on average | **5–6 years minimum** |
	- **Key consequences he drew**:
		- Server spend is **demand-gated, not committed**: *"we have strong visibility into customer demand before we trigger the spend. If the demand isn't there, we won't spend the capital."* The optionality sits in the shorter-cycle half.
		- Most AI capacity is **contracted for at least five-year terms**, against a <3-year breakeven — meaning **2–3 years of significant FCF per server generation** after payback.
		- A 30+ year data center supports **5–6 generations of server economics**, with later generations structurally better because the upfront shell capital isn't repeated.
		- Near-term: explicit **FCF headwinds** while data centers are built ahead of monetization. Inflection comes when *"revenue growth outpaces the incremental CapEx growth, which will happen at some point."*
		- Track record caveat offered as support: AWS has historically **pulled breakevens forward** and **extended useful life**.
	- **The benchmark to hold management to**: *"we see the margins and returns in AI tracking what we saw with Core at the same point of evolution, actually a little ahead."*

- ## Capex Raised — and it's a memory story
	- **2026 cash capex now ~\$220B, up from ~\$200B.** Jassy's stated reason is singular: **"the higher cost of memory pushing this number up."** Q2 cash capex was \$53.1B.
	- Olsavsky's risk-factor language now explicitly names **"resource and supply volatility, including for memory chips."**
	- On whether contracts absorb this: existing signed deals lock price for the contract duration; **new agreements price in current costs.** Jassy: *"it's no secret right now to any company in the world that there are inflated prices right now on some of the components like memory and hard drives and SSDs."* Cost inflation therefore reprices with a lag, on renewal.
	- Funding: debt already issued this year; Olsavsky said there are "a lot of options" but **declined to commit** to a financing plan.

- ## Silicon: Trainium, Graviton, and a possible merchant pivot
	- **Trainium** — multi-year, **multi-gigawatt commitments from both Anthropic and OpenAI**. Adopters named: unicorns Neurorobotics and Odyssey; startups Twelve Labs, Descartes Labs, Poolside AI, Karakuri, Metagenomi, NetoAI, Splash Music; larger firms **Uber and Pinterest**.
	- **Graviton** — used by **98% of the top 1,000 EC2 customers**; revenue commitments up **nearly 3x QoQ**; **Graviton5 ramping ~2x faster than Graviton4 did**.
	- **The strategically significant answer**: asked about selling Trainium into third-party data centers, Jassy said there is *"an increasing number of customers who are interested in us providing the Trainium chips to them, separate from our cloud, and we're actively having those conversations… I expect there's a real chance we'll do that in the future."* That would convert AWS silicon from a captive cost advantage into a **merchant chip business** competing directly with NVIDIA — watch for this.
	- NVIDIA relationship reaffirmed: AWS will remain "the best place to run NVIDIA chips," with choice framed as the lever that drives inference cost down.

- ## The Barbell: Jassy's demand model, including the hedge
	- | End of barbell | Who | State |
	  |------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
	  | High end | AI labs consuming "gobs and gobs" of compute, plus runaway apps (Claude Code, ChatGPT) | Wildly steep trajectory today |
	  | Low end | Enterprises getting cost avoidance / productivity (customer service, BPA, fraud) | Real value, already deployed |
	  | **The middle** | **Existing enterprise production workloads** — most **not yet** using inference pervasively | **"will be the largest absolute segment"** |
	- **The honest hedge, and the single most important sentence for the bull case**: *"I don't know if the trajectory of that middle part of the barbell will be the same wildly steep trajectory that we've seen with the current barbell AI labs piece."* Amazon's \$1T AWS ambition rests on a segment whose adoption slope management explicitly will not forecast.
	- Demand visibility: lion's share of 2027 capacity **already reserved**; *"the demand we already have for 2028 is striking."*

- ## Frontier Model: "we don't need one" — and they're building one
	- Jassy's position is deliberately two-sided. First: *"AWS and Amazon can have a wildly successful business without its own frontier model"* because *"there is not going to be one model to rule the world."* Bedrock's value is breadth — models "will each leapfrog each other at different times."
	- Then the reversal: **"All that said, we are pursuing our own frontier model."** Three stated reasons — **cost control** (for Amazon's own consumer apps and to push customer prices down), **prioritization control** (training for workloads Amazon specifically cares about), and **speed**.
	- Concrete prediction: *"within the next few years, you're going to have at least a half dozen models that are comparably good to each other. They'll all be in Bedrock, one of them will be ours."* That is a direct commoditization call on frontier models from the largest neutral distributor.
	- **Bedrock traction datapoint** (from the analyst's question, acknowledged by management): customers **spent more on Bedrock this quarter than in all prior quarters combined**.
	- Agentic stack build-out: **Bedrock Agents** adding policies (deterministic controls), payments (autonomous transactions), web search, and the Strands harness. **Kiro** — spec-driven coding agent, claimed **up to 50% more cost-effective** than alternatives, **usage tripled QoQ**. **Amazon Q** now with autonomous background agents and 16 new integrations; enterprise logos include 3M, Allianz, AstraZeneca, BMW, Exxon, FINRA, Hyundai, Intuit, NBA, NFL, Southwest. **AWS Continuum** — new agentic security product that ingests vulnerability backlogs, validates in a sandbox to kill false positives, and recommends fixes.

- ## Retail, Ads, and Cost to Serve
	- **Two one-time items reduced expenses by ~\$1.2B**: ~\$600M tariff refunds (North America; described as "the significant majority of refunds we expect to receive") and ~\$600M from **fair-value remeasurement of energy contracts under derivative accounting** (primarily AWS). The latter is new — Olsavsky noted such adjustments "have not been significant in prior quarters" and **can vary**, so it is a fresh source of AWS margin noise.
	- Segments: North America \$116.2B (+16%), 7.9% margin; International \$42.2B (+15% ex-FX), 4.1% margin. Worldwide paid units **+17%**.
	- **Advertising \$19.8B, +26%** — the standout non-AWS line. Agentic surfaces are now measurably monetizing: shoppers clicking a **sponsored prompt convert 48% more often and spend 21% more**. Ads Agent delivers 8% lower CPI and 6% lower CPA.
	- **Grocery is the retail inflection**: >\$150B gross merchandise sales last year (second-largest US grocer). Same-day perishables now in **2,300 US cities**, where **9 of the top 10 best sellers are perishables**; monthly active perishables customers **+50% since the start of the year**; same-day orders with perishables carry **3x more units**. Jassy called this *"a real needle mover… changing the trajectory of our everyday essentials business."*
	- **Alexa as a commerce funnel**: Alexa for Shopping used by **350M+ customers** in 12 months, active users **nearly doubled QoQ**, interactions **+5x YoY**. US users spend **40%+ more per order**; Alexa+ triers convert to Prime at **~25% higher rates**.
	- **Cost to serve**: robotic arm fleet (Cardinal, Sparrow) to **more than double in 2026**. Headwinds from **fuel inflation tied to the Middle East conflict** and higher line-haul rates from driver capacity limits, partly offset by the April **FBA fuel and logistics surcharge**. Ex-fuel and line-haul, shipping cost growth ran **below unit growth**.
	- **Tariffs**: refunds were small because Amazon forward-bought inventory and **is not the importer of record for most items sold**. Costs were "largely absorbed" rather than passed through; prices average **14% below other retailers** (Profitero).

- ## Guidance and the Prime Day distortion
	- Q3 net sales **\$197–202B**; operating income **\$22.5–26.5B**.
	- **The deceleration is optical**: Prime Day fell in Q2 this year for most large countries including the US, versus entirely in Q3 in 2025. **Adjusting for Prime Day in both years, Q3 YoY growth would be nearly 400bps higher.** FX is a further ~80bps headwind. Management explicitly flagged the modelling noise and said underlying stores trends continue.
