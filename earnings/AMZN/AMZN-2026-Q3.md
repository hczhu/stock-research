- **Source**: Amazon Q3 2026 earnings call, October 29, 2026 (Andy Jassy, Brian Olsavsky, Dave Fildes). Prior-quarter comparisons are from [[AMZN-2026-Q2]].
- **Related memos**: [[2026-11-04-expert-call-aws-bedrock-sales-motion-anthropic-economics]] (Bedrock sales motion and economics), [[2026-05-27-aws-bedrock-anthropic-hyperscaler-margins]], [[2026-04-09-Trainium]], [[2026-11-07-stratechery-ai-on-the-ballot-political-compute-constraint]] (permitting as the next compute constraint).
- **Theme**: **AWS accelerated for a sixth straight quarter to 39.3%, and Jassy put numbers on the long term for the first time: 40%+ growth in each of 2027 and 2028, and a trillion-dollar revenue business in five to ten years.** Backlog rose \$82B in one quarter to \$578B. The rest of the company was steady but muddied by the Prime Day shift: retail grew 6% in North America and ads slowed to 12%. Operating income of \$28.1B beat the top of guidance by \$1.6B, and the 13.9% operating margin is Amazon's highest ever. Jassy's main argument was structural: Amazon's owned data centers and own chips give AWS a cost advantage that neoclouds and GPU-only clouds can't match.

- ## AWS: acceleration, backlog and the trillion-dollar claim
	- | Metric | Q3 2026 | Q2 2026 |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Revenue growth | 39.3% | 36.7% |
	  | QoQ revenue added | \$3.8B | \$4.6B |
	  | Run rate | \$184B | \$169B |
	  | Backlog | **\$578B**, triple-digit growth, "well-diversified" | \$496B |
	  | Operating margin | 38.7% | 39% |
	- **Fastest growth in nearly five years**, despite lapping a quarter a year ago when Amazon added a lot of capacity. Core compute and storage grew at their fastest rates in several years.
	- **Smaller QoQ dollar add**: Justin Post (BofA) asked why the sequential add slowed from \$4.6B to \$3.8B. Jassy's answer: capacity comes online in uneven amounts each quarter, and more is planned over the next eight to ten quarters. The sequential add is set by capacity, not demand.
	- **"An AI accelerator about every 14 seconds"**, and "almost as fast as we install capacity, customers are gobbling it up."
	- **How Jassy gets to \$1 trillion**: large capacity blocks (hundreds of megawatts up to a gigawatt) are reserved well in advance, which gives "very good visibility" into 2027, 2028 and some of 2029. Growing 40% a year from a \$184B run rate compounds quickly: two years at 40% gives ~\$360B. On top of that come use cases that are only starting: enterprises running inference in production, security (some think a significant share of tokens over the next year or two could go to security), coding, AI work companions, and rewriting "virtually every modern enterprise production application" to be agent-capable.
	- **The flywheel claim**: customers want production inference near their data and apps, so a leading core cloud pulls in AI demand. AI then pulls in core demand, because reinforcement learning, post-training and tool use run mostly on CPUs. Jassy contrasted this with "primarily hosting GPUs or renting short-term data center capacity" and said it gives attractive economics "regardless of supply scarcity."
	- **On-premises is still ~85% of global IT spend.** Mainframes and storage area networks, long seen as impossible to move, are now migrating with AWS replacements and AI-driven migration tools.
	- **Inference is "well over half" of AWS's AI revenue** and growing, with substantial training revenue as well. AI revenue is growing triple digits.

- ## AWS margins: why Amazon thinks its cost structure is different
	- **AI margins are improving fast**: Jassy said AI operating margins are "well ahead of where they were on core at a similar time," even with higher memory costs and heavy depreciation. Capex payback keeps getting shorter.
	- **Olsavsky didn't guide margins** but listed the moving parts. Helping: efficiency gains, capacity optimization, fixed-cost control and more custom chips. Hurting: AI growing faster than core at lower margins (a mix effect) and component pricing (memory). The current margin is "not a random output."
	- **The neocloud comparison (Ross Sandler, Barclays)**: Jassy said neoclouds are **about 3,500 basis points worse in operating margin** than long-leased capacity. Most AWS capacity is owned or long-leased, and running on its own chips gives a second advantage. Clouds that rely heavily on colocation or neoclouds, and use the same hardware as everyone else, end up with the same cost structure as everyone else.
	- **Most of Bedrock runs on Trainium**, which keeps prices low for customers and gives margins Amazon likes. As AWS shifts from selling raw infrastructure to selling inference as a service, more of the stack runs on Amazon's own hardware.
	- **Read**: the 3,500bps figure is the sharpest statement yet of the cost gap between hyperscalers and neoclouds, and it reads as aimed at rivals that lease heavily from neoclouds, Microsoft most of all. It reinforces the point in [[2026-11-07-stratechery-ai-on-the-ballot-political-compute-constraint]] that hyperscalers with existing sites are the winners when capacity is scarce.

- ## Bedrock, agents and AI services
	- **Bedrock is the fastest-growing multi-billion-dollar service in AWS history.** Fortune 500 spend is up nearly **7×** YoY and total token volume nearly **17×**. OpenAI models were added recently and are growing quickly; Bedrock-managed Kimi, GLM and **SpaceX** models were just added.
	- **Jassy's market view**: within two to three years there will be **six to eight models** about equally good, or good enough for 95%+ of workloads, each with different strengths (reasoning, coding, vision, latency, cost). Customers will use several, often in the same application, and want one service with broad selection, price performance and security. He thinks the Western world is "very hungry" for Western open models and expects them to arrive. **Bedrock could become as large as EC2.**
	- **Security as a selling point**: inputs and outputs are never shared with model providers or used to train base models. Fine-tuned models are private copies, encrypted in transit and at rest.
	- **AWS Continuum** finds, prioritizes, validates and fixes code vulnerabilities "at machine speed." Customers include Capital One, IBM, Infor, Luminate and Robinhood. It is being integrated into **Claude Code, OpenAI's Codex and Kiro**. Customer interest "exceeds anything we have ever seen for a new security service."
	- **AgentCore**: tens of thousands of customers; **twice as many agents deployed in Q3 as in the prior three quarters combined**.
	- **Turnkey agents**: Kiro (coding), Amazon Connect (contact centers), AWS Transform (migrations) and **Amazon Quick** (AI work companion), which is growing very fast at Mondelez, EchoStar, 3M, Southwest Airlines and DXC.
	- **DuckDB acquisition (DuckLabs)**: DuckDB will become the universal SQL dialect across AWS storage and analytics. Customers write a query once and run it on any AWS analytics engine, with "zero migration or portability costs between query engines" for the first time. Jassy also pointed to its value for agents querying data. This is a direct move against Snowflake and Databricks's engine lock-in.

- ## Custom chips: Trainium to be sold outside AWS from late 2027
	- **Chips revenue is growing triple digits.** Revenue commitments for Graviton and Trainium are each up **more than 20×** YoY. Customers using custom silicon saved more than **\$2B in Q3 alone**.
	- **Graviton**: 30–40% better price performance than x86 alternatives. After Graviton5 launched, backlog for older Graviton instances still more than doubled QoQ.
	- **Trainium**: short-term capacity on older Trainium instances now sells at a premium to the first half. **TorchNeuron** is open-sourced, so existing PyTorch code runs on Trainium without rewriting.
	- **External sales from late 2027**: customers keep asking to use Trainium outside AWS. Jassy framed the trade-off explicitly. Everyone makes advanced chips at the same foundry and gets an allocation. Selling a chip externally books revenue immediately; using it inside AWS earns more total revenue and free cash flow over a five-to-six-year life. Only two companies have both a large cloud and a chip good enough to sell (implicitly Google with TPU), and Amazon has so far chosen differently from the other. He expects both to be "very significant" and will revisit the split yearly.
	- **Read**: Amazon entering merchant accelerator sales makes Trainium a direct Nvidia competitor outside AWS, following Google's move to sell TPUs outside its own cloud. The comment that external sales are worth less per chip than internal use suggests Amazon will keep most of its foundry allocation for AWS.

- ## Capex
	- Q3 cash capex was **\$75.9B**, mostly for AWS and AI. The 2026 total is unchanged at **~\$220B** (raised from ~\$200B in Q2 because of memory costs), and 2027 will be "meaningfully more."
	- **On returns (Doug Anmuth, JPMorgan)**: payback periods keep shortening and useful lives keep being extended. "Would it be more convenient if this type of acceleration and opportunity happened over 25 years and you could spread the CapEx over 25 years? Yeah... but that's not the world that we live in." "We can fund this plan."
	- **Read**: management is no longer defending capex as a bet; it now ties capex to reserved capacity (the \$578B backlog) and a growth target. The risk shifts from demand to execution: power, permits and component costs.

- ## Stores: margins, speed and grocery
	- **Scale and share**: \$90B+ of gross sales added over the last twelve months, about **twice the quarterly gross sales added by the next-largest US competitor**. North America and International margins are each up nearly 10 points over the past few years. About 80% of retail still happens in physical stores.
	- **Delivery speed**: the fastest Q3 ever, with **1.5B+ items delivered same day or overnight** to Prime members. **Amazon Now** (ultrafast) is in 450+ towns and cities, with items ordered up **10×** YoY.
	- **Cost to serve**: on track to fall per unit for a fourth straight year. Shipping costs rose 11% against 12% unit growth; **excluding fuel and line-haul inflation, they rose 8%**, about 400bps below unit growth. An FBA fuel surcharge introduced this year recovers part of the fuel cost.
	- **Grocery** (Mark Mahaney, Evercore): gross sales nearly **\$250B** over the last twelve months. Fresh groceries and Everyday Essentials grew about **3× faster** than the rest of the store and are about half of all units. Customers buying perishables same day buy about **7×** more Everyday Essentials units. Grocery customers shop about **4× more** non-grocery categories and spend about **2× more** on them. Prime members' monthly orders of fresh groceries with free same-day delivery more than tripled in a year. Whole Foods is growing faster than the grocery segment, with the Daily Shop urban format rolling out over the next couple of years. Jassy called grocery the biggest gap between what households spend and what they spend with Amazon. He didn't quantify coverage or uplift per household.
	- **Pharmacy**: customers up 60%+ YoY, with growth accelerating each quarter of 2026; repeat customers up 50%+; three-hour prescription delivery in 350+ cities.
	- **Robotics**: more than **1M robots** in the fulfillment network; the fleet of robotic arms (Cardinal, Sparrow) doubled in 2026; Amazon is building robotics foundation models. Safety numbers are at record lows. Jassy expects many repetitive jobs to move to robots and possibly help with last-100-yard delivery. New same-day facilities are "very heavy on robotics." Robots are deployed when productivity, safety, cost and delivery time are all at least as good.

- ## Advertising and agentic shopping
	- **Ads \$19.6B, +12%**, distorted by three timing effects: Prime Day moved into Q2 (850bps hit to Q3 growth); Q2 had NBA on Prime while Q3 had none (it resumes in Q4); and Q3 2025's change to search-engine marketing, which sent more traffic to Amazon search pages, is now being lapped. **Management expects high-teens growth in Q4.**
	- **External shopping agents aren't hurting ads**: few customers shop that way yet, because "the experience is still not great nor more convenient" than going straight to a retailer.
	- **Alexa for Shopping**: 400M+ customers in the last twelve months; interactions **up 570%** YoY; US users spend **50% more per order**. Jassy expects it to be "the primary shopping agent customers use to shop Amazon," while Amazon also works with horizontal agents.
	- **Amazon Ads Agent**: one AI-first platform for the full ads offering. New **full-funnel campaigns** combine sponsored ads, display, video and streaming TV; the advertiser sets budget and duration and Amazon's AI plans and optimizes the rest.
	- **Read**: the ads slowdown is mostly timing, but the lapping of the search-marketing change is a real base effect, not a one-quarter shift. Q4's high-teens guide is the test.

- ## Other businesses
	- **Prime Video**: run rate in the tens of billions of dollars and **profitable on its own**. Thursday Night Football had its best start ever, alongside NBA, NASCAR, the Masters and the Champions League.
	- **Devices**: 34 new products across Echo, Kindle, Alexa tablets, Fire TV, Ring and Bee, off to a "very strong start."
	- **Amazon Leo** (satellite broadband): initial service on track to start this year; cost capitalization slips to Q1 2027.
	- **Jassy's closing**: "There has never been a business with a trillion dollars of annual revenue. Amazon is on a path to have two of them in AWS and stores."

- ## Questions the quarter leaves open
	- **Capacity versus demand**: the smaller sequential AWS add (\$3.8B vs \$4.6B) shows growth is set by how fast capacity comes online. The 40%+ targets for 2027–28 depend on power, permits and memory supply, not on demand.
	- **Margin mix**: whether more Trainium inside Bedrock keeps AI margins rising fast enough to offset AI's growing share of AWS and higher component costs.
	- **Trainium outside AWS**: pricing, customers and how much foundry allocation goes to external sales when they start in late 2027.
	- **Ads**: whether growth returns to the high teens in Q4 once Prime Day and NBA timing effects wash out.
	- **Free cash flow**: \$220B of 2026 capex and more in 2027 against Q3 operating income annualizing to ~\$112B; Q3 gave no free-cash-flow commentary on the call.
