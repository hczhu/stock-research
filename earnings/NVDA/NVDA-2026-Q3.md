- ## Nvidia 2026 Q3 (FY2027 Q3) earnings
	- Quarter ended October 25, 2026. Nvidia reported on November 17, 2026, five days after Jensen Huang's resignation.
	- **Source**: Nvidia's Q3 FY2027 press release and CFO commentary ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27pr.htm)), and the earnings call transcript supplied by the user. Speakers: Chairman Harvey Jones, Interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress, and Ian Buck for Q&A. Nvidia has not yet filed the 10-Q.
	- **Management's account**: the business kept accelerating through the leadership shock. Vera Rubin generated nearly **\$20B** in its first quarter of production shipments, the fastest ramp in Nvidia's history, and Nvidia raised its FY2028 growth outlook to **~75%** from 70%. Kress described demand as "far exceeding supply" and said customers and suppliers are "100% up behind us." The new disclosures are about risk: **\$390B** of purchase commitments, **\$118B** of guarantees, and a more selective stance on financing neoclouds.
	- Related: [[2026-11-12-nvidia-huang-exit-hbm-vendor-agreements]], [[2026-09-11-nvidia-backstop-universe-credit-support]], [[2026-08-24-wsj-usvyatsky-big-tech-off-balance-sheet-commitments]] and [[2026-09-01-stratechery-nvidia-earnings-dollars-per-gigawatt-hugging-face]].
- ## Leadership and governance
	- **CEO exit**: on Nov 12, the board said Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned as President, CEO and board member after "disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms." The board unanimously accepted and **does not intend to reinstate** Huang. Kress is Interim CEO, and Heidrick & Struggles is running the search for a permanent CEO, considering internal and external candidates.
	- **New board committee**: a **National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee** of independent directors, senior external advisors and legal counsel, to "enhance our engagement with the U.S. government." Jones said the next CEO will have to navigate national-security dynamics "very carefully."
	- **Ground rules**: no questions on the circumstances of the resignation or the "ongoing investigation." Kress said the priority is employees, while acknowledging "diverse opinions" inside the company. Kress said TSMC's leadership was in Nvidia's offices that week, and that memory and advanced packaging are secured.

- ## Demand signals
	- **Pricing for compute is rising, not falling**: the chain runs from cheaper tokens to dearer GPUs.
		- Token prices fell nearly 60% since May, which drove token usage up (see the OpenRouter figures below).
		- Higher usage lifted compute utilization. Nvidia compute is "fully utilized across every hyperscaler and neocloud we serve."
		- Full utilization pushed rental prices up. **Multi-year Hopper renewals have reached up to 2× the original price**, and **B300 rental rates are up more than 60%** since launch.
		- SpaceX reported the same pattern on its Q3 call: compute pricing above \$50 per watt, with short-term contracts renewing at higher \$/W ([[SPCX-2026-Q3]]).
	- **Cloud backlog**: the cloud industry's backlog is **\$2.5T**, up from \$2T three months earlier. Hyperscale customers' cloud revenue growth accelerated for the sixth straight quarter.
	- **Frontier labs**: expected to be **~15% of data center revenue** this year, roughly flat with summer disclosures. Kress cited Anthropic's annualized revenue of **\$81B** at the end of October.
	- **Open models and agents**: weekly OpenRouter token volume is up **35× YoY and 3.5× in three months**, and agentic workloads are ~**80%** of tokens. Meta's Muse, launched on Nvidia infrastructure, passed 10M setups in the US and Canada in six weeks, and Meta intends to expand its Vera Rubin deployment.
	- **CPUs**: agentic AI is driving CPU demand "far outstripping supply." CoreWeave, Meta, Nscale and OpenAI adopted the Vera CPU, joining AWS, OCI and SpaceXSI. Nvidia expects **total CPU revenue to more than double in FY2028**.
	- **Other growth areas**:
		- | Area | Q3 signal | Outlook |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Sovereign AI | Revenue more than tripled YoY. Australia partnership for up to 2GW of land, power and shell by end of 2027 | |
		  | Enterprise | Financial services strongest; on-prem revenue nearly **\$5B** TTM, more than doubled YoY, led by quant trading | Enterprise revenue to more than double in FY2028 |
		  | Physical AI | Revenue nearly tripled YoY | On track for **>\$25B** this year |
- ## Products and roadmap
	- **Vera Rubin ramp**: production shipments began in August, and racks are now at every major hyperscaler and leading neoclouds. Hyperscalers brought Rubin into production in about **one month, half the time** Grace Blackwell took. On MLPerf Endpoints, Rubin delivered **10× more tokens per watt** than GB300.
	- **Roadmap**: Vera Rubin Ultra (2027) and Feynman (2028) remain **on schedule**.
	- **Co-packaged optics (CPO)**: scale-out CPO is in volume production. Lambda deployed the first production Nvidia Photonics CPO switches, connecting 10,000+ GB300 GPUs, with **5× better power efficiency, 15× higher reliability and 10% lower latency** than pluggable optics.
	- **DSX POD**: a modular, full-stack AI factory reference design that moves cluster assembly into the supply chain, cutting deployment from months to weeks. Kress: a 1GW AI factory generates annual revenue approaching **\$50B**, so reaching first token one quarter earlier is worth more than **\$10B** to the operator. First pods ship to the US and Central Europe early next year.
	- **Open models and agent safety**: Nvidia agreed in September to acquire **Hugging Face** (18M+ developers, 200,000 companies). Nemotron is the most widely used US open model on OpenRouter. The Open Agent Safety Platform launched with 100+ partners: OpenShell limits agents' access to files and tools, and NVIDIA Sentry on BlueField-4 stops agents that overstep limits within milliseconds.
- ## Supply, commitments and guarantees
	- **Supply**: new agreements with **Kinsus** and **Samsung Electro-Mechanics** (substrates) and **SPIL** (advanced packaging), adding to last month's Amkor, IBIDEN, KYEC and Unimicron deals.
	- | Commitment | End of Q3 FY27 | Notes |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Purchase commitments | **\$390B** | "reflecting our confidence in the long-term sustainability of demand" |
	  | Data center lease and cloud service commitments | **\$102B** (Kress: "a little over \$100B") | Mainly long-term leases, for R&D in hardware, software and open models. **Includes the \$32B of neocloud agreements** below |
	  | Of which neocloud agreements | **\$32B** | Nvidia earns revenue from the upfront infrastructure sale and shares in rental revenue above a minimum commitment |
	  | Guarantees | **\$118B** | Includes **\$105B** of credit support for land, power and shell at the **PORTS-Pike** campus, which will exclusively host Nvidia infrastructure under a 20-year lease to OpenAI. SoftBank Energy capacity comes online in phases from 2028 |
	- **When guarantees take effect** (Stacy Rasgon, Bernstein, asked about a new CFO-commentary sentence): Kress said guarantees begin only once the counterparty has raised its own equity or debt, built the site and is ready to install compute. If the build is delayed or fails, "our guarantee doesn't exist anymore." Kress said the practice is not new; the sentence was added for clarity.
	- **A more selective financing stance**: Kress said Nvidia is "rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees" as other capital sources mature. Land, power and shell is the top priority for any future support. Nvidia may also use prepayments from investment-grade customers, and "a lot of things have changed over this last quarter."
	- **Capacity allocation**: based on whether land, power and shell are ready, whether the customer's capital is in place and, for non-investment-grade customers, solid offtake. "All are getting the same pricing."
	- **Two buyer groups, two dependencies** (user-provided framing, Nov 19, 2026):
		- | Buyer group | Q3 Data Center revenue | Builds competing chips? | Depends on Nvidia's balance sheet? |
		  |---|---|---|---|
		  | Hyperscalers (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta) | \$60.1B | **Yes**, racing to replace Nvidia with custom silicon | No |
		  | Neoclouds and frontier labs (ACIE) | \$47.5B | **No** | **Yes**, to scale |
		- **Share ticked down as financing tightened**: the non-hyperscaler share of Data Center revenue rose to 45% in Q2, then slipped to 44% in Q3, the quarter Nvidia signaled tighter financing terms. Kress set a firmer bar: operators must bring both powered shells and their own capital.
- ## Margins, pricing and capital return
	- **Gross margin**: Q4 at 71–72% is "our bottom," because Nvidia is honoring POs placed before rising supply costs. Margins then improve to **72–73% in FY2028** under new pricing, unchanged from last quarter's outlook. Asked about ASIC competition and pricing power, Kress said "our pricing is well thought through, but that is something for the future."
	- **Capital return**: a record **\$28B** in Q3 (\$22B of buybacks and \$6B of dividends), and **65%** of free cash flow returned year to date. Nvidia added a **\$150B** buyback authorization, the largest in history, leaving **\$227B**, which it plans to complete by the end of FY2028. It intends to return all excess free cash flow, net of strategic uses.
	- **Capital priorities**: Kress called shareholder returns "super top priority." For other uses, Kress put **supplier prepayments** first, then remaining committed investments, then selective financing or guarantees for strong customers.
- ## Q&A notes
	- **Demand above supply**: Joseph Moore (Morgan Stanley) asked whether demand still supports something like 100% growth. Kress said the 75% reflects both growing demand and added supply, and that Nvidia is still assessing the new export controls.
	- **Post-2027 policy risk**: Ben Reitzes (Melius) asked about a 2028 slowdown under a new government after the "blue wave." Kress cited continued customer requests for more supply and community acceptance of US data centers, and gave no specifics on government talks.
