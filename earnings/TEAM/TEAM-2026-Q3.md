- ## Atlassian — 2026 Q3 Earnings
	- **Period**: Calendar Q3 2026, quarter ended September 30, 2026; Atlassian **FY2027 Q1**. Call and shareholder letter dated November 5, 2026. Calendar-quarter naming is used for comparability with other companies.
	- **Source**: [FY2027 Q1 shareholder letter](</Users/hc/Downloads/TEAM-Q1-2027-Shareholder-Letter.pdf>) and [earnings call transcript](</Users/hc/Library/Mobile Documents/com~apple~Pages/Documents/Atlassian-First-Quarter-Fiscal-Year-2027-Earnings_Transcript.pdf>). References below use PDF page numbers, including covers.
	- **Thesis**: Atlassian is monetizing AI through collection upgrades, broader workflows, and seat expansion before charging materially for consumption. Agent activity supports its coordination-platform thesis, while Data Center accounting and longer contracts complicate the headline growth picture.
- ## AI monetization: upgrades first, consumption later
	- Management explicitly identifies **Teamwork Collection**, followed by Service Collection, as the principal AI monetization vehicles. More bundled credits encourage customers to consolidate workflows and upgrade subscriptions rather than purchase isolated AI functionality. [Call pp. 13–14]
	- | Commercial lever | Disclosure | Business significance |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Teamwork Collection adoption | Over 3,000 customers, up 7x YoY | Collection cross-sell is gaining scale |
	  | Collection allowance | 10x more Rovo credits | Larger bundles offer predictable spending and pooled usage |
	  | Standard → Premium → Enterprise | 2–3x more credits at each upgrade | AI supports ARPU uplift within existing editions |
	  | Collection engagement | Customers run 2x as many agents; seat growth is faster than non-collection customers | Current monetization combines higher subscription value with seat expansion |
	  | Usage-based pricing | Enforcement begins early December 2026; **no meaningful UBP revenue assumed in FY2027** | Adoption metrics should not be translated directly into near-term consumption revenue |
	- Meters include automation, Rovo credits, indexed objects, and Teamwork Graph queries. Customers are asking for attribution by agent and employee, usage visibility, and spending controls. Management describes UBP as particularly relevant to extreme usage, while retaining generous bundled allowances.
	- **AI economics are improving alongside usage**: GAAP gross margin reached **85.2%, up more than 3 percentage points YoY**. Management attributes improvement to infrastructure optimization, model blending, token efficiency, and its AI Gateway; it says cost per Rovo chat continues to fall while quality improves. This is company-level margin evidence, not a disclosed standalone Rovo margin. [Letter p. 18; call p. 14]
- ## Agents appear additive to engagement; substitution remains the debate
	- Asked whether agents reduce the need for human collaboration software, the CEO argues that more generated work creates more coordination: people set intent, manage exceptions, and collaborate across technical and business teams. That is management's thesis, not proof that seats cannot be cannibalized. [Call pp. 7–8]
	- | Adoption indicator | Disclosed result | Interpretation |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Human–agent collaboration | Over 10M interactions monthly; doubled in six months | Activity is scaling beyond isolated chat |
	  | Rovo agent automations | Up 9x YoY | Shift toward executing workflows |
	  | Atlassian MCP Server | Over 2M monthly active users, up 8x since March; nearly half from enterprise customers | External agent interfaces are a meaningful distribution channel |
	  | MCP tool calls | Over 15M daily, up 15x since March | Agents read and write work through Atlassian infrastructure |
	  | Activity after MCP adoption | Users create 50% more in Jira and 75% more in Confluence on average | More contribution to the underlying work platform |
	  | Human platform activity | 98% of MCP users also active on Atlassian in the same month | Evidence against immediate wholesale replacement of application usage |
	  | MCP customer growth | ARR growing approximately 2x the rate of non-adopters | Commercial association, with potential selection effects |
	  | Enterprise Rovo penetration | More than 87% of enterprise Cloud customers | AI adoption is broad within the enterprise Cloud base |
	- Sources: letter pp. 7, 17. Human–agent interactions, agent automations, and MCP calls are different metrics and should not be added together. Adoption correlations do not establish that AI caused the incremental ARR or seat growth.
- ## Teamwork Graph and AMP: context plus collaboration
	- **Teamwork Graph** connects organizational knowledge, work, people, communications, code, assets, and data. It now contains **over 250B objects and relationships**, versus 150B six months earlier, with **80+ third-party connectors**; more than 60% of customers connect at least four context types. [Letter p. 5]
	- Customers with richer graphs are **2x as likely** to become deep Rovo adopters, and deep adopters grow ARR **more than 2x faster** than light users. The potential advantage is organization-specific context accumulated through workflows; this comparison does not isolate causality.
	- The expanded OpenAI partnership connects ChatGPT and Codex to Teamwork Graph. Atlassian is seeking to remain the context and workflow layer even when users begin work in an external assistant.
	- **AMP — Agentic Multiplayer Protocol** adds identity, attribution, and scoped authority so people and agents can work together. Management positions it as an experience/collaboration layer built into the platform, complementary to MCP and A2A, rather than a separately sold product. [Letter pp. 6, 10; call pp. 9–10]
	- The architecture graphic places Teamwork Graph beneath applications and agents, with extensibility and governance across the stack. The commercial proposition is a shared work platform spanning human and agent interfaces, not only a chatbot embedded in Jira. [Letter p. 4]
	- The redesigned MCP server exposes **220+ tools** and aims to reduce token consumption without losing context. DX's AI Measurement product links developer AI adoption and agent investment to engineering delivery outcomes—an attempt to make AI ROI measurable inside the platform. [Letter p. 11]
- ## Enterprise expansion and service-management displacement
	- | Enterprise signal | Result | Read-through |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Service Collection | Over 70,000 customers; approximately $1B ARR, growing over 30% | A second collection-led growth engine beyond Jira/Confluence |
	  | Service competitive displacement | Seat growth from competitive wins over 40% YoY | Evidence of share capture, though no competitor-specific win rate was disclosed |
	  | Largest customer cohorts | Customers spending over \$1M growing north of 30%; over \$3M growing over 50%; over \$5M growing about 70% YoY | Expansion is strongest at the largest accounts |
	  | Fortune 500 | 85% penetration, approximately 10% of revenue | Management sees substantial room to expand within existing enterprises |
	  | Contracted backlog | RPO up 44% to $4.7B; current RPO up 28% | Longer commitments contribute to total RPO growth; it is not equivalent to near-term revenue growth |
	- Sources: call pp. 4, 10, 15–17; letter p. 17. Customer-cohort growth refers to customer counts, not revenue growth within each cohort.
	- **Morgan Stanley** is moving Jira and Confluence to Cloud and deploying Rovo to thousands of employees. The example connects AI adoption with migration of a complex regulated enterprise. [Call p. 7]
	- **Forward Deployed Engineering** has expanded, with Expedia and Reddit cited in the letter. Engineers connect customer systems, build agents and production code, and train customer teams to continue rollout. They also feed product improvements back to Atlassian. Management says this is rarely charged and is not intended as a professional-services revenue stream; scarce talent limits scaling. [Letter p. 5; call pp. 8–9]

- ## Data Center: migration demand versus revenue recognition
	- Management says retention among the largest, most complex Data Center customers exceeded expectations. Cloud capability, AI, and public-sector commitments help retain customers through multiyear migrations. [Call p. 6]
	- **Hybrid agreements split revenue between Data Center and Cloud** while customers migrate, without charging twice. This shifted some reported revenue toward Data Center and away from Cloud; management did not quantify the amount.
	- Data Center migrations contributed **mid-to-high single-digit percentage points** to Cloud growth. Migrating customers spend approximately **1.75x as much three years later**, according to management—an expansion opportunity beyond the initial migration. [Call pp. 6, 15–16]
	- The **March 2029 Data Center end-of-life** announcement pulled customer purchases and term-license revenue into FY2026. FY2027 therefore faces difficult comparisons, alongside migration and slower expansion on the legacy platform. Subscription ARR helps normalize the ASC 606 timing distortion. [Letter p. 23]
	- The CFO said the **\$20M Cloud revenue beat** flowed through into the annual outlook, while the **\$33M Data Center beat** did not fully flow through because part reflected revenue-recognition timing. This is why treating the entire quarterly beat as incremental recurring demand would overstate the signal. [Call p. 12]
	- On the expected Cloud growth slowdown, management cited prudent macro assumptions, the upcoming DX acquisition anniversary, tougher second-half comparisons, and an enterprise sales engine still scaling. [Call p. 10; letter p. 23]
	- **Unanswered question**: an analyst asked what proportion of Data Center business might remain after March 2029. The CFO reiterated that migrations were on pace but did not provide a percentage. The transcript therefore does not establish full migration by the deadline. [Call pp. 15–16]
