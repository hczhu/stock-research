- **Source**: Atlassian FY2026 Q4 earnings call and shareholder letter, August 6, 2026 (Mike Cannon-Brookes, James Chuong). Calendar Q2 2026, quarter ended June 30, 2026.
- **Theme**: A very strong printed quarter — Cloud accelerating to 31%, GAAP operating margin swinging +14pts to 12% — sitting directly on top of an **FY27 guide of only ~13% total revenue growth**. The gap is almost entirely **Data Center**: the March-2029 end-of-life is pulling term-license revenue forward, flattering FY26 and reversing to **−17% in FY27**. Cloud at ~25.5% is the clean signal.

---

- ## Headline
	- Revenue **\$1,766M, +28%**; Cloud **\$1,213M, +31%** — a fourth straight quarter of Cloud acceleration (26% → 26% → 29% → 31%).
	- **GAAP operating margin 11.9%**, up **14pts** YoY; non-GAAP 36.0%, up 12pts. GAAP net income **\$139M** vs a \$24M loss.
	- Subscription ARR **\$6,606M (+23%)**; **RPO \$4.8B (+44%)**, but **cRPO +27%** — the RPO figure is being driven by contract duration, not just bookings.
	- **All-time record quarter for \$1M+, \$3M+, and \$5M+ ACV deals**, including **the largest enterprise deal in Atlassian history** with one of the world's largest consumer technology companies.
	- **The FY27 guide is the story**: ~13% total revenue growth, ~25.5% Cloud, **~(17)% Data Center**, ~18% subscription ARR.

- ## The Data Center Distortion (read this before the growth rates)
	- Following the **September 2025 announcement to end-of-life Data Center in March 2029**, a higher proportion of DC contract value is recognized **upfront as term license revenue**. Q4 recognized **greater-than-expected** term license revenue as customers renewed.
	- Consequence: **DC grew 21% in Q4 and 44% in Q3'26** — then guides to **−4% in Q1'27 and −17% in FY27**. The FY26 total-revenue line is flattered by a pull-forward that mechanically reverses.
	- | Deployment | Q4'26 | FY27 guide |
	  |----------------------|-----------|-------------|
	  | Cloud | **+31%** | **~+25.5%** |
	  | Data Center | +21% | **~(17.0)%** |
	  | Marketplace and other | +20% | ~+12.0% |
	  | **Total** | **+28%** | **~+13.0%** |
	- **Practical read**: judge the business on Cloud revenue and subscription ARR, not total revenue, through FY27. Management flagged DC migrations contributed only **mid-to-high single digits** to Cloud growth and were **in line with expectations — not a contributor to the \$49M Cloud beat**.

- ## What actually drove the beat
	- Chuong named **two primary drivers**, both organic: **cross-sell** (Service Collection and Teamwork Collection) and **seat expansion** in core Jira and Confluence — the same combination as Q3.
	- **Service Collection revenue growth accelerated** in Q4, with **agentic automations up nearly 3x over six months** as customers deploy agents beyond IT into HR, legal, finance, and marketing. Named a Leader in Forrester Wave for Conversational AI Platforms for Employee Services.
	- **Teamwork Collection** is explicitly positioned as **the AI monetization vehicle** — customers upgrade for ~10x more Rovo credits, which gives them **predictability and the ability to load-balance tokens** across the organization. This is what drives the ARPU uplift.
	- **Teamwork Collection customers carry 4–5x more paid seats** than standalone Jira or Confluence customers, use **>2x more AI credits per user**, and deploy **2x more active agents**.
	- Seat expansion is broad, not developer-only: **roughly two-thirds of Jira and Confluence users are knowledge workers** (HR, legal, finance), against a disclosed mix of Jira 65/35, JSM 77/23, Confluence 68/32 knowledge-worker-to-developer.

- ## Teamwork Graph — the stated structural advantage
	- Cannon-Brookes' framing: *"Models will keep improving, organizations will hire that intelligence by the token. Context… is much harder for organizations to build and it cannot be hired."*
	- **>200 billion objects and connections** across all customer graphs, spanning **six contexts** woven into one ontology: Knowledge, Work, Communications, Code, Assets, People.
	- **The claimed payoff, and the most checkable number in the letter**: agents grounded in the Teamwork Graph deliver **up to 44% more accurate answers while consuming 48% fewer tokens**. Customers using the graph heavily "are spending less on tokens than their peers for equivalent work" — a cost argument, not just a quality one, which is what makes it land with CFOs.
	- Cannon-Brookes on the competitive question (asked directly whether the graph becomes a feature): *"I don't think it's as simple as every organization will have one graph… most organizations will have **three to five large-scale knowledge graphs, and we intend to be one of those three**."* A notably bounded claim rather than a winner-take-all one.
	- The graph was **opened up** at Team '26 via MCP Server and the Teamwork Graph CLI, deliberately making it accessible from any agentic harness — plus Cursor, Claude, and GitHub agents released inside Jira, and expanded partnerships with all the foundational labs.

- ## Agentic Adoption Metrics
	- | Metric | Q4'26 |
	  |------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
	  | MCP Server + Teamwork Graph CLI MAU | **>1 million**, more than doubled QoQ |
	  | MCP calls | **+400% QoQ** |
	  | Jira items / Confluence pages via MCP | **~4x QoQ** |
	  | MCP users also active in Jira UI | **98%** in the same month |
	  | Rovo assisted actions | **+50% QoQ** |
	  | Rovo penetration | **>80% of Fortune 500** |
	  | Chat quality and satisfaction | **+20%** |
	- **The most important qualifier**, from Cannon-Brookes: MCP calls were up 400% *"with barely a blip in people actually using the applications through the web or through a mobile client."* Agent usage is **additive to human usage, not substitutional** — the direct rebuttal to the seat-cannibalization bear case.
	- **Agents are contributors, not just consumers**: work items and pages generated via MCP feed back into the graph, which management frames as the compounding loop.
	- **Rovo adopters grow ARR >2x faster** than non-adopters, complete **20% more Jira work items**, and create/edit **25% more Confluence pages**. MCP adopters likewise grow ARR **2x faster**.
	- Asked whether this is just early-adopter selection bias (Canaccord), Cannon-Brookes rejected it on penetration grounds — 80% of the Fortune 500 already use Rovo, so the cohort is no longer a leading-edge sample.

- ## Enterprise Motion
	- | Cohort | Count | Growth |
	  |-------------------------|--------|-----------|
	  | \$1M+ ARR customers | **>700** | — |
	  | \$3M+ ARR customers | **164** | **>50% YoY** |
	  | \$5M+ ARR customers | **69** | **>70% YoY** |
	  - Customers with **>\$10,000 Cloud ARR: 57,334** (from 51,978 a year ago), now **>85% of total Cloud ARR**.
	- **The headroom stat management leans on**: Atlassian is in **85% of the Fortune 500, but that represents only ~10% of total revenue.** Against \$6.6B of ARR and a **\$14B addressable market inside the existing customer base** without changing products or pricing.
	- NRR **north of 120%**. Asked what "unlocked" the enterprise motion this quarter, Cannon-Brookes was explicit that **there was no unlock** — "many years of hard work," pointing to HIPAA compliance for Rovo shipped in the quarter as an example of the unglamorous enterprise groundwork.
	- Named upgrades to Teamwork Collection: **Warner Bros, Xero, and a leading AI chip manufacturer**; Adobe and Google Cloud deepening commitments.

- ## Margins and Capital Return
	- GAAP gross margin **86.5%** (+3.4pts); non-GAAP **88.7%**. Cannon-Brookes attributed the AI cost structure to a multi-year investment in the **AI gateway** and **blending multiple models** — "we've been blending multiple models for a multi-year period, and we're getting really, really good at it." This was his answer to why Atlassian's margins held while peers' compressed on AI mix.
	- Non-GAAP operating margin **36.0%** in Q4, helped by revenue outperformance and **lower employment expense following the Q3'26 restructuring** — but guided down to **28.5% in Q1'27 and 25.0% for FY27**. Q4's 36% is a peak, not a run rate.
	- **FY26 free cash flow was \$1,319M, down from \$1,416M** despite revenue growing 26% — including ~\$70M of severance payments tied to the Q3'26 restructuring. Worth noting given the profitability narrative.
	- Buyback: **4.6M shares / \$348M** in the quarter; **19.1M shares / \$1.8B** for the year, a **~4% net reduction** in share count, with ~\$1.9B authorization remaining.
	- **Ken Exner appointed Chief Product Officer for Enterprise and Emerging** (30+ years building developer platforms).

- ## Guidance
	- | Metric | Q1'27 | FY27 |
	  |----------------------------|---------------------|---------------|
	  | Revenue | \$1,705–1,715M | ~+13.0% |
	  | Cloud growth | ~28.5% | ~25.5% |
	  | Data Center growth | ~(4.0)% | ~(17.0)% |
	  | Marketplace and other | ~12.5% | ~12.0% |
	  | GAAP gross / operating margin | 85.0% / 6.5% | 84.5% / 4.5% |
	  | Non-GAAP gross / operating margin | 87.0% / 28.5% | 86.5% / 25.0% |
	  | Subscription ARR growth | — | **~18.0%** |
	- **On the ARR deceleration from 23% to 18%** — first full-year ARR guide the company has issued. Chuong's stated bridge: (1) **~1 point of FY26 growth came from the DX acquisition** (closed November 2025), which now laps; (2) lapping the H2 FY26 acceleration; (3) **extra prudence specifically in the second half of FY27** given macro, fiscal policy, and geopolitics.
	- Wolfe noted the guide implies **roughly flat organic net-new ARR** in FY27; Chuong did not dispute the arithmetic, reiterating only that the approach is "balanced and thoughtful."

- ## Consumption Pricing — still early
	- Two mechanisms exist today but neither is material: a **direct consumption-based offering for overages**, and a **Flex model** letting customers make a dollar-based commitment usable across collections and consumptive elements.
	- Cannon-Brookes was clear the seat-plus-credits bundle remains the model: *"the Teamwork Collection and the Service Collection are the vehicles for AI monetization."* Rovo assisted actions are described as "a rough proxy for credit usage," explicitly **not linear**.
	- Scale context: **>350K customers**; **\$140B market opportunity growing at 14% CAGR**, split Teamwork \$67B, Service \$28B, Platform \$22B, Software \$11B, Product \$9B, Strategy \$3B. Revenue mix Americas 48% / EMEA 41% / APAC 11%.
