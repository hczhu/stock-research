- ## SpaceX 2026 Q3 earnings
	- Quarter ended September 30, 2026. SpaceX reported and held its webcast on October 20, 2026. Prior quarter: [[SPCX-2026-Q2]].
	- **Source**: Q3 2026 earnings release and edited webcast transcript, user-provided. Speakers: Elon Musk, Gwynne Shotwell (President and COO), Bret Johnsen (CFO), Andrea Williams (IR).
	- **Thesis**: SpaceX's revenue is now led by AI compute, ahead of Starlink and a launch business that carries about 90% of global mass to orbit. The Super Intelligence (SI) segment, renamed from "AI," became **52% of revenue** at a **66% segment EBITDA margin** as cloud-services contracts switched on. Management guided Q1 2027 revenue to **\$33–37B**, more than double Q3's \$14.6B. The cost is capex running far ahead of operating cash flow.

- ## Segment mix and margins
	- | Segment | Q3 2026 revenue | YoY growth | Share of revenue (Q3 2025 → Q3 2026) | Segment adj. EBITDA margin (Q3 2025 → Q3 2026) | Q3 2026 capex |
	  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
	  | Super Intelligence (SI) | \$7.56B | +830% | 15% → **52%** | −53% → **66%** | **\$16.4B** |
	  | Connectivity (Starlink) | \$5.25B | +69% | 56% → 36% | 62% → 67% | \$1.5B |
	  | Space (launch) | \$1.77B | +6% | 30% → 12% | 40% → 20% | \$1.6B |
	- **SI profitability flipped**: SI went from a \$1.7B operating loss a year ago to \$431M operating income, or **\$1.6B excluding \$1.2B of Cursor-related stock compensation**. Johnsen attributed the gain to "higher-margin cloud service agreements."
	- **Cash burn**: nine-month operating cash flow was \$13.1B against \$48.1B of capex. The nine-month gap was funded by the IPO (\$85.7B net proceeds) and net new debt. Q4 capex guidance is **\$35–40B**, up from \$19.6B in Q3.
	- **Q3 earnings quality**: net income of \$4.4B exceeded operating income of \$2.8B. The difference came from \$873M of interest income on IPO cash, \$1.0B of other income (\$561M from related parties) and a \$390M tax benefit.
	- **Margins will slip in Q4**: Q3 Connectivity gross margin of 55% included a roughly 3-point one-time benefit from tariff refunds. Management expects Q4 company gross margin to be closer to Q2's.

- ## SI: compute buildout, pricing and demand
	- **Nameplate compute**:
		- | Date | Nameplate compute | Status |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Sep 30, 2025 | 0.6GW | Reported |
		  | Jun 30, 2026 | 1.4GW | Reported |
		  | Sep 30, 2026 | 1.7GW | Reported |
		  | End of Oct 2026 | Nearly 2GW | On track (CFO) |
		  | End of 2026 | 2.5–3GW | "Clear line of sight" (CFO) |
		  | End of 2027 | **About 13GW** (+10GW in 2027) | Musk is "highly confident." He would "be surprised" by less than 13GW |
		  | End of 2027, stretch | Over 20GW | "A small possibility, but it used to be zero" |
	- **Deployment rate**: about **200,000 GB300s per month** at Colossus II in Memphis for the rest of 2026, at a similar pace in 2027 on mostly Vera Rubin. A **440,000-Vera Rubin training cluster** is planned by the end of Q2 2027 to make Grok "extremely competitive."
	- **Power, not chips, is the constraint**: Musk says the binding constraint is power, cooling and electrical infrastructure. SpaceX targets **+20GW of power and cooling in 2027**, roughly double the planned compute, so that power never limits GPU turn-on.
		- **Turbine castings**: SpaceX is casting turbine blades and vanes using its rocket-engine casting shop. Turbine suppliers told SpaceX that blades and vanes run **12–18 months behind** the rest of the turbine, and Musk claims this lets SpaceX bring power online 12–18 months faster than rivals.
		- **Other power sources**: solar, wind, and **factory-built modular inference clusters** with power and cooling built in, to be distributed widely.
	- **Pricing is rising, not falling**:
		- Compute pricing is tracking **slightly above the high end of the \$30–50 per watt** range cited in Q2 ([[2026-06-05-gpu-cluster-rental-unit-economics-spacex-google-anthropic]]).
		- New deployments pay back in **under one year**. Musk said a **six-month payback** in 2027 is "not impossible."
		- Short-term contracts are **renewing at higher \$/W**. Even H100s rent for more than when new. Musk's thesis is that compute value per watt rises as models extract more "useful intelligence per watt," so SpaceX deliberately rents at **spot-like rates on short-term contracts** rather than locking in long-term deals.
		- SpaceX could rent out its compute "several times over." When it tried to offload inference to other clouds to free capacity for training, none had GB300s, GB200s, TPUs, Trainium or even H100s available.
	- **Internal vs. external split**: roughly **50/50** between internal use (Grok, Grok Bot, Cursor training and inference) and rental to others, flexing up to 60/40 either way month to month. Current contracts let SpaceX pull capacity back for its own training.
	- **Backlog**: \$63B as of September 30, including \$17.5B of deferred revenue. About \$42B is expected to be recognized within 12 months. Excluding Cursor, SI solutions and infrastructure revenue grew **192% QoQ** on new cloud-services contracts.

- ## Grok Bot, Cursor and advertising
	- **Grok Bot**: an always-on agentic assistant launched in August.
		- Paid weekly users passed **800,000** in early October, **more than doubling in a month**. Musk: "the fastest growth of a product that I've ever seen."
		- Usage per user **rises** over time.
		- B2B is the fastest-growing category, with companies using it across sales, marketing, IT and engineering as an "always-on teammate." Wide enterprise deployment was expected within weeks.
		- Musk expects Grok Bot may become the largest consumer of SpaceX's SI compute.
	- **Cursor**: the acquisition closed August 14 at a \$60B implied equity value.
		- GAAP purchase consideration was \$42.5B. **\$23.4B** of equity awards for retention and replacement will be expensed as stock compensation over about **three years**.
		- Cursor contributed **\$608M of revenue** in its first 47 days (August 14 to September 30), an annualized pace of roughly \$4.7B (own calculation).
		- Its scaled enterprise sales force now sells Grok. Grok Enterprise customers above \$1M run-rate revenue, including Cursor, grew **50% QoQ** pro forma.
		- A new single plan bundles **Grok, Grok Bot, Cursor and X Premium**.
	- **Advertising (X)**: \$538M, +19% YoY, as the new ad-tech platform launched in Q2 ramps.

- ## Connectivity: Starlink broadband, aviation and Mobile
	- **Consumer**: 13.6M subscribers (+1.6M QoQ, +89% YoY), with growth now led by **developed markets**. ARPU rose 5% QoQ to \$69 on mix but is down from \$83 a year ago. Production of the **V5 kit** is easing inventory constraints, and SpaceX prioritized retail channels ahead of the holidays, so it expects higher Q4 activations. Connectivity SG&A tripled YoY to \$325M on marketing.
	- **Enterprise and government**: revenue grew **91% YoY**, driven by Starshield and aviation. Management reiterated that this business will eventually **exceed consumer revenue**.
		- Starlink is contracted for about **12,000 aircraft**, nearly a quarter of available aircraft, with 3,800+ already equipped. Three of the four major US carriers have signed.
		- Aviation production has quadrupled since January and is expected to double again by year-end.
		- Airlines report higher load factors on Starlink-equipped flights.
	- **V3 satellites change capacity per launch**:
		- Starship Flight 14, only about half loaded, deployed **26 Tbps**, the equivalent of 10 Falcon 9 launches. Each Starship launch is ramping toward **>20× the downlink capacity** of a Falcon 9 Starlink launch.
		- Shotwell says users need **a few hundred V3 satellites**, likely by mid-2027, to feel the difference.
		- Musk expects about **100× today's constellation bandwidth within about three years**: roughly 100,000 satellites, each with more than 10× current bandwidth. He says Starlink revenue should scale roughly with bandwidth delivered.
	- **Starlink Mobile**:
		- 22M monthly users, **+37% QoQ**. The FCC approved **15,000 Mobile V2 satellites**, which will start flying on Starship in 2027.
		- SpaceX agreed to buy **800 MHz low-band spectrum**, up to 14 MHz paired nationwide, with closing expected in 1H 2027 and US service targeted for **early 2028**. The goal is to become a major US mobile carrier.
		- In Shotwell's design, S-band carries most traffic, low band provides building penetration, and small radios are added on existing Starlink installs and towers. She notes 85–93% of mobile data already goes over Wi-Fi. See [[2026-07-19-starlink-us-cellular-terrestrial-network-spectrum-moat]].
	- **Musk's long-term claims**: bandwidth demand could be **1,000× higher in 3–5 years**, because AI agents will each use far more bandwidth than a person and will outnumber people. He sees "a clear path to several hundred billion dollars a year in connectivity revenue" and promised a separate presentation on it.

- ## Space: Starship and launch
	- **Starship milestones**: Flight 14 was the first orbital Starship flight and the first revenue-generating Starship mission. **Flight 15**, expected in November, will attempt to **catch both booster and ship**, which would be the first full recovery of an orbital launch vehicle. Musk calls success "more likely than not."
	- **Starship roadmap**:
		- | Milestone | Timing |
		  |---|---|
		  | Refly a second stage | Early 2027 |
		  | On-orbit refueling attempt | Early 2027 |
		  | Weekly flights | 1H 2027 |
		  | Twice-weekly or more | 2H 2027 |
		  | Mobile V2 and Starmind satellites on Starship | 2027 |
		  | First launch from Starbase, Louisiana (20+ towers eventually) | Internal goal 2028; "highly confident" of launching at scale in 2029 |
	- **Launch position**: SpaceX carries almost **90% of global mass to orbit**, with China at about 6% and everyone else, including the rest of the US, at about 4%. Q3 launches fell to 35 from 44 a year ago, but R&D rose 44% YoY to \$1.2B as Starship spending accelerated, leaving the segment at breakeven.
	- **New contracts**: 3 Dragon crew missions (through 2028), 18 National Security Space Launch missions, 3 Starfall microgravity missions, a Starship lunar-surface payload, and Starlink communications for Artemis III.

- ## Terafab, funding and a possible Tesla merger
	- **Terafab chip fab**: a test SRAM chip at the Austin research fab is targeted for **May–June 2027**, with **volume production around Q3–Q4 2028** at the main Terafab site, Gibbons Creek. Musk put "big error bars" on these dates and thanked ASML and other suppliers for accelerating equipment deliveries. He did not directly confirm EUV orders. See [[2026-05-31-mediatek-terafab-strategic-asic-partner]].
	- **Funding the 2027 buildout**: per Johnsen, SpaceX will draw on operating cash flow, multiple debt pools ("investment grade and a very strong balance sheet"), and partnerships with vendors and customers across the value chain. The short payback "starts to fund itself" in 1H 2027. On September 30 SpaceX held \$89.9B of cash and marketable securities against \$38.8B of debt and finance leases.
	- **Tesla merger**: both companies have formed special committees of independent directors at shareholders' request. Musk is conflicted and excluded from the talks. **No terms have been agreed**, and Tesla would need approval from 50% of all outstanding shares, which Musk called "quite a high hurdle." He favors a combination for SI software, chip design, Terafab, robotics, mass to orbit and connectivity.
