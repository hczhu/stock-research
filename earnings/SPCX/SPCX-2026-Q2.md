- ## SpaceX 2026 Q2 earnings
	- Quarter ended June 30, 2026, SpaceX's first quarter as a public company. SpaceX held its webcast on August 4, 2026. Next quarter: [[SPCX-2026-Q3]].
	- **Source**: Q2 2026 earnings release and edited webcast transcript, user-provided. Speakers: Elon Musk, Gwynne Shotwell (President and COO), Bret Johnsen (CFO), Andrea Williams (IR).
	- **Thesis**: Q2 was the quarter AI compute rental started to pay off. Cloud-services contracts turned the AI segment EBITDA-positive for the first time, at **\$1.1B on \$2.6B of revenue**, even as AI capex reached \$15.8B in the quarter. Management framed AI capex as close to cost of goods because new compute pays back in under a year, and said \$100B of December 2026 annualized revenue was already secured.

- ## Segment mix and margins
	- | Segment | Q2 2026 revenue | YoY growth | Share of revenue (Q2 2025 → Q2 2026) | Segment adj. EBITDA margin (Q2 2025 → Q2 2026) | Q2 2026 capex |
	  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
	  | AI (renamed SI in Q3) | \$2.56B | +247% (+213% QoQ) | 18% → 33% | −37% → **45%** | **\$15.8B** |
	  | Connectivity (Starlink) | \$4.29B | +66% | 64% → 55% | 61% → 61% | \$1.4B |
	  | Space (launch) | \$0.96B | +29% | 18% → 12% | −12% → −21% | \$1.2B |
	- **AI turned EBITDA-positive**: AI solutions and infrastructure revenue jumped to \$2.19B from \$475M in Q1. Johnsen said new hosting deals carried "high incremental EBITDA margins as we monetized available compute capacity." The AI operating loss narrowed 49% QoQ to \$1.3B.
	- **Advertising (X)**: \$367M, down 14% YoY but up 7% QoQ, while the ad platform was rebuilt for AI features and self-service.
	- **Space** stayed EBITDA-negative because segment R&D, mostly Starship, rose to \$1.08B (+55% YoY). Starship aims for 4× Falcon 9's payload at one-tenth the launch cost.

- ## AI: cloud services, compute and Grok
	- **Cloud-services contracts**: SpaceX signed \$14.1B of contracted sales for access to Colossus and Colossus II compute, adding \$1.6B of revenue in Q2. Customers named on the call were **Google and Anthropic** (see [[2026-06-05-gpu-cluster-rental-unit-economics-spacex-google-anthropic]]). In the first weeks of Q3 SpaceX contracted another **\$6.7B over six months**, ramping from October. Johnsen: "increasingly favorable economics with each agreement we signed."
	- **Compute**: nameplate capacity reached 1.4GW, up from 1.0GW in Q1 and 0.4GW a year earlier. Management targeted over 2GW by end-2026 and, per Musk, end-2027 capacity "closer to 10 gigawatts of compute than 5."
		- SpaceX is planning **20GW of power and cooling projects** by end-2027 and expects about 15GW to land, assuming a quarter of projects slip. The goal is far more power than GPUs, "given the relative expense of GPUs versus balance of system."
		- SpaceX will **build exclusively on NVIDIA** (Vera Rubin) and expects to receive "a very significant percent" of NVIDIA's GPUs in 2027.
		- **Starmind AI satellites**, optimized Vera Rubin NVL72 computers, will start launching in 2027. SpaceX will also deploy the simplified NVL72 design on the ground.
	- **Pricing and payback**: new compute deployments pay back in under one year. Musk's "guess" for Rubin monetization was **\$30–50 per watt**. Johnsen said fast payback makes AI compute capex behave almost like cost of goods, unlike launch-site assets that pay off over decades.
	- **Musk's case that prices keep rising**: intelligence per watt is improving, smaller models keep getting more capable, and **memory** is the binding input. Memory output grows about 20% a year against demand growth of about 200%, so "the price increases; it does not decrease." He also credited Anthropic's Claude 4.5 as "a shock to the system."
	- **Compute allocation**: Musk expects the share of compute used for Grok training to fall to about **10%**, with the rest used for inference or rented to others.
	- **Grok**:
		- Grok 4.5 (July) was trained alongside Cursor and incorporates a 1.5-trillion-parameter V9 foundation model. **Token consumption tripled** after its release.
		- Grok 4.6 and 4.7 were due within weeks. **Grok 5**, due by year-end, will be trained on SpaceX's entire engineering data corpus to make it "by far the best engineer."
	- **Cursor**: SpaceX agreed to acquire Cursor for \$60B, expected to close in Q3.

- ## Connectivity: Starlink broadband, enterprise and Mobile
	- **Consumer**: 12.0M subscribers, double a year earlier. Q2's **+1.7M was a record**, up from +1.4M in Q1. ARPU was flat QoQ at \$66 but down from \$85 a year earlier, and Johnsen warned that geographic expansion "may drive down blended ARPU over time." Service reached 167 markets.
	- **Constellation**: about 10,200 operational satellites, including 9,600 broadband satellites delivering about **800 Tbps** of total downlink. Musk says V3 satellites are about 10× as capable as V2 and SpaceX will launch about 10× as many, so bandwidth rises about 100×. "Even if our monetization per bit dropped by a factor of 10, that would still mean a 10x increase in the revenue of Starlink." Musk said users would feel V3 once about **1,000 V3 satellites** are up, around Q2 2027.
	- **Enterprise and government**: revenue grew **108% YoY**.
		- SpaceX won **\$6B+ of multi-year Starshield contracts**, mainly two Space Force LEO communications and sensing constellations.
		- Aviation is **less than 10% penetrated**. American Airlines signed a major deal, and Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia and Aer Lingus were activated. One airline reported passengers choosing connecting flights to get Starlink.
		- Shotwell: "We have never lost an enterprise customer." She sees maritime as a large new market for ships that cannot afford VSAT.
		- Musk expects enterprise revenue to "substantially exceed consumer revenue." SpaceX is building an enterprise sales team to show customers that Starlink can be a primary provider, not a backup.
	- **Starlink Mobile**:
		- The FCC approved the EchoStar transfer of **65 MHz of US spectrum**, compared with the roughly 5 MHz SpaceX currently uses through telco partners. SpaceX paid \$856M in Q2 under the related spectrum credit agreement.
		- **Mobile V2** satellites start flying in 2027, about 10× as many satellites. Shotwell estimates the service will be about 100× better than today, with US service starting at the end of 2027.
		- Shotwell sized the market as the **roughly \$600B a year** that AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile collect, and expects to win "quite a few" of their customers.
		- **Terrestrial build**: instead of large cell towers, SpaceX plans small base stations and femtocells mounted on existing Starlink dish installs on homes and businesses, which Shotwell called "quite capex efficient." See [[2026-07-19-starlink-us-cellular-terrestrial-network-spectrum-moat]].
		- New international partners included SoftBank, NTT Docomo and Spark NZ.

- ## Space: Starship and launch
	- **Launch volume**: Falcon delivers roughly 2,500 tons a year to orbit, against about 300 tons for the rest of the world combined. Musk's Starship aspiration is **over 1 million tons a year**, eventually 10 million.
	- **Starship V3 flights**: Flight 12 (May) was the first V3 flight, suborbital, from the new Starbase pad. Flight 13 (July) deployed 20 production V3 satellites, relit a Raptor engine in space, and came back with an intact heat shield. Musk considers the heat-shield problem "solved" and sees no technical obstacle to full and rapid reuse.
	- **Musk's cadence claim**: "probably a year from now we will be doing at least one flight a day."
	- **Human landing system**: Artemis III docking with Orion in 2027, then a direct-to-lunar cargo mission, with crewed Moon landings targeted for 2028.

- ## Capital, funding and outlook
	- **IPO and debt**: the IPO closed June 15, with 638.9M Class A shares sold for **\$85.7B of net proceeds**. SpaceX then issued a **\$25B inaugural investment-grade bond**: five tranches maturing 2031–2056, coupons of 5.35–6.65%, a 5.855% weighted average and 11.7-year average maturity. Part of it repaid a \$20B bridge loan. Quarter-end cash and marketable securities were **\$100B**, with \$47.5B of backlog.
