- ## TSMC 2026 Q3 earnings strategy notes
	- **Source**: [TSMC Q3 2026 results](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/quarterly-results/2026/q3), October 15, 2026. Based on the supplied LSEG StreetEvents call transcript and TSMC earnings presentation. Prior-quarter comparisons reference [[TSM-2026-Q2]]. Forward-looking figures are management’s expectations as of the call.
	- **Strategic shift**: TSMC is adding capacity to N3 after it reached its original target, even as it ramps N2 and prepares A14. Management says assessed AI demand remains well above available supply and prioritizes getting customer products to market across both wafer fabrication and packaging.

- ## TSMC sent its own teams to check the U.S. AI buildout
	- **What TSMC checked firsthand**: C.C. Wei said TSMC sent its own teams to check **U.S. data-center construction progress and power availability**. This was an extra step beyond speaking with chip customers and their end customers about multiyear product roadmaps and production plans. [Q3 call transcript, pp. 4–5](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/eba4fac72873bf546b669bd2b5d973df5c93215d/TSMC%203Q26%20Transcript.pdf#page=5)
	- **How it informs wafer planning**: TSMC combines those checks with a top-down and bottom-up assessment of cloud providers’ capex, financing, and the availability of other components. Wei said the feedback had been encouraging and cloud providers appeared well prepared. After factoring in these constraints, TSMC still assessed demand as well above its ability to supply, but Wei did not say whether the gap would widen in 2027.

- ## AI demand extends beyond accelerators
	- **Accelerators, CPUs and networking**: C.C. Wei sees agentic workloads, the shift from chatbots to assistants, and adoption of open models expanding AI usage. Management sees additional demand for CPUs and networking chips alongside strong accelerator demand, but did not forecast a shift in the relative mix.
	- **The accelerator forecast excludes CPUs**: The prior 2024–2029 outlook was a mid- to high-50% accelerator revenue CAGR. Wei said growth could be higher but did not give a new target. TSMC does not count CPUs in this metric because it cannot reliably distinguish conventional from AI server use. HPC revenue is a broader platform measure, not an AI revenue proxy.
	- **Consumer demand is diverging from leading-edge demand**: Higher component prices and macro uncertainty are pressuring price-sensitive consumer segments. Management is planning prudently for those segments into 2027 even as it expects strong leading-edge demand from a wider customer base.

- ## Capital spending and wafer allocation
	- **Multiyear capex step-up**: The 2026 budget remains US$60–64 billion. Management expects 2026–2028 spending to substantially exceed the prior three years, without giving a new annual budget or total. Tool, labor and material inflation are real, but management says long-term demand is the main driver and expects revenue growth to outpace capex growth if execution goes to plan.
	- **Keep control over allocation**: TSMC generally prohibits customers from subletting or auctioning wafer capacity; it allows limited collaboration via mask-licensing agreements in a small number of cases. It says it treats integrated device manufacturers and fabless customers alike while accounting for the former’s in-house capacity when planning.

- ## N3 expansion and the N2-to-A14 roadmap
	- **An exception to the usual capacity policy**: TSMC historically stops adding capacity to a node once it reaches its target. Wei said TSMC was executing its global plan to add **three new N3 fabs** based on its assessment of sustained multiyear 3 nm demand. He also disclosed the decision to add another N3 fab in Tainan. The U.S. buildout checks were one input to TSMC’s broader capacity assessment, not the sole stated basis for the N3 plan.
	- | Process or site | Timing as of the Q3 call | What changed or why it matters |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | N3, Tainan | First new fab: H1 2027 volume production. Additional fab: H1 2028. | The extra Tainan fab extends an unusual buildout for a node that had already reached its target capacity. |
	  | N3, Arizona Fab 2 | Q3 2027 volume production, slightly ahead of the prior schedule. | Construction was complete and tool installation was underway at the call. |
	  | N3, Japan Fab 2 | Volume production scheduled for 2028. | The planned second fab adds a Japan-based N3 option. |
	  | N2 family, Hsinchu and Kaohsiung | High-volume manufacturing began in Q4 2025; annual capacity expected to more than double in 2027. | Both smartphone and HPC/AI are driving the ramp. Small mobile dies and large AI dies are ramping at the same time. |
	  | A14 | Risk production in 2027; volume production in 2028. | Wei reported more customer activity than for N2 at a comparable point and expects A14 to be a larger node. He did not confirm any specific customer’s outsourcing plans. |
	- **More output from existing assets**: TSMC is converting N5 tools to N3 in Tainan, sharing capacity where possible across N7, N5 and N3, and improving fab productivity. These measures supplement new fab construction.
	- **Backside power is a design decision**: Wei did not see tighter power supply accelerating customer adoption of Super Power Rail. He said demand depends on a product’s needs; some accelerators may use it, whereas mobile phones do not.

- ## Global expansion and the margin cost of manufacturing overseas
	- **Taiwan remains the main buildout**: Management described nearly 20 fabs in progress globally, including 13 leading-edge and advanced-packaging fabs in Taiwan over several years and another five to six overseas.
	- **Texas and Terafab are exploratory**: TSMC sent teams to evaluate Texas beyond its Arizona footprint and discussed potential use of capacity associated with a customer’s Terafab project. Wei did not announce a deal and said any fab under such an arrangement would have to be run by TSMC.
	- **Separate the two margin effects**: Q3 gross margin fell 1.3 percentage points to 66.4%, primarily on N2 startup dilution. Separately, the CFO expects overseas fabs to dilute full-year 2026 gross margin by about **2 percentage points**, widening to **3–4 points in the latter part of the previously discussed five-year period** as more U.S. fabs ramp. Management pointed to Arizona scale, productivity and work with customers and suppliers to manage costs.
	- **Pricing depends on the product and the site**: Wei said pricing varies by technology, market segment and production location, including the U.S. versus Taiwan. He did not confirm reported price increases or say whether they apply to existing contracts or only incremental capacity.

- ## High-NA EUV: why TSMC plans to wait until 2030
	- **TSMC is waiting for production economics, not dismissing the technology.** Asked by TD Cowen’s Krish Sankar about being later than rivals, C.C. Wei said High-NA has advantages at smaller geometries, but a tool must be mature enough for mass production and deliver those advantages at a reasonable cost. Wei rejected the idea that adopting it later necessarily puts TSMC behind. TSMC plans to start using High-NA in high-volume manufacturing in **2030**; that is a plan, not a commitment to a named node or every layer. [Q3 call transcript, p. 10](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/eba4fac72873bf546b669bd2b5d973df5c93215d/TSMC%203Q26%20Transcript.pdf#page=10)
	- **How it works**: Both generations use **13.5 nm EUV light** and precision mirrors to project a reduced image of a reflective photomask, or reticle, onto light-sensitive resist on the wafer. Higher numerical aperture means the optics can accept light over a wider angle and resolve finer detail without changing the wavelength. [ASML EUV systems](https://www.asml.com/en/products/euv-lithography-systems)
	- | Tool characteristic | Standard EUV (NXE) | High-NA EUV (EXE), with current masks |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Numerical aperture and nominal resolution | 0.33 NA; about 13 nm | 0.55 NA; about 8 nm |
	  | Demagnification and maximum wafer exposure field | 4× on each axis; 26 × 33 mm, or 858 mm² | 4× on one axis and 8× on the other; 26 × 16.5 mm, or 429 mm² |
	  | Indicative scanner price, not TSMC’s contracted price | About US$200 million | About US$400 million |
	- **Single exposure can replace multiple exposures on some critical layers.** Printing a dense pattern in one shot can eliminate extra masks, process steps and alignment between split patterns. ASML cites 40% higher imaging contrast for the EXE:5200B and a potential **2.9× density improvement** from finer patterning. That is a tool-level scaling comparison, not a guaranteed density, speed or power gain for a finished chip. The 8 nm figure is optical resolution, not the name of the chip’s process node. [ASML EXE:5200B specifications](https://www.asml.com/en/products/euv-lithography-systems/twinscan-exe-5200b)
	- **Large dies face the opposite trade-off.** The reticle field limits the size of a monolithic die that fits in one shot. A large AI die that fits a standard EUV field may require two adjacent High-NA exposures stitched together, adding alignment and throughput costs. Using separately fabricated chiplets is a different workaround: it avoids stitching a monolithic die but requires advanced packaging. Smaller dies that fit the half-field do not have that stitching penalty. [ASML on half-field optics](https://www.asml.com/en/company/stories/2024/5-things-high-na-euv)
	- **Compare cost per good patterned wafer, not just scanner price.** Mature standard EUV remains an option where its full field and established process are economical. As finer layers require more exposures with standard EUV, High-NA can save enough process steps to justify a more expensive scanner. At the same node and design, the change is primarily in how a layer is manufactured; a faster or lower-power chip is not automatic. [Reuters scanner price estimates](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/asml-extends-chipmaking-dominance-as-customers-embrace-high-na-ce785bdcd881f420)
	- **2030 production is separate from the larger-mask roadmap.** High-NA can enter production using existing **6-inch masks**. ASML and TSMC are targeting a **12-inch mask pilot line by 2031** and **system readiness for advanced-node production by 2033**, aiming to reduce stitching constraints and improve throughput and cost. The pilot is not a production date, and the larger format is not a prerequisite for the initial rollout. [ASML–TSMC announcement](https://investor.asml.com/news-releases/news-release-details/asml-and-tsmc-announce-initiative-pioneer-industry-transition-large-format-photomasks-high-na-euv)

The **more than one million wafers** Intel reported in September 2026 include R&D, certification and production, not just commercial output. [Intel–ASML update](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/intel-foundry/intel-foundry-asml-accelerate-industry-readiness-for-high-na-euv.html) |

- ## TSMC’s advanced packaging advantage is production at scale
	- **Wei’s claim is about production, not what rivals can build in a lab.** On the Q3 call, Wei said TSMC was producing roughly **5× reticle** packages with excellent yields and that CoWoS remained customers’ first choice. TSMC’s symposium materials confirm **5.5× CoWoS** was in production. Intel has demonstrated larger designs, but those results do not show comparable customer shipment volumes or final assembly yields. The evidence supports a manufacturing head start, not a monopoly on large packages. [Q3 call transcript, pp. 12–13](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/eba4fac72873bf546b669bd2b5d973df5c93215d/TSMC%203Q26%20Transcript.pdf#page=12) · [TSMC technology symposium](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3302)
	- **What 5× reticle means**: A standard EUV exposure field is about **858 mm²**, so five fields are roughly **4,300 mm²**. Advanced packaging joins separately fabricated compute dies and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) on one assembly with short, dense connections. That lets an accelerator scale beyond a single exposure field without building one giant monolithic die. A reticle multiple describes area, not one exposure or necessarily the outside dimensions of the package. TSMC quotes interposer area; Intel’s ECTC example quotes combined compute and memory silicon area, so the multiples are not a like-for-like benchmark. [ASML field dimensions](https://www.asml.com/en/products/euv-lithography-systems/twinscan-nxe-3600d)
	- **CoWoS-S versus CoWoS-L**: CoWoS-S places dies on a continuous silicon interposer, the wiring layer between the dies and the package substrate. CoWoS-L uses small local silicon interconnects where dense die-to-die wiring is needed and a larger redistribution layer (RDL) for the rest. That avoids having to make the entire interposer from a single sheet of silicon. [TSMC CoWoS platform](https://3dfabric.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/technology/cowos.htm)
	- **EMIB-T versus CoPoS**: Intel embeds silicon bridges directly in the package substrate instead of using a full-area interposer; EMIB-T adds through-silicon vias to bring power through the bridge, including for HBM4-class memory. CoPoS (chip-on-panel-on-substrate) changes how TSMC assembles large packages: rectangular panels can use area more efficiently than round wafers. Wei described CoPoS as a route to lower costs; TSMC’s **14× production target is for CoWoS**, not a confirmed CoPoS volume date. [Intel EMIB-T explanation](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/intel-foundry/intels-us-advanced-packaging-enables-next-generation-ai-semiconductors.html)
	- **Yield is the economic test**: Larger assemblies are harder to keep flat through heating and cooling, and a misplaced connection can make an assembly containing costly known-good logic dies and HBM unusable. Wei did not disclose a yield percentage. Reported CoWoS-L assembly yields and early EMIB substrate yields are neither verified here nor comparable: they measure different steps rather than the same finished package.
