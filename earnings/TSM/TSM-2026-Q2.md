- **Source**: TSMC 2026 Q2 earnings call transcript, July 16, 2026; LSEG StreetEvents edited transcript supplied by the user
- **Quarter covered**: Calendar 2026 Q2
- **Thesis**: The quarter strengthens the case that TSMC is the governor of the AI semiconductor cycle. Demand is accelerating across accelerators, CPUs, networking, packaging, testing, and AI-related mature-node chips, but TSMC is raising capacity cautiously, validating end deployment before spending, and preserving customer economics rather than maximizing scarcity pricing.

- ## Key Takeaways

	- **AI growth expectations increased again**: TSMC raised 2026 revenue growth to slightly above 40% in USD terms and lifted the capital budget from an initial USD 52-56 billion to USD 60-64 billion. Management said the prior mid-to-high-50% five-year AI semiconductor CAGR assumption is now stronger, but declined to reset the number because demand continues to rise.
	- **Agentic AI broadens TSMC's content opportunity**: Management sees agentic workloads reviving the role of CPUs alongside GPUs and custom accelerators. Whether customers choose x86, Arm, or RISC-V, nearly all relevant CPU suppliers use TSMC, so CPU-heavy agentic systems add wafer demand rather than cannibalize accelerator demand.
	- **Advanced packaging is the immediate binding constraint**: C.C. Wei said packaging shortages are limiting customer growth. TSMC therefore welcomes Intel EMIB-T and other backend alternatives because they can release packaged products and pull more TSMC front-end wafers.
	- **The capacity gap remains unusually large**: Management expects strong demand through 2029-2030, while acknowledging possible interim dips. It would not quantify the shortage but described the demand-supply gap as very large despite another CapEx increase.
	- **TSMC is actively policing double-counted demand**: Management does not simply add every customer's forecast. It cross-checks data-center construction, location, power availability, rack deployments, and end-customer plans to reduce the risk that wafers become inventory.
	- **Near-term margins remain exceptional despite two structural diluters**: N2 ramp costs should reduce second-half gross margin by 3-4 percentage points, while overseas fabs should dilute gross margin by 2-3 points initially and 3-4 points later. Q3 gross-margin guidance still centers on 66% because utilization, cost improvement, and leading-edge demand provide substantial offsets.

- ## AI Demand Is Expanding Across the System

	- TSMC's opportunity is no longer limited to AI accelerators. Management identified incremental demand across CPUs, GPUs, custom XPUs, networking, power management, sensors, packaging, and testing.
	- Agentic AI is specifically increasing CPU relevance in data centers because agents need general-purpose compute for orchestration, tool execution, and control-plane workloads around accelerators.
	- TSMC is working with customers to allocate constrained leading-edge wafers among CPUs, GPUs, and XPUs rather than betting on one architecture. This makes architecture share shifts less important to TSMC than total compute growth.
	- Management's confidence comes from both direct chip customers and cloud service providers. The company can see multi-year product roadmaps and production plans because leading-edge development and capacity preparation now take more than five years.
	- C.C. Wei described AI as a new industry layer that will spread into automobiles, humanoid robots, and other sectors. The key qualification is that management would not guarantee uninterrupted growth; temporary demand dips remain possible inside the 2026-2030 structural uptrend.
	- Weakness is concentrated elsewhere: consumer and price-sensitive end markets face higher component prices and macro uncertainty. The full-year growth upgrade therefore appears AI-led rather than broad semiconductor-cycle acceleration.

- ## Capacity Planning and Capital Discipline

	- TSMC increased the 2026 capital budget twice: from USD 52-56 billion in January, toward the top of that range in April, and then to USD 60-64 billion in July.
	- Spending allocation remains approximately:
		- 70%-80% for advanced process technologies.
		- About 10% for specialty technologies.
		- 10%-20% for advanced packaging, testing, mask making, and other uses.
	- Management attributed the revision primarily to higher customer demand and secondarily to inflation in tool prices.
	- TSMC will not provide a fixed three-year capital plan. The operating philosophy is to invest whenever validated opportunity exists while keeping fab timing flexible enough to respond to actual demand.
	- The validation process is unusually granular. TSMC checks data-center construction progress, locations, power, and rack deployment to avoid building wafers ahead of deployable infrastructure.
	- C.C. Wei cautioned that every customer may give an honest but aggressive forecast; adding all forecasts together still overstates reality. This is the most important discipline standing between strong AI demand and a foundry overbuild.
	- TSMC expects no tool bottleneck to its expansion because it plans with equipment suppliers well in advance. This shifts the execution risk toward construction, qualification, utilities, labor, and backend capacity rather than tool-slot availability alone.

- ## Geographic Expansion

	- **Arizona**: The additional USD 100 billion raises planned investment to USD 265 billion and covers four or more additional logic and advanced-packaging fabs. No fixed completion schedule was provided; TSMC intends to move quickly but will sequence capacity to demand.
	- **Taiwan**: TSMC is building 13 leading-edge and advanced-packaging fabs over the next several years. This scale confirms that overseas expansion supplements rather than replaces Taiwan.
	- **N3**: Three additional 3nm fabs are planned - one each in Taiwan, Arizona, and Japan. TSMC is also converting N5 tools to N3 in Taiwan and using flexible support across N7, N5, and N3.
	- **Japan and Germany mature nodes**: JASM Fab 1 adds higher-value CMOS image-sensor capacity, while ESMC targets automotive and industrial applications.
	- The financial tension remains clear: global redundancy and customer proximity improve resilience but create 2-4 points of gross-margin dilution as overseas operations scale.

- ## Advanced Packaging: Constraint and Strategic Lever

	- Packaging capacity is short enough to limit customer growth. This is a stronger statement than ordinary tightness: insufficient backend throughput can prevent already-fabricated TSMC wafers from becoming revenue-generating systems.
	- TSMC does not view Intel EMIB-T traction as an immediate threat to front-end foundry share. Management welcomes alternative packaging because it can relieve TSMC's backend constraint and allow customers to consume more TSMC wafers.
	- This response implies that TSMC currently maximizes value by expanding the total AI system market, not by insisting on owning every backend dollar.
	- Management rejected the idea that packaging automatically creates a path into front-end foundry. It treats front-end technology and backend assembly as separate competitive capabilities.
	- The 10%-20% backend-related CapEx range stays intentionally broad because the bottleneck moves among packaging tools, testers, masks, and other equipment. Management specifically noted that some new products require unexpectedly high tester capacity.
	- **COUPE**: Production has begun. Management expects the platform to become important over the next few years as AI data centers require greater communication bandwidth at lower power.
	- **Glass-related packaging**: TSMC is building an alternative technology pipeline to reduce cost and expects roughly another year before it is mature enough for customer production.
	- **Large CoWoS formats**: The analyst discussion referenced a roadmap beyond 14x reticle size, reinforcing that package area and system integration continue to scale faster than a single reticle.

- ## N2 and A14 Roadmap

	- N2 reached 3% of wafer revenue in Q2 and is entering a steep ramp. Inventory days increased by seven to 87 primarily because of that ramp.
	- N2's 3-4-point second-half margin dilution shows that a major node transition remains expensive even with strong demand. The relevant test is how quickly yield and utilization recover the initial drag.
	- At the Technology Symposium, TSMC had shown approximately 70% capacity CAGR for the N2 family from 2026 to 2028 and approximately 25% for N3 plus N5 from 2022 to 2027. Asked whether those figures still held, management said current plans are larger, without issuing revised numbers.
	- A14 is TSMC's second-generation nanosheet platform and is intended to be larger and longer-lived than N2:
		- 10%-15% higher speed at the same power versus N2.
		- 25%-30% lower power at the same speed.
		- Close to 20% greater chip density.
		- Internal product-like vehicles showed close to 90% device performance and close to 90% yield on 256Mb SRAM.
		- Risk production begins in 2027; volume production is scheduled for 2028.
	- A13 and A12 extend the A14 family into 2029. A13 adds more than 6% die-area savings with backward-compatible design rules; A12 adds Super Power Rail backside power delivery.
	- Customer tap-out activity for A14 is ahead of schedule across both smartphone and HPC/AI applications, suggesting the node is not dependent on one end market.

- ## High-NA EUV Positioning

	- TSMC considers High-NA EUV technically capable but not yet automatically economical for production.
	- The half-field exposure size creates stitching and manufacturing-cost complications. Adoption will depend on maturity, total process cost, and manufacturability rather than equipment availability or headline resolution alone.
	- TSMC is working with ASML to improve cost and production suitability. The tone remains pragmatic: use the tool when it improves the full manufacturing equation, not simply because it is the newest lithography platform.

- ## Profitability and Pricing Discipline

	- Q2 gross margin reached 67.7% as cost improvements and higher utilization more than offset overseas-fab dilution. Q3 guidance of 65%-67% embeds the steep N2 ramp.
	- TSMC's pricing philosophy remains deliberately different from memory suppliers during a shortage. Management wants to earn enough to fund sustained expansion but will not impose abrupt multi-fold price increases that damage customer viability.
	- This limits near-term scarcity rent but strengthens customer trust and long-term share. TSMC is choosing durable ecosystem economics over extracting the maximum possible price from a constrained wafer.
	- The combination of N2 ramp dilution, overseas-fab dilution, and high CapEx is the central earnings tension. The counterweight is extreme utilization and mix toward nodes with the greatest differentiation.

- ## Mature Nodes Are Bifurcated

	- Management rejected a blanket mature-node recovery thesis. Commodity mature-node demand remains soft where exposure is tied to weak consumer markets.
	- AI-related mature-node products are the exception:
		- Power-management ICs at nodes such as 180nm and 90nm are in shortage because AI data centers require substantial power delivery.
		- Sensors are tight because data centers need environmental sensing and telemetry.
		- CMOS image sensors also justify targeted capacity additions in Japan.
	- TSMC will increase mature-node capacity selectively in higher-value and strategic segments rather than chase commodity volume.

- ## Competition, Customer Concentration, and Business Model

	- C.C. Wei framed TSMC's moat as the combination of technology, manufacturing, and customer trust. A customer cannot switch leading-edge foundries like a commodity purchase because qualification, test chips, co-optimization, capacity planning, and ramp execution take years.
	- ### The "Buying Milk at 7-Eleven" Analogy
		- **Management's analogy**: C.C. Wei said choosing and ramping a foundry technology is "not buying milk from 7-Eleven." He noted that the phrase came from a customer.
		- Buying milk is a low-friction commodity decision: if another store has a better product, the buyer can switch immediately. A leading-edge process is a technology partnership that cannot be changed casually after a competing option appears.
		- Before production, the customer must understand the process, evaluate it with test chips, work jointly with the foundry, reserve capacity, and complete a high-volume ramp. Wei estimated that this sequence takes about five years.
		- **Read-through**: Customer discussions, test programs, or government-backed capacity at Samsung, Intel, or a new foundry do not by themselves imply production migration. The real switching cost is the combined loss of design work, process learning, yield maturity, reserved capacity, and trust.
		- **Moat mechanism**: Technology leadership wins the initial engagement; manufacturing execution and customer trust then turn that engagement into a multi-year commitment. Capital and subsidies can fund fabs, but they cannot instantly reproduce the accumulated qualification cycle.
	- Government subsidies and competitor capital matter, but they cannot compress the five-to-seven-year learning and production cycle for a new node.
	- Management is not concerned about customer concentration because existing large customers continue to grow while new AI players are emerging. This answer should be tested against reported customer mix over time rather than accepted uncritically.
	- TSMC does not currently plan to finance customers' customers or use financial arrangements to stimulate AI demand. It views the existing foundry model as successful and does not need vendor financing to create end demand.
	- The stance is an important quality signal: TSMC is raising capacity based on deployable infrastructure and customer roadmaps, not by funding marginal buyers.

- ## What Changed This Quarter

	- The 2026 revenue outlook moved to slightly above 40% growth, confirming that AI is more than offsetting soft consumer end markets.
	- The capital budget rose to USD 60-64 billion, roughly USD 8 billion above the original midpoint.
	- The prior mid-to-high-50% AI semiconductor CAGR was described as too low directionally, although management withheld a new forecast.
	- Agentic AI added CPUs to the explicit growth thesis rather than leaving the story centered only on GPUs and custom accelerators.
	- Packaging tightness escalated from a capacity issue to a constraint on customer growth, while TSMC publicly welcomed competing backend capacity.
	- Management extended confidence in structural AI demand through 2029-2030 while explicitly allowing for cyclical dips.
	- N2 began contributing meaningful revenue and became the largest identified near-term margin headwind.
	- A14 customer activity moved ahead of schedule, and management expanded the family with A13 and A12.

- ## Stock Read-Throughs

	- **[[$TSM]]**: The quarter raises both the growth ceiling and the required capital base. The favorable read is that demand is accelerating faster than TSMC can serve it; the offset is that CapEx, overseas duplication, and advanced-node ramps absorb more cash and pressure margins before capacity matures.
	- **[[$ASML]]**: TSMC sees no equipment bottleneck to its expansion and is planning well in advance with suppliers, supporting multi-year tool demand. High-NA adoption remains conditional on cost and manufacturing maturity, so unit demand does not automatically shift to the most expensive platform.
	- **Intel**: EMIB-T can gain backend volume without immediately displacing TSMC front-end wafers. In the near term, Intel packaging success may expand the addressable market for TSMC wafers more than it threatens foundry share.
	- **OSAT and test suppliers**: Packaging and tester shortages are now direct limiters of AI-system output. Incremental spending can move among CoWoS-class packaging, substrates, test equipment, and alternative backend platforms depending on the bottleneck.
	- **AI CPU vendors**: Agentic workloads improve the demand outlook for server CPUs and accompanying memory, networking, and power-management content. Architecture shifts among x86, Arm, and RISC-V are largely neutral to TSMC if all remain on its leading-edge nodes.
	- **Mature-node suppliers**: AI does not lift every mature-node category. The favorable pockets are power management and sensors; consumer-oriented commodity capacity remains exposed to weak demand.

- ## Evidence Tests

	- Whether the 2026 capital increase produces enough N2, N3, packaging, and test capacity to narrow shortages without depressing utilization later.
	- How quickly N2's 3-4-point margin dilution recedes as yield, productivity, and utilization improve.
	- Whether overseas-fab dilution stays within the 2-4-point range as Arizona, Japan, and Germany scale.
	- Whether independent packaging capacity actually accelerates TSMC front-end wafer shipments or becomes a route for competitors to bundle foundry and backend services.
	- Whether AI-related CPU demand becomes visible in HPC revenue and leading-edge wafer allocation rather than remaining a conceptual agentic-AI benefit.
	- Whether TSMC's data-center deployment checks successfully prevent customer double-ordering and inventory accumulation.
	- Whether High-NA economics improve enough for A14-family production, or TSMC continues extending conventional EUV through process and design co-optimization.
	- Whether the A14 tap-out pipeline converts into a broader customer base and a larger, longer-lived node than N2.
