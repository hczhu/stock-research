- ## Datadog Q3 2026 — Broader Growth Meets Large-Customer Repricing
	- **Source** — Datadog Q3 2026 earnings call, November 4, 2026, supplied transcript. Page references below refer to the PDF. Selected quotations reflect the user's hand-picked passages, with line wrapping normalized.
	- **Thesis** — Non-AI customers continued to accelerate and the AI cohort broadened, while usage optimization and early renewals at lower unit prices complicated quarterly revenue progression. The central modeling variable is spending above the largest customer's contractual minimum.
	- Related notes — [[DDOG-2026-Q2]], [[2026-11-07-datadog-2026q3-q4-guide-min-commitment-renewal-pricing]]. The latter is a separate FUNDA research excerpt; this note follows the call itself.

- ## OpenAI — The Largest Customer and the Elephant in the Room
	- **Identity attribution** — OpenAI is the user's identification of the largest customer. Management refers to “our largest customer” throughout this discussion without naming it. The call separately names OpenAI as a marketplace partner.
	- **Q3 consumption fell but remained materially above the floor.** Obstler stated, “our largest customer reduced their usage in the third quarter, while their usage remained above their minimum commitment.” In Q&A he strengthened the characterization to “substantially above the minimum commitment.” The customer renewed, and management confirmed that growth excluding this account accelerated again. [pp. 7, 9–10]
	- | Modeling question | Management disclosure | Interpretation |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | What does Q4 assume? | Largest-customer revenue at its contractual minimum. | Spending above that floor would create upside relative to this assumption. |
	  | How much did this account contribute to the Q3 beat? | CFO declined to quantify it. | The call does not support assigning a specific portion of the beat to this account. |
	  | What does the rest of Q4 look like? | Revenue growth excluding the largest customer is guided to low-to-mid-30s%, versus 25–26% overall. | This account materially affects the growth comparison, but the ranges do not reveal its revenue or commitment. |
	  | How is the rest of the customer base modeled? | Recent organic growth is discounted, including early-renewal customers. | The minimum-only approach is specific to the largest-customer discussion. |
	  | What should be assumed for 2027? | Management deferred guidance to the year-end call. | Neither next year's usage nor contract terms were established in this call. |
	- **Selected CFO excerpt** — “The customer can't go below the minimum commitment, but can be various degrees above the minimum commitment.” The amount above the floor is outside Datadog's control and drives uncertainty in sequential results. [p. 10]
	- **What changed since August.** Obstler said, “we essentially extend and increase the commit, but do better pricing for that.” This applied to renewals/extensions across several large customers, including AI customers. Pomel also mentioned another early renewal in October. These changes create near-term revenue headwinds despite larger commitments. [p. 9]
	- **The Q3/Q4 guidance wording has nuance.** In the first Q&A response, Obstler describes minimum-commit guidance as the existing approach. When asked later to quantify the Q3 beat, he characterizes Q3 as conservative relative to spending and Q4 as explicitly at the minimum. The transcript supports the Q4 floor assumption clearly, while leaving the exact Q3 bridge undisclosed. [pp. 9, 12]
	- **2027 contract duration remains unconfirmed.** An analyst presumed a one-year contract in the question; the CFO did not confirm that premise. He also declined to forecast additional use cases for the account. [pp. 13, 18]
	- **Analytical read** — A contractual floor provides visibility during the applicable contract period. Actual usage, spending above the floor, and future renewal economics still determine the growth path. Broader AI-customer growth can diversify the business, but the call gives no largest-customer revenue share with which to measure that progress.

- ## Large-Customer Spending Cycle — User's Framework
	- **User's framework** — Volume/usage rises steeply → spending rises steeply → cost awareness → cost control → pricing negotiation → return to normal volume/usage growth.
	- **Management's description supports the mechanism.** Customers renew earlier than expected, increase commitments and extend contracts, and obtain volume discounts. They also review traffic, product mix, and data-retention tiers. New workload growth can then build on the revised pricing base. [pp. 9–11, 15–16]
	- **Selected exchange.** Barclays asked whether lower pricing creates an immediate headwind on the same volume before growth restores normal progression. Pomel answered, “Yes, that's broadly what happened,” adding that “there's a bit of account optimization there” and that customers are “looking at doubling, tripling, quadrupling their footprint with us over time.” [p. 11]
	- **Analytical bridge** — Revenue progression depends on billable volume, realized unit pricing, product/retention mix, and contractual commitments. Usage can grow while revenue growth slows if price reductions or account optimization offset that growth.
	- **Timing is unresolved.** Goldman explicitly asked whether two quarters would restore steady state or annual renewals would cause recurring steps down. Management gave no fixed normalization timetable. Pomel's response emphasized a broader AI cohort and better medium-/long-term visibility than very-short-term visibility. [p. 10]
	- **Three sequential effects.** The CFO identified renewal repricing, the largest-customer guidance assumption, and Q4 seasonality. Late-December vacations and code freezes can reduce usage growth. [pp. 10–11, 15–16]
	- **Cause of early renewals.** Asked whether broader enterprise AI-budget pressure was driving them, Pomel attributed the cited renewals primarily to the growth of large AI labs and technology companies. AI-spend governance is a separate product demand driver. [pp. 13–14]

- ## Non-AI Growth — The Broad Base Continues to Accelerate
	- **Selected excerpt** — “Revenue growth from our non-AI customers accelerated for the fifth straight quarter” and reached “the high 20s percent year-over-year,” up about one percentage point sequentially and from about 20% a year earlier. The CFO repeated the same trend. [pp. 4, 7]
	- **Non-AI customer classification still includes AI adoption.** Management tied growth to more cloud consumption, more software development, and tool consolidation. Increased coding output creates demand for CI, deployment, and testing products alongside core observability. [pp. 9, 11]
	- **Source consistency** — This note retains Q3 management's “fifth straight quarter” wording. The existing Q2 note also records a fifth-quarter claim; a longer consecutive-quarter count should await reconciliation against the original Q2 source.
	- New customers contributed about 30% of year-over-year revenue growth, versus 25% a year earlier. Management attributed larger initial deployments to both fast-scaling AI companies and new enterprise segments. The defense-agency and Japanese-bank wins had not yet ramped into meaningful usage revenue. [pp. 7, 12]

- ## AI Coding Expansion and Other Wins That Change the Opportunity
	- **Selected AI coding deal excerpt** — “a 7-figure annualized expansion for an 8-figure annualized deal with a leading AI coding company.” The company renewed early to support rapid growth, will use 13 products, is one of Datadog's largest MCP Server users, and is adding Flex Logs and Infinite Cardinality Metrics to manage volume cost-effectively. These are annualized amounts, rather than disclosed total contract values. [pp. 6–7]
	- **User's identity question** — Devin/Cognition, Cursor, or Anthropic? The transcript does not identify the company. The operational details are insufficient to choose among these possibilities.
	- **Large technology company's AI division** — Eight-figure annualized expansion nearly tripled its annual commitment only two quarters after landing. The expansion spans many training workloads, with APM and Bits AI added. Management sees more labs and companies training/customizing models, but described mass-market training adoption as early. [pp. 7, 13]
	- **European AI lab** — Six-figure annualized new deal for CI Visibility and Test Optimization, replacing another commercial tool. This extends the AI opportunity into software delivery workflows. [p. 6]
	- **U.S. federal defense agency** — Five-year, seven-figure annualized deal starting with nine products, including infrastructure and cloud security. Management linked the win to FedRAMP High certification and continued investment toward IL5 support. [p. 6]

- ## Product Economics — AI Credits, Own Models, and Platform Expansion
	- **Bits AI** has the fastest adoption of any Datadog product by customer/user uptake, according to management. Packaging moved from charging specifically for investigations to AI credits across multiple agent workflows. [pp. 5, 14]
	- **Scout** is Datadog's SRE reasoning model powering Bits Investigations. Management claims comparable or better alert-investigation performance than frontier models at a fraction of inference cost, using proprietary production data, workflows, and post-training. The call supplies no benchmark or cost ratio. [p. 5]
	- **Cost reductions can flow to customers.** Pomel said AI-credit pricing stays close to inference costs so savings can support more investigations. Management expects both direct Bits monetization and broader platform usage, without choosing which will dominate. [p. 16]
	- **Distribution expanded.** Agent Observability is available through Anthropic's marketplace, and Datadog was an OpenAI enterprise-marketplace launch partner. These relationships are separately disclosed from the unnamed customer contracts. [p. 5]
	- **Platform breadth** — Customer adoption of 4+/6+/10+ products reached 61%/41%/16%, versus 54%/31%/8% a year earlier. Management said AI-native customers adopt more products than comparable cohorts five or six years ago, with cross-sell occurring both at renewal and organically. [pp. 5, 15]
