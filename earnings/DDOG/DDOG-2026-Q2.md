- **Source**: Datadog Q2 2026 earnings call, August 6, 2026 (Olivier Pomel, David Obstler, Yuka Broderick)
- **Theme**: Everything accelerated except the largest customer. Core non-AI growth hit **high-20s — a fifth straight quarter of acceleration** — with record sequential revenue adds, while a **usage reduction at the largest customer (a leading AI company, just renewed)** is fully de-risked out of H2 guidance. Q3 guide decelerates to 28–29% almost entirely on that one account.

---

- ## Headline
	- Revenue **\$1.12B, +36% YoY**, above the high end of guidance. **QoQ growth 11% — highest since Q2 2022**; **\$115M sequential add is a record by a significant margin**.
	- **Non-AI customer revenue growth accelerated to high-20s%**, from mid-20s last quarter and **18% in the year-ago quarter**. Pomel: *"five quarters of continuous acceleration from the rest of the business."*
	- **The largest customer is already not additive**: *"if you back out our largest customer from our growth, you get pretty much the same growth rate."* Management "took the liberty to fully de-risk the guidance for the rest of the year with respect to that customer."
	- FCF **\$279M (25% margin)**; operating income \$257M (**23% margin**, from 22% last quarter and 20% year-ago).

- ## The Largest-Customer De-Risking (the central issue)
	- The account: a **nine-figure renewal with a leading AI company**, a longtime customer using **17 Datadog products**, with a **usage reduction starting in Q3**.
	- Management repeatedly declined to characterize whether the decline is price, churn, or down-sell. Obstler on the renewal: only that *"most of our enterprise customers have annual plus, and the pricing is generally volume-based."* Pomel: *"there's a lot of continuity in that renewal."*
	- Asked directly by Stifel whether the core guide would be **300–400bps higher** ex this customer, Pomel declined to speculate — but did not dispute the framing, saying only that the business grows "at the same rate if you exclude that customer."
	- **Why this matters more than the number**: Datadog's largest customer being an AI lab means the concentration risk and the AI-native growth story are the same exposure. The de-risking is a deliberate choice to remove an overhang — Pomel: *"we don't want that to be an overhang on what is otherwise a business that is accelerating and performing extremely well."*
	- Guidance methodology unchanged: base levels from commitment plus a usage model, with conservatism applied to recent trends.

	  | Customers ≥\$100K ARR | **~4,720** | ~3,850 |
	  | Share of ARR from ≥\$100K cohort | **~91%** | — |
	  | NRR (trailing twelve months) | **low-120s** | similar QoQ |
	  | Gross revenue retention | mid-to-high 90s | — |
	  - Billings \$1.18B (+38%); RPO **\$3.47B (+43%)**, current RPO ~+40%, RPO duration up YoY. Management repeated its standing caveat that **revenue is a better indicator than billings or RPO**.
	- **New-logo contribution is compounding**: the share of YoY revenue growth from customers not present a year ago rose to **~30%, from ~25% in Q1**. New-logo annualized bookings **more than doubled YoY**, with enterprise strength.
	- Weak *count* additions were challenged by Oppenheimer as possibly "the weakest quarter I ever remember." Obstler's answer: gross additions are on trend; variability sits at the **free-to-contract border at the very low end**, with "very low effect on revenues." Pomel added that cohorts above \$10K, \$100K, and \$1M are all "trending very well."

- ## Platform Consolidation Continues
	- | Products per customer | Q2 2026 | Year ago |
	  |-----------------------|---------|----------|
	  | 4 or more | **58%** | 52% |
	  | 6 or more | **37%** | 29% |
	  | 10 or more | **13%** | 7% |
	- The 10+ cohort nearly doubling YoY is the sharpest consolidation signal in the set.
	- **RUM passed \$200M ARR and accelerated to >50% growth** — used increasingly alongside the newer Product Analytics.
	- Land motion clarified: *"we typically land with two or more products… two products in general is a good land."* Consolidation happens later; the multi-product deals cited on calls are the larger, later-stage examples rather than typical lands.

- ## AI Metrics
	- **>750 AI customers** use Datadog; **all 10 of the top 10 AI leaders** are customers.
	- Within that cohort: **31 customers spending >\$1M annually, of which 8 spend >\$10M** (vs 22 and 5 respectively in [[DDOG-2026-Q1]] — a meaningful sequential step-up in both tiers).
	- **MCP tool calls quadrupled again QoQ and are up >22x versus Q4 2025** — the cleanest agentic-activity proxy Datadog discloses, and worth tracking as a leading indicator.
	- The AI cohort now includes **hyperscalers using Datadog for in-house AI labs**, distinct from neolabs. Pomel drew the distinction explicitly: neolabs are companies that "didn't exist a few years ago" and are landed **during model training** — "not really a business area for us a couple of years ago" — whereas hyperscaler labs are very large companies replacing **homegrown** observability.
	- **Customer concern has rotated from correctness to cost**: last year customers were validating that AI produced correct outcomes; *"three to six months ago, the focus has moved quite a bit towards cost."* This is the demand driver behind Agent Console and cost-management products.

- ## Strategic Framing from Q&A
	- **Inference over training**: *"inference will be the dominant workload… you probably will want to infer more than you train as a rule of thumb."* Opportunity claimed at every layer — GPU infrastructure, the LLM itself, tool calls, the applications agents call, and agent outcome/alignment measurement.
	- **The CPU renaissance has a specific mechanism**: Pomel attributed accelerating infrastructure-product consumption **not** to CPUs attached to GPUs but to the fact that *"AI agents are largely spending a good amount of their time… calling tools, and tools are just applications that already existed. And those applications typically run on CPUs."* Agentic AI drives legacy-infrastructure monitoring.
	- **Model diversification is a tailwind, not a threat**: more models and open-source models "opens the door to customers doing a lot more training on their own. And so that's a new market for us." Pomel tied this to a long-held thesis that the ecosystem would not consolidate to one or two model providers.
	- **On automation cannibalizing consumption** (the key bear question, from BofA): Pomel rejected the zero-sum framing — *"when they use Bits AI, they use more of our product, they deploy more of it, they create more dashboards and alerts… it's not a zero-sum game."* His stated future of observability is "not just observing, it's fixing."
	- **Security requires a full rebuild**: *"you can't wait for putting humans in the loop… it's a complete rebuild for most of the industry."* Datadog **separated Bits Security Analyst from its own SIEM** so it runs on third-party SIEMs — deliberately widening the market beyond customers willing to re-platform, and pointing toward an AI SOC.
	- **Sustainability vs 2021**: growth rates are accelerating "but still far below what we were seeing at that time," so Datadog avoids "the same issue of customers having to digest very large increases multiple years in a row."

- ## Pricing and Packaging Changes
	- **Infinite Cardinality Metrics** addresses what Pomel called *"one of the longest standing sources of frustration for customers"* — unpredictable bills when customers send more fine-grained tags. Solved **both technically and commercially** "by packaging our metrics a little bit differently." Directly relevant as customers build AI applications that emit more tags and ask more complex questions.
	- **A new AI-credits model for Bits AI is rolling out**, because the surface of contact widened from alert-triggered investigations to chat, monitoring management, detection, code generation, and managed tests.
	- Both changes matter for modelling: they convert previously volatile, complaint-generating consumption into packaged pricing — the Goldman question on CFO pushback was answered with these as the response.

- ## Notable Deals
	- **>\$30M TCV multiyear** with one of the world's largest online media companies — standardizing on Datadog, **displacing four commercial and internal tools**, plus the **largest Bring Your Own Cloud win to date**, displacing a legacy commercial logging tool **at petabyte scale**.
	- **Seven-figure annualized deals with two neolabs** — landed on training infrastructure and GPU fleet observability ahead of major product launches.
	- **Seven-figure expansion of an eight-figure deal** with a Fortune 100 health insurer, expanding to **19 products**; HIPAA compliance and PII handling in RUM/Logs/Cloud SIEM cited as the competitive differentiator.
	- Seven-figure with a South American bank consolidating **11 products** from mainframe through microservices; six-figure with a **Fortune 10** company across Log Management plus 10 other products.

- ## Product and R&D
	- **DASH (June): 100+ new products and features**; event cost ~\$15M, embedded in opex. Sixth consecutive year as a Leader in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms.
	- **Bits AI expanded across two loops** — DevOps (create/maintain monitors, root-cause within minutes, implement fixes, detect symptomatic behavior pre-escalation) and development (Bits Release as validation agent, Bits Code generating fixes **grounded in real production behavior**, Bits Testing for synthetic tests).
	- **Datadog for AI**: Data Observability (lineage, quality, jobs), Bits Data Analysis, **Agent Console** for agent usage/cost/effectiveness; Agent Observability adds **Patterns** (clustering user interactions into behavior groups) and Bits Evals.
	- **Data-boundary expansion**: **Federated Logs** query external stores including **Databricks and ClickHouse**; **BYOC** extends the full Datadog experience to logs kept in customer infrastructure, and is being brought to **Metrics and Traces** as well. This is Datadog conceding storage location to defend the analysis layer.
	- **Security**: AI Guard for Agent Discovery, Custom Agents, and Coding Agents; **Runtime Prioritization Engine cutting vulnerability noise by >95%**.
	- **Research**: **TOTO v2** time-series model — SOTA on key benchmarks, and more importantly *"demonstrated for the first time true scalability for time-series models, allowing us to target the same improvement path language models have followed since 2020."* Work is extending to larger dedicated models, post-training for Bits AI, and other modalities toward world models. **Adaptive ML acquisition closed in June** to accelerate this.
