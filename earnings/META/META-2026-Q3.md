- ## Meta 2026 Q3 earnings
	- Quarter ended September 30, 2026. Meta reported on October 21.
	- **Source**: Meta's Q3 earnings release (Exhibit 99.1), earnings call, and follow-up analyst call. Page references use the supplied PDFs.
	- **Thesis**: Meta is using the same model investments to improve its ads business, build a consumer agent network, and sell enterprise inference. Muse's early shopping activity gives Meta a path to transaction revenue, while serving costs make monetization and efficiency part of the rollout.

- ## Consumer AI and Zuckerberg's vision for Muse
	- **Personal superintelligence for everyone**. Zuckerberg wants a proactive agent that understands a person's goals and acts on them across shopping, productivity, creativity, and everyday life. **“We think that billions of Muses in a network at scale will be able to get more done for people and businesses by working together.”** Meta is building capacity to serve more than one billion people over the next few years, contingent on serving efficiency. [Main call pp. 1–3 and 10–11]
	- | Early signal | Disclosure |
	  |---|---|
	  | Setups and daily use | More than 10M people set up Muse in the US and Canada in about six weeks. Roughly 25% of people who had set it up used it on a given day |
	  | Shopping activity | Over 25% of weekly actives used shopping. Management cited an average purchase above $200, including travel |
	  | Connectors | Nearly 30% of users connected at least one service. Connector applications more than doubled from the 1,500-plus announced at Connect |
	  | Early adopter mix | More than 20% of US Muse users already generated at least $1,000 annually for Meta's Family of Apps. Zuckerberg's expectation that Muse could eventually exceed the existing Family of Apps revenue per user in this group is a long-term view |
	- **Distribution roadmap**. Meta plans more countries, local partners and connectors, voice mode, and deeper integrations with its apps, ads, and purchasing. The initial US and Canada rollout reflects localization work and compute constraints. Muse Spark is the free default. Muse Wonder is available to paid users and has improved quality, engagement, and retention, according to Zuckerberg. Meta plans to bring comparable capability to broader use through smaller models.
	- **Trust and permissions**. Each user's Muse runs in an isolated cloud VM that holds memory, recurring tasks, and artifacts. A separate Sentinel agent checks proposed actions and grants permissions. Credentials sit in secure storage, sensitive actions prompt the user, and users have an audit trail. The planned Muse Confidential VM is designed to keep users' content private even from Meta.
	- **Business model**. Subscriptions were the only direct Muse revenue stream at the call. Zuckerberg envisions a free core experience funded over time by merchant-paid fees on transactions Meta helps facilitate, with subscriptions for more advanced models, extra tokens, and business or workplace features. He expects to work on monetization sooner than Meta has for earlier products because serving costs are material. Li said transaction fees require more users and partners, and kept direct advertising open as a possibility.
	- **Glasses as an everyday interface**. At the October call, Zuckerberg said Muse would start coming to Meta Glasses the following month. He sees glasses as a way to carry a personal agent throughout the day and prices hardware to support adoption and future software subscriptions. Reality Labs will pace VR launches around major breakthroughs while prioritizing personal AI.
	- Sources: main call pp. 1–4, 8, 10–11, 17 and 19–20, follow-up pp. 2, 4–6 and 8–12.

- ## Enterprise AI and Zuckerberg's vision for a network of business agents
	- **Near-term offerings**. The Meta Enterprise Platform, led by CJ Desai, starts with the **model API and Muse Code**, alongside **Meta Business Agents** for sales and customer support. Zuckerberg said all three were growing quickly. He framed training as a large fixed cost and said **“we want to serve as much high-margin inference demand as possible.”** [Main call pp. 3 and 11]
	- **From business messaging to running a business**. Muse for small business launched in late September as skills and connectors inside Muse. It connects to Meta pages and ad accounts as well as tools such as Shopify, QuickBooks, and Canva. Early use includes campaign optimization, organizing DMs, drafting responses, and keeping websites current with inventory. Li cited an estimated **500M self-identified small businesses** already using Meta's apps to reach customers and generate revenue, based on a survey.
	- **Corporate Muse**. Zuckerberg wants a version employees can bring into corporate environments. Li sees opportunities in coding, productivity, sales, marketing, and customer service, with recurring revenue from subscriptions, usage-based API access, and messaging. Meta plans to scale investment as customer demand and product fit become clearer.
	- **Consumer agents transacting with business agents**. In Zuckerberg's example, a personal Muse contacts a merchant's agent to make a purchase. Li expects authenticated APIs and approved integrations to support these interactions. Meta previewed the **Personal Agent Protocol** in October with partners including Sierra, Walmart, Stripe, and Shopify, with controls for businesses and consumers. Management expects many interactions to remain assisted while those integrations mature.
	- **Commerce distribution**. Li described Meta's feeds as a source of discovery and Muse as a way to act on explicit intent, compare options, and facilitate purchases. She pointed to measurable merchant value as the basis for charging and left open how much checkout will happen on Meta.
	- Sources: main call pp. 2–3, 8, 11–15 and 17–18, follow-up pp. 2 and 5–6, 10–12.

- ## Frontier models, serving economics, and capacity
	- **Train larger, serve smaller**. Zuckerberg views Muse Wonder as Meta's first frontier model and expects scaling to produce more capable successors. Meta is training larger models and distilling frontier capabilities into smaller models tuned for personal agents. Other releases included Spark 1.2 and 1.3, Muse Code, Muse Voice, and the open-weight Muse Glimmer and Muse Spark Open.
	- **Compute efficiency target**. Meta expects **more than 10× improvement in Muse compute cost efficiency over the following year**, through smaller and task-specific models, inference and product optimization, and shifting suitable work to off-peak capacity. Li said the full improvement requires technological advances beyond the near-term gains. Management expects total serving costs to rise as usage expands, and it did not disclose current per-user costs.
	- **Capacity is shared across products**. Meta designs infrastructure to flex among model training, core ads and recommendations, consumer Muse, and enterprise services. Owned data centers allow workload optimization, with third-party cloud accelerating near-term supply. Meta is preparing land and sites while pacing server orders. Large clusters matter most for base-model pretraining, with smaller clusters useful for post-training.
	- **Internal application**. Meta set up an evaluation for Muse Wonder to improve its recommendations and ads systems autonomously. In the follow-up, Heaton said software output per engineer more than doubled YoY and analytics output rose approximately 10–20%, with more than 70% of measured analytics output AI-assisted.
	- Sources: main call pp. 3, 8–10 and 19–20, follow-up pp. 3–8.

- ## Core business AI performance
	- | Metric or deployment | Q3 result |
	  |---|---|
	  | Family of Apps engagement and monetization | 3.64B daily active people in September (+3% YoY), ad impressions up 10%, and average ad price up 14%. Li attributed pricing gains to ad performance and improved macro conditions |
	  | Instagram Reels relevance | Ranking sequences of Reels and richer watch signals helped Q3 relevance launches increase Reels time spent by more than 4% |
	  | Facebook generative retrieval | Semantic representations help surface new posts sooner. Rollout covered about one-quarter of unconnected Feed views and half of Reels views, with a 1% lift in Reels watch time |
	  | First-party Muse content understanding | More than 80% lower per-unit processing costs in offline evaluations, with better performance than the prior production models |
	  | Ad retrieval and ranking | Generative Recommender lifted conversions nearly 2% in targeted Facebook Feed segments. Expanded GEM added 1.7% for offsite campaigns and 9.6% for app ads. Andromeda improvements lifted Facebook conversions 1.4% |
	  | Advertiser AI adoption | Nearly 10M advertisers used AI creative tools. Meta estimated nearly 5% more conversions at the same cost per conversion and 9% higher spend among adopters of its AI business assistant's recommendations, compared with similar non-adopters |
	  | Partnership Ads and Meta One | Partnership Ads grew more than 97% YoY and exceeded $15B annual revenue run-rate. Meta One passed 15M subscriptions and trials combined |
	- Sources: release p. 1, main call pp. 4–8, 16 and follow-up p. 9.
