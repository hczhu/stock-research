- **Source**: Microsoft **FY2026 Q4** earnings call, July 29, 2026 (Satya Nadella, Amy Hood, Jonathan Neilson). Fiscal Q4 = **calendar Q2 2026**, quarter ended June 30, 2026. Full-year FY26 close.
- **Theme**: The quarter Microsoft made its **margin story independent of Azure pricing**. Azure accelerated to 43% on efficiency rather than new capacity, but the durable news is the quantified substitution of first-party MAI models into first-party apps (**up to 89% GPU cost reduction**), the shift from per-seat to **seat-plus-consumption** across M365/Dynamics/GitHub, and the useful-life change that reclassifies future leases out of reported CapEx. Third-party commentary on this quarter is in [[2026-08-04-stratechery-microsoft-vs-meta-efficiency-payoff]]; this file is the primary record.

- ## The useful-life change, in management's own words
	- **Effective at the start of FY27**, estimated useful lives of **data centers and office buildings extended from 15 to 25 years**, "reflecting our operating history and expected use of these assets." Already reflected in the guidance given on this call.
	- Hood was explicit that the earnings effect is not the point: the change *"affects only the timing of future depreciation and is expected to have a **minimal benefit to FY27 operating income**. The greater impact is on capital expenditures as more of our future datacenter leases will **shift from finance leases to operating leases**… Finance leases are included in capital expenditures while operating leases are not."*
	- **The cleanest statement of what did and did not change**: *"Outside of this useful life impact, our **calendar year 2026 CapEx investment expectations remain unchanged**. However, the shift from finance to operating leases adjusts our expectation to approximately **\$175 billion**."* The spending plan is identical; only the reported number moved. See [[2026-06-10-morgan-stanley-hyperscaler-capex-accounting-risk]] for the same off-headline-CapEx pattern across the group.
	- **Baseline for comparison**: two quarters earlier Hood described long-lived assets as supporting monetization "for the next **15 years and beyond**" — recorded in [[MSFT-2025-Q4]]. Any FY26 CapEx trend line spanning both quarters is measuring an assumption change, not a spending change.
	- Q1 FY27 CapEx guided **>\$50B including** the reclassification benefit, and FY27 CapEx to **grow year-over-year**. The reclassification lowers the reported number while the underlying commitment still rises.

- ## Azure 43% came from efficiency, not capacity
	- Thill asked directly whether the acceleration is a changed environment or better execution. Hood: constraints remain — *"demand continues to exceed available supply… You can even see it in some of the **pricing that's occurring in the spot market for assets**."*
	- The mechanism is explicit and repeatable: efficiency gains across the CPU **and** GPU fleet, plus lead-time process improvements over the past 90 days. *"Because of the supply demand imbalance… when we can make efficiency gains, they are **quickly monetized in quarter**."* That in-quarter monetization is what produced the beat, and Hood said the same dynamic is embedded in the Q1 guide.
	- Supporting operational disclosure: **31 new data centers across 5 continents this quarter, 88 for the year**; **dock-to-live time for new GPUs in the largest regions cut ~50%** over the fiscal year; **~1GW added in the quarter**; on track to **roughly double total capacity in two years**.
	- **Copilot workload throughput up 4x since the start of the year** — the single best proxy for how much of the "acceleration" is software rather than steel.
	- Azure Q1 FY27 guided to **~45% cc**, with H1 growth still expected to accelerate. Full-year FY26: Azure crossed **\$100B, +41%**.
	- Also flattering Intelligent Cloud: **stronger-than-expected GitHub Copilot consumption** after the June repricing — the segment's gross margin "improved through the quarter with the June business model change to usage-based pricing." A pricing change, not a demand change, fixed a margin leak.

- ## The MAI substitution economics — the most investable new disclosure
	- Nadella's architecture claim is the same middleware thesis as before — *"the harness, context, memory, and action space are separate from any one model family, thereby moving the frontier on the **cost-to-outcome curve**"* — but this is the first quarter it comes with numbers.
	- | Deployment | First-party model | Disclosed effect |
	  |----------------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
	  | Dynamics 365 | MAI-Voice-2-Flash | **89% reduction in GPU costs** |
	  | PowerPoint | MAI-Image-2.5 | **up to 84% reduced GPU costs** |
	  | Security (vs. Mythos) | MAI-Cyber-1-Flash | **~half the cost at better performance** |
	  | GitHub Copilot | MAI-Code-1-Flash | higher code acceptance, **10% lower median token usage** |
	  | Excel | MAI-Code-1-Flash | comparable quality to GPT-5.6 at significantly lower cost |
	  | MAI models on Maia 200 | co-designed silicon | **40% better performance per watt** |
	- The security case includes the routing ratio that generalizes the whole approach: **90% of tasks handled by the small first-party model, 10% escalated to the frontier.**
	- **Why this is the real margin lever**: it lowers first-party AI COGS without touching customer pricing, without requiring more capacity, and without depending on frontier-model price cuts. It is also the mechanism that makes Microsoft a **less captive buyer** of OpenAI inference over time, even while OpenAI remains its largest contracted customer.
	- Nadella added a resilience argument that is new this quarter, citing **the Hugging Face incident**: *"you can't depend on any one model. You will maybe need multiple models to even remediate some challenges that get caused by one model… you can't be subject to the **refusals** of one model."* Model substitutability is being sold as business continuity, not just cost.
	- Platform scale behind it: **11,000+ models** in the catalog; customers building with **multiple providers up 5x** since the start of the year; **100,000 Foundry customers with revenue more than doubled**; Foundry customers at a **1T-token annualized run rate up 4x YoY**.

- ## RPO: better decomposition, shorter duration
	- | Metric | FY26 Q4 | Prior quarter reference |
	  |---------------------------------------|--------------------|--------------------------------|
	  | Commercial RPO | **\$678B, +84%** | \$625B, +110% in [[MSFT-2025-Q4]] |
	  | RPO excluding OpenAI | **+25%** | +28% (ex-OpenAI portion) |
	  | Weighted average duration | **2.3 years** | 2.5 years |
	  | Recognized in next 12 months | ~30%, **+37% YoY** | ~25%, +39% |
	  | Recognized beyond 12 months | **+112%** | +156% |
	  | Commercial bookings ex-OpenAI | **+18%** | — |
	  | Commercial bookings incl. OpenAI | +10% (+11% cc) | +230% |
	- **The disclosure that answers last quarter's concentration question**: *"All sequential commercial RPO growth was driven by commitments from **customers outside of frontier model companies**."* Reinforced on the revenue side — FY26 cloud revenue of \$214B was **"nearly 90% from customers outside of frontier model companies."**
	- **What to hold management to**: duration shortened from 2.5 to 2.3 years even as the beyond-12-month bucket grew 112%. That combination implies heavy addition of shorter-dated contracts. It also sits awkwardly against Hood's FY26 Q2 defense that GPUs are "already sold for the entirety of their useful life" against a six-year depreciation schedule — a shortening blended duration is the wrong direction for that argument, and no analyst pressed it.

- ## The Anthropic gain — a second frontier-lab mark in earnings
	- Discrete items contributed **\$0.27 to diluted EPS** versus April guidance, including a **\$3.2B gain on the investment in Anthropic** and lower-than-expected Voluntary Retirement Program expense, partly offset by **severance and Xbox impairment charges**. Ex-OpenAI other income and expense was **\$2.8B**, driven by that Anthropic gain.
	- **Microsoft now marks two frontier labs through its P&L** — OpenAI under the equity method (recording its share of the change in OpenAI's net assets, per [[MSFT-2025-Q4]]) and now a gain on Anthropic. Both are simultaneously **Azure customers, model suppliers on Foundry, and competitors** to MAI.
	- EPS of **\$4.74 (+23%)** is stated "when adjusted for the impact from our investment in OpenAI." Between the OpenAI adjustment, the Anthropic gain, VRP, severance, and Xbox impairments, **the GAAP-to-clean bridge this quarter is unusually crowded** — the \$0.27 of discrete benefit is roughly 6% of EPS.

- ## The business-model shift: per-seat → seat-plus-consumption
	- This is being rolled out across every major franchise **simultaneously**, and it is the answer to the "how does AI monetize" question:
		- **M365**: usage-based billing added to **Cowork** earlier in July, "with thousands of customers already paying for and actively using it."
		- **Dynamics 365**: **650,000+ MCP actions** exposed across sales, finance, supply chain, HR, and customer service; usage-based **credit consumption in customer service up 4x QoQ**.
		- **GitHub Copilot**: usage-based billing introduced in June; **Copilot revenue accelerated over 60% QoQ** with Business and Enterprise seat growth continuing alongside it.
	- Nadella's framing of why this expands the TAM rather than cannibalizing seats: *"if I think about historically, Office compared to what Copilot is, is much more narrower. This is the first time where you really have an enterprise-wide tool, which has **both per-seat and usage-based pricing**."*
	- Hood on **E7** (Copilot + E5 + Entra + Agent 365) as the packaging vehicle: the value sits in Agent 365 — *"everyone is going to need both **observability of token spend and the manageability of token spend** for all business processes."* Selling token governance to the buyer who is about to have a token-spend problem.
	- Hood guided **M365 Commercial cloud growth to accelerate through FY27** on premium SKU momentum plus usage-based billing — while flagging the offset that new seat adds skew to **lower-ARPU frontline and SMB** SKUs.

- ## Copilot adoption — the deployment-curve metrics matter more than the seat count
	- | Metric | FY26 Q4 |
	  |------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
	  | Paid M365 Copilot seats | **>30M**; net adds **more than doubled QoQ** |
	  | Customers with >50,000 seats | **+7x YoY** |
	  | Customers deploying to majority of info workers| **+~75% QoQ** |
	  | Time from deployment to >80% MAU | **months → days** over the past year |
	  | Conversations per user | **nearly doubled YoY** |
	  | Weekly engagement | **on par with Outlook and Teams** |
	  | User satisfaction | **doubled over three quarters**, all-time high; latency **−25%** |
	  | GitHub Copilot users / GitHub users | **50M / 225M**; **1 in 3 PRs involves an agent** |
	  | Agent 365 agents registered (2 months) | **~40M** across tens of thousands of companies |
	  | Purview-audited Copilot interactions | **50B cumulative, +~360% YoY** |
	- **The two metrics that matter most** are time-to-high-usage collapsing from months to days, and engagement reaching Outlook/Teams levels. Together they are the argument that Copilot has crossed from pilot to habit — which is what makes the consumption layer collectible rather than theoretical.
	- Named deployments: **NHS England 505,000** clinicians and staff (trial showed 43 minutes saved per employee per day), **KPMG 276,000+**, **HSBC 200,000**, **EY 400,000 on E7** — plus AstraZeneca, Boeing, Infosys, Koch, P&G, Stellantis, TCS, UPMC, Wells Fargo, and Wipro each at 60,000+. E7 booked **millions of seats across hundreds of enterprises within two months** of launch.
	- A "**super app**" unifying chat, Cowork, Autopilots, and Code across consumer and commercial is promised for the current quarter. **Autopilots** — always-on long-running autonomous agents — include a personal agent powered by **OpenClaw**.
	- **Microsoft Frontier Co.** launched this month: **6,000 embedded industry and engineering experts** as "the largest outcome-driven engineering organization in the industry," after piloting **330 projects across 164 customers**. This is a services/FDE motion — watch it for gross-margin dilution as it scales, since none was quantified.

- ## Data estate and distribution
	- **PostgreSQL revenue +55%**, third consecutive quarter of acceleration; PostgreSQL customers also using Foundry **+80%**. **Horizon DB** (managed PostgreSQL) claims **3x the throughput** of self-managed. **Fabric: 40,000+ paid customers, +60%**; Foundry-and-Fabric overlap **17,000 customers, +60%**. **Rayfin** agent-first SDK at 2,500 customers, now powering Replit app backends.
	- **Web IQ** — agent access to web intelligence — *"is already used by the most popular AI assistants, **including ChatGPT**."* Microsoft selling a retrieval layer into the assistant that competes with Copilot is a notable, under-discussed distribution win.
	- **~90% of the Fortune 500** grounding agents in Foundry, Fabric, or Work IQ; **>90% of the Fortune 500** on GitHub.
	- Silicon: **Maia 200** at **30% better performance per dollar** than the latest-generation fleet hardware, now serving **both OpenAI and MAI models**; **Cobalt 200 racks in >25 data centers** by month-end, with Adobe, Arm, Elastic, OpenAI, Sprinklr, and TomTom as named CPU customers; among the first to deploy **AMD Helios** and **NVIDIA Vera Rubin** rack-scale systems. Sovereignty push includes a **Mistral partnership** for fully disconnected environments.

- ## Overbuild, component costs, and what management concedes
	- Moerdler asked how Microsoft protects itself against overcapacity **and** component inflation. Hood's flexibility argument: **~two-thirds of CapEx is short-lived assets** (CPUs/GPUs) with short lead times, which is "the largest component and the driver of COGS" — so a demand break gets absorbed by slowing the biggest line first. Land and builds are a smaller, timing-flexible share. Plus a diversified book by geo/segment/industry and a large first-party app business consuming the same capacity: *"the ability to **late bind** some of the more expensive components."*
	- On component pricing she made no margin promise — efficiency work, the cloud-vs-on-prem ROI argument ("the price increases are even more hard for customers"), and **new contracts priced to reflect higher costs**.
	- **Nadella's tell, and the most candid line on the call**: *"All of us are reading this **1873** as the book to be read."* An unprompted reference to the railway-overbuild panic from the CEO doing the building. His conclusion is not that the cycle is safe but that the cycle is real — *"there will be ups and downs of what is the cycle here, but the secular shift is clear"* — with product shape, portfolio mix, customer mix, and "running an efficient railroad" as the defenses.
	- Hood on ROI (Borges): *"I don't know that, quite frankly, **my math has changed**"* versus a year ago — what changed is confidence in TAM expansion and the number of margin levers (silicon price/performance, first-party silicon, model diversification, token efficiency, hyperscale operating efficiency). Closing on the same note as the rest of the call: *"this is the **grind work**… We actually are quite good at that grind."*

- ## Financial structure and the weak spots
	- FY26: revenue **\$331B, +18%** (accelerating); operating income **>\$155B, +21%**, outpacing revenue; **\$43B+ returned** to shareholders. Q4 revenue **\$90B, +18%**; operating margin **45%**, up slightly; **headcount −2% YoY**.
	- Company gross margin **67%** and Microsoft Cloud gross margin **65%**, both down YoY on Azure mix and AI infrastructure — partly offset by efficiency gains. Cloud GM guided **roughly stable QoQ**, which is the first time in several quarters it is not guided down.
	- **Cash conversion is the pressure point**: operating cash flow **\$55.4B (+30%)** against **FCF of \$19.6B**, with CapEx of **\$41B** (~two-thirds short-lived), finance leases of **\$5.6B**, and cash paid for PP&E of **\$35.8B**. Note operating cash flow is now being reduced by **rising operating-lease payments** — the same reclassification that lowers reported CapEx moves cost into OCF over time, so FCF flatters less than the CapEx optics suggest. FY27 is guided only to "**remain free cash flow positive**," a notably low bar.
	- FY27 framing: double-digit revenue and operating income growth; opex **+mid-to-high single digits**; operating margins **down less than a point**; tax rate ~20%.
	- **More Personal Computing is now a real drag**: segment revenue **−4%**, operating margin down to 21%. Windows OEM and Devices **−7%** with OEMs building inventory ahead of component prices, guided to **−low twenties** in Q1 and **−high teens for FY27** as higher memory costs raise device pricing. Xbox revenue **−10%** with impairments and a stated **reset**, with return to growth deferred to **FY2027**.
	- Dynamics 365 **+13%**: ERP bookings healthy but **CRM moderating with longer sales cycles** — the one enterprise demand signal on the call pointing the wrong way, and it sits in the category most exposed to agent-driven seat compression.
