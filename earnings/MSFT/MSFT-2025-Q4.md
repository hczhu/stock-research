- **Source**: Microsoft **FY2026 Q2** earnings call, January 28, 2026 (Satya Nadella, Amy Hood, Jonathan Neilson). Fiscal Q2 = **calendar Q4 2025**, quarter ended December 31, 2025 — hence the calendar-quarter filename.
- **Theme**: The quarter Microsoft stopped letting investors read CapEx straight through to Azure. Hood reframed the Azure guide as an **allocated-capacity guide**, disclosed that ~**45% of a \$625B RPO is OpenAI**, and argued the GPU depreciation risk is largely absent because the fleet is **pre-sold for its useful life**. Azure grew 39% while demand still exceeded supply, and the stock traded down anyway on the CapEx-to-revenue gap.

---

- ## The reframing that matters: CapEx is not an Azure input
	- Keith Weiss (Morgan Stanley) opened by naming the disconnect directly — EPS +24%, margins expanding, "and the stock is still down" — attributing it to CapEx growing faster than expected against Azure growing slower than expected, i.e. **a CapEx ROI concern**.
	- Hood's answer rejected the correlation investors are drawing: *"Sometimes I think it's probably better to think about the Azure guidance that we give as an **allocated capacity guide** about what we can deliver in Azure revenue."* Capacity is rationed across four competing claims **before** Azure gets the residual:
		- 1. Increased usage and sales of **first-party apps** — M365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot
		- 2. **R&D allocation** to AI teams ("compute is also R&D") — explicitly credited for recent product acceleration
		- 3. Replacement of **end-of-life** server and networking equipment
		- 4. The remainder → **Azure third-party demand**
	- **The most quotable number on the call**: *"if I had taken the GPUs that just came online in Q1 and Q2, in terms of GPUs, and allocated them all to Azure, the KPI would have been **over 40**."* Azure printed 39%. Management is stating that reported Azure growth is a **allocation choice**, not a demand ceiling — which conveniently makes the headline KPI unfalsifiable from outside.
	- Nadella's version is an LTV-portfolio argument: *"we don't want to maximize just one business of ours. We want to be able to allocate capacity while we're supply constrained in a way that allows us to essentially build the best LTV portfolio."* Azure, M365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Dragon Copilot, and Security Copilot each have their own GM profile and lifetime value.
	- **How to use this**: Azure growth is now a managed output. The honest read-through is that CapEx ROI has to be judged on **total commercial cloud plus first-party AI app revenue**, not the Azure line — which is exactly the framing that makes the spend harder for outsiders to audit.

- ## The OpenAI concentration disclosure
	- | RPO component | Amount | Growth |
	  |------------------------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|
	  | Commercial RPO (net of reserves) | **\$625B** | **+110% YoY** |
	  | OpenAI-related share | **~45%** (~\$280B) | — |
	  | Everything else | **~55%, ~\$350B** | **+28% YoY** |
	  | Recognized in next 12 months | ~25% of total | +39% YoY |
	  | Recognized beyond 12 months | — | **+156%** |
	- Weighted average duration extended from **~2 years to ~2.5 years**; commercial bookings **+230%**, driven by the OpenAI Azure commitment plus the **November Anthropic commitment**.
	- Brent Thill (Jefferies) asked about durability of the OpenAI exposure. Hood **reframed rather than answered**: the point of disclosing 45% was to direct attention to the other 55% growing 28%, "larger than most peers, more diversified than most peers." On OpenAI itself she offered only that "it's a great partnership… we continue to be their provider of scale."
	- **The unaddressed question**: a single counterparty holding ~\$280B of contracted obligation, whose own funding depends on continued capital markets access. Hood pre-warned that the OpenAI contract "will result in some quarterly volatility in both bookings and RPO growth rates going forward" — so the RPO series is now non-comparable QoQ without stripping OpenAI out.
	- Also note the **circularity**: Microsoft books RPO from OpenAI while separately recognizing an OpenAI equity gain (below), and Anthropic — an Azure customer as of November — is simultaneously a model competitor sold **on** Foundry.

- ## The depreciation rebuttal — "already sold for the entirety of their useful life"
	- Mark Moerdler (Bernstein) posed the core bear case: servers capitalized over **six years** against RPO duration of only 2.5 years — how do investors get comfortable that AI-centric CapEx earns out across the full six?
	- Hood's answer is the single most important disclosure for the depreciation debate: *"the majority of the capital that we're spending today, and a lot of the GPUs that we're buying are **already contracted for most of their useful life**… they're already sold for the entirety of their useful life."* She attributes the short blended RPO duration to **M365 and Biz Apps three-year contracts**, not to Azure — "if you look at the Azure-only RPO, it's a little bit more extended," and GPU contracts for the largest customers are sold for the full GPU life.
	- Nadella's supporting mechanism: software keeps the aging fleet productive — *"we do use software to continuously run even the latest models on the fleet that is aging… each year you write the Moore's Law, you add, you use software, and then you optimize."* The fleet is aged continuously rather than bought in vintages.
	- Hood's closing point inverts the usual assumption: **margins improve over the asset's life**, because delivery efficiency rises against a price already locked at sale — "we see that obviously in the CPU fleet all the time."
	- **The load-bearing assumption to test**: this only holds if the pre-sold contracts are with counterparties good for the full term. Given ~45% of RPO is OpenAI, "already sold for the entirety of their useful life" and the concentration disclosure are the **same risk viewed from two angles**. Compare the independent framework in [[2026-07-30-jassy-aws-data-center-server-capital-cycle]].

- ## CapEx composition — and the useful-life baseline before it changed
	- | CapEx item | Q2 (Dec 2025) |
	  |--------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
	  | Total capital expenditures | **\$37.5B** |
	  | Short-lived assets (GPUs and CPUs) | **~two-thirds** |
	  | Finance leases (mostly large DC sites)| **\$6.7B** |
	  | Cash paid for PP&E | **\$29.9B** |
	  | Capacity added in the quarter | **~1 gigawatt** |
	- **Important baseline for the accounting story**: on this call Hood described long-lived assets as supporting *"monetization for the next **15 years and beyond**."* Two quarters later, on the FY26 Q4 call, Microsoft **extended that estimate to 25 years**, which pushed future data-center leases from finance to operating classification and cut announced CapEx by ~\$15B — see [[2026-08-04-stratechery-microsoft-vs-meta-efficiency-payoff]] and [[2026-06-10-morgan-stanley-hyperscaler-capex-accounting-risk]]. **This file is the pre-change baseline**; any FY26 CapEx trend line that spans both quarters is measuring an assumption change, not a spending change.
	- Cash flow shows the strain the headline hides: operating cash flow **\$35.8B (+60%)** but free cash flow only **\$5.9B**, down sequentially "reflecting the higher cash capital expenditures from a lower mix of finance leases." **The finance-lease mix is what determines reported FCF quarter to quarter** — and it is discretionary.
	- Q3 guide: CapEx **decreases sequentially** on normal buildout variability and finance-lease delivery timing; short-lived mix stays similar.

- ## The OpenAI equity-method change
	- Microsoft still accounts for OpenAI under the **equity method**, but post-recapitalization now records its share of the change in OpenAI's **net assets (balance sheet)** rather than its share of operating profit/loss (income statement).
	- Effect: a gain that drove GAAP **other income and expense to \$10B**. Adjusted for OpenAI, OIE was **slightly negative** and below expectations on net investment losses.
	- **Read**: reported EPS of \$4.14 (+24%, +21% cc) is stated "when adjusted for the impact from our investment in OpenAI," but the GAAP line now imports OpenAI balance-sheet marks directly into Microsoft's earnings. This is a **new source of non-operating EPS volatility** tied to a private company's valuation, and it moves in the same direction as the RPO exposure.

- ## Silicon and fleet economics
	- **The stated optimization target is "tokens per watt per dollar"** — decomposed into raising utilization and lowering TCO through silicon, systems, and software. This is the cleanest articulation of a hyperscaler unit-economics KPI on any call this cycle.
	- **50% throughput increase** achieved on one of the highest-volume workloads — **OpenAI inferencing powering the Copilots**. Pure software/systems gain on installed hardware.
	- **Maia 200** brought online: **10+ petaFLOPS at FP4**, **>30% improved TCO** versus the latest generation in the fleet. Scaling first on inferencing and synthetic data generation for the superintelligence team, then Copilot and Foundry inference.
	- **Cobalt 200**: >50% higher performance than the first-gen custom CPU. Nadella's framing that AI workloads are not only accelerator workloads — agents "spawn through tools used, maybe a container, which runs on compute… **They don't need GPUs. They need computers**" — is the same CPU-attach mechanism Datadog described independently in [[DDOG-2026-Q2]].
	- **Fairwater**: Atlanta and Wisconsin sites connected via **AI WAN** into "a first-of-its-kind AI superfactory"; two-story design and liquid cooling enable higher GPU density. Hood deliberately deflected focus from named sites — "those are multiyear deliveries… I would definitely abstract away from that."
	- Nadella on merchant vs. custom silicon: *"we're not locked into any one thing… you have to be ahead **for all time to come**"* — the argument for keeping NVIDIA and AMD alongside Maia is that no single vendor stays TCO-optimal across generations. Vertical integration as an option, not a commitment.

- ## Memory pricing is a named, two-sided exposure
	- **Pull-forward (helping now)**: on-premises server revenue beat on "higher transactional purchasing **ahead of memory price increases**," and Windows OEM grew 5% with elevated inventory for the same reason. Both are borrowing from future quarters.
	- **Payback (guided)**: Windows OEM guided to **~−10%** with "the range of potential outcomes wider than normal, in part due to the potential impact on the PC market from increased memory pricing." On-prem server guided to low-single-digit decline.
	- **CapEx and margin**: Hood said rising memory prices "would impact capital expenditures, though the impact on Microsoft Cloud gross margins will **build more gradually as these assets depreciate over six years**." The six-year depreciation schedule converts a memory-cost spike into a slow, multi-year gross-margin drag rather than a visible hit — a materially different transmission than at hardware companies (contrast [[AAPL-2026-Q2]], where memory cost explained >100% of sequential GM compression within the quarter).

- ## Agent platform and Copilot adoption
	- Nadella organized the whole call around three stack layers: **Cloud & Token Factory → Agent Platform → High Value Agentic Experiences**. "Agents are the new Apps."
	- | Metric | Disclosed |
	  |-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
	  | Paid M365 Copilot seats | **15M**, seat adds **+160% YoY** (record) |
	  | M365 Copilot daily active users | **+10x YoY** |
	  | Conversations per user | **doubled YoY** |
	  | Customers with >35,000 seats | **tripled YoY** |
	  | Paid GitHub Copilot subscribers | **4.7M, +75% YoY**; Pro+ **+77% QoQ** |
	  | Consumer Copilot app daily users | **~3x YoY** |
	  | Foundry customers spending >\$1M/quarter | **+80% YoY** |
	  | Customers on track for >1T tokens/yr on Foundry | **250+** |
	  | Fabric annual run rate | **>\$2B**, +60%, 31,000+ customers |
	  | Purview-audited Copilot interactions | **24B in the quarter, +9x YoY** |
	  | Fortune 500 with agents on Copilot Studio / Agent Builder | **>80%** |
	- Named large seat purchases: **Publicis 95,000+**; Fiserv, ING, NASA, University of Kentucky, University of Manchester, US Dept. of the Interior, Westpac all >35,000. Siemens went all-in on GitHub after a 30,000-developer Copilot rollout.
	- **Agent 365** — extending existing identity, security, governance, and management to agents, **across clouds**, with Adobe, Databricks, Genspark, Glean, NVIDIA, SAP, ServiceNow, and Workday integrating. Microsoft claims first-mover on an agent **"control plane."** This is the concrete product behind the "model middleware" thesis; the cross-cloud scope is the tell that the control plane, not the model, is the intended lock-in.
	- **Multi-model is the stated position**: GPT-5.2 and **Claude 4.5** both added this quarter; **>1,500 customers have used both Anthropic and OpenAI models on Foundry**; Mistral and Cohere added for sovereignty. GitHub Agent HQ organizes coding agents from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Cognition, and xAI inside customer repos.
	- **Work IQ** is the differentiation claim — reasoning over M365 people/roles/artifacts/communications/history within the security boundary, exposed as an **MCP server** into GitHub workflows. Nadella: the M365 substrate is "the most important database underneath for any company that uses Microsoft today" because of its **tacit information**.
	- Customers wanting to "capture the tacit knowledge they possess **inside of model weights as their core IP**" is framed as "probably the most important sovereign consideration for firms." Sovereignty was a recurring theme — **DC investments announced in seven countries this quarter alone**.

- ## Operating detail worth keeping
	- **Opex grew only 5% (4% cc)** against 17% revenue growth — including gaming impairment charges. Operating margin **47%**, up YoY and ahead of expectations; FY26 operating margins now guided **up slightly** (previously flat), helped by favorable mix from Windows OEM and on-prem.
	- Company gross margin **68%**, down slightly; Microsoft Cloud GM **67%**, guided to **~65%** next quarter on continued AI investment. The AI mix shift is visible and management is not fighting it.
	- Segment operating margins: Productivity and Business Processes **60%** (up), Intelligent Cloud **42%** (down slightly), More Personal Computing **27%** (flat).
	- **Softness that got no airtime**: Gaming revenue **−9%** with Xbox content and services **−5%, below expectations**, plus impairment charges; Search and news ex-TAC **+10%, slightly below expectations** on "some execution challenges." Paid M365 commercial seats grew only **6%** to >450M, concentrated in **SMB and frontline** — the Copilot ARPU story is being carried by price and attach, not seat expansion.
	- Other franchise markers: **1B Windows 11 users (+45%)**; **1.6M security customers**, >1M using four or more workloads; new SQL Server at **>2x** the IaaS adoption of the prior version; Dragon Copilot documenting **21M patient encounters (+3x)** across 100,000+ providers; Security Copilot rolling out to **all E5 customers**.
	- \$12.7B returned to shareholders (**+32% YoY**) — Microsoft is still funding buybacks and dividends through the buildout, which is the structural contrast with [[META-2026-Q2]].
