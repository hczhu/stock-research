- ## Unity 2026 Q3 earnings
	- Quarter ended September 30, 2026. Unity reported on November 5, 2026.
	- **Source**: Unity's Q3 2026 press release (PDF supplied by the user) and the earnings call transcript with CEO Matt Bromberg and CFO Jarrod Yahes.
	- **Management's account**: Vector, Unity's AI ad-prediction engine, grew **40% QoQ against a 20% guide**, a step change that Unity attributes to model updates, infrastructure efficiency and the Day 28 ROAS product, not to take rate. Unity posted its **first GAAP-profitable quarter since the IPO**. Bromberg frames the business as a flywheel: AI makes game creation easier, more games raise the need for discovery, and discovery feeds the ad business. Opening the editor to coding agents and the Google-partnered Unity Spark are meant to widen the top of that funnel.

- ## Vector: what drove the step change
	- **Three pillars**, per Bromberg: continuous product releases, ever higher-quality data (including runtime data), and models that are retrained daily. "There is no one silver bullet." Q3's outperformance came from "highly effective model updates, major advancements in infrastructure efficiency, and continued robust momentum in our Day 28 ROAS product" (Yahes).
	- **Not take rate**: growth is "primarily a result of greater volume of installs and higher player values, not changes in take rate." Asked whether take rate could rise, Bromberg said "we just do not think about take rate... It is not a lever that we use."
	- **Broad-based**: across customers, operating systems, campaign types and geographies.
	- **New advertisers**: Vector advertiser accounts grew **27% QoQ**, a single-quarter sequential increase. Still, **90% of Q3 growth came from existing advertisers**, so the new accounts have only started spending.
	- **Day 28 optimizer**: about a quarter old. Bromberg calls it "table stakes" for a player at scale and says the roadmap has "a really long list of fundamental changes" as impactful. A typical quarter brings six to ten significant product changes, which Unity won't list in advance.
	- **Growth pattern and guidance**: Vector is seven quarters old. Bromberg expects "incremental solid growth and then occasional step change functions" whose timing can't be predicted, and said "every quarter is not going to be a 40% quarter." Yahes said the guidance philosophy is unchanged: guide on run rates, seasonality and known releases, so unforecast step changes show up as large beats.
	- **Market size**: Bromberg puts the mobile game advertising opportunity at "at least \$40 billion," growing double digits.
	- **The AppLovin MAX data dispute** ([[2026-11-07-applovin-unity-max-data-dispute-vector-competition]]) wasn't raised on the call.
- ## Runtime data and the LTV side of the equation
	- Bromberg says the industry talks about user acquisition given a lifetime value (LTV), but publishers spend "99% of their time" on raising engagement and LTV. Because games are built on Unity and connected to its runtime, Unity sees in-game player behavior in real time: "what they are bored with, what they are interested in, what they would like to spend." Bromberg calls this "a unique advantage that exists nowhere else" and "a long-term tactical and strategic moat."
	- **Opt-in**: runtime-data opt-in on newly created projects remains **over 90%**. **30% of the top 1,000 Vector spenders** now share runtime data, and Unity expects that to grow.
	- Bromberg says the runtime opportunity isn't static coverage: Unity will get better at modeling consumer behavior from the same data over time.
- ## Creation: coding agents and Unity Spark
	- **Coding agents**: Unity opened its editor to coding agents and added a command-line interface (announced at Unite Seoul), making it "a fully programmable agentic automation target." It is model agnostic, so customers avoid vendor lock-in and can optimize token spend. Coding-agent use rose sharply, and **creator DAU is up 25% since early August**, when Unity opened to agents.
	- **Services bound at creation**: Bromberg says the old flow had developers download Unity, learn it, then later find and integrate Vector's SDK. In agentic creation, Unity services including Vector are "bound instantly at the moment of creation," without separate downloads, including Unity itself.
	- **Unity Spark** (announced early October with Google): a browser-based, no-code, collaborative creation platform paired with the Unity Asset Store. Closed beta this year, full launch in 2027.
		- **Google partnership**: an upgrade path for casual users of Google's Playground creation product, and distribution to the users of Google's consumer products. The partnership is **non-exclusive**; Bromberg expects Spark to be "distributed everywhere."
		- **Positioning**: "we have no interest in the very casual end of this market." Spark targets high-quality games by artists who can't code, coders who can't make art, and level designers. Bromberg pitched it against the view that frontier-lab tools make game engines unnecessary: Unity's edge is 20 years of knowledge of how games get made, runtime feedback, and build-once distribution to 25+ platforms.
		- **Audience**: Bromberg expects non-professional creators eventually to be "10 or 20 or 30 times as large as the audience of professional creators."
		- **Monetization** (pricing not announced): freemium with a daily allocation, then subscriptions, consumption-based revenue and advertising. The ads would sit outside the mediated ecosystem, giving Unity its own **owned-and-operated inventory**, which Yahes calls "a new tremendous opportunity."
		- **Margin levers** (Yahes): every prompt can be used to fine-tune and optimize models, lowering token cost per creation. Unity-owned Asset Store assets used in creation are "100% flow-through margin," and sales of artist-owned assets add incremental margin.
		- **Human-made art**: Bromberg cites "a meaningful and significant backlash against AI-created art" and the "genericization" of model output. Bromberg sees the Asset Store, "by far the largest source of artist-created art in the world," as a way to help creators build more appealing games.
	- **Where AI shows up in the numbers**: asked whether the AI inflection reaches Create or Grow first, Bromberg said it drives "all of our business." Bromberg expects the transition to be "a little bit bumpy" and to "create some dislocation among developers."
- ## Margin structure and modeling
	- **Gross margin**: adjusted gross margin rose 200 bps QoQ to 85%, mainly from leverage on cloud costs in cost of goods sold. Adjusted R&D, S&M and G&A were flat to down in dollars from Q2.
	- **S&M**: adjusted S&M fell **\$22M QoQ** to 12% of revenue (21% a year earlier), mainly from the Supersonic sale.
	- **Amortization falls off sharply**: intangible assets fell from \$651M at end-2025 to \$86M, after \$279M of 9M impairments (mostly in cost of revenue, in H1) and \$272M of amortization. Amortization goes from \$77M in Q3 to **\$50M in Q4** and **\$15M a quarter in 2027**.
	- **2027 modeling inputs**: stock compensation of **\$75M a quarter** (Q3: \$63M) and an effective tax rate of **5–10%**.
	- **Why GAAP profit matters to management**: potential eligibility for major equity indices, a broader institutional investor base, and credit-rating upside.
	- **Working capital**: accounts receivable rose \$140M in Q3 and publisher payables \$72M, as ad-network volume grew.
- ## Capital allocation
	- The \$558M of convertible notes due **November 15, 2026** will be repaid with cash on hand. \$1.68B of longer-dated convertibles remain.
	- Cash generation gives flexibility for product investment, potential M&A, and returning capital to shareholders. No buyback was announced.
- ## Other notes
	- **3 billion** people a month play a made-with-Unity game. Bromberg clarified that this is a consumer reach figure, not advertising supply.
