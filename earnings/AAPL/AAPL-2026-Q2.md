- **Source**: Apple FY2026 Q3 earnings call (calendar Q2 2026, quarter ended June 27, 2026). Tim Cook, Kevan Parekh; incoming CEO John Ternus also on the call. **Tim Cook's final earnings call.**
- **Related memos**: [[2026-07-04-horace-dediu-memory-shock-apple-supply-chain]] predicted exactly this dynamic — the memory shock breaking Apple's historical procurement advantage; Cook now confirms it on the record. [[2026-07-30-asymco-apple-15b-paid-subscriptions]] is validated here — Apple officially confirmed surpassing 1.5B paid subscriptions.
- **Theme**: **A demand-driven blowout quarter running straight into two cost/supply walls.** iPhone +22% and Mac +29% produced a June record, but management is now constrained by advanced-node SoC availability and is absorbing what Cook called a **"100-year flood" in memory pricing** — which explains *more than 100%* of gross margin compression both this quarter and next. Apple has, unusually, **raised prices**.

- ## The Memory Shock — the dominant story
	- Cook's framing: **"a 100-year flood on the memory pricing, with exponential increases in memory prices."** Apple "reluctantly raised prices" on iPad and Mac as a direct result.
	- **Cost trajectory disclosed across four quarters**: paid more in the March quarter than December; **significantly more in June than March**; expects to pay **even higher in September**; and beyond September sees **market pricing continuing to increase**, which "could drive an increasing impact on our business."
	- **The margin bridge is the cleanest disclosure on the call** — Parekh quantified it twice, and memory dominates both legs:
		- | Sequential step (ex-tariff refund) | Change | Attribution |
		  |------------------------------------|-----------|--------------------------------------------|
		  | March 49.3% → June 48.1% | **−120bps** | **">100% explained by memory cost change"** |
		  | June 48.1% → Sept 46.5% (guide mid) | **−160bps** | Again "more than that is explained by memory" |
		- FX was explicitly described as **"a pretty minimal impact when you look at that versus the memory."** This is a cost-of-goods story, not a currency story.
	- **Three named offsets, two of them decaying**:
		- **Carry-in inventory** benefit — helped June, helps September, but "we believe we'll see decreasing benefit from this over time beyond the September quarter." This is the temporary cushion running out.
		- **Lower costs on certain non-memory components** in the BOM.
		- Favorable mix.
	- **Structural constraint acknowledged**: "primarily the DRAM market has three suppliers. Obviously if there were more suppliers, that would be good, and it would help us on the supply side and perhaps the pricing side. **It's unclear on the pricing side**, it could help on the supply side." Apple is "evaluating all options" for sourcing flexibility — Cook separates the *supply* benefit (likely) from the *pricing* benefit (doubtful) of adding suppliers.
	- **Pricing philosophy under cost pressure** — useful for modelling future price actions: Apple looks at **units, revenue, and margin together** and makes "a business judgment," explicitly **not a mathematical formula** targeting either gross margin percentage or gross profit dollars, and judged "over the long term, instead of a 90-day clock."
	- Elasticity is **not yet measurable**: price increases on iPad and Mac are too recent, since "it takes a little while for the channels to adjust" given channel inventory. Cook said they'll "understand that more in the weeks ahead."

- ## Supply Constraints: a demand-forecast miss, not a supplier failure
	- Cook was unusually direct on root cause: **"the root cause of it is not a regular supply issue. It's a demand forecast issue, to be candid, where the iPhone and the Mac are both doing remarkably better than we thought they would do."** Expectations were high and still too low.
	- Binding constraint is **availability of the advanced nodes Apple's SoCs are produced on**. June constraints hit **primarily Mac**, and iPhone/iPad to a lesser extent. September constraints **increase significantly** and hit **iPhone, Mac, and iPad**.
	- Apple has been **pulling supply ahead** and is near the limit — "we've got a quarter that we're going to be scrambling on the supply side."
	- Asked directly whether Apple would broaden silicon suppliers to relieve this, Cook **declined the premise**: "this isn't a partner or supplier issue." He redirected to Arizona, where Apple sources **>100M components this year** and is "more than pleased with how that fab has ramped."
	- **The two constraints are distinct and should not be conflated**: advanced-node SoC scarcity limits *units*; DRAM pricing compresses *margin*. Both are worsening into September.

- ## Guidance and the deceleration bridge
	- | Metric | September quarter guide |
	  |--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
	  | Total revenue growth | **9–11% YoY** |
	  | iPhone growth | **mid-teens YoY** |
	  | Services growth | ≈ June's rate less ~2.5pp incremental FX |
	  | Gross margin | **47–48%**, incl. ~1pp tariff-refund benefit |
	  | Operating expenses | \$19.1–19.4B |
	  | OI&E / tax rate | ~\$350M / ~16.5% |
	- The ~500bps deceleration from June decomposes into **~2.5pp FX** plus **significantly increased supply constraints** — Parekh: "when you combine those two factors, we get pretty close to the June overall total company growth rate." **Underlying demand is not decelerating.**
	- Management **explicitly refused to guide beyond September**, declining a direct question on the December quarter.

- ## Results: what actually changed
	- | Segment | Revenue | YoY | Note |
	  |-------------------------|-----------|---------|--------------------------------------------------|
	  | Total | \$109.4B | **+16%** | June quarter record; records in **every** geo segment |
	  | iPhone | \$54.3B | **+22%** | June records in every geo segment; record upgraders; **share gain (IDC)** |
	  | Mac | \$10.4B | **+29%** | June record **despite** significant supply constraints; **share gain (IDC)**; all-time China Mainland record |
	  | iPad | \$6.2B | **−6%** | Compare against prior-year A16 iPad launch |
	  | Wearables/Home/Access. | \$7.9B | +6% | Apple Watch June record for upgraders |
	  | Services | \$30.7B | **+12%** | June record; records in **every** category |
	- Products revenue \$78.7B (+18%). Gross margin **50.1%** (+80bps sequential) including **~2pp tariff-refund benefit** — ex-refund, at the **midpoint of prior guidance**. Products GM 40.1% (refund benefit >2.5pp); Services GM 75.6% (−110bps on mix).
	- EPS **\$2.02, +29%**, including **\$0.11** from tariff refunds. Operating cash flow \$34.4B. OpEx \$19.1B, **+23% YoY on R&D** — the AI investment showing up in the P&L.
	- Install base **>2.5B active devices**, all-time high across all major categories and geographies. **Paid subscriptions surpassed 1.5B.** Transacting and paid accounts both at all-time highs, double-digit growth in emerging markets.
	- Capital return: **\$33B** returned (\$25.8B buyback, \$4B dividends). Cash \$147B against \$84B debt. Dividend \$0.27/share.

- ## Services deceleration: four named causes
	- 16% (March) → 12% (June), and guided lower again. Parekh's decomposition:
		- **FX is the main driver** — and it hits Services harder than the company overall: **~5pp headwind** to the YoY growth rate from March to September, with **~2.5pp incremental** from June to September.
		- **F1 theatrical comp** — last year's release was one of the highest-grossing sports films in history, with no theatrical release this year. It flatters the year-ago **June *and* September** quarters, so this comp persists.
		- **Mobile gaming softness in the App Store** — the one item Parekh admitted missed plan: "we roughly met our expectations, but we did see a bit more softness on mobile gaming in the App Store."
		- **App Store business model changes** in certain countries, plus the **US link-out court ruling** still in effect. **The Supreme Court will hear Apple's appeal** — a genuine binary catalyst for App Store economics.
	- Offsetting strength: all-time records in **cloud services and payment services**; June records in advertising, App Store, AppleCare, Music, and TV+ (viewership at an all-time high). Apple Pay hit record users in both developed and emerging markets.

- ## Siri AI: capability confident, economics undefined
	- Cook is emphatic on reception — developer and public beta feedback "overwhelmingly positive" — but was **candid that the cost model is unresolved**: "it's obviously early going for us. **I don't want to say that we have a complete plan for that.**"
	- **Monetization mechanism named**: heavy users are expected to **"buy up the stack on iCloud+."** That is the disclosed path to recovering inference cost — no separate AI subscription was mentioned.
	- **Capital intensity deliberately left open**: hybrid of third-party cloud and Apple's own data centers, "there will be a mix." AI spend appears in **OpEx and COGS**, and "what the balance of that is a bit uncertain at the moment." Notably Apple gave **no capex figure** — a sharp contrast to hyperscaler disclosure.
	- **Strategic argument**: running "some percentage of requests on device is also very strategic and sort of a competitive weapon." Apple traces the groundwork to the **2017 Neural Engine**, claiming differentiation from unified memory bandwidth + power-efficient performance + on-device intelligence.
	- **Regulatory gating is the concrete near-term risk**:
		- **EU** — Siri AI **not launching**; Apple is "working closely with the Commission." Notable asymmetry: **Mac will get Siri AI in the EU because "the Mac is not covered by the same regulations as the iPhone and the iPad."**
		- **China** — approval received "last week" for the *original* Apple Intelligence features (cleanup etc.); **Siri AI requires further work and is at "the front end"** of that process.

- ## Mac as an AI workstation — the displacement story
	- The most concrete competitive-share evidence on the call: **about half of MacBook Neo large purchases by US education institutions displaced Windows and Chromebook devices.** Named districts: Pinellas County (25,000 students off Windows, 18 high schools), Peninsula SD 401 (>8,000 off Chromebooks), Midwest City-Del City (>6,000).
	- Enterprise on-device-AI proof points, all framed around **cloud cost avoidance and data control**: Disney creative teams using Mac to **"reduce overall cloud token costs and keep their IP secure"**; Crédit Agricole cutting manual regulatory processing time **>80%** on MacBook Pro; Morgan Stanley deploying **>20,000 iPhone 17** in a shift from employee-owned to corporate-owned.
	- Cook positions Mac for **local frontier-model inference**: Mac mini as an agentic AI platform, and **"clusters of Mac Studio systems to run frontier class models locally."** This is Apple explicitly courting the AI-developer workload.

- ## US Manufacturing and Other Items
	- **Broadcom agreement — expected to exceed \$30B**, multi-year, for custom silicon components and wireless connectivity. Called Apple's **largest-ever American manufacturing program commitment** and a step toward "an end-to-end silicon supply chain here in the U.S."
	- Sits inside the prior **\$600B / four-year US commitment**; Apple reiterates it will **reinvest tariff refunds into the US**.
	- **Apple Advanced Manufacturing Center, Houston** — in a facility that **already assembles advanced AI servers**; will build **Mac mini** there later this year.
	- **Apple Upgrade** — new US hardware leasing program with **Klarna**, retail stores only, not broadly channel-distributed. Cook's angle is residual value: Apple products hold value better than competitors', making leasing economics favorable. Too early for uptake data.
	- **CEO transition**: Cook's final call; Ternus on the line but gave only a brief, non-substantive answer when asked about competitive threats from OpenAI/SpaceX devices — no strategic detail offered.
