- **Source**: Micron 2026Q1 earnings call, Mar. 18, 2026 (4:30 p.m. ET); CEO Sanjay Mehrotra, CFO Mark Murphy
- **Quarter labeling**: Micron reports this as fiscal Q2; here it is **2026Q1** (calendar). Guidance quarter (Micron's fiscal Q3) = **2026Q2**. Prior quarter (the previous FCF record) = **2025Q4**. Annual figures labeled "fiscal 2026 / 2027" are Micron's fiscal years (~Sep–Aug).
- **Thesis**: AI has "recast memory as a defining strategic asset." Micron is running into a structural, multi-year memory shortage — pricing, margins, and FCF are at all-time records and management argues this is *not* a normal cycle that reverts to the historical mean. The bottleneck is physical (greenfield cleanroom + multi-year lead times), so the upcycle is supply-durable, not just demand-driven.

- ## Key Value-Driver Metrics (what to track)
- **Memory pricing (QoQ %)** — the dominant lever: DRAM +~66%, NAND +~77% this quarter. At 81% GM, mgmt notes **incremental price has diminishing GM impact**.
- **Gross margin trajectory** — 57%→75%→~81% (guide). Mgmt declined to guide 2026Q2-out but says conditions "remain tight beyond 2026."
- **Bit-shipment growth** — Micron DRAM/NAND supply to grow ~**in line with industry** in 2026 (DRAM industry bits +low-20s%, NAND +~20%).
- **HBM ramp** — HBM4 36GB 12-Hi in volume (designed into NVIDIA Vera Rubin); reaching mature yields faster than HBM3E.
- **Data-center mix** — DC bits to exceed **>50% of industry TAM for the first time in calendar 2026**.
- **CapEx intensity** — fiscal 2026 **>$25B**; fiscal 2027 stepping up meaningfully (construction CapEx +>$10B YoY) → watch FCF conversion as capex ramps.
- **FCF / net cash** — $6.9B FCF, $6.5B net cash; FCF guided to ~double QoQ. ROIC **>30%, headed toward 50%**.
- **Demand-fill ratio** — key customers receiving **only 50%–two-thirds of demand** (unchanged QoQ) → backlog/visibility proxy.

- ## Supply Shortage — the core argument
- "We are now seeing **NAND demand significantly in excess of available supply for the foreseeable future**."
- Key customers can be filled **only 50%–two-thirds of demand** in the medium term (still the case vs last quarter).
- Both DRAM and NAND **bit demand in calendar 2026 constrained by supply**; tightness expected to **persist beyond calendar 2026**.
- Structural supply constraints (durable, not cyclical):
	- Low industry inventory levels.
	- **Declining bits-per-wafer growth** from node migration.
	- **Higher HBM trade ratio** (HBM consumes more wafer per usable bit) + higher HBM growth.
	- Cleanroom space limits; some NAND suppliers redirecting cleanroom to DRAM.
	- New capacity must be **greenfield** → long construction lead times.
- Mgmt pushback on "revert to historical mean" (prior GM peaks ~low-60s): AI is a **transformational secular driver**; memory is now "more valuable and an efficient way to monetize AI" (lowers energy/token, raises tokens & intelligence). Both the *value of memory* and the *structural supply challenge* are "durable."

- ## Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs) — new structure
- Signed **first-ever 5-year SCA** with a **large customer**; in discussions with **multiple customers across multiple markets**.
- Differs from prior **LTAs** (typically 1-year): SCAs are **multiyear with specific volume + supply commitments** for both sides → visibility & stability in the business model.
- Confidential terms; mgmt would not confirm whether they cap downside GM, but called provisions "robust" for Micron and customers, meant to span tight *and* looser environments.
- Side benefit: deeper **R&D collaboration / roadmap planning** and earlier engagement (relevant to custom HBM base-die co-design with GPU/XPU customers).

- ## Demand Drivers by End Market
- **AI / data center**: DRAM content in advanced AI accelerators **doubled YoY**. Server units +low-teens % in 2026 (AI + traditional refresh + agentic-AI workloads). Both AI and traditional server demand are **supply-constrained**.
- **HBM roadmap**: HBM4 12-Hi shipping → HBM4 16-Hi sampled (48GB, +33% capacity); HBM4E volume in **calendar 2027** (on 1γ node); HBM4 customization (base-die) = differentiation.
- **LPDRAM in data center**: ~1/3 the power of DDR server modules; sampled industry-first **256GB LP SoC-M2** (1γ) → 2TB/CPU, 4× content YoY. NVIDIA Grok 3 LPX rack uses up to **12TB DDR5**.
- **Data-center SSD**: G9-based **PCIe Gen6** SSDs in high-volume production; **122TB** high-capacity SSD (16× seq read/W vs HDD). DC SSD share up 4th consecutive year (2025) to record; **enterprise SSD ~½ of NAND mix**, roughly doubled QoQ.
- **PC / smartphone** (price-sensitive): units could **decline low-double-digit %** in 2026 on supply constraints + high prices, but content rising:
	- Flagship phones with **≥12GB DRAM jumped from <20% → ~80% YoY** (calendar Q4).
	- Agentic-AI PCs recommend **≥32GB** (2× avg PC); AI workstations (DGX Spark, Ryzen AI Halo) ship **128GB**.
- **Automotive**: L2 car ≈16GB DRAM vs **L4 >300GB**; L2+ ADAS rolling out fast → long-term content growth. First auto-grade 1γ LPDDR5; first G9 UFS 4.1 auto.
- **Robotics**: mgmt calls it a **"20-year growth vector"**; humanoids ≈ high-end L4 car compute → large memory/storage content.

- ## Technology Roadmap & Execution
- **1γ (1-gamma) DRAM**: fastest ramp to mature yields + **fastest volume ramp in company history**; majority of DRAM bit mix by **mid-calendar 2026**. To become highest-volume node ever.
- **1δ (1-delta) DRAM**: increasing EUV adoption with latest-gen EUV tools (cleanroom efficiency + patterning).
- **G9 NAND**: majority of bits by **mid-calendar 2026**; record QLC mix.
- Co-locating R&D + HVM at **Boise** and **Singapore** to speed time-to-market.
- A **clear majority of customers rank Micron #1 in quality.**

- ## Notable Q&A Takeaways
- **GM sustainability (Sankar/Arya)**: 81% contemplates HBM4 growth; at these levels incremental price matters less. Don't assume reversion to historical ~low-60s peak — AI value of memory + structural supply are durable.
- **Allocation / demand destruction (Moore)**: supply tight across all markets; price-sensitive consumer demand dented but Micron wants a **diversified end-market mix**; data center takes a growing share.
- **HBM vs DDR5 margins (Muse)**: **non-HBM margins currently *higher* than HBM** — but Micron manages full portfolio (HBM/LP/SoC-M/DDR5/SSD), not chasing one product.
- **HBM share (Arya)**: hit target of HBM share ≈ DRAM share in **calendar 2025**; no longer breaking out HBM share quarterly; supplying both HBM4 and HBM3E in 2026.
- **SRAM/LPU architectures (O'Malley)**: efficiency gains grow the pie; NVIDIA Grok LPX still uses 12TB DRAM/rack — complementary, expands TAM.
- **HBF (high-bandwidth flash) (Sur)**: interesting capacity attributes but limited by NAND write speed/power/retention — early, studying it.

  ---
- ## Risks / Watch-items
- **Cyclicality**: memory has always mean-reverted; the bull case hinges on "this time is structural." SCAs' downside protection unconfirmed.
- **Price-led growth**: bit shipments only up low/mid-single digits — revenue is overwhelmingly price; a price rollover would hit hard given diminishing volume cushion.
- **CapEx ramp**: fiscal-2027 construction CapEx +>$10B YoY → FCF conversion risk if demand softens before greenfield capacity lands (2027–2028).
- **Buyback constrained** by CHIPS terms.
- **Geopolitical / trade** explicitly excluded from guidance.
- **Consumer unit declines** (PC/phone -low-double-digits) if prices stay elevated.
