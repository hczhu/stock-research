- **Source**: Micron FY2026 Q3 earnings deck and post-earnings analyst call transcript
- **Quarter covered**: Calendar 2026 Q2 / Micron FY2026 Q3; reported June 24, 2026
- **Theme**: Micron is explicitly trying to convert the memory upcycle from spot-driven commodity pricing into a contracted, take-or-pay, supply-assurance model. Management framed demand as supply-limited rather than demand-limited, with DRAM / HBM especially tight through at least 2028 and NAND also structurally constrained.

- ## Headline
	- **Business-model change is the quarter's main signal**: Micron has moved from ordinary LTAs toward non-cancelable Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs) with take-or-pay commitments, price floors / ceilings, and upfront deposits.
	- **Supply, not demand, now determines bit shipment growth**: management said demand is so far above industry supply that near- and medium-term shipped bits are more constrained by capacity than by end-market demand.
	- **HBM tightness pulled forward**: management previously discussed HBM TAM crossing `\$100B` in 2028; this quarter it said HBM TAM should "easily" cross that level in 2027.
	- **Non-HBM DRAM is also in the same scarcity category**: management explicitly said the demand-above-supply dynamic applies not only to HBM, but also to non-HBM DRAM.
	- **NAND is constrained too, but customer urgency is higher in DRAM**: customers want NAND access across SCAs, especially enterprise SSDs, but management described DRAM as the more acute bottleneck.

- ## Strategic Customer Agreements
	- SCAs are designed as take-or-pay agreements, not soft forecast agreements. Customers have no contractual right to walk away; if they do not take product, they still owe price times volume.
	- Most non-automotive SCAs are five-year agreements running through calendar 2030; automotive agreements are generally shorter because that market has different procurement patterns.
	- Current signed SCAs cover `16` customers across data center, consumer, and automotive; they represent roughly `20%` of DRAM volume and roughly one-third of NAND volume over the term.
	- Micron expects SCAs to eventually cover roughly half or more of company revenue. This is the more important number than current coverage because it describes management's intended business model, not just signed backlog.
	- Several hyperscalers are already included; where their purchasing requires HBM, HBM is part of the agreement.
	- NAND is also part of the SCA strategy. The ability to offer both DRAM and NAND appears competitively useful because AI customers need an integrated memory / storage hierarchy, not only HBM.

	| SCA feature | Management detail | Investment read-through |
	|---|---|---|
	| Contract form | Take-or-pay, non-cancelable | Converts part of memory demand from forecast to obligation |
	| Duration | Usually five years outside automotive; through calendar 2030 for many large SCAs | Extends visibility across a full fab / node investment cycle |
	| Pricing | Price bands with floors and ceilings for many agreements; some fixed-price or market-price structures | Reduces downside pricing volatility while preserving negotiated upside for new products |
	| Upfront customer commitment | Cash deposits and related financial commitments; return is back-end loaded and can be decremented under contract remedies | Customers are paying for supply assurance, validating scarcity |
	| Product scope | DRAM including HBM where needed, plus NAND | Reinforces memory hierarchy bundling rather than single-product sourcing |
	| New product premiums | Premiums contemplated for LPDDR6, DDR6, new HBM generations, and higher-performance / higher-capacity products | Protects Micron from rising bit costs in high-complexity products |

	- The most important structural claim: SCA floor prices are designed to produce margins well above historical peak-cycle margins. If credible, this directly challenges the old assumption that memory upcycles necessarily mean later margin collapse.
	- Management emphasized that the cash deposit is not prepaid revenue. It is a customer commitment and remedy pool; it is returned on a predefined schedule weighted toward the second half of the agreement term unless reduced under contract terms.

- ## Supply-Demand Regime
	- Management no longer wants to give long-range demand CAGR forecasts because, in its framing, demand is not the limiting factor. The practical ceiling is industry supply growth.
	- Micron said it does not have line of sight to when industry supply catches up with demand, even though supply should gradually improve in 2028.
	- The supply bottleneck is structural:
		- Node transitions generate less bit growth than they used to.
		- HBM consumes more wafers per bit as trade ratios rise by generation.
		- Greenfield capacity is required for incremental wafer growth.
		- Greenfield projects are constrained by construction lead times, skilled labor, permitting, and power infrastructure.
		- NAND cleanroom supply is also constrained because some industry cleanroom is being redirected from NAND to DRAM.
	- Management described the shortage as broad-based, but not evenly allocated. Critical markets such as automotive, defense, aerospace, industrial, and medical cannot simply absorb large supply shortfalls, so Micron tries to minimize disruption there even while aggregate demand is far above aggregate supply.

- ## HBM
	- HBM demand for HBM3E, HBM4, and future HBM4E is above Micron's ability to supply not only in 2027 but also into 2028.
	- Customers are trying to commit to more HBM volume than Micron can support. In SCA negotiations, Micron spends time explaining why the customer cannot get all requested supply.
	- HBM4 12-high ramp is tracking twice as fast as HBM3E 12-high, and management expects mature yields significantly faster than HBM3E.
	- HBM4E is still ahead of qualification, but customer demand discussions already extend into that generation.
	- Micron reaffirmed the strategic goal of having HBM share consistent with its DRAM share over time, rather than maximizing HBM at the expense of all other DRAM categories.
	- **Read-through**: HBM is no longer merely a high-margin product pocket. It is becoming a multi-year allocation product where customer demand is visible before qualification and where lack of supply is the binding constraint.

- ## Non-HBM DRAM, LPDRAM, And SOCAMM
	- Management explicitly pushed back on the idea that only HBM matters. It wants to support HBM, non-HBM DRAM, and NAND across all segments.
	- Agentic AI is increasing CPU server demand and CPU memory demand. Micron sees customers planning both DDR5-based servers and LPDRAM-based servers.
	- When LPDRAM is used in data centers, it will use the SOCAMM form factor. Micron claims it pioneered LPDRAM in data centers and was sole-sourced for the longest time in that category.
	- SOCAMM's value proposition is lower power, higher performance, and smaller memory footprint versus conventional server memory form factors.
	- The main technical hurdle is RAS: LPDRAM was not originally designed for data centers, so reliability, availability, and serviceability complications need to be solved. Micron frames this as a differentiation opportunity.
	- The deck also links agentic AI to a broader data-center architecture shift: accelerator racks are joined by CPU racks for control-plane / program execution and storage racks for expanding context stores.
	- **Read-through**: Micron's non-HBM DRAM story is increasingly tied to agentic CPU scaling, not only traditional server replacement or PC / mobile cycles.

- ## NAND And Enterprise SSDs
	- Management described enterprise SSD momentum as exceptionally strong and said Micron has been gaining record data-center SSD share over time.
	- The deck ties NAND demand to AI context-memory storage and HDD displacement, expanding the addressable market for SSDs.
	- Product differentiation points called out:
		- QLC leadership, including data-center SSDs.
		- First-to-market Gen6 drives, now in volume.
		- Leadership in very high-capacity `245TB` drives.
	- NAND is constrained, but management repeatedly framed DRAM as the higher-urgency customer concern.
	- **Read-through**: NAND is becoming the lower-cost storage tier in the AI memory hierarchy. It does not have HBM's same bottleneck status, but AI context storage and HDD displacement are enough to pull NAND into the same strategic supply discussion.

- ## Technology Roadmap And Cost Structure
	- Micron's 1-gamma DRAM and G9 NAND nodes are ramping well and are expected to become the highest-volume nodes in company history.
	- Next-generation DRAM and NAND nodes are on track to begin volume production in the second half of calendar 2027.
	- Micron signed a multi-year EUV supply agreement with ASML to support increased EUV adoption at 1-delta and future DRAM nodes.
	- Management expects blended DRAM cost per bit to rise from current levels, not fall, because higher-performance memory and greenfield expansion both raise costs.
	- Explicit cost-up drivers:
		- Higher HBM trade ratios.
		- LPDRAM trade-ratio pressure.
		- LPDDR5 to LPDDR6 and DDR5 to DDR6 transitions.
		- Newer HBM generations.
		- Greenfield fabs ramping before they reach efficient scale.
	- Startup costs begin to matter more from fiscal Q4 into the first half of fiscal 2027, but management argued the incremental bits are far more valuable than the startup-cost drag.
	- **Read-through**: This is not a classic memory cycle where better nodes automatically lower cost per bit enough to flood supply. The product mix and fab geography are biasing cost per bit upward.

- ## Capacity Expansion
	- Micron is accelerating supply but emphasized that greenfield capacity mainly starts contributing meaningful bits in calendar 2028.
	- ID1 in Idaho is on track for first wafer output in mid-calendar 2027; ID2 is on track for late calendar 2028.
	- Tongluo in Taiwan should support meaningful shipments from the existing fab in mid-calendar 2027, about a quarter earlier than prior expectations. Micron has also started construction on a second cleanroom at the site that will support EUV equipment.
	- Singapore will become another advanced-packaging center of excellence and should contribute meaningfully to HBM packaging capacity beginning in the first half of calendar 2027.
	- More than half of the fiscal 2027 capex increase is expected to be construction rather than immediately productive equipment, which matters because construction dollars do not produce near-term bits.
	- **Read-through**: Even aggressive capex does not solve the 2026-2027 shortage because the spend is front-loaded into construction, not immediately into bit output.

- ## End-Market Demand Signals
	- **Data center**: AI is driving demand across GPU, ASIC, and CPU architectures; all share the same dependency on memory subsystem performance and capacity.
	- **Agentic AI**: CPU racks and storage racks become part of the AI infrastructure buildout, not afterthoughts. This supports both DRAM and SSD demand.
	- **PC / mobile**: Micron expects high-end devices and on-device AI to sustain memory demand despite unit weakness; edge platforms such as OpenClaw and NemoClaw were cited as examples of AI orchestration between cloud and edge.
	- **Automotive**: L2+ and above vehicles have more than `5x` the memory and storage content of an average vehicle; that vehicle mix is expected to move from above `20%` this year to above `40%` by 2030.
	- **Robotics**: Humanoid robots carry `10x` the memory of an average L2+ vehicle. Micron expects this to become a substantial multi-decade demand cycle starting later this decade.

- ## China Competition
	- Micron acknowledged CXMT and YMTC have improved capabilities and share, but said the overwhelming majority of their output remains sold inside China.
	- Management has not seen much competition from them outside China.
	- Micron's response is not to compete in the easiest commodity pockets. It is focusing on the highest-performing and hardest-to-build products:
		- HBM.
		- High-capacity DIMMs.
		- LPDRAM leadership in data centers, mobile, and client.
		- QLC, Gen6 SSDs, and `245TB` SSDs.
	- Micron also emphasized its patent portfolio and willingness to defend IP.
	- **Read-through**: Management is implicitly saying China supply matters most in lower-complexity domestic markets, while Micron is trying to move the portfolio into products where qualification, IP, customer co-development, and roadmap execution matter more than wafer count alone.

- ## Capital Return And Balance-Sheet Framing
	- Management intends to increase capital return after the second anniversary of the CHIPS agreement signature in December 2026.
	- The main capital-return mechanism will be repurchases, while dividends are expected to grow over time.
	- Micron still plans to hold enough cash to invest through all seasons of the business.
	- **Read-through**: management is signaling confidence that the SCA model and structural supply shortage make the cash-flow profile durable enough to support both greenfield investment and buybacks.

- ## What Changed This Quarter
	- SCA structure became concrete: non-cancelable, take-or-pay, multi-year, with price floors / ceilings and deposits.
	- HBM TAM timing moved forward: crossing `\$100B` is now framed as a 2027 event rather than a 2028 event.
	- Supply-demand language hardened: management has no clear projection for when supply meets demand.
	- Non-HBM DRAM became more explicitly tied to agentic AI and CPU server growth.
	- NAND was pulled further into the AI hierarchy via context storage, enterprise SSDs, and HDD displacement.
	- Micron explicitly warned that DRAM cost per bit may rise because of HBM trade ratios, LPDRAM, DDR6 / LPDDR6 transitions, and greenfield fabs.

- ## Watch Items
	- Whether additional SCAs push contracted revenue toward the "half or more" target without sacrificing too much upside.
	- Whether SCA price floors actually hold through the next weaker demand patch.
	- HBM4E qualification timing and whether Micron can maintain HBM share aligned with DRAM share.
	- LPDRAM / SOCAMM adoption in data-center CPU servers and whether RAS complications slow deployment.
	- Whether NAND remains disciplined or whether YMTC / other suppliers break production discipline.
	- Whether construction-heavy capex converts into bits fast enough by 2028 without creating later oversupply.

- ## Caveats
	- This note intentionally excludes superficial quarterly financial recaps and guidance details; the included numbers are only those that affect the strategic interpretation.
	- The SCA model is new for Micron at this scale. Legal enforceability, customer behavior in a downturn, and renegotiation risk still need to be monitored.
	- Management tone is extremely bullish because the company is in a severe shortage environment. The key test is whether pricing discipline and SCA floors survive once supply improves.
