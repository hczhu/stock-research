- ## Micron FQ4 2026 (calendar Q3 2026) earnings
	- **Sources** (user-provided PDFs, all dated September 30, 2026):
		- Press release, "Micron Technology, Inc. Reports Record Fiscal Fourth-Quarter and Full-Year 2026 Results"
		- Earnings-call prepared remarks
		- FQ4-26 earnings deck
		- Earnings-call transcript, including Q&A, plus an "At a glance" consensus summary (user-provided text)
	- **Quarter covered**: fiscal Q4 2026, ended **September 3, 2026**, roughly calendar Q3 2026. The previous quarter is in [[MU-2026-Q2]] (FQ3-26).
	- **Thesis**:
		- **A record quarter that beat guidance**:
			- Revenue was **\$54.23B, up 31% QoQ and 379% YoY**, beating the roughly \$50B guide.
			- Non-GAAP gross margin was **87.0%**, and non-GAAP EPS was **\$33.42**.
			- Fiscal 2026 revenue was **\$133.2B (+256%)**, with DRAM above \$100B.
		- **The growth came from conventional DRAM and NAND, not HBM**:
			- The **Core Data Center unit (CDBU) grew 56% QoQ** and became Micron's largest business unit.
			- The HBM-heavy **Cloud Memory unit (CMBU) grew 18%**.
			- Management said CY27 HBM contracts carry "significant price increases year over year, **narrowing the gross margin gap with conventional DRAM**." That confirms HBM currently earns less than conventional DRAM.
		- **Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs)**: these are multi-year take-or-pay contracts.
			- They rose to **26 agreements**, covering more than **35% of revenue through 2030**.
			- Remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue, **RPO**) are about **\$150B**.
			- Customer financial commitments total **\$32B**, mostly cash deposits.
		- **Outlook**:
			- FQ1-27 revenue guide is **\$61.5B ± \$1.5B**.
			- Management expects revenue to grow every quarter of fiscal 2027.
			- Supply is "much tighter" in 2027–28 than in 2026, and management has "no line of sight" to balance.
			- Capex rises sharply, mostly for cleanroom construction.

- ## HBM
	- **Growth**: HBM revenue in FQ4 "grew faster than total company revenue" (+31%) as shipments ramped to more customers. Micron did not disclose a dollar figure.
	- **2027 contracts**: Micron has completed agreements for "the vast majority of our calendar 2027 HBM bit supply with **significant price increases year over year, narrowing the gross margin gap with conventional DRAM**."
	- **HBM4** is ramping well.
	- **NVHBM**: Micron is working with Nvidia on "the industry's first custom-HBM4E implementation, **NVHBM**," for Nvidia's next-generation GPUs and **NVLink Fusion** platforms. For base-die context, see [[2026-11-20-tsmc-custom-hbm-base-die-strategic-leverage]] and [[2026-09-04-hbm-base-die-becomes-a-foundry-business]].
	- **Bit growth**: industry HBM bit shipments are expected to grow faster than conventional DRAM through calendar 2028.
	- **Packaging**: cleanroom work at Singapore's HBM advanced-packaging facility is ahead of plan, with initial output in early calendar 2027.
- ## NAND and data-center SSDs
	- **Data-center SSD revenue was nearly \$10B** in FQ4. That is more than 10× the year-ago quarter and **over two-thirds of NAND revenue** (memo calculation: about 70% of \$14.1B).
	- **Drivers**: AI context-memory storage for **KV-cache offload** and replacing hard drives (HDDs) are expanding the SSD market. The 7600 (PCIe Gen5) and 9650 (PCIe Gen6) SSDs are shipping to leading customers for KV-cache use. The 6600 ION had a strong volume ramp.
	- **Share**: Micron is on track for a fifth straight year of record data-center SSD share in calendar 2026.
- ## Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs)
	- **What they are**: multi-year **take-or-pay** agreements. Micron says SCAs "accelerate the transformation of our business." **Every SCA has take-or-pay contracted volumes.**
		- **For Micron**: they "sharpen our long-term supply planning and enhance the durability and predictability of our strong financial performance."
		- **For customers**: they provide "supply assurance and deepen technology roadmap collaboration." That helps customers "invest more confidently in their business."
	- ### Scale and terms
		- | Item | FQ4-26 disclosure |
		  |---|---|
		  | Signed SCAs | **26** |
		  | Share of revenue through 2030 | **Over 35%** (Micron's estimate) |
		  | Pricing | **Three-quarters** of expected SCA revenue has a defined pricing framework, a **majority of it in bands with floor and ceiling prices**. The remaining quarter is negotiated periodically at market prices |
		  | Duration | Customers want supply assured beyond 2030. Some new SCAs **extend into 2031**, and **two agreements received one-year extensions to 2031** |
		  | New negotiations | Any new SCA that involves pricing is "negotiated with **higher pricing** based on prevailing market conditions and outlook" |
		  | Customer financial commitments | **\$32B** across the 26 SCAs and extensions, "the vast majority of which are cash deposits" |
		  | Margins at floor prices | "Even at floor prices, we expect margins **meaningfully above any prior cycle peak margins**" (reiterated from the FQ3 call) |
	- ### Cash deposits and accounting
		- | Item | Value |
		  |---|---|
		  | Deposits received in FQ4 | **\$12.3B** |
		  | Deposits received in fiscal 2026 (cash flow statement, financing) | \$12,747M |
		  | Deposits on the balance sheet at quarter end | **\$12.7B** |
		  | Noncurrent customer contract liabilities | \$568M (FQ3) → **\$12,895M** (FQ4) |
		- **Treatment**: deposits are reported as **financing cash flow**, so they **don't count toward free cash flow**. They are **unrestricted cash**. They are returned toward the second half of each agreement's term, provided the customer meets minimum purchases. The FQ3 call described them as a remedy pool, not prepaid revenue.
		- *Memo's reading*:
			- Customers have committed \$32B, "vast majority" cash, but only \$12.7B was on the balance sheet. So much of the committed cash has probably not been received yet.
			- The deposits are also part of the \$73.5B cash pile and the \$68.3B net cash, so not all of that cash is Micron's to keep.
	- ### How SCAs changed since the FQ3 call
		- | Item | FQ3-26 call (June 2026) | FQ4-26 call (September 2026) |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Count | **16 customers** | **26 SCAs**. This counts agreements, which may not be 26 distinct customers |
		  | Long-run coverage target | Half or more of revenue | "Still around **50%** of our revenue through the 2030 timeframe" |
		  | Coverage | About 20% of DRAM volume and about one-third of NAND volume over the term; target of half or more of revenue over time | **Over 35% of revenue through 2030** |
		  | Tenor | Mostly five years, through calendar 2030 | Into **2031** |
		  | Disclosed backlog | — | **RPO about \$150B** |
		  | Cash on the balance sheet | Deposits being collected (noncurrent customer contract liabilities \$568M) | \$12.7B, with **\$32B** of total commitments |
		- Source for the FQ3 column: [[MU-2026-Q2]].
		- *Memo's reading*: coverage rose from about 20% of DRAM volume to over 35% of total revenue in one quarter, toward management's goal of half or more. The FQ3 measure was volume and the FQ4 measure is revenue, so the two are not strictly comparable.
	- ### What the Q&A added on SCAs
		- **2027 output is mostly committed**: "**More than 75% of our output is already committed for 2027**." This counts **SCA and non-SCA customers together**, the latter through early purchase orders. Mehrotra added that "a majority of discussions with our customers today are already around 2028."
		- **Customers want more**: "SCA customers are asking for more supply. Non-SCA customers, we are getting POs."
		- **The target is unchanged at about 50% of revenue**:
			- Asked whether coverage could reach 60–70%, Mehrotra said the goal is "still around **50% of our revenue through the 2030 timeframe**," and "that could be less too, depending upon the revenue of all the rest of the business."
			- Some large customers are deliberately kept on **annual** contracts.
			- Micron wants to keep flexibility for new customers and end markets.
		- **Mehrotra's framing**: SCAs give visibility for long-lead investments, which he called "a **fundamental change to the nature of this industry**" compared with past volatility.
		- *Memo's reading*:
			- The 75% figure is a 2027 volume commitment across all customers.
			- The over-35% figure is SCA revenue through 2030.
			- The about-50% figure is the long-run SCA ceiling.
			- They are three different measures. None implies SCAs will cover most of revenue.
- ## Remaining performance obligations (RPO)
	- **Management's statement** (FQ4-26 earnings call): "To date we have signed 26 SCAs in total, and our remaining performance obligations, or RPO, is approximately \$150 billion. All SCAs have take-or-pay contracted volumes, and RPO reflects the contract value for only SCAs that have a determined pricing framework, which can be either a fixed price or subject to a pricing floor and ceiling. RPO is based on committed volumes and minimum pricing and is inherently conservative. As mentioned in our last earnings call, even at floor prices, we expect margins meaningfully above any prior cycle peak margins. We expect revenue to well exceed the associated RPO over the terms."
	- | Item | Disclosure |
	  |---|---|
	  | RPO | **About \$150B** |
	  | Contracts behind it | **26 SCAs** signed to date, all with **take-or-pay contracted volumes** |
	  | What's included | Only SCAs with a **determined pricing framework**: a fixed price, or a band with a **floor and ceiling** |
	  | How it's valued | **Committed volumes × minimum prices**. Micron calls it "**inherently conservative**" |
	  | Revenue vs. RPO | Micron expects revenue to "**well exceed**" the associated RPO over the agreements' terms |
	  | Margins at floor prices | "**Meaningfully above any prior cycle peak margins**," repeated from the FQ3 call |
	- *Memo calculations*:
		- \$150B is about **1.1× fiscal 2026 revenue** (\$133.2B), or about 2.4 quarters at the FQ1-27 guide.
		- RPO covers only the three-quarters of SCA revenue with defined pricing, at floor prices. Expected SCA revenue is therefore at least about **\$200B** (\$150B ÷ 0.75), and more at current market prices.
		- Most SCAs run through calendar 2030, with some into 2031. If RPO were spread evenly over about four to five years, it would be about **\$30–38B a year** at floor prices. Micron didn't disclose the timing.
	- **Related**: the 26 SCAs also carry **\$32B** of customer financial commitments, mostly cash deposits. See "Cash deposits and accounting" in the SCA section.
- ## Market outlook
	- | Item | Calendar 2026 | Calendar 2027–28 |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Industry DRAM bit shipments | Up mid-20s% | Up about low-20s% a year; **supply-constrained** |
	  | Micron DRAM supply | About in line with the industry | |
	  | Industry NAND bit shipments | Up low-20s%, slightly above the prior view | Up about mid-20s% a year; **supply-constrained** |
	  | Micron NAND supply | Below industry growth | |
	  | Server units | Up high-teens % | Up high-teens % (2027) |
	- **Supply**: conditions will be "**much tighter**" in 2027 and 2028 than in 2026. Even with planned cleanroom additions and "new upside requests from customers," Micron has "**no line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance**."
	- **Server content**: strong server unit growth comes with "a modestly lower rate of content growth than prior expectations, amid tight memory supply." Memo's reading: the shortage is limiting how much memory goes into each server.
	- **PC and mobile**: industry revenue still grows in 2026 on premium devices, despite possible double-digit unit declines in both markets.
	- **Physical AI**: L4+ autonomous vehicles carry more than 200GB of memory and multiple TB of storage, over 10× L2+/L3 vehicles. Humanoid robots are expected to need similar amounts.

		- Revenue: a record year, with **sequential revenue growth every quarter**.

		- Capex:
			- **First half about \$25B**, and higher in the second half.

- ## Q&A highlights
	- ### Capital return (Arcuri, UBS; Arya, BofA)
		- **Free cash flow**: FQ1 free cash flow will be "**significantly higher than the \$33 billion**" in FQ4, even with more R&D and capex. Murphy called the free-cash-flow strength "more durable."
		- **Target cash**: Micron expects to be around its **target cash level by the end of FQ1**. Excess cash will be returned "**primarily** through share repurchase," with the pace set by "various factors." Murphy gave no target cash number. Arcuri noted Micron already holds about twice the cash of Apple or Nvidia.
		- **Buyback authorization**: the current authorization is only **\$2.2B**, and Micron "will seek additional authorization in the near term." Larger returns start **December 9, 2026**.
		- **No return number**: Arya estimated cash generation of **\$100B+ from FQ2 to FQ4** and asked why returns shouldn't match it. Murphy only restated the "ability and the intent" to increase returns.
	- ### Capex and capital intensity (Arcuri; Sankar, TD Cowen)
		- **Analysts' estimates**: fiscal 2027 capex of **over \$50B**, which Arcuri put at roughly high-teens % of revenue, and Sankar free cash flow of **over \$100B**. The transcript renders Arcuri's capex figure as "\$5.5 billion," probably a transcription error. Micron did not give a full-year number.
		- **Capital intensity**: Murphy agreed capital intensity is "**low on historic levels**," historically about the mid-30s % of revenue. That is "reflective of the strategic asset that memory and storage has become, and the industry is **structurally reset**." He gave no new long-run range when asked whether 20–25% is the new norm.
		- **Construction-heavy spending**: the shift toward construction capex continues for the "next few years." Murphy tied it to greenfield lead times and the supply-demand gap, not to shortages of tool supply. Fabs get equipment as demand warrants.
	- ### The gross-margin bridge (Muse, Cantor)
		- **About \$1B of extra cost in FQ1**: mostly fiscal 2026 incentive pay absorbed into inventory in FQ4, plus startup and other costs. Incentive pay is "the big driver."
		- **About \$1B of ongoing extra cost through fiscal 2027**, on top of normal volume and depreciation costs, from incentive pay, higher startup costs and other items. The **fiscal 2027 incentive pay** shows up in FQ1 opex and starts hitting manufacturing margins in **FQ2**.
		- **Partly temporary**: Murphy said some of these costs "can be considered variable or temporal." FQ1 is the floor, with higher margins afterward on "continued price increases and strong operating performance."
		- *Memo calculation*: \$1B on \$61.5B of revenue is about **160bp**. Without it, FQ1 gross margin would be about **87.9%**, above FQ4's 87.0%.
	- ### HBM pricing (Muse)
		- 2026 HBM prices were negotiated in 2025. For 2027, "a large part of the volume is already sold out," and "**the prices are much higher than 2026 prices**."
		- The increases **start in calendar 2027**, narrowing the margin gap with non-HBM DRAM. Micron gave no percentage.
		- **HBM4E**: products arrive **late next year** (the transcript wording is garbled).
	- ### 2028 pricing and customers cutting memory per server (Arya; Sankar)
		- **Supply headwinds Mehrotra cited**:
			- Cleanrooms take a long time to build.
			- Output ramps only gradually after first wafers.
			- The **HBM trade ratio rises** from HBM3E to HBM4 and HBM4E.
			- Future node transitions give "**less productivity gain per wafer**."
		- **Demand**: some servers get "moderately lower" memory content so customers can **ship more units**. Server units grow high-teens % in both 2026 and 2027, which "sets the stage" for 2028 DRAM demand. Mehrotra said this "bodes well for the industry pricing environment."
		- **Reported cut in HBM at one large customer** (Sankar): Mehrotra did not address the customer. He said HBM demand outgrows industry DRAM in 2027 and 2028, and that such optimizations "have **diminishing return**" and don't remove the "**latent nature of a need for more memory**."
		- *Memo's reading*: Sankar also asked whether HBM wafers moved back to DDR would add much DDR supply. That question was not answered.
		- **Valuation context**: Arya said memory stock valuations are "very depressed," reflecting a view that 2027 may be the pricing and earnings peak.
	- ### NAND investment (Sur, JPMorgan)
		- **Analyst's framing**: Micron is about **No. 4–5 in NAND bit share** but about **No. 2 in data-center and enterprise SSD**. That reflects controller, firmware and customization, plus Micron's role in Nvidia's initiative for direct GPU access to storage. KV-cache offload is pushing more inference work onto flash.
		- **Management's answer**: NAND R&D and NAND's share of capex are both "increasing." The Singapore NAND fab is on track for "late calendar 2028," vs. "second half of calendar 2028" in the prepared remarks. Capex also supports the G9 transition and equipment productivity.
