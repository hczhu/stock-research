- ## Snowflake 2026Q2: AI monetization and faster core-platform adoption
	- **Period convention**: This note uses **2026Q2**, as requested. The source identifies the results as **Snowflake FY2027 Q2**; the call was held on September 2, 2026. The filename is a research-label convention, not a claim that Snowflake reported the calendar quarter ending June 30.
	- **Source**: User-provided FactSet corrected transcript, *Snowflake, Inc. (SNOW), Q2 2027 Earnings Call*, September 2, 2026. Page references below refer to the PDF. Figures and customer outcomes are management statements unless identified as calculations or interpretation.
	- **Thesis**: Snowflake's AI products are becoming both a revenue stream and a way to accelerate migrations, deploy workloads, and reach business users. The economic trade-off is lower gross margin from AI consumption, offset so far by slower hiring and operating leverage; management has not yet quantified the incremental lifetime consumption of AI adopters.
- ## Acceleration is broader than AI-native customer spending
	- Product revenue growth reached **37% YoY**, versus **30% exiting the prior fiscal year's Q4**, with a second consecutive record quarter of sequential dollar growth. The seven-point acceleration matters more than the standalone quarterly revenue figure. [pp. 4, 6]
	- Asked to separate newer AI products from the core, Ramaswamy attributed **approximately half the acceleration** to AI products, with the rest supported by other platform workloads and faster migrations. This is **not** a claim that AI contributes half of revenue; no separate AI revenue figure or precise attribution bridge was disclosed. [pp. 9-10]
	- Management described demand as broad across customer types and regions, with AI-native companies still a **small, unquantified share of revenue**. This contrasts with the IPO-era exposure to cloud-native companies spending ahead of profitability. [pp. 8, 12]
	- | Expansion signal | Reported evidence | Context |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | New customer acquisition | 692 net new customers, up 32% YoY; total 14,554 | Growth is not solely expansion within existing AI adopters. |
	  | Large-enterprise penetration | 14 net new Global 2000 customers; total 829, over 41% of the group | Adds a large-account adoption signal alongside customer counts. |
	  | Existing-customer expansion | Net revenue retention of 126%; gross retention described as relatively flat over recent quarters | Expansion, rather than a newly disclosed improvement in churn, supports growth. |
	  | Million-dollar cohort | 828 customers above $1 million in trailing-12-month spend; 48 net additions in Q2 | Measures scaling within the installed base, not quarterly spending. |
	  | Largest accounts | 65 customers above $10 million in trailing-12-month product revenue | Evidence of enterprise-scale deployments; not a disclosure of customer concentration. |
	  | Data-sharing network | 43% of customers share data with at least one stable edge | Supports the cross-organization data-network proposition; not a share of revenue. |
	- Customer metrics above: pp. 4, 7. RPO rose **30% to $9 billion**, but the portion expected to convert within 12 months grew approximately **42%**; **54%** of total RPO is expected in that window. Management also flagged a shift toward Q4 renewals, making headline bookings timing an imperfect consumption signal. [p. 7]
- ## CoCo and CoWork: adoption is becoming a consumption mechanism
	- | Product | Adoption in the source quarter | What management says it enables |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | Cortex Code / CoCo | More than 9,100 accounts; over 2,000 net additions in the quarter | Coding, migrations, data pipelines, optimization, and agent construction; existing teams can finish projects faster. |
	  | CoWork | 5,800 accounts; nearly 11% QoQ growth | Conversational interaction with enterprise data and workflows, extending Snowflake to business users. |
	- Source: p. 5. These are **accounts**, not paid seats, unique companies, daily active users, or proof of equal usage intensity. The transcript uses the name CoWork; the earlier Q1 note used Snowflake Intelligence, but this call does not explicitly explain the naming transition.
	- CoCo adopters also consume more of the core data platform, according to the CFO. Management describes two linked effects: direct consumption of AI products and additional data-platform work generated or accelerated by those products. [pp. 4, 9]
	- Snowflake tracks new-logo cohorts against **80% of purchased consumption**, describing the fraction reaching that threshold after a set interval, such as three months. Newer cohorts have improved visibly, but management provided **no actual cohort percentages or time-series values**. This is a useful activation signal, not a disclosed numerical uplift. [p. 13]
	- When directly asked whether AI merely pulls projects forward, management pointed to broader use cases and deeper adoption but **declined to disclose exact consumption-uplift numbers**. Incremental lifetime value remains a hypothesis supported by anecdotes rather than a quantified cohort comparison. [p. 14]
	- Management also encourages optimization: CoCo's cost-management skill is among its **top 10 skills**, and users can identify slow queries and idle warehouses. **Interpretation**: Snowflake is accepting savings on existing workloads to earn trust and stimulate new workloads; near-term consumption growth should not be confused with an absence of optimization pressure. [p. 8]

- ## Gross-margin dilution and operating leverage can coexist
	- **AI mix changes the cost structure**: fast-growing AI workloads carry lower contribution margins today, driving the **FY2027 non-GAAP product gross-margin outlook to 74%**. This is a full-year outlook, not the reported Q2 gross margin. [pp. 7, 11]
	- At the same time, **Q2 non-GAAP operating margin reached 15%, up over 400 basis points YoY**, and full-year operating-margin guidance increased from **13.5% to 14.5%**. Management's explanation is strong revenue growth and slower personnel-cost expansion offsetting higher cloud costs. [p. 7]
	- Year-to-date employee additions were **334, including 173 from Observe**, versus **935** a year earlier. Excluding the disclosed acquired employees leaves **161 additions** (calculated); this is not a fully acquisition-adjusted comparison because the prior-year acquisition contribution was not supplied. The figures matter as evidence for operating leverage, not as an organizational-size datapoint. [p. 7]
	- The CFO explicitly prioritized **product quality, adoption, and revenue before optimizing product margins**, while retaining the commitment to overall operating leverage. Management reiterated a **GAAP profitability target in FY2028 Q4**; the target is not equivalent to current GAAP profitability. [pp. 6, 11]
	- **Interpretation**: stronger AI revenue need not improve gross margin initially. The investment case depends on enough additional revenue and organizational efficiency to outweigh inference expense; the call does not disclose product-level unit economics to establish that outcome independently.
- ## Model neutrality is a cost lever and a customer-commitment proposition
	- **Cortex AI Gateway** routes tasks across models using customer policies and performance data, with cost and governance controls. Many harness requests use an automatic-selection mode, giving Snowflake discretion to match models to tasks. [pp. 5, 10]
	- Management sees customers seeking alternatives after committing heavily to a single model provider. A Snowflake commitment is positioned as preserving model choice, extending the company's multi-cloud neutrality argument to AI. [pp. 10-11]
	- Snowflake runs inference for supported open models itself and described those economics as different from purchased frontier-model access. That creates potential for margin optimization, but **no realized margin differential, routing mix, or savings percentage was disclosed**. [p. 10]
	- Customers are showing early interest in **post-training open models**, which could add a training workload alongside inference. Arctic development remains focused on narrower tasks such as document processing, embeddings, and AI functions, **not competing to train a general frontier model**. [pp. 10, 15]
	- Management cited frontier-model price reductions and additional model tiers as offsets to rising usage. **Interpretation**: the potential advantage is orchestrating models and enterprise context efficiently, not winning the frontier-training spending race. [p. 11]
- ## Owning the workflow, not merely supplying the database
	- **Cortex Sense** captures business definitions and institutional knowledge and supplies context when agents answer questions. This extends the product proposition from storing data to helping agents interpret it. [p. 5]
	- **Natoma integration** enables actions such as sending emails, summarizing Slack conversations, and opening Jira tickets from CoWork or CoCo. The transcript contains an audio gap after those examples; no additional action is inferred here. [p. 5]
	- Ramaswamy explicitly said **owning the user experience is critical**. CoCo and CoWork are intended to preserve Snowflake's direct relationship with users even as frontier labs and application vendors compete for the interface. Interoperability remains necessary because Snowflake is only one part of the customer's software estate. [p. 17]
	- Hybrid tables and Postgres were highlighted as foundations for **operational applications and systems of record**, not only analytics. Management expects agentic applications both with and without traditional UIs. This is product direction, not a disclosed revenue contribution from these workloads. [pp. 17-18]
	- **Interpretation**: Snowflake is simultaneously defending centralized enterprise data and moving upward into applications. As software creation becomes cheaper, the proposed differentiation shifts toward data semantics, permissions, context, and the execution environment.
- ## Sales motion: business buyers, adoption stages, and outcome delivery
	- CFOs, CROs, CMOs, and CEOs increasingly participate in purchase decisions, broadening the buyer set beyond traditional data teams. Internal demonstrations give Snowflake a concrete way to sell workflow transformation. [pp. 8-9]
	- CoCo adoption is managed as a pipeline from legal acceptance to daily usage, supported by in-product guidance and hands-on labs. Account availability alone therefore should not be equated with mature deployment. [p. 15]
	- Use cases deployed rose **89% YoY** and use cases won per account executive rose **43% YoY**. These indicate deployment throughput and sales productivity, not contract value or revenue per representative. [p. 6]
	- The [prior-quarter note](SNOW-2026-Q1.md) recorded corresponding growth of **114%** and **86%**. **Comparison**: both growth rates moderated even as product-revenue growth accelerated; different bases, implementation timing, and consumption per project mean they should not be treated as interchangeable revenue forecasts.
	- The frontier-engineering program combines industry expertise with the data platform and agent tools. Sanofi was named as a public engagement; in certain engagements management said Snowflake is **paid only when it delivers outcomes**. This is not a statement that the entire platform has switched to outcome-based pricing. [p. 16]
	- Management argues customers can extend delivered projects using their own data teams rather than returning to a proprietary implementation provider. This is Snowflake's competitive positioning, not independently verified superiority over an identified competitor. [pp. 16-17]
