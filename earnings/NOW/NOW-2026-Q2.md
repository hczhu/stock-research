- **Source**: ServiceNow Q2 2026 earnings call transcript
- **Thesis**: Agentic AI is becoming a measurable expansion engine rather than a defensive feature, while AI Control Tower, Veza, and Armis are turning ServiceNow's CMDB and workflow base into a broader cybersecurity control plane.

- ## AI Monetization Is Ahead of Plan
	- AI ACV surpassed **\$1B**, keeping ServiceNow on track to exceed its **\$1.5B year-end 2026 target**.
	- AI net new ACV grew more than **40% quarter-over-quarter**.
	- Management said AI is already tracking ahead of the target for **30% of company ACV by 2030**.
	- New AI-native SKUs are broadening adoption beyond existing AI customers:
		- Deal volume among first-time agentic AI buyers grew more than **45% year-over-year**.
		- The share of renewing customers purchasing agentic AI for the first time doubled both quarter-over-quarter and year-over-year.
		- Customers with agentic AI in production increased **9x in nine months**.
	- Monetization remains strong despite debate about AI pricing pressure:
		- Pro Plus SKU upgrades continue to generate pricing uplifts above **30%**.
		- New AI-native SKU upgrades are producing **20%-30%** uplifts, in line with the Financial Analyst Day framework.
		- Deals containing at least five ServiceNow AI products grew **5.5x year-over-year**, driving a tripling of AI deals above $1M.
	- The adoption pattern is expanding from generative search into resolution planning, incident management, triage, and full case execution, increasing cross-sell across departments.

- ## Autonomous ITSM Has Reached Production
	- More than **40 customers** are already using ServiceNow's Level 1 ITSM AI specialists.
	- Early deployments autonomously close roughly **80%-85% of service requests** without human interaction.
	- Management cited tasks that previously took humans two days being resolved in about **20 minutes**.
	- The product performs the entire task rather than merely suggesting an answer, allowing ServiceNow to monetize labor replacement and a larger volume of completed actions.
	- A City of Raleigh deployment demonstrates that customers can introduce autonomous support without an internal AI engineering team, then expand one ticket category at a time.
	- ServiceNow has deployed Level 1 support and more than **20 other AI specialists** internally and with customers.
	- The next AI-native product is a conversational service desk with no tickets and AI-coded automation:
		- Several customers are in beta, with general availability expected soon.
		- A new product-led motion will target the broader "Fortune 500,000," extending beyond ServiceNow's traditional large-enterprise sales model.
		- Management described this as the first of several net-new AI-native products coming to market.

- ## AI Control Tower Is Becoming the Governance Layer
	- More than **500 customers** were live on AI Control Tower within its first six months.
	- The product extends ServiceNow's CMDB model from hardware and software assets to agents, models, identities, devices, costs, and policies across heterogeneous environments.
	- Its differentiation is the ability to discover third-party and homegrown agents, govern their lifecycle and access, observe activity, and stop rogue agents through a kill switch.
	- ServiceNow AI Agent Fabric lets third-party agents—including Claude, Copilot, and homegrown systems—invoke governed enterprise actions without requiring a ServiceNow user interface.
	- Management framed the platform as model- and cloud-neutral: whichever chip, model lab, or token-price regime wins, enterprises still need a governed system of action.
	- The scale of existing context is the strategic asset:
		- Management cited roughly **100B workflows** and nearly **8T transactions** in flight.
		- The proprietary workflow context is used to improve customer outcomes but is not exposed to competitors or model providers.
	- Enterprise examples include NTT Data governing internal AI globally, Tech Mahindra managing assets and workflows through a five-year transformation, and Maybank building secure autonomous regional operations.

- ## Cybersecurity Is Becoming a Second Core Platform
	- Security and risk is already a **$1B-plus** business; management characterized it as the eighth-largest enterprise cybersecurity business and the fastest-growing among the top ten.
	- Security and risk appeared in **16 of the top 20 deals** and produced **24 deals above $1M** in the quarter.
	- Armis and Veza are pulling through the core rather than operating as isolated acquisitions:
		- Armis adds real-time discovery and continuous vulnerability detection across devices and cyber-physical assets; it tracks **7B devices** today.
		- Veza adds access mapping across human, machine, and AI identities.
		- AI Control Tower unifies governance and action across these assets and identities.
	- The combined architecture spans pre-breach vulnerability and exposure management, runtime security, post-breach incident response, identity and access, risk, and compliance.
	- ServiceNow is not attempting to replace every security vendor; it intends to remain an integration and action layer for partners such as Palo Alto Networks and CrowdStrike while building its own platform around areas adjacent to CMDB and SecOps.
	- Management expects cybersecurity to become so large that it could eventually overshadow the workflow automation narrative, an unusually aggressive statement about the company's future mix.

- ## Core Platform and Adjacencies Are Strengthening
	- ITOM was present in **18 of the top 20 deals**, including 14 deals above $1M; attach rates to ITSM continued to rise year-over-year.
	- Workflow Data Fabric was in **17 of the top 20 deals**, while RaptorDB Pro deal volume grew **80% year-over-year**.
	- Eighteen of the top 20 deals included at least **eight products**, evidence that AI and security are reinforcing platform consolidation.
	- CRM reached approximately **$2B in ACV**; net new ACV accelerated both year-over-year and sequentially.
		- Sales CRM average deal size doubled year-over-year.
		- A partner completed a full replacement of a major incumbent CRM deployment in two months.
		- One telecom infrastructure customer expects to triple quote volume without adding headcount and reduce repricing from weeks to hours.
		- A large airline is running **5M annual customer-service voice calls** through ServiceNow voice AI agents in production.
	- EmployeeWorks deal volume grew more than **150% quarter-over-quarter**, and management said the conversational employee entry point is pulling through broader HR and employee-experience demand.
	- Fifty percent of net new business is already non-seat-based, reducing direct exposure to seat compression while preserving seat pricing where customers value predictability.

- ## Pricing, Margins, and Go-to-Market
	- ServiceNow favors hybrid license-and-usage pricing over a narrowly defined per-outcome metric, arguing it combines budget predictability with consumption upside.
	- Management prices the complete solution rather than passing through individual token costs, preserving room to route tasks among frontier, open-weight, proprietary, and domain-specific models.
	- AI consumption and faster-than-planned use of hyperscaler infrastructure are creating near-term gross-margin pressure; 2026 subscription gross-margin guidance is **81%**.
	- Management expects hyperscaler unit costs and model costs to improve over time, while solution-based pricing should retain the economic benefit.
	- Operating leverage remains a separate priority: Q2 operating margin was **29.5%**, three points above guidance, and the full-year target remains **31.5%**.
	- Despite adding quota-carrying sales capacity and employees through Moveworks, Veza, and Armis, management committed to begin 2027 with the same total headcount ServiceNow had before those acquisitions.
	- This implies substantial integration-driven headcount reduction or productivity gains while continuing to hire in revenue-producing roles.

- ## Guidance Context
	- Subscription revenue grew **23% in constant currency**, while cRPO grew **21.5%**, beating guidance by 150 and 200 basis points, respectively.
	- RPO reached approximately **$29B**, with longer average contract duration supporting visibility.
	- Roughly half of the Q2 revenue beat came from federal on-prem revenue pulled forward from Q3; management passed the net-new ACV outperformance into full-year guidance but retained prudence for the second half.
	- The modest **$15M midpoint increase** to full-year subscription revenue guidance therefore understates the strength of bookings but also embeds execution risk following a large quarter.
	- The long-term framework remains **$32B of revenue in 2030** with Rule of 60 performance or better.

- ## Management Signals and Predictions
	- AI adoption has reached an inflection point: production customers, first-time buyers, SKU uplift, and multi-product deals are all accelerating simultaneously.
	- Autonomous Level 1 ITSM is likely to increase consumption faster than copilots because each completed workflow burns more assists and replaces measurable labor cost.
	- AI Control Tower can become the control plane across competing models and agents if ServiceNow preserves neutrality while using CMDB, identity, workflow, and transaction context as its moat.
	- Cybersecurity should rise materially as a share of growth as Armis and Veza expand ServiceNow from post-breach workflow into pre-breach discovery, identity, exposure, and runtime control.
	- CRM and EmployeeWorks show that the same workflow-plus-context architecture can expand beyond IT; the strongest evidence is larger deal sizes, incumbent replacement, and production voice AI rather than product announcements alone.
	- Near-term gross-margin dilution from AI and hyperscalers is a real cost of adoption, but model routing, falling inference costs, and solution-level pricing provide a credible path to recover economics.
