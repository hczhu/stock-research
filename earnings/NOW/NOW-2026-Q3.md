- **Source**: ServiceNow Q3 2026 earnings release (Oct 28, 2026; user-provided PDF) and earnings call transcript (Bill McDermott, Gina Mastantuono, Amit Zavery). Growth rates are constant currency unless marked. Prior-quarter comparisons come from [[NOW-2026-Q2]].
- **Related memos**: [[2026-11-09-saas-turf-wars-incumbents-invading-adjacent-markets]] (ServiceNow vs Salesforce in CRM, Atlassian vs ServiceNow in ITSM), [[2026-10-27-servicenow-repvue-sales-sentiment-and-retention]].
- **Theme**: **Q3 beat guidance on every metric, but the stock fell after hours on the Q4 guide, especially cRPO growth of 18.5%, down from 21%.** AI is accelerating: AI net new ACV grew 65%, and the year-end AI ACV target was raised a second time, to \$1.7B. Management is also shifting the business toward consumption pricing and paying for some customer deployments itself. Below the headline, the quarter shows the costs of that strategy: subscription gross margin is down 3 points YoY, professional services is now a loss, and interest on acquisition debt has wiped out net interest income. Non-GAAP EPS grew only 5%.

	  | Non-GAAP operating margin | 32% | 31.5% (unchanged; +50bps YoY) |

	  | Free cash flow margin | — | **36%** (raised from 35%, on lower capex) |
	  | Diluted shares | 1.05B | 1.04B |
	- **The full-year raise passed through only half of the Q3 beat**, plus FX. The other half went into delivery services for large AI deals (below). Those services cut subscription revenue growth by ~25bps in Q3 and ~50bps in Q4. Excluding them, Q4 subscription growth would be about 22% cc.
	- **Why cRPO decelerates by 2.5 points** (Mastantuono's bridge):
		- **Early renewals**: about half of Q3's 100bps beat came from renewals pulled forward from Q4, which now count against Q4.
		- **Public sector on-prem mix**: ~25bps headwind. On-prem deals count toward revenue but not cRPO.
		- **Termination-for-convenience provisions** in government contracts: ~50bps of YoY subscription backlog growth is excluded from cRPO in Q4 but will still become revenue.
		- **Delivery services**: a "modest" RPO headwind.
		- **Read**: the quantified items total ~75bps, and the early-renewal payback probably adds about another 50bps. That still leaves roughly 1 point of the slowdown unexplained, which is why investors focused on it. Management's answer is that cRPO "does not necessarily capture all of the momentum," and that the AI momentum will show up in 2027 revenue.
	- **Implied Q4 free cash flow**: a 36% full-year margin requires about \$2.7B in Q4, against \$3.1B in the first nine months (my estimate, assuming about \$16.2B of full-year total revenue). Q4 is seasonally the strongest collections quarter.
- ## AI monetization
	- **AI ACV target raised to \$1.7B+ by year-end**, from \$1B at the start of the year and \$1.5B at Financial Analyst Day in May. McDermott has "challenged the team" to beat it. ServiceNow says it is on track, or ahead, for AI to be 30% of ACV by 2030.
	- | AI metric | Q3 2026 | Q2 2026 |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | AI net new ACV growth | **+65% YoY** | >40% QoQ |
	  | \$1M+ AI deals | **4.5× YoY** | Tripled |
	  | Deals with 5+ AI products | **8.5× YoY** | 5.5× |
	  | Agentic AI consumption in production | 13× YoY (year to date) | 9× growth in production customers over nine months |
	  | Control Tower net new ACV | **>6× YoY** | 500+ customers live |
	  | EmployeeWorks | **\$100M ACV** in under three quarters | Deal volume +150% QoQ |
	- **AI growth by workflow**: ITSM AI deal volume rose nearly 80%, HR service delivery AI nearly 2×, ITOM AI >7× and portfolio management >6.5×. Net new ACV: security and risk AI >4.5×, App Engine AI >4×, asset management AI ~3×, and **CRM AI tripled both QoQ and YoY**.
	- **Action Fabric**: assists from external agents grew **500% month over month**. When ChatGPT, Gemini Enterprise or other agents call ServiceNow through MCP or A2A, the work runs inside ServiceNow and consumes assists. Zavery: "we get full value for it from the monetization perspective." This is how ServiceNow plans to earn money as an action layer even when the user interface belongs to someone else.
	- **Pricing**: the hybrid model keeps seat subscriptions for budget predictability and adds consumption on top. McDermott said consumption can raise revenue per user by "a factor of 5x." Mastantuono said customers are "not asking at all for outcome pricing."
- ## Paying for customer deployments
	- **What changed**: for select large AI deals, ServiceNow now includes delivery expertise (forward-deployed engineers) in the contract. Under the accounting rules, part of the contract value is carved out of subscription revenue and booked as professional services. On renewal, that value returns to subscription revenue.
	- **Why**: customers given deployment support adopted AI **2× faster in the first five weeks**, with "multiples higher" assist consumption. Time to value is 80% faster year to date. One European customer went live in 12 weeks and is on track for ~1.5M assists a year.
	- **Cost shows in the P&L**: non-GAAP professional services gross margin was **−20%**, against +11% a year ago. Management called the program temporary for the first wave of adopters, and Zavery said AI implementation agents do much of the work.
	- **Read**: ServiceNow is giving up some reported subscription revenue now in exchange for consumption later. Whether the 2027 growth acceleration management promised arrives will show if that trade worked.
- ## Margins, capex and the balance sheet
	- **Subscription gross margin is falling**: non-GAAP 80%, against 83% a year ago. GAAP subscription cost of revenue rose 58% YoY while revenue rose 21%. The Q2 call blamed AI consumption and faster use of hyperscaler capacity, and the acquired businesses probably also carry lower margins.
	- **Capex fell to 2.2% of revenue** (6.9% a year ago), and this is what drove the free cash flow margin raise. Management presents this as capital-efficient AI ("not compute constrained"), in contrast with investor concern about AI capex. **Read**: some of the cost has moved from capex into gross margin, because capacity rented from hyperscalers is expensed instead of capitalized.
	- **Operating margin held up because of headcount cuts**: internal AI use generates **>\$1B of annualized value**, double the level at the end of 2025. Total headcount is slightly down even after the Moveworks, Veza and Armis acquisitions. Quota-carrying sales headcount is up double digits, while sales operations is down significantly. Severance was \$87M in Q3 and \$167M year to date.
	- **The acquisitions changed the balance sheet**:
		- \$9.2B spent on business combinations year to date.
		- Goodwill rose to \$10.0B from \$3.6B.
		- Debt rose to **\$7.5B** (\$2.1B short-term, including commercial paper, plus \$5.4B long-term) from \$1.5B.
		- Cash and investments fell to \$7.0B from \$10.1B, taking ServiceNow from ~\$8.6B of net cash to roughly **\$0.6B of net debt**.
		- No buybacks in Q3 (\$2.2B year to date).
	- **Net interest went from +\$109M to −\$12M** (interest income \$69M vs \$115M; interest expense \$81M vs \$6M). This is why non-GAAP operating income grew 17% but non-GAAP net income grew only 5%.

- ## CRM and the low end of IT service
	- **CRM**: McDermott said "customers rip and replace, not just add alongside." The new CRM "has to work across your entire business," not just the sales team.
		- The **Sweep** tuck-in acquisition targets CRM's main barrier, long implementations. McDermott says replacements now take "30 days, 60 days for a serious company of serious scale" instead of years.
		- Logik.ai, acquired a year ago, is now a Forrester Wave leader for configure-price-quote software.
		- New CRM customers include **Jack Henry**, **HPE** (customer support and field service for its Cloud & AI business), **Thrive** (73% first-contact resolution) and the **Bundesliga**, which became the official CRM partner. The Bundesliga consolidated seven fan channels in about a month and resolves 89% of inquiries at first contact.
		- This continues the share gains in [[2026-11-09-saas-turf-wars-incumbents-invading-adjacent-markets]].
	- **Flow**: a new AI-native, self-serve, consumption-billed service desk that runs outside the ServiceNow platform and can go live the same day. It targets the "Fortune 500,000" that aren't ready for formal ITSM. McDermott said standalone AI service desk providers "are no longer required," which is "obviously a problem for the competition." **Read**: this is a direct defense of the low end of ITSM against Atlassian JSM and AI-native service desk startups.
	- **AI Specialists**: more than 100 customers. The Level 1 service desk specialist resolves incidents 85% faster than human agents. The AIOps specialist cut median time to resolution by 40% in its first month. AIOps, HR service delivery and CRM specialists become generally available in Q4.
- ## Control Tower, security and partnerships
	- **"Super Intelligence Control Tower"** is the new name for ServiceNow's governance pitch: discovering and inventorying all AI, controlling agents, and providing a kill switch.
		- **AI Passport** issues every agent an attested record of its owner, permissions, data access and risk limits. When the Control Tower revokes a passport, NVIDIA's enforcement layers (OpenShell, BlueField DPUs) stop honoring it. Okta, Azure AI Foundry and Gemini Enterprise also receive the signal.
		- Cited figures: 94% of enterprises report gaps in AI visibility, and 88% can't tell personal AI accounts from corporate ones.
	- **Autonomous Security**: a suite of six solutions with Armis and Veza feeding the Control Tower ("Shift Zero": from remediation to prevention). McDermott says the average enterprise runs 70+ security tools, and one global tech customer ran about 80. He again claimed security could become "bigger in a few years than all of ServiceNow is today."
	- **Control Tower Alliance**: founding members include Anthropic, AWS, Cisco, NVIDIA, Okta, OpenAI, Stripe, HiddenLayer, Kong, WitnessAI and Zenity.
	- **OpenAI partnership expanded**: a ServiceNow plugin for ChatGPT built on Action Fabric, security products using OpenAI cyber models, Codex in AI Workflow Factory, and voice-driven governance. The Google Gemini Enterprise partnership was also expanded.
	- **Customer examples**:
		- Accenture: every corporate function runs on ServiceNow, and the Control Tower governs ~900 native AI assets.
		- Aramco Digital: workflows across affiliates in more than 50 countries.
		- Nestlé: agentic marketing operations in 60–90 countries.
		- IHG: uses Veza to govern access across 7,000 hotels.
		- Stagwell: consolidated four service desks across 70+ agencies.
		- An Asian telecom: zero-touch IT and HR help desk handling 70K IT and 6K HR requests a year.
- ## Questions the quarter leaves open
	- **Is the cRPO slowdown only timing?** About 1 point of the 2.5-point Q4 slowdown isn't explained by the items management named. Q1 2027 cRPO, after early-renewal and on-prem effects wash out, will be the cleaner reading.
	- **When does consumption show up in revenue?** AI ACV, assists and Action Fabric usage are growing very fast, but subscription growth has slowed from 23% to 20.5% and cRPO from 21.5% to 21%, with 18.5% guided. Management points to 2027 for "true acceleration."
	- **Margin trade-offs**: subscription gross margin fell 3 points, professional services is loss-making, and the operating margin depends on cutting headcount. How far can AI efficiency keep offsetting AI costs?
	- **How much growth is acquired?** Moveworks, Veza, Armis, Logik.ai and Sweep all contribute, but neither source splits organic from inorganic growth.
	- **Debt and buybacks**: with net debt and no Q3 buybacks, share count support is weaker, and interest costs now hold EPS growth well below revenue growth.
	- **Seller morale vs results**: RepVue sentiment has declined ([[2026-10-27-servicenow-repvue-sales-sentiment-and-retention]]), while ServiceNow is raising quota-carrying headcount and cutting sales operations.
