- ## Data Points & Facts
	- **WeChat mini-app commission**: Apple has fought long standoffs with app developers to make sure it collects its fee on digital transactions inside apps. Tencent's WeChat let other companies launch mini-apps inside its chat app, which avoided Apple's commission. In **2025**, Apple and Tencent agreed that Apple takes a **15% cut** of payments inside WeChat mini-apps, ending a multiyear stalemate.
		- **Related**: [[2026-08-19-stratechery-apple-app-store-fee-settlements-att-germany]] argues that legal proceedings are converging on about **15% of app revenue** as the value of App Store presence, and that Apple is losing its payments cut globally.
	- **Apple's active installed base** (estimates, from the doc's chart):
		- | Device category | Estimated active units | Notes |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | iPhone | **About 1.55B** | The anchor: over 60% of Apple's active base. Apple passed 1B active iPhones in early 2021 and has kept adding net new users alongside a large upgrade cycle |
		  | iPad | About 300M | iPads last unusually long, so the active base stays large and sticky even when annual sales fluctuate |
		  | Apple Watch | About 175M | Counts active users, not cumulative units sold. Pairing a Watch with an iPhone drives the Wearables segment |
		  | Mac | About 110M | Lifted by the multi-year move to Apple Silicon (M-series), which drove upgrades and brought new users to the Mac |
		  | Other (Apple TV, HomePod, Vision Pro, etc.) | About 350M+ | Devices that draw on iCloud and Services. Typically excludes AirPods, which don't register as active network devices in the same way |
		- *Memo calculation*: the rows sum to about **2.49B**, consistent with Apple's roughly 2.5B active devices. Asymco puts active iPhones at about 1.46B and paid subscriptions at about 1.5B ([[2026-07-30-asymco-apple-15b-paid-subscriptions]]).

- ## Anecdotes & Opinions
	- **"Apple is buying RAM aggressively and trying to win market share"** (2026-04, from an unnamed source quoted in the doc): Apple is "more aggressive this year than ever before." The source says Apple is buying up all available mobile DRAM "at extremely high prices, even at the cost of operating profit losses," to drive up DRAM prices so that competitors can't get memory and can't build devices. The source expects "tremendous market share gains this year."
	- **How later disclosures compare** (memo's reading):
		- Apple's July 2026 call described memory as a cost Apple was absorbing, not a weapon. Cook called it "a 100-year flood on the memory pricing," and Apple "reluctantly raised prices" on iPad and Mac. iPhone revenue rose 22% and Mac 29% in that quarter ([[AAPL-2026-Q2]]).
		- Stratechery reads Apple's large memory purchase commitments as a loss of its old supply-chain leverage, not a squeeze on rivals ([[2026-11-03-stratechery-apple-memory-purchase-commitments-consumer-memory-market]]). Horace Dediu makes a similar argument ([[2026-07-04-horace-dediu-memory-shock-apple-supply-chain]]).
		- Rivals did cut handset build plans because of memory shortages and prices ([[2026-11-12-qualcomm-handset-softness-memory-apple-and-pricing]]). That fits the doc's short-term narrative, whatever Apple's intent.
