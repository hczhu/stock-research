- ## Main Narrative
	- **The doc's two sizing questions**:
		- Is the market big enough for **10×** current revenue?
		- Can ServiceNow at least **4×** its revenue in 10 years?
	- ### SAP's revenue is ServiceNow's TAM
		- Bill McDermott came to ServiceNow after two decades of enterprise software leadership at **SAP**, including a long run as CEO.
		- | Feature | SAP (the "digital core") | ServiceNow (the "action layer") |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Primary goal | Executing core business transactions (accounting, supply chain) | Automating workflows across departments (IT, HR, facilities) |
		  | Data focus | **System of record**: high data integrity for audits and financial "truth" | **System of action**: tracking the progress and state of tasks and requests |
		  | User experience | Traditionally complex; optimized for power users in finance and operations | Modern and intuitive; optimized for general employees and self-service |
		  | Core product | S/4HANA (ERP) | Now Platform (workflow) |
	- ### Vibe check (the owner's view)
		- "The current CEO is a typical salesperson. In this paradigm shift for SaaS businesses, it desires a **product-centered CEO**, more preferably, a **founder CEO**."
		- Northwise sees McDermott's enterprise selling as a strength for selling AI governance to the C-suite. It also flags the same limit: ServiceNow "is not an AI-native company," and "the narrative gets ahead of product capability" is a recurring risk.

- ## Earnings: 2026Q1
	- **Customers**:
		- **16 transactions** over \$5M in net new annual contract value (ACV).
		- **630 customers** above \$5M ACV, up 22% YoY.
		- **Manufacturing push**: ServiceNow is expanding in manufacturing through workflow automation.
			- It released **any.site**, built with **Bosch Rexroth**, a Bosch Group subsidiary that makes drive and control technology for industrial machinery ("muscles" and "brains").
			- any.site is an industry knowledge network for AI-powered maintenance and service. It is manufacturer-agnostic, covers a machine's whole lifecycle, and leaves customers in control of their data.
	- **Acquisitions**:
		- **Armis, \$7.75B**, ServiceNow's largest-ever acquisition.
			- Armis gives continuous, real-time visibility, management and security across every connected cyber asset through non-invasive discovery.
			- It **tracks nearly 7 billion devices in real time**, including operational technology (OT), IoT, medical devices, physical AI, code and cloud.
			- The doc calls ServiceNow's advantage here "architectural."
		- **Veza, \$1B**: an identity-security company focused on "access intelligence," answering "Who can take what action on what data?"
	- **Sentiment**:
		- **An analyst's view**: the print was disappointing. The FY26 constant-currency subscription guide, excluding M&A, was cut by **25bps**.
			- Management blamed delayed **Middle East on-premise orders** hit by the war.
			- The analyst sees it as reflecting continued **deceleration of the organic core**, and says ServiceNow "is starting to resemble CRM from several years ago": relying more on M&A to offset slowing core growth, which reduces transparency and pressures cash flow and margins.
		- **Another analyst**: the results "highlight a slight worsening in enterprise IT spending, with deals taking longer to close, likely denting 2Q organic subscription growth."
		- **Management**: "2026 margins are temporarily pressured by acquisition integration. They expect AI and leverage to expand and reaccelerate margins next year."

- ## The Platform of Platforms
	- **The design**: ServiceNow is built as an enterprise "platform of platforms," able to connect to almost any modern SaaS app or legacy on-premise system.
	- **IntegrationHub** drives this, using prebuilt connectors called **Spokes** plus standard REST and SOAP APIs to run workflows across environments.
	- | Connected system | Example integration |
	  |---|---|
	  | **HR** (Workday, SAP SuccessFactors) | Onboarding and offboarding: a new hire added in Workday triggers ServiceNow workflows that provision a laptop, create software accounts and set up payroll |
	  | **ERP** (SAP, Oracle) | Procurement, hardware asset management and financial reconciliation: a server request checks budget in SAP and generates a purchase order automatically |
	  | **CRM** (Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics) | Customer service: an issue logged in Salesforce opens an incident ticket in ServiceNow for IT or engineering, keeping support and IT in sync |
	- ### ServiceNow vs. Jira and Jira Service Management (JSM)
		- **Short answer**: Jira plus JSM is **not** a platform of platforms in the same architectural sense, though Atlassian competes hard for the same enterprise contracts.
		- | Dimension | ServiceNow ("the consolidator") | Jira/JSM ("the accelerator") |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Architecture | Single unified data model (Now Platform) and a centralized **CMDB**. Consolidates HR, finance, IT and customer service workflows into one heavily governed system of record | **Federated** data model. Connects development, IT operations and business teams, assuming specialist teams keep their own tools; integrates the collaboration layer rather than absorbing systems of record |
		  | Integrations | IntegrationHub and enterprise Spokes | Native links to Jira Software and Confluence (an incident links to the bug ticket and the post-mortem doc), the open **Atlassian Marketplace**, and **Forge** for custom serverless apps |
		  | AI | Autonomous, cross-platform agents. With read and write access to HR and ERP data, they can run multi-system tasks such as new-hire procurement, licensing and payroll in the background | "Assistive AI" (Atlassian Intelligence): summarizing tickets, drafting replies and surfacing context from code and Confluence |
		  | Best fit | Strict ITIL compliance, rigid governance, consolidating HR, finance and IT under one roof | Developer velocity, bridging IT ops and engineering, and deployment in weeks rather than months |
		- **Related**: [[Atlassian-TEAM-thesis]], [[2026-11-09-saas-turf-wars-incumbents-invading-adjacent-markets]], [[2026-11-10-servicenow-workflows-what-they-are-and-achieve]], [[2026-11-10-servicenow-itsm-reddit-practitioner-experiences]].

- ## The Northwise Thesis (free excerpt)
	- **The question**: ServiceNow has fallen with the rest of SaaS on fears that AI compresses seat pricing, replaces routine automation and adds orchestration layers controlled by hyperscalers or model providers. Northwise asks whether ServiceNow is an AI **casualty or beneficiary**, and argues the fear may be misapplied to it.
	- **What ServiceNow is**: a **workflow layer**, not generic SaaS. It routes, approves, escalates and documents work across IT, HR, security, customer service and finance.
		- The right forecasting variables are **workflow density** (customers × modules × operational volume), module attach rates, retention in the largest accounts and contract duration, not seats.
		- If workflow density rises, revenue rises even if seat counts fall.
	- **Business model**:
		- Q1 2026 subscription revenue grew **22%**. The FY2026 subscription guide is **\$15.74–15.78B**, and total revenue about **\$16.2B**.
		- RPO is about **\$27.7B, up 25%**.
		- Growth comes mostly from expansion in existing accounts, with net retention "well above 100%."
		- Multi-year contracts delay any AI displacement until renewal.
	- **The AI fork**: does AI reduce the volume and complexity of work routed through enterprise systems, or increase it?
		- **Bear**: AI moves *where workflow logic lives*, into hyperscaler platforms, model-provider orchestration or internal agent frameworks. ServiceNow keeps contracted revenue but loses its role as the place new automation gets built.
		- **Bull**: AI **increases** complexity. More agents mean more events, exceptions, permissions and audit trails, so governance demand rises.
	- **The "AI control tower" argument**:
		- Regulated enterprises cannot let agents "run wild." They need to know who authorized an action, what data was accessed, which permissions were used, what was affected downstream, how to roll it back, and whether the audit trail holds up.
		- ServiceNow already runs approvals, routing, incident and change management, identity-linked actions and audit trails. It needs to **extend** these to agents, not reinvent them.
		- **Three steps**: agents trigger workflows through ServiceNow's approval logic; ServiceNow keeps audit and identity context for agent actions; and it exposes orchestration primitives that other AI systems can call.
		- ServiceNow "is not competing to build the best AI models. It is positioning itself as the system enterprises may rely on to make AI behave like enterprise software instead of experimental code."
	- **Stickiness**: lock-in is **organizational, not technical**. Workflows, training, compliance procedures and audit reports all reference ServiceNow ticket numbers, so replacing it is "closer to a reorganization." AI-driven workflow growth could deepen this, unless the new workflows build up outside ServiceNow.
	- **Leadership**:
		- **Strengths**: McDermott's C-suite, multi-domain enterprise selling fits a pitch to CFOs and chief risk officers about risk reduction and audit defensibility. GTM execution and large-deal capability are among the best in enterprise software.
		- **Limits**: ServiceNow is not AI-native, and investors should look for real **adoption** of AI features, not just announcements.
	- **SBC and dilution**:
		- | Year | SBC | Revenue | SBC % of revenue |
		  |---|---|---|---|
		  | 2023 | \$1.60B | \$8.97B | 17.9% |
		  | 2024 | \$1.746B | \$10.98B | 15.9% |
		  | 2025 | \$1.955B | \$13.28B | 14.7% |
		  | 2026 (guide) | About \$2.4B | About \$16.2B | About 15% |
		- ServiceNow repurchased **20.1M shares in Q1 2026**, including an accelerated buyback. The FY2026 diluted share guide is about **1.04B**.
		- Northwise treats buybacks as **dilution control** until the share count falls materially and persistently.
	- **2030 scenario model** (base: 2026 revenue of \$16.2B; net income about 81% of operating income):
		- | Scenario | Revenue CAGR 2026–30 | 2030 revenue | Operating margin | 2030 EPS | Story |
		  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
		  | Bear | 10–12% | \$23.7–25.5B | 29–30% | \$5.46–6.07 | Slower-growth enterprise utility. AI commoditizes some automation, net retention drifts lower, share count drifts up |
		  | Base | 14–16% | \$27.4–29.3B | 32–33% | \$7.09–7.84 | Platform expansion continues. AI helpful, not transformational. Buybacks mostly offset SBC |
		  | Bull | 18–20% | \$31.4–33.6B | 34–35% | \$8.83–9.72 | AI raises workflow intensity. ServiceNow becomes the coordination system between humans, software and agents |
		  | Ultra bull | 25–28% | \$39.6–43.5B | 36–38% | \$12.01–13.94 | ServiceNow becomes the control tower for agentic operations, a new category of machine-driven demand |
		- *Memo calculation*: revenue × margin × 81% ÷ 1.04B shares gives EPS about **2–8% below** Northwise's stated ranges (for example, \$6.83 vs. \$7.09 at the base low end). The ranges probably include items, such as interest income, that the excerpt doesn't state.
		- **Dilution sensitivity** of 2030 EPS:
			- | Net share change by 2030 | Share count | Bear EPS | Base EPS | Bull EPS | Ultra bull EPS |
			  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
			  | −5% | 0.99B | \$5.95 | \$7.55 | \$9.19 | \$12.61 |
			  | 0% | 1.04B | \$5.65 | \$7.17 | \$8.73 | \$11.98 |
			  | +5% | 1.09B | \$5.38 | \$6.83 | \$8.31 | \$11.41 |
			  | +10% | 1.14B | \$5.14 | \$6.52 | \$7.93 | \$10.89 |
			  | +15% | 1.20B | \$4.91 | \$6.24 | \$7.59 | \$10.42 |
		- At **+10%** net dilution, EPS is about **9%** lower than with a flat share count, and at **+15%** about **13%** lower.
	- **Risk matrix**:
		- | Risk | Northwise's description |
		  |---|---|
		  | **Disintermediation** (central bear case) | AI-native tools, hyperscaler orchestration or internal agent frameworks become where new automation is built |
		  | **Fragmentation** | AI workflows split into domain-specific tools, with no single control layer |
		  | **Pricing compression** | Customers expect AI automation as a standard feature, not a premium upgrade |
		  | **Over-automation resistance** | Security, legal and compliance caution keeps agent authority narrow |
		  | **Execution** | ServiceNow ends up as the legacy layer *underneath* a newer AI control system |
		  | **Macro and budgets** | Software budgets tighten. Multi-year contracts delay but don't prevent the hit |
		  | **SBC and dilution** | Per-share value erodes even if the business grows |
		- **The thesis breaks if**: AI workflows move outside ServiceNow; net retention compresses more than expected; SBC stays structurally high; ServiceNow fails to become the agentic control layer; or margins fail to expand.
	- **How later results compare** (memo's reading): the Q2 and Q3 2026 calls point toward Northwise's bull path on AI adoption.
		- **AI Control Tower** net new ACV grew more than 6×, with 500+ customers live.
		- **AI net new ACV** grew 65%.
		- Armis and Veza extend the CMDB into a security control plane.
		- **But** cRPO growth is guided to slow to **18.5%** in Q4 ([[NOW-2026-Q2]], [[NOW-2026-Q3]]).
