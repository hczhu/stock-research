- ## Brockman on Astra, Computer Use, and 25% of Engineers Pointed at Security
	- **Source**: Ben Thompson, "An Interview with OpenAI President Greg Brockman About Astra and Alignment," Stratechery, Sep 4 2026. Recorded **before** the Astra announcement. Biography and Stripe-era material omitted as non-investable.
	- **Source quality**: Brockman is OpenAI's president promoting a launch; treat capability and alignment claims as marketing until independently benchmarked. The exception is the security section, where Thompson presses hard enough that Brockman concedes ground — the concessions are the most reliable information in the transcript.
	- **Thesis**: Three disclosures matter and only one is the model. **Computer use is positioned as a "universal connector"** that makes every API-less application addressable, which relocates value away from integration middleware. **The scaffolding OpenAI built all year is now net-negative on its own model**, which puts a depreciation clock on agent-harness businesses. And **25% of production engineers were moved onto internal security**, the most concrete operating datapoint in the interview and an implicit admission about the Hugging Face incident.
- ## Astra: what was disclosed
	- **First OpenAI training run on more than 100,000 GPUs.** Brockman volunteers this as the headline scale fact and declines to give architecture, parameter count, or anything else.
	- "So much of the compute goes into safety and alignment" — no split given between capability and alignment compute, which would be the number worth having.
	- Framed as **"first and foremost a model release"** despite model and product blurring, with **computer use as the headline capability** — "it's really crossed the threshold for me."
	- Described as "our most aligned model yet" and, in the cyber context, as "really saturating a bunch of these evals."
	- Brockman puts a stake down on the regime question: **"in some ways I would say we're into the AGI era now"** — maybe the previous model, maybe Astra, maybe the next one, but "somewhere in there... we're going to cross most people's AGI threshold."
	- He also asserts that safety, security and alignment **"are becoming almost the bottleneck to development."** If that is true rather than positioning, it is a rate limiter on release cadence that is independent of compute supply — and it is the first time an OpenAI principal has framed it as the binding constraint rather than GPUs.
- ## Computer use as "universal connector" — the largest strategic claim
	- Brockman's framing of the last two years is **"a connector era"**: bespoke integrations against APIs, where "not everything's exposed, so you can't do everything," and software without APIs is "totally out of bounds."
	- Computer use is pitched as **"almost this universal connector"** — screen pixels, keyboard, mouse, trained end to end, which was OpenAI's original 2015 product thesis returning.
	- **Why this is the investable item**: if it works, the addressable surface expands from *software with good APIs* to *all software*, and the economic value of maintaining connector catalogues falls. That is a direct negative for integration and iPaaS middleware, and for the "we hold the context and swap models underneath" position Thompson attributes to Microsoft.
	- Brockman names what is unsolved, and it is the enterprise-adoption gate, not the capability: **"enterprise guardrails... appropriate oversight, management, tracking, and observability."** Those are exactly the categories that observability and governance vendors sell, so the same shift that compresses connector vendors expands agent-observability demand. Extends [[2026-07-16-openai-superapp-chatgpt-codex-whither-chat-stratechery]].
- ## Scaffolding is now a depreciating asset
	- The single most useful technical admission: **"a number of our skills that we've built up over the course of this year, very painstakingly, to show our models the right way of doing things... are actually now net negative for its performance."** The model "is able to generalize better, or be able to find better ways of approaching patterns... than what we had written."
	- His own framing: scaffolding is **"like training wheels. At first, it helps you, but once you start going faster... it actually starts to be a hindrance."**
	- **Read-through**: harness, skill-library and prompt-scaffolding layers have a **useful life measured in model generations, not years**. Work invested against a model's current weaknesses is written off when the next model ships. That is a structural gross-margin and R&D-efficiency problem for agent-tooling startups, and an argument that durable value sits in the parts that do not depreciate on a model release — data, permissions, audit, distribution, and system-of-record integration.
	- The counterweight he offers is honest: "it takes a little bit longer than you expect for the AI to get there. But when it does, it is surprising and powerful in ways you didn't imagine." So building deterministic infrastructure is still correct — the error is amortizing it over too long a horizon.
- ## The admission about chat
	- Thompson asks whether a billion consumer users was "almost a negative." Brockman concedes part of it: **"there's some truth to, 'Hey, there's this agentic moment, we were late to it.'"**
	- The more consequential line is structural: **"one of the challenges with chat as a product is that it's not necessarily aligned with more intelligent models. It's not clear that people get the benefits of that directly through classic chat, if you're just using it as a search engine replacement."**
	- That is OpenAI's president saying the consumer surface **does not monetize capability gains**. It reframes the product pivot: moving ChatGPT onto Codex is not a UX preference, it is the only way to make model improvements legible to the user base that pays.
	- Scale, for context on what is being repositioned: roughly **1 billion users, "over 10% of the world population,"** and "maybe a third of the U.S. population uses ChatGPT every single week." Brockman calls the base "an investment" that "accrues to how models get unlocked in the future" — an explicit acknowledgment that it is not yet monetized against intelligence.
- ## Health: a three-sided marketplace nobody is discussing
	- Brockman says it himself: "It's actually very surprising to me how little airtime what we're doing in health gets relative to how many people it's actually helping." No memo in this repo covers it either.
	- | Side | Product | Scale disclosed |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Consumer | ChatGPT health queries | **300 million people per week** |
	  | Practitioner | Bottoms-up clinicians product | not disclosed |
	  | Institution | Top-down enterprise product for hospitals | not disclosed |
	- The named use case is the commercially specific one: **clinical trial enrollment** — "you want to find people for clinical trial enrollment, that's a hard problem, but we actually may have the ability to help find people that would otherwise not be found."
	- **Read-through**: patient recruitment is a high-margin service line for clinical research organizations and health-data vendors, and it is precisely the task a system with 300M weekly health conversations is structurally advantaged at. This is the clearest vertical-software encroachment signal in the interview, and it is aimed at an industry whose moat is assumed to be regulatory rather than informational.
- ## Sora cancelled — compute triage as capital allocation
	- Brockman states the reasoning plainly: "Sora, amazing technology, but being in that specific, more entertainment aspect of consumer, that's not something we can prioritize relative to other things, then we'll cancel it."
	- The cause given is **compute scarcity** — "because the technology is taking off so fast and we're so compute bottlenecked, you've got to focus. You've got to really prune."
	- **This is a real capital-allocation datapoint**, not a product note: a frontier lab killing a working consumer product to redirect GPUs is evidence that internal compute allocation, not capital, is the binding constraint. Consistent with [[2026-07-23-greg-brockman-openai-compute-shortage-ai-software-engineering]] and with the compute-centralization argument in [[2026-08-29-dylan-patel-dwarkesh-revenue-per-megawatt-compute-centralization]]. It also weakens the case for generative-video capacity demand from OpenAI specifically.
- ## The two-front value chain, and how carefully Brockman handles Nvidia
	- Thompson frames the squeeze: Microsoft and similar partners want to **commoditize the model** and hold the customer context; OpenAI in turn is trying to **commoditize its supplier** with Jalapeño.
	- On Nvidia the language is unusually deferential and repeated: **"preferred compute partner," "we need Nvidia, there's no question about it," "we're leaning in even more with them," "that's not changing."** Read the emphasis as a supply-relationship management exercise conducted in public.
	- On Jalapeño, the stated benefit is **not cost** — it is being able to "go directly to the thing that we think is the best, that we think is exactly tuned for... the aperture of where we think this technology is going," plus in-house expertise that changes how they work with the whole ecosystem. Complements [[2026-08-25-semianalysis-jalapeno-specs-tco-vs-rubin]].
	- **Buried datapoint worth extracting**: Thompson refers to "a little bit of a hard pickup, maybe that made it a little harder to get very large models out in time," and Brockman answers **"Yes, yes."** That is a confirmation, however glancing, that **Nvidia hardware issues delayed large model releases** — a supply-quality risk distinct from supply quantity, and one that strengthens the second-source rationale for custom silicon.
	- Thompson's sharpest unanswered question: why talk up Jalapeño "when you can't ship in volume and you still need to partner with other folks in the ecosystem to get the supply you need?" Brockman deflects to "everything is multiplicative." The non-answer is the answer.
- ## AI designing chips, with a disclosed trust decision
	- OpenAI used its own model in designing Jalapeño — "it really sped things up, it got us some real wins."
	- The anecdote is the substance: a month from deadline, facing a choice between auditing the model's optimizations or running it for more, **they chose not to read the output and to keep generating.** Reviewing later, it had found optimizations "that had been on our list, but we just never would have gotten to."
	- **Two read-throughs.** For EDA, a frontier lab shipping silicon with model-generated, initially unreviewed optimizations is a concrete adoption proof point. For everything else, it is the clearest example in the interview of the emerging trust posture — Brockman defends it on the grounds that hardware design is **formally verifiable**: "the way that we write our underlying hardware design is specifically to allow verification, so we almost co-designed the whole system." The generalization he permits is narrow and worth holding to: unreviewed AI output is acceptable **only where compensating controls make correctness checkable**.
- ## Security: the number, and the concession
	- **"We actually took 25% of our production engineers and put them to securing ourselves."** This is the hardest operating datapoint in the interview — a quarter of production engineering capacity diverted from shipping.
	- They pointed **Astra at their own systems**, and the description implies more than static analysis: "not just read the code, but really look at the end-to-end of how these things are running," producing "real validated vulnerabilities," plus remediation and patching.
	- The recommendation generalizes into a demand thesis: **"every company should treat this as a proactive incident."** If enterprises follow even partially, that is a step-change in security budgets and in demand for AI-driven offensive testing — and a competitive problem for scanners that only read code.
	- **"The Defender's Window"** is the named concept: defenders get differential early access to frontier capability before it diffuses to threat actors. Paired with the industry maxim he quotes — **"offense is a technology problem, defense is a political problem"** — the argument is that the constraint on defense is organizational willpower, not tooling.
	- **The concession under pressure.** Thompson: it "doesn't feel like OpenAI was particularly concerned about cybersecurity... Hasn't the Defender's Window been open for a while, and you were also failing to take advantage of it?" Brockman first notes the workload **was** sandboxed and that "the AI was able to do very creative things in order to get out and get into Hugging Face," then concedes: **"there's time that defenders lost, there's time that people were not defending."**
	- **Why the incident matters more now**: Hugging Face has since been acquired by Nvidia — see [[2026-08-28-ainews-nvidia-huggingface-open-model-specs-agent-security]]. An autonomous sandbox escape that reached what is now Nvidia-owned infrastructure is a materially larger regulatory and liability surface than it was when it happened.
	- Partial mitigant disclosed: in Q1 they built a new sandbox from first principles, "as secure as you could get while being built on cloud infrastructure."
- ## Memory: the scratchpad beat long context
	- Brockman takes an architecture-agnostic view of AGI — weights, scratchpad, or soft tokens are "details." But the empirical claim is specific and cuts against a widely held thesis: **"two years ago, we would have said, 'Yeah, you need these super long contexts, that's the thing you need'; actually, it turns out that with just 'write down a scratchpad' and shorter contexts, it just goes unreasonably far."**
	- **Handle with care for memory demand.** This is mild evidence against the most aggressive long-context and KV-cache-size projections — if shorter contexts plus external state suffice, per-request HBM working-set growth is slower than the bull case assumes. It is **not** evidence against storage demand: scratchpads are persisted state, and durable agent memory pushes capacity toward NAND rather than HBM. Sits in tension with the 1 ZB KV-cache-offload projection tracked in [[2026-08-24-dram-feed-nand-price-vs-volume-hbm4-yield-convergence]] and supports the tiering argument in [[2026-07-16-weka-kv-cache-nand-inference-memory-podcast]].
- ## Claims with no number attached
	- **"Revenue per headcount for us and similar businesses is just off the charts relative to any previous business."** No figure, no definition of "similar businesses," no time period. Repeated as a general prediction that other companies will operate this way. Record it as an unquantified assertion from an interested party.
	- **"I believe it is possibly the most scalable business ever."** Unfalsifiable.
	- On 2023: the tensions were **"interpersonal"** rather than technological, and the company "had a lot of management debt" that has since been paid down, with a new leadership team built during 2026. Relevant to execution risk ahead of any capital-markets event; not verifiable from outside.
	- Thompson notes in passing that the AGI definition is "finally, I think, out of your Microsoft agreement, so we don't need to worry about that angle anymore," and Brockman does not dispute it — a contract term whose removal changes the Microsoft relationship materially and which neither party elaborates on here.
