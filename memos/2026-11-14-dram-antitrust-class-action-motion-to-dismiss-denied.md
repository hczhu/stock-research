- ## DRAM Conspiracy Class Action Survives Motion to Dismiss
	- **Source**: *Garciaguirre v. Samsung Electronics Co.*, No. 5:26-cv-06345-NW (N.D. Cal.), ECF No. 68, "Order Denying Motion to Dismiss," Judge Noël Wise, filed **Nov 14, 2026** (20 pp., user-supplied PDF). The industry facts below are **plaintiffs' allegations**, which the court accepts as true at this stage. They are not findings. The complaint (filed Jun 25, 2026) was first noted in [[2026-07-03-dram-hbm-supercycle-news-roundup-jun-jul-2026]].
	- **Thesis**: The court found it plausible that the DRAM triopoly's post-2022 **supply discipline** was coordinated: the production cuts, the HBM pivot, the DDR4 exit, and no new commodity capacity despite a roughly 700% price rise. The same behavior is the core of the memory **super-cycle bull case**. The case now goes to discovery, which creates a legal overhang on \$MU, SK hynix and Samsung. Memo inference: it also raises the cost of continuing to withhold conventional DRAM capacity and of the customer-vetting practices.
- ## Market Structure
	- | Q3 2025 global DRAM revenue share | Share |
	  | --- | --- |
	  | SK hynix | 33.2% |
	  | Samsung | 32.6% |
	  | Micron | 25.7% |
	  | Three combined | **91.5%** (down from about 96% in the 2016–18 case) |
	  | Small specialty makers and Chinese firms | About 8%. Chinese makers hold **5% or less of global capacity**. |
	- **Shift in leadership**: in 2016 Samsung held about half the market. Today Samsung and SK hynix each hold about a third. The court noted that moves by SK hynix and Micron are therefore no longer simple "follow-the-leader" moves.
	- **Wafer intensity**: HBM uses about **3–4x the wafer area per GB** of DDR5. It sells only to a narrow set of AI-accelerator customers and cannot substitute for commodity DRAM.
- ## Supply-Discipline Timeline (as alleged)
	- | Date | Event |
	  | --- | --- |
	  | Sep 29, 2022 | Micron tells investors its output will be "ramped in line with industry demand growth" and it aims to keep a "stable bit supply share" |
	  | Oct 26, 2022 | SK hynix cuts capex and DRAM production |
	  | Nov 16, 2022 | Micron cuts DRAM production, three weeks after SK hynix |
	  | Q1 2023 | Trough: Samsung's chip division loses \$3.51B and SK hynix loses \$2.61B in operating terms. Micron's net loss for FY23 (to Aug 2023) is \$5.833B. |
	  | Jan 2023 → Apr 7, 2023 | Samsung says there will be "no artificial production cut", then reverses and cuts. It did not cut in the 2008–09 or 2019 downturns. |
	  | 2023 onward | All three shift capacity and capex to HBM. The share of capacity dedicated to HBM rises about **10x**. |
	  | Jan 2024 | Samsung reports accelerated depletion of DRAM inventory |
	  | 2024 | Samsung and SK hynix retire their DDR3 lines. Micron keeps making limited DDR3. |
	  | Apr–Jun 2025 | All three announce they will wind down mainstream DDR4. OEMs panic-buy, and **DDR4 ends up costing more per GB than DDR5**. |
	  | Oct 2025 | Samsung and SK hynix sign preliminary deals to supply OpenAI's Stargate with up to **900k DRAM wafers per month**, about **40% of global DRAM output**, for one customer |
	  | Dec 3, 2025 | Micron announces the shutdown of **Crucial**, its 30-year consumer brand with multibillion-dollar revenue, near the price peak |
	  | Jan 2026 | All three introduce near-identical **customer vetting**, asking buyers about end users, order quantities and whether demand is "real" |
	  | 2023–Jun 2026 | Samsung and Micron delay or halt several DRAM fabs and limit purchases of lithography tools. **None of the three announces a meaningful expansion of commodity capacity**, while small rivals expand from late 2025 |
	- Plaintiffs also allege that Samsung and SK hynix **signed long-term HBM contracts while refusing long-term agreements with major conventional-DRAM customers**. By November 2026 the vendors report LTAs that cover DRAM as well, with SK hynix's DRAM and HBM effectively sold out through 2029 (see [[2026-11-12-nvidia-huang-exit-hbm-vendor-agreements]]).
- ## Price Data Points (as alleged)
	- | Period | Price change |
	  | --- | --- |
	  | Aug 2016 – Aug 2018, the prior case | +165% |
	  | Conventional DRAM, year to Q3 2025 | +171.8% |
	  | DRAM contract price, Q4 2025 | About +50% QoQ |
	  | DRAM contract price, Q1 2026 | **+93%** QoQ |
	  | DRAM contract price, Q2 2026 | +58% QoQ |
	  | Retail DRAM since early Oct 2025 | +500% |
	  | Conventional DRAM over 18 months | **+697%** |
	- **Profitability crossover**: plaintiffs say conventional DRAM became **more profitable than HBM** sometime between Q2 2025 and Q1 2026. The court noted the profitability dispute turns on the metric used (per GB versus per wafer) and on HBM's roughly 3:1 bit penalty, and left it unresolved.
	- Plaintiffs say the defendants were guiding to **output restraint beyond calendar 2026** as of the complaint.
- ## How the Court Scored the Evidence
	- | Plus factor | Ruling |
	  | --- | --- |
	  | HBM pivot together with AI-supply lock-ups, Crucial's closure, and no re-expansion | **Favors plaintiffs**. The pivot alone would not; the hard-to-reverse lock-ups tip it. |
	  | Delayed or halted DRAM fabs | **Favors plaintiffs**. Capacity decisions are not easily reversed, the same reasoning as the petroleum-refinery cases. |
	  | Same-quarter DDR4 exit | **Favors plaintiffs**. DDR3 was treated as a normal phase-out and excluded. |
	  | Simultaneous customer vetting (Jan 2026) | **Strongly favors plaintiffs**: it resembles the monitoring used by the 1998–2002 cartel |
	  | 1998–2002 price-fixing cartel (Samsung fined \$300M, Hynix \$185M, Micron took immunity) | Favors plaintiffs |
	  | Public "supply discipline" statements | Not credited, following the 2022 appeals ruling that ended the earlier DRAM case |
	  | Meetings at industry conferences | Not credited, because no specific exchange of information was alleged |
	  | Oligopoly market structure | Neutral |
	- **Why this differs from the dismissed 2018 case**: the cuts were started by the No. 2 and No. 3 players rather than a dominant leader, the moves were hard to reverse, and none of the three cashed in on a roughly 700% price rise. In the court's words, this is "hawks flying in formation", not merely watching each other.
- ## Case Status
	- Defendants' answers are due **Dec 7, 2026**, and the initial case management conference is set for **Jan 12, 2027**. The case moves toward discovery on the Sherman Act §1 claim and on state-law claims in CA, FL, MN, NY and WI.
	- The 2018 case over the 2016–18 price spike was dismissed with prejudice, and the dismissal was upheld on appeal in 2022. Unlike that case, this one has survived the pleading stage.
