- ## Anthropic S-1: Hypergrowth Converts Compute Into Operating Leverage

	- **Source**: Anthropic, PBC [Form S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm), filed October 26, 2026, the user-provided analysis screenshot and follow-up commentary, and the Reuters marketplace analysis cited below. Financial statement amounts are in billions of dollars and H1 2026 figures are unaudited. The R&D-compute figures, marketplace-fee scenarios, 37% incremental fee comparison, and approximately \$855B commitment total are external estimates, not Anthropic disclosures.
	- **Thesis**: Anthropic's H1 2026 revenue grew far faster than its committed compute base, lifting reported gross margin to 85% and producing its first profitable quarter. Management attributes the efficiency to a four-chip-family fleet and compounding compute multipliers. The cost curve has real software-like leverage, but reported gross margin and revenue overstate the economics because channel fees and most model-development compute sit below gross profit. Applying a 25%–35% marketplace take rate converts \$16.286B of reported H1 revenue into roughly \$13.55B–\$14.33B of economic net revenue; against that leverage sits an estimated commitment stack of approximately \$855B, including at least \$451.6B of identified non-cancelable minimums.
- ## Financial and Compute Build

	- | Period | Revenue | Cost of revenue and implied gross margin | Reported R&D and screenshot-estimated R&D compute | Total compute and compute intensity |
	  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
	  | 2023 | **\$0.041B** | **\$0.026B; 36.6% margin** | **\$0.422B R&D; ~\$0.37B compute** | **\$0.433B; 1,056% of revenue** |
	  | 2024 | **\$0.386B** | **\$0.752B; (94.8)% margin** | **\$2.275B R&D; ~\$1.71B compute** | **\$2.522B; 653% of revenue** |
	  | 2025 | **\$4.585B** | **\$2.773B; 39.5% margin** | **\$6.338B R&D; ~\$4.38B compute** | **\$7.326B; 160% of revenue** |
	  | H1 2025 | **\$1.163B** | **\$1.019B; 12.4% margin** | **\$1.850B R&D; ~\$1.26B compute** | **\$2.334B; 201% of revenue** |
	  | H1 2026 | **\$16.286B** | **\$2.380B; 85.4% margin** | **\$9.741B R&D; ~\$6.25B compute** | **\$8.945B; 55% of revenue** |
	- **The operating leverage is real**: H1 revenue increased approximately **14x**, or **1,300%**, year over year while total compute expense increased **3.8x**, or approximately **283%**. Compute intensity fell from 201% to 55% of revenue and cost of revenue fell from 88% to 15%.
	- **Reported gross margin is not the full AI-lab margin**: Anthropic puts customer-serving compute in cost of revenue, but model development, research, and internal Claude usage in R&D; free-access compute sits in sales and marketing. On the screenshot's estimate, H1 2026 R&D compute consumed about 38% of revenue, leaving roughly **45% of revenue after all compute**, versus the reported 85% gross margin.
	- **The R&D-compute estimate is directional**: Anthropic says it operates committed compute as one shared resource and plans and assesses it on a total basis rather than by workload. The screenshot estimates R&D compute as a residual after customer-serving and assumed free-access compute; the filing does not disclose a precise workload split.
	- **Timing can make margins volatile**: Anthropic generally contracts compute **12–18 months before availability**. The expense is recognized when capacity becomes available and is largely fixed in the near term, so margins depend on whether revenue ramps quickly enough to absorb each new block of capacity.
- ## Channel-Adjusted Margin and the Frontier R&D Race

	- **The cost curve looks like a technology company rather than a reseller**: H1 2026 revenue expanded much faster than customer-serving compute, allowing reported gross margin to reach **85.4%**. Once demand is large enough to absorb committed capacity, incremental revenue can carry high margins even though the product is compute-intensive.
	- **Anthropic reports marketplace sales gross**: Anthropic sets the price, contracts with the customer, and delivers the service, so a customer purchase of \$100 through AWS, Google Cloud, or Azure can appear as the full \$100 of revenue. The cloud provider's fee is recorded in sales, marketing, and partnerships rather than as a reduction of revenue or gross profit. The revenue is real customer spend, but a net-of-distribution view is more comparable for valuation and unit economics.
	- **H1 2026 gross-to-net starting point**: Reported revenue was **\$16.286B** and marketplaces generated **48%**, implying approximately **\$7.82B of marketplace revenue** and **\$8.47B of direct revenue**.
	- | Marketplace fee assumption | Implied channel fees | Economic net revenue | Net revenue versus reported | Revenue-multiple uplift on net revenue |
	  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
	  | 16% historical benchmark | **\$1.25B** | **\$15.04B** | **92.3%** | **8.3%** |
	  | 25% lower current case | **\$1.95B** | **\$14.33B** | **88.0%** | **13.6%** |
	  | 30% working midpoint | **\$2.35B** | **\$13.94B** | **85.6%** | **16.8%** |
	  | 35% upper current case | **\$2.74B** | **\$13.55B** | **83.2%** | **20.2%** |
	- **The historical anchor was about 16%**: A [Reuters analysis summarized by CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/r8sh14ux4) calculated approximately **\$351M of 2025 distribution fees on \$2.16B of marketplace sales**, or roughly 16 cents per marketplace dollar. Applying that rate to H1 2026 produces about \$15.0B of economic net revenue.
	- **The 2026 incremental signal points higher, but is not a clean take rate**: H1 marketplace revenue rose from approximately **\$0.51B** in 2025 (44% of \$1.163B) to **\$7.82B** in 2026 (48% of \$16.286B), an increase of about **\$7.31B**. Anthropic says channel-partner fees increased by **\$2.7B**, making the incremental fee increase equal to roughly **37%** of incremental marketplace revenue. The filing does not establish that every dollar of that expense is a marketplace fee, so 37% is evidence for a higher current cost—not a proven blended rate.
	- **Update (2026-11-19)**: user-provided commentary puts the marketplace fee at **about 35% of marketplace revenue now, up from about 16% in 2025**. That supports the 35% upper case in the table above.
	- **A roughly \$14B working net-revenue figure is reasonable**: The 25%–35% cases produce \$13.55B–\$14.33B, centered near \$14B. At the 30% midpoint, a valuation multiple calculated on economic net revenue is about **17% higher** than the same valuation divided by reported revenue; the uplift reaches approximately **20%** only in the 35% case.
	- **The fuller cost bridge still shows leverage**: Total compute consumed an estimated **55% of H1 2026 reported revenue**. Deducting that burden plus marketplace fees leaves approximately **28%–33% of reported revenue** before personnel, other operating costs, and non-channel sales and marketing under the 25%–35% marketplace-fee range. This remains directional because both the fee rate and compute workload allocation are estimates.
	- **R&D now dominates the compute bill**: The screenshot estimates H1 2026 R&D compute at **about 70% of total compute**, versus roughly **27%** for cost-of-revenue compute and a small residual for free access. Estimated R&D compute rose almost **5x year over year**, from 1.26 to 6.25, while cost of revenue rose about **2.3x**, from 1.019 to 2.380.
	- **The least controllable cost is competitive, not transactional**: Anthropic can improve inference efficiency and utilization, but the required frontier-training budget depends on how aggressively OpenAI, Google, Meta, and other labs advance the state of the art. Current revenue rests partly on Claude remaining a frontier model, so R&D compute may keep compounding faster than inference even as serving margins improve.
	- **“Pacing the frontier” has an economic incentive**: Slower industry-wide escalation would let Anthropic defer some frontier-training spend or redirect capacity toward revenue-generating inference. That interpretation does not prove coordination or predict lower spending; it explains why an otherwise high-margin company could favor limits on the R&D arms race that preserve more of its operating leverage.
- ## Fixed Costs That Behave Like Marginal Costs (commentary, added 2026-11-19)
	- **Source**: user-provided commentary on the S-1 (author not given).
	- **Why the 85% gross margin is so high**:
		- Inference efficiency improved enormously over the last 18 months, across the whole industry.
		- The explosive product-market fit in software development lets Anthropic "more or less name its price" during a severe compute shortage. The author compares this with oil companies, which also post high gross margins in a shortage.
	- **Models depreciate fast**: Anthropic spent about **60% of H1 revenue on R&D**, mostly training the next model. That matches the \$9.741B of R&D on \$16.286B of revenue above. The filing also says **80% of Q3 revenue came from models trained in Q2 and Q3**. The author cites the line that frontier models are "the most expensive and fastest depreciating asset ever created."
	- **Margin stack**:
		- | Level | Margin | What it covers |
		  | --- | --- | --- |
		  | Selling an existing model (gross margin) | **About 85%** | Inference compute only |
		  | Building and selling models | **About 25%** | 85% gross margin minus about 60% of revenue spent on R&D |
		  | Whole company | **About breakeven** | After hyperscaler revenue shares and other operating costs |
		- This is consistent with the H1 2026 GAAP operating margin of about −4% and Q2's 8% shown below.
	- **The cost structure is inverted**: marginal cost (inference) has stayed flat while revenue exploded, but the supposedly fixed cost of R&D has scaled with revenue. In the author's words, "the fixed costs look like marginal costs and vice versa."
- ## Growth and Profitability Inflection

	- Quarterly revenue rose from **\$0.376B in Q1 2025 to \$11.555B in Q2 2026**. Q2 2026 revenue grew **1,368% year over year** and approximately **144% sequentially**.
	- Q2 2026 produced **\$0.974B of GAAP operating income**, an **8% margin**, after a **\$1.667B operating loss** in Q1. Anthropic's adjusted measure, which excludes stock compensation and certain noncash items, reached **\$2.235B**, or **19%**, in Q2.
	- H1 2026 still recorded a **\$0.693B GAAP operating loss**, or roughly **4% of revenue**, but adjusted operating income was **\$1.618B**, or **10%**. The filing therefore shows a sharp quarterly inflection rather than a long record of profitability.
	- The scale driver was broader enterprise usage: customers generating at least **\$100,000 of trailing-12-month revenue** increased from approximately **400 in March 2025 to more than 6,000 in June 2026**, a greater-than-15x expansion.
- ## Cash Flow and Accounting Context

	- Anthropic's **\$80.748B H1 2026 net loss** is not a measure of operating cash burn. It includes a **\$72.388B noncash fair-value expense** on convertible notes, derivative liabilities, and SAFEs, primarily caused by the increase in Anthropic's enterprise value.
	- Operating cash flow was **positive \$3.929B** in H1 2026 versus **negative \$1.513B** a year earlier. Purchases of property and equipment were **\$0.482B**, leaving approximately **\$3.447B** of operating cash flow after those purchases before investment-portfolio activity.
	- Anthropic had raised **\$141.1B** of capital by June 30, 2026 and held **\$112.4B of cash and cash equivalents plus \$19.3B of short-term investments**. That liquidity can fund the near-term build, but it is small relative to the multi-year commitments reconstructed below.
- ## Compute Advantages: Multi-Chip Fleet and Compute Multipliers

	- **Source**: an excerpt of Anthropic management remarks supplied by the user on Nov 17, 2026 (speaker and venue not given), read against the S-1's "Compute Efficiency" (MD&A) and "Our Approach to Compute" (Business) sections. Related: [[2026-11-10-circuit-anthropic-s1-q3-slowdown-compute-constraint]].
	- **Management's framing**: Anthropic focuses on **total effective compute = physical capacity × compute multipliers**. It names two drivers of its compute advantage: a multi-chip fleet that widens physical capacity, and a fast rate of discovering compute multipliers that raises the output of each unit. The S-1 adds that Anthropic has "consistently operated in a compute-constrained environment where customer demand exceeds available compute," which is why getting more out of each chip matters.
	- | Advantage | Management's remarks | S-1 context |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | **1. Multi-chip fleet** | Anthropic trains and serves Claude across "all four major chip families." It was an early, "unconventional bet": many thought running models on different chips "wasn't worth the effort," because each chip needs its own software and inference stack. After years of doing it, "we've gotten incredibly good at it" | The four families are **AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs and AMD GPUs**, run as one fungible pool through a proprietary orchestration layer. Anthropic says this expands available compute, reduces dependence on any single vendor, routes each workload to the best price-performance hardware, and is "difficult to replicate at scale" |
	  | **2. Faster chip onboarding** | Anthropic started using AMD GPUs for the first time this year. A new platform used to take "dozens of engineers months of work." This time **Mythos got Claude running on AMD in three days, "with basically no human intervention," porting or writing almost 100% of the kernels** | Anthropic can "onboard new chip platforms in a fraction of the time this once required." AMD capacity includes **Instinct MI450 Series** GPUs, plus a multi-year engineering collaboration with AMD |
	  | **3. Compute multipliers** | Example: if a model needs 100 units of compute, a **4× multiplier** lets Anthropic train the same model with 25 units, or use all 100 to train a model that would have needed 400. Multipliers "build on each other... They stack," so they are hard to replicate. Management expects discovery to accelerate as it approaches **recursive self-improvement**, with Claude eventually finding multipliers "entirely by itself" | The S-1 defines compute multipliers as "algorithmic and methodological improvements in training and inference that increase the effective output of a given amount of compute." It calls their accelerating pace a direct expression of "closing the loop," and says Claude "has helped write and iterate on compute multipliers that are central to efficient inference" |
	- **Financial evidence the S-1 cites**: revenue grew **1,368%** YoY in Q2 2026 while compute and infrastructure expenses grew **300%**. In the H1 table above, compute intensity fell from 201% to 55% of revenue.
	- **Not quantified**: the remarks and the S-1 give no count or size of Anthropic's compute multipliers, no split of workloads or capacity by chip family, and no AMD capacity figure. The 4× example is illustrative, and recursive self-improvement is management's expectation, not a disclosed result.
- ## Altimeter's Read: Revenue per Gigawatt and Where the Compute Goes

	- **Source**: two Altimeter presentation slides (pages 15 and 17), photographed and supplied by the user on Nov 17, 2026. The event and presenter weren't given. The footnotes are only partly legible. The slide footnote discloses that Altimeter-managed funds hold positions in Anthropic.
	- **Altimeter's "compute multiplier" = ARR per GW of inference capacity**: this is a revenue-productivity measure, not Anthropic's algorithmic definition in the section above. Altimeter puts it at **~3× in 12 months**.
		- | Quarter | ARR | Inference capacity | ARR per GW |
		  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
		  | Q4 2025 | \$9.4B | 0.34 GW | ~\$28B |
		  | Q2 2026 | \$58B | 1.03 GW | ~\$56B |
		  | Q3 2026E | \$72B | 1.08 GW | ~\$67B |
		  | Q4 2026E | \$86B | 1.13 GW | ~\$76B |
		- **Method, per the footnote**: capacity comes from SemiAnalysis's Tokenomics model (Oct 2026), "Total Inference Compute" in MW. SemiAnalysis derives that series by dividing annualized inference revenue by an assumed maximum revenue per inference GW. ARR is a last-28-day run rate; Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 are company figures, and Q3 and Q4 2026 are Altimeter estimates. ARR per GW is ARR divided by inference capacity in the same quarter. The footnote labels it "for illustrative purposes only."
		- Capacity grows only ~10% from Q2 to Q4 2026E (1.03 to 1.13 GW), so nearly all of the projected gain in ARR per GW comes from ARR growth on roughly flat inference capacity.
	- **API vs. subscription revenue per GW** (user-provided, Nov 19, 2026): the S-1 says subscriptions were **20% of H1 2026 revenue** but does not say how much compute they use. SemiAnalysis estimates Claude subscriptions take **about 40% of serving compute**.
		- | Product | Share of revenue | Share of serving compute | Revenue per GW-year |
		  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
		  | API | About 80% | About 60% | **About \$84B** |
		  | Subscriptions | 20% (S-1) | About 40% (SemiAnalysis estimate) | **About \$34B** |
		- **A subscription watt earns about 40% of what an API watt earns**: (20% ÷ 40%) ÷ (80% ÷ 60%) is about 0.38 (memo calculation).
		- **Cross-check**: weighting the two by compute share (60% × \$84B + 40% × \$34B) gives about **\$64B per GW-year** blended (memo calculation). That is close to Altimeter's ~\$67B for Q3 2026E above.
	- **Compute allocation, training vs. inference** (Altimeter, from the S-1/A filed Nov 2, 2026):
		- | Period | Total compute | Training, research, internal use and free tier (R&D + S&M) | Serving paying customers (cost of revenue) |
		  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
		  | FY2023 | \$0.4B | 94% | 6% |
		  | FY2024 | \$2.5B | 70% | 30% |
		  | FY2025 | \$7.3B | 62% | 38% |
		  | 9M 2025 | \$4.4B | 59% | 41% |
		  | **9M 2026** | **\$18.8B** | **74%** | **26%** |
		- **Method**: the paid share is cost of revenue divided by compute and infrastructure expense. Altimeter calls it an upper bound, because cost of revenue also includes some personnel and allocated overhead. The remainder is a residual covering R&D (model development, research, internal usage) and free-tier compute booked in S&M, which the filing doesn't disclose separately.
		- **Trend**: the serving share rose from 6% to 41% through 9M 2025, then fell to 26% in 9M 2026 as total compute grew about 4.3× YoY (\$4.4B to \$18.8B).
		- **Consistency with this memo**: the FY totals match the compute build above (\$0.433B, \$2.522B and \$7.326B). The 9M 2026 split of 74% / 26% is close to the screenshot's H1 2026 estimate of about 70% R&D compute and 27% cost-of-revenue compute.
		- **Independent read (user-provided, Nov 18, 2026)**: "Anthropic's S-1 showed it pointing about three-quarters of its fleet at research, training and the free tier," consistent with Altimeter's 74%.
- ## Compute Commitment Stack

	- **Estimated total**: Approximately **\$855B**, derived by summing disclosed or identified commitment categories. The figure is not locked in stone: categories may overlap, some obligations arise only when capacity or equipment is accepted, and the SpaceXAI component is cancelable.
	- **What the public filing actually says**: Most compute is procured under multi-year commitments signed well before deployment, and some contracts require payment regardless of utilization. The S-1 leaves the aggregate commitment amounts blank, while stating that a portion runs through 2030 and substantially all of the remainder runs from 2031 through 2045. The \$855B figure is therefore a reconstruction around redacted totals, not a reported contractual-obligations subtotal.
	- | Counterparty or category | Estimated commitment | Contract character and timing |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | AWS, Google, and Microsoft cloud spend | **\$269.4B**: Amazon \$110.0B, Google \$114.5B, Microsoft \$44.9B | Non-cancelable minimum cloud spend, paid "regardless of actual usage" |
	  | Broadcom equipment-related leases | **\$161.2B** | Reconstructed amount; non-cancelable and paid as equipment becomes ready. The public S-1 separately identifies \$125.2B of five-year rent excluding interest, with payments beginning at “ready for service” |
	  | Unspecified short-term counterparties | **\$21.0B** | Non-cancelable short-term commitments |
	  | Nscale and Lambda capacity | **\$85.9B** | Becomes committed as capacity is accepted |
	  | SpaceXAI capacity | **Up to \$120.5B** | Cancelable with 90 days' notice beginning in 2027 |
	  | Unallocated remainder | **~\$197.0B** | Catch-all for unidentified commitments and possible estimation differences |
	  | Reconstructed total | **~\$855.0B** | May contain overlap; mixes firm, contingent, and cancelable obligations |
	- **The firm floor is lower than the headline total**: The three explicitly non-cancelable categories sum to **\$451.6B**, or roughly **53%** of the reconstructed total. Adding Nscale and Lambda capacity upon acceptance raises the identified committed or acceptance-triggered stack to **\$537.5B**, about **63%** of the total.
	- **Flexibility is concentrated in one large contract**: The maximum SpaceXAI commitment represents approximately **14%** of the headline total and can be canceled with 90 days' notice starting in 2027. Treating the full amount like an unconditional liability would overstate the fixed burden.
	- **The unknown bucket is material**: Approximately **\$197B**, or **23%** of the reconstructed total, is not mapped to a named commitment. This prevents precise counterparty, duration, and cash-timing analysis and is the main reason to treat \$855B as an order-of-magnitude estimate rather than a single liability figure.
	- **Scale relative to current revenue is enormous**: Q2 2026 revenue annualizes to approximately **\$46.2B**, making the \$855B headline stack about **18.5x** that run rate and the \$451.6B non-cancelable floor about **9.8x**. Those ratios are not debt metrics because the commitments span multiple years and Anthropic is growing rapidly, but they show how much future utilization is already embedded in the strategy.
	- **Economic exposure arrives in stages**: These are not all current balance-sheet liabilities or immediately payable debt. Cloud minimums, equipment availability, capacity acceptance, and cancellation terms determine when commitments become expenses and cash outflows; utilization and revenue growth determine whether they create operating leverage or stranded capacity.

- ## Demand Quality and Distribution Economics

	- Remaining performance obligations were **\$10.8B** at June 30, 2026, with approximately **75%** expected to be recognized over the following 12 months. This provides some visibility but remains small relative to the H1 revenue run rate because much consumption is usage-based or cancellable.
	- No customer represented more than 10% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, but the top five represented approximately **25%**. Many large customers lack long-term contracts or can reduce usage, so rapid growth does not equal fully contracted durability.
	- **Top-five customer detail** (X post by @modestproposal1, user-provided screenshot, Nov 19, 2026):
		- | Customer | H1 2026 spend with Anthropic | Context |
		  | --- | --- | --- |
		  | Largest customer | **\$1.8B** | Equal to **1.5% of that customer's revenue** and **5% of its non-GAAP cash opex** |
		  | Next four combined | **\$3.0B** | |
		  | Top five combined | **\$4.8B** (memo calculation) | About **29% of H1 revenue** of \$16.286B (memo calculation), in line with the ~25% top-five share for the first nine months |
		- **Growth is slowing**: the top five grew their spend **80% QoQ in Q2** but only **20% QoQ in Q3**. Anthropic's total revenue grew about 45% QoQ in Q3 ([[2026-10-17-anthropic-q3-revenue-ipo-valuation-compute-commitments]]), so concentration in the top five fell in Q3 (memo calculation).
		- **Concentration confirmed falling** (user-provided, Nov 19, 2026): **79% of Q3 2026 revenue came from outside the top five customers, up from 64% in Q1 2026**. The top-five share therefore fell from about 36% to about 21% in two quarters (memo calculation).
		- **Implied size of the largest customer** (memo calculation): \$1.8B at 1.5% of revenue implies about **\$120B of H1 revenue**, and at 5% of cash opex about **\$36B of H1 cash opex**. A repo anecdote says Meta is very likely Anthropic's largest customer ([[Meta-META-thesis]]). That is consistent with the implied size, but neither source confirms it.
		- **Penetration even at the top**: the largest customer's spend is 5% of its cash opex. The Fortune 500 figures below use a different base, total expenses, so the two are not directly comparable.
	- CSP marketplaces generated **48% of H1 2026 revenue**, up from **11% in 2023**, **32% in 2024**, and **47% in 2025**. These channels accelerate enterprise distribution but create platform concentration and revenue-sharing costs.
	- Sales, marketing, and partnership expense increased by approximately **\$3.0B** year over year in H1 2026, driven primarily by a **\$2.7B increase in channel-partner fees** rather than a conventional salesforce build. That cost is economically tied to revenue delivery even though it sits below reported gross profit.
	- **Expansion headroom and go-to-market** (user-provided, Nov 19, 2026, including Q3 2026 figures):
		- **Agents drive consumption**: Claude Code shows how fast spending grows once agents take over more of a developer's work. The **top 5% of enterprise Claude Code users spent 20× the average**.
		- **Repeating the playbook beyond coding**: first-party apps and industry-specific offerings let Anthropic extend the same agent-led consumption model across other enterprise functions. The platform also offers tools for customers and partners to build their own agents.
		- **Penetration of large customers is low**:
			- | Customer group | Anthropic revenue as a share of the customer's total expenses, Q3 2026 |
			  | --- | --- |
			  | Fortune 500 customers, average | **Less than 0.1%** |
			  | Top 10 Fortune 500 customers | **Below 1%** |
		- **Distribution**: Anthropic reaches customers through joint go-to-market partnerships with the top three clouds while keeping direct customer relationships. This is the channel behind the 48% of H1 revenue from cloud marketplaces above.
		- **Lower cost per task**: the latest Opus release cut customers' **cost per task by about 40% while preserving Anthropic's margins**. The source says this could unlock more adoption in Q4 2026.
- ## Offering and Control Structure

	- Anthropic applied to list Class A shares on Nasdaq under **ANT**, but the initial filing leaves the share count, price range, proceeds, and therefore IPO valuation blank.
	- A single non-economic Class F share held by the seven-co-founder Founder LLC will carry **50.1% of general voting power**. Separately, the Long-Term Benefit Trust's Class T stock generally elects a majority of the authorized board, leaving public Class A investors with economics but limited governance influence.
