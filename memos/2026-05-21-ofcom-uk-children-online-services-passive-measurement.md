- ## Ofcom: How UK Children Aged 8-14 Actually Use Online Services
	- **Source**: [Ofcom - Children's Passive Online Measurement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/childrens-passive-online-measurement-report.pdf?v=418343), published 21 May 2026.
	- **Study**: Passive monitoring of websites and apps used by a nationally representative, weighted sample of **701 UK online children aged 8-14**. Each child was measured for 28 consecutive days between 9 October 2025 and 7 March 2026.
	- **Thesis**: Children's online attention is highly concentrated but platform leadership differs by metric. Alphabet owns near-universal discovery and the largest absolute attention pool through YouTube; Snapchat has lower reach but extraordinary depth among teens; Roblox dominates measured mobile gaming, especially among younger children; and standalone AI has already crossed majority adoption. The same evidence raises regulatory risk: stated minimum ages have little relationship to actual reach among under-13s.

- ## Executive Takeaways
	- **Online use is already a several-hours-per-day habit**: UK 8-14s averaged **3h36m per day** on measured smartphones, tablets, and computers, rising from **2h53m** at ages 8-9 to **4h19m** at ages 13-14. The excluded-device caveats imply total digital time is higher.
	- **Attention is extremely unequal**: the top quartile averaged **6h42m per day**, versus **51m** for the bottom quartile. Platform averages conceal a small group of very heavy users.
	- **Social/video captures most measured time**: social-media and video-sharing services accounted for **55%** of online time. YouTube and Snapchat alone represented **41%**, or **1h28m per child per day**.
	- **Reach and engagement tell different stories**: Google Search reached 94% but drew only 3 minutes per visitor per day; Snapchat reached 44% but drew 1h27m per visitor per day. A platform can dominate distribution without dominating time, or vice versa.
	- **Age reshuffles the attention leaderboard**: YouTube's share of time fell from **39% at ages 8-9** to **14% at ages 13-14**, while Snapchat rose from **5% to 28%** and TikTok from **3% to 10%**.
	- **Standalone AI is mainstream before high school**: 55% of 8-14s used an AI service during the month; ChatGPT alone reached 33%. AI reach increased sharply with age and exceeded adult ChatGPT reach by ages 13-14.
	- **Age gates do not prevent access**: 97% of 8-12s visited at least one of YouTube excluding Kids, Facebook/Messenger, TikTok, Instagram, or Snapchat, despite those services generally requiring users to be at least 13.
	- **Apps own the experience**: 95% of measured online time occurred in apps. Children averaged 28 apps per month, rising from 22 at ages 8-9 to 34 at ages 13-14 and 41 among 15-17s.

- ## Total Online Time
	- | Cohort | Average daily time | Bottom-quartile time | Top-quartile time |
	  |---|---:|---:|---:|
	  | Ages 8-14 | 3h36m | 51m | 6h42m |
	  | Ages 8-9 | 2h53m | 42m | 5h00m |
	  | Ages 10-12 | 3h34m | 58m | 6h59m |
	  | Ages 13-14 | 4h19m | 52m | 6h56m |
	  | Ages 15-17, separate Ipsos sample | 6h20m | Not reported | Not reported |
	- **Weekend uplift**: 8-14s averaged **3h24m on weekdays** and **4h07m on weekend days**, a 43-minute increase. The 13-14 cohort reached **4h49m per weekend day**.
	- **Daypart**: 47% of 8-14s' time occurred from 15:00-20:59. Late-night use from 23:00-04:59 averaged 18 minutes, rising from 14 minutes at ages 8-9 to 24 minutes at ages 13-14.
	- **Gender**: boys averaged 3h48m daily versus 3h23m for girls on measured devices. The relationship reversed at ages 13-14, when girls averaged 4h27m versus 4h11m for boys; excluded console use likely understates older boys' total time.

	  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
	  | YouTube | 91% | 94% | 95% | 52m/day | 37m/day |
	  | Facebook including Messenger | 71% | 80% | 87% | 8m/day | 4m/day |
	  | TikTok | 43% | 69% | 80% | 25m/day | 34m/day |
	  | Instagram | 30% | 47% | 69% | 13m/day | 21m/day |
	  | Snapchat | 22% | 43% | 67% | 1h27m/day | 1h49m/day |
	  | Reddit | 12% | 25% | 44% | Less than 1m/day | Less than 1m/day |
	  | Pinterest | 11% | 28% | 39% | 4m/day | 5m/day |
	  | X | 9% | 15% | 22% | 7m/day | 12m/day |
	- **[[$SNAP]] has the steepest engagement curve**: 67% of 13-14s used Snapchat and users averaged 1h49m daily. Snapchat accounted for **28% of all measured online time** among 13-14s, twice YouTube's 14%.
	- **YouTube is strongest with younger children**: it represented 39% of total time at ages 8-9, with users averaging 1h15m daily. Its share falls with age, but reach remains at least 91% in every cohort.
	- **TikTok and Instagram are transition-to-teen platforms**: TikTok reach almost doubled from 43% at ages 8-9 to 80% at ages 13-14; Instagram more than doubled from 30% to 69%.
	- **Gender segmentation**: X reached 19% of boys versus 11% of girls, with boys spending 10m/day versus girls' 1m/day. Pinterest showed the reverse: 38% of girls at 6m/day versus 16% of boys at 2m/day.

- ## Heavy Users Drive Disproportionate Value and Risk
	- | Service | Bottom-quartile visitor time | Top-quartile visitor time | Heavy-to-light pattern |
	  |---|---:|---:|---|
	  | Snapchat | 1m/day | 4h12m/day | Extreme habitual use |
	  | YouTube | 1m/day | 2h38m/day | Broad platform with a very deep heavy-user tail |
	  | TikTok | Less than 1m/day | 1h31m/day | Engagement concentrated in heavy users |
	  | Instagram | Less than 1m/day | 47m/day | Similar concentration at lower absolute time |
	  | Facebook including Messenger | Less than 1m/day | 30m/day | High reach does not imply uniformly high engagement |
	  | WhatsApp | 2m/day | 1h28m/day | Communications intensity varies dramatically |
	  | Roblox app | 1m/day | 1h19m/day | Heavy gamers create most time spent |
	- **Illustrative day, not an average**: one heavy-user 12-year-old logged **10h20m** on a smartphone in one day, including 4h49m across 69 Snapchat visits, 2h31m across 31 TikTok visits, and 59m across 16 Instagram visits.
	- **Interpretation**: monetization, network effects, and safety exposure are likely concentrated in the same relatively small cohort. Mean engagement alone overstates the typical child but may understate the commercial value of the heaviest users.

- ## Under-13 Use and Regulatory Exposure
	- **Ninety-seven per cent of ages 8-12** visited at least one of five platforms generally requiring users to be 13+: YouTube excluding Kids, Facebook/Messenger, TikTok, Instagram, and Snapchat.
	- | Service nominally requiring age 13+ | Ages 8-12 monthly reach | Time per visitor per day | Top-quartile visitor time |
	  |---|---:|---:|---:|
	  | YouTube excluding Kids | 90% | 55m | 2h40m/day |
	  | Facebook including Messenger | 76% | 10m | 37m/day |
	  | TikTok | 59% | 20m | 1h13m/day |
	  | Instagram | 40% | 7m | 25m/day |
	  | Snapchat | 35% | 1h10m | 3h24m/day |
	- YouTube Kids, designed specifically for children aged 12 and under, reached only **14% of 8-12s**, versus 90% for the main YouTube service.
	- WhatsApp reached **63% of 8-12s** even though its minimum age was 13 during the study. Parent-managed accounts for under-13s were being introduced as of March 2026.
	- Telegram, with a minimum age of 18, reached **10% of 8-14s**. Nextdoor, also 18+, reached 9%.
	- **Investment implication**: passive evidence gives Ofcom a stronger basis for enforcement under the Online Safety Act. Platforms face higher costs for age assurance, child-safe defaults, parental controls, recommender restrictions, and product redesign. Enforcement that meaningfully works could reduce reported reach and engagement even if user demand is unchanged.

- ## Messaging and Communication
	- **Category reach**: 88% of 8-14s visited a messaging or calling service.
	- **WhatsApp**: 69% monthly reach, 57% daily reach, 440 visits and 13h36m per visitor during the 28-day period. Reach rose from 40% at ages 8-9 to 78% at ages 10-12 and 85% at ages 13-14.
	- **Discord**: only 16% reach, but visitors averaged **261 visits and 7h49m per month**. Top-quartile users averaged 1h04m per day, making Discord another narrow-but-deep platform.
	- **Life360**: reached 18% of 8-14s and 26% of 13-14s, indicating meaningful adoption of family-location and family-messaging products.
	- **Read-through for [[$META]]**: WhatsApp becomes a daily utility before adulthood and may seed durable family and peer-group networks, but parent-managed accounts and child-safety regulation increase product-compliance complexity.

- ## Artificial Intelligence
	- **Category reach by age**: 40% at ages 8-9, 54% at ages 10-12, and 72% at ages 13-14 used at least one standalone AI service.

	  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
	  | ChatGPT | 33% | 16% | 35% | 48% | 1h03m |
	  | Microsoft Copilot | 6% | 4% | 6% | 9% | 14m |
	  | AI Gallery | 5% | 7% | 6% | 2% | 33m |
	  | Google Gemini | 5% | 3% | 5% | 7% | 3m |
	  | PolyBuzz | 2% | 1% | 2% | 4% | 2h10m |
	  | Grok | 2% | 2% | 3% | 1% | 22m |
	  | Character AI | 2% | Less than 1% | 2% | 3% | 2h58m |
	  | DeepSeek | 1% | 1% | 2% | 2% | 1h57m |
	  | Perplexity | 1% | 1% | 2% | 1% | 44m |
	- **OpenAI has a large first-mover lead**: ChatGPT reached 33% versus 6% for Copilot and 5% for Gemini. At ages 13-14, ChatGPT reach was 48%, compared with 31% among UK adults in a separate November 2025 Ipsos sample.
	- **Usage is task-like rather than feed-like**: ChatGPT users averaged 45 visits per month, about two minutes per visit and 1h03m total. Top-quartile users spent 3h54m per month, versus less than one minute for the bottom quartile.
	- **Character companions are small but unusually deep**: Character AI and PolyBuzz each reached 2%, yet users spent 2h58m and 2h10m per month respectively. This suggests high engagement and heightened child-safety exposure within a small cohort.
	- **Measurement understates embedded AI**: Snapchat My AI, Meta AI, Grok inside X, and Gemini summaries inside Search were excluded. The 55% category reach is therefore a lower bound for exposure to AI functionality.

- ## Measurement Caveats
	- **No valid year-over-year comparison**: Ofcom changed recruitment, monitoring, and quality controls after phase one. The 2025 and 2026 results are not like-for-like.
	- **Important devices are excluded**: consoles, TV sets, smart devices, Amazon Fire tablets, Chromebooks, and school-only devices were not measured. Among the sample, parents reported 398 console users, 98 Chromebook users, and 56 Fire-tablet users.
	- **Most children had only one tracked device**: 142 of 701 had two or more devices measured. Total usage and cross-device reach are therefore understated.
	- **A visit requires only more than one second**: reach can include accidental visits, blocked age-assurance screens, login pages, or authentication flows. Reach and time spent must be considered together.
	- **Time starts when an app or site opens**: passive measurement cannot prove active attention throughout a session. Picture-in-picture video and background audio are generally not captured.
	- **VPN use can create missing data**, and awareness of monitoring may alter behavior. The final sample may also over-index digitally engaged families willing to participate.
	- **Averages are skewed**: upper- and lower-quartile results show that platform use is highly uneven. Median behavior would often be more representative than the mean but is not provided.

- ## Investment Implications
	- **[[$GOOGL]] - strongest aggregate position**: Google owns discovery, education access, email, Android distribution, and the broadest attention product in YouTube. YouTube's younger skew is a strength for lifetime user acquisition but raises safety and age-assurance exposure.
	- **[[$SNAP]] - exceptional teen attention, concentrated risk**: Snapchat's 44% reach understates its economic relevance because users average 1h27m/day and heavy users exceed four hours. Its intensity creates monetization potential but also makes it a prime regulatory target around persuasive design and late-night use.
	- **[[$META]] - diversified youth network**: Facebook/Messenger, WhatsApp, and Instagram cover identity, messaging, and social discovery. WhatsApp's use below age 13 is especially important for long-term network formation and compliance costs.
	- **[[$RBLX]] - dominant measured gaming reach**: Roblox has nearly four times the reach of the next gaming app and remains stable through age 14. The key strategic challenge is retaining engagement as users age into teen social/video platforms.
	- **OpenAI - early consumer default**: ChatGPT has a roughly 5-6x reach lead over Copilot or Gemini among 8-14s. If maintained, this could create a generation for whom ChatGPT is the default interface for knowledge work before entering the workforce.
	- **[[$AMZN]] and Chinese commerce - early funnel competition**: Amazon leads youth retail reach, but Temu is close and Shein has much higher frequency. Children's browsing behavior points to future customer-acquisition economics rather than current transaction value.
	- **[[$AAPL]] - device share does not automatically equal service attention**: Apple devices represented a majority of monitored smartphones, but Apple Music's 57% reach produced only 3m/day and App Store measurement was anomalously low. Hardware distribution remains powerful, while passive foreground data is insufficient to establish service dominance.
	- **Online-safety regulation is a sector-wide margin headwind**: near-universal underage access gives Ofcom evidence to demand stronger age assurance and child-specific product changes. Compliance may reduce measured reach, add friction, constrain recommendations and notifications, and shift product-development resources away from monetization.

- ## What to Monitor
	- Whether Ofcom's future passive studies show lower under-13 reach after enforcement of children's safety duties; phase-two methodology should become the baseline rather than comparing with phase one.
	- YouTube's ability to retain share as children age and Snapchat/TikTok gain attention.
	- Snapchat's 13-14 engagement, late-night visit frequency, and any regulator-mandated changes to streaks, notifications, or recommendations.
	- Roblox engagement beyond age 14 and whether aging-up initiatives narrow the gap with teen social/video services.
	- ChatGPT versus Gemini/Copilot reach among children, especially as AI becomes embedded in search, education, and device operating systems.
	- The effect of parent-managed WhatsApp accounts and age-assurance systems on Meta's reported child reach.
	- Whether heavy-user concentration persists; movement in the top quartile may matter more economically than changes in average reach.
