- ## The Economist: Nvidia is the central bank of AI
	- **Source**: *The Economist*, Briefing, "Nvidia is the central bank of AI: But will its loans prove sound?", Sept 3, 2026, print headline "The central bank of AI" (user-provided text). Exposure estimates come from Morgan Stanley, SemiAnalysis and Bloomberg Intelligence, as cited in the article.
	- **Thesis**: Nvidia now finances its own demand at a scale that earns it the "central bank of AI" label.
		- It has pledged over **\$70bn** to startups and offered about **\$300bn** of financial support to customers in three years. The tools are equity stakes, income floors for neoclouds, lease and power guarantees, and residual-value support.
		- The motive is to reduce dependence on hyperscalers, which are about half of revenue and are turning into chip rivals, by making cheaper credit available to everyone else.
		- It all rests on two assumptions: that **chips hold their value** and that **compute demand keeps growing fast**.
		- Nvidia can absorb the exposure today. But if growth merely *disappoints*, guarantees would be triggered just as sales fall, and the risk rises as commitments keep growing.
	- Related: [[2026-09-11-nvidia-backstop-universe-credit-support]] (SemiAnalysis's detailed version of the same exposure), [[2026-06-11-nvidia-coreweave-nebius-circular-financing]], [[2026-10-29-google-tpu-repurchase-guarantees]], [[2026-09-01-stratechery-nvidia-earnings-dollars-per-gigawatt-hugging-face]].

- ## The financing toolkit
	- | Tool | Date | Example and size | How it works |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | **Startup equity** | Ongoing | Over **\$70bn** pledged in 3 years. About **90** deals last year (nearly 2× two years earlier) and **60-odd** so far this year | Stakes in future customers or in firms that drive chip demand indirectly |
	  | **Open-weight model bets** | Aug 2026 (Poolside) | **Poolside**: \$6bn to license its coding models plus \$1bn for a stake. **Hugging Face**: agreed acquisition for **\$12.9bn** | Multiply AI products and companies independent of hyperscalers |
	  | **Neocloud income floors** | July 2026 | **Sharon AI**: about \$4.9bn backstop to deploy about 40,000 Nvidia chips. **Firmus**: plans up to 170,000 chips | Nvidia pays a set price for compute, often for **6 years**, and shares in any upside above it. Predictable revenue lowers the neoclouds' borrowing cost |
	  | **Lease and power guarantees** | Mid-Aug 2026 | **Ohio data centre**: up to **\$105bn**. Owner SB Energy (SoftBank), tenant OpenAI. Uses **1.5m** Nvidia processors over multiple upgrade cycles | Guarantees the land and building lease and the power-purchase contract. Runs **20 years from 2028** |
	  | **Wall Street partnership** | Aug 2026 | More than **\$500bn** of AI-infrastructure investment with **six** Wall Street firms | Sovereign funds, insurers and pension funds finance independent vehicles that buy Nvidia hardware and sell compute. Nvidia puts in no cash and no debt, but gives **residual-value support of up to 25%** of deal value, plus technical support |
	  | **Layered leases** | Reported | **Anthropic**: about **\$35bn** to rent capacity from Lambda, in which Nvidia has a stake. Lambda uses a **Hut 8** facility whose entire capacity Nvidia had leased (and may have largely sublet) | Nvidia sits in the middle of even a Google- and Amazon-chip customer's compute chain |
	- **Huang's framing**: these deals unlock "perfectly viable projects" that struggle to borrow at the right price, and make compute "an investable asset class."
- ## Why Nvidia is doing it
	- **Its biggest customers are becoming rivals**:
		- | Data point | Value |
		  |---|---|
		  | Hyperscalers' share of Nvidia revenue | About **half** |
		  | Hyperscaler investment this year | About **\$800bn**, mostly AI infrastructure |
		  | Cost of custom chips relative to Nvidia's | **One-fifth to one-third** |
		  | Custom chips' share of the AI processor market (Bloomberg Intelligence) | About **40%** this year, rising to about **50%** by the end of the decade |
		- Some are also selling their chips to others. Google has sold specialized processors to Anthropic, and Amazon expects its custom-chip business to become a sizeable revenue source.
	- **The borrowing-cost gap it is trying to close**:
		- | Borrower | Deal | Rate |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Alphabet (investment grade) | **\$2.75bn** of 50-year bonds, November | **5.7%** |
		  | CoreWeave (biggest neocloud) | **\$2.6bn**, July | "Almost double" Alphabet's (about 11% by my math) |

- ## The two assumptions underneath
	- **1. Chips hold their value.**
		- **Huang's case**: chips are "fungible" and durable, software upgrades extend their useful life, and they are bankable collateral.
		- **Burry's case**: cloud providers inflate profits by depreciating chips over 5–6 years instead of 2–3.
		- **Evidence for Huang so far**:
			- | Evidence | Data |
			  |---|---|
			  | CoreWeave A100 contract | Runs into **2029**, for a chip launched in **2020** |
			  | H100 rental price (SemiAnalysis) | About **\$2.80/hour** on a one-year contract, only about **10%** below its early-2023 launch level |
		- **The counterargument**: longevity and price may be "artefacts of scarcity." Older chips are kept busy on inference because compute is short. Nvidia also ships a new generation every year to replace the last, so it is arguing both that old chips hold value and that new ones should supplant them.
	- **2. Compute demand keeps growing fast.**
		- Supply is growing. Hyperscalers, and even OpenAI, are building their own inference chips now that inference spending has overtaken training.
		- Tim Davis (founder of an AI hardware firm acquired by Qualcomm) expects chip economics to "start to compress."
		- **The failure mode does not need a collapse**: merely disappointing growth leaves neoclouds unable to sell capacity. That triggers Nvidia's backstops (buying unused compute, covering price shortfalls, paying for unused power) just as Nvidia's own sales and cash flow fall.
- ## Who says what
	- | Person | Affiliation | View |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Jensen Huang | Nvidia CEO | Financing unlocks viable projects. The buildout is "at full steam" |
	  | Jay Goldberg | Seaport Research Partners | Nvidia walks a line between "enabling demand" and "creating it." Not crossed yet, but "getting pretty close" |
	  | Michael Burry | Investor | Skeptical. Slower chip demand, more supply and falling prices would hit both profits and the guarantees |
	  | Dan Nishball | SemiAnalysis | Backstops "buy time" for lenders to get comfortable. Not a permanent feature |
	  | Andy Li | CreditSights | Worries Nvidia will keep "pushing the pedal" until "something breaks" |
	- **The dotcom analogy**: Cisco and Lucent lent billions to telecom carriers that bought their gear, and took big losses when some carriers collapsed.
	- **Others doing the same**:
		- AMD offered big stakes in itself to OpenAI and Meta in exchange for large chip contracts.
		- In April, Google assembled a consortium to finance Anthropic's **\$35bn** purchase of its chips, and Broadcom agreed to cover any shortfall if Anthropic fails to pay.
	- **The Economist's conclusion**: "the more of this boom [Nvidia's investors] finance, the more of any bust they will have to bear."
- ## My notes
	- **The numbers line up with SemiAnalysis's Sept 11 report** ([[2026-09-11-nvidia-backstop-universe-credit-support]]):
		- Both have the **\$105bn** Ohio (PORTS-Pike) cap, about **\$33bn** of debt and about **\$25bn** of committed equity.
		- The Economist's **\$5.9bn per 100MW** is SemiAnalysis's **\$59B/GW** for the neocloud income-floor (AICP) programme.
		- Differences: Sharon AI is **\$4.9bn** here and **\$4.2B** in SemiAnalysis. SemiAnalysis also says Nvidia **paused new AICP deals** shortly before Sept 11, so the **\$175bn**-by-2028 path is an upper bound unless the programme restarts.
		- The **\$99bn** cash and securities figure differs from SemiAnalysis's **\$22B** cash plus **\$128B** investment book. That is a definitional difference, not a contradiction.
	- **The two sources disagree on what breaks it.** The Economist stresses a demand slowdown. SemiAnalysis argues a normal slowdown is absorbable, and that the real failure mode is a **competitive accelerator transition**, where guarantees are called just as Nvidia must cut prices.
		- The Economist's own custom-chip data (one-fifth to one-third the cost, share rising from 40% to 50%) is evidence for SemiAnalysis's version.
		- Either way the guarantees are **correlated with Nvidia's core business**. They pay out in exactly the states where chip sales are also weak.
	- **End demand is concentrated**: the largest single guarantee backs OpenAI, and the Lambda/Hut 8 chain ends at Anthropic. Spreading guarantees across many neoclouds doesn't spread the end-demand risk, which sits mostly with two frontier labs. That matches SemiAnalysis's finding that OpenAI is 97% of the guarantee cap.
	- **Why this differs from Cisco and Lucent**: Lucent lent cash up front. Most of Nvidia's exposure is contingent, long-dated (20 years for Ohio) and partly recoverable (re-tenanting, collateral). Nvidia also earns full margin on the chips when they are sold.
		- *My note*: the closest danger is the 2026–2028 ramp, when guarantees take effect (all-in debt rises from \$53bn to \$200bn) while custom silicon's share is still rising.
	- **The metric to watch is the H100 rental price.** At about \$2.80/hour, roughly 10% below launch, it holds up both the collateral case and the depreciation debate. A sharp fall as supply comes online would hit the residual-value guarantees first ([[2026-06-05-gpu-cluster-rental-unit-economics-spacex-google-anthropic]]).
	- **The equity bets are demand diversification**: Poolside, Hugging Face and the open-weight push build a customer base outside the hyperscalers. They answer the custom-chip threat with more independent buyers, not with lower prices.
