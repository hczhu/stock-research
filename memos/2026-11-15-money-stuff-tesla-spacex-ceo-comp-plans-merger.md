- ## Tesla and SpaceX CEO Pay Plans Under a Possible Merger
	- **Source**: Matt Levine, *Money Stuff* (Bloomberg), "SpaceX/Tesla comp packages", mid-November 2026 (the excerpt gives no exact date). Levine draws on Tesla's 2025 CEO Performance Award summary and plan agreement, SpaceX's award agreement (January) and its March modification for the xAI acquisition, and SpaceX's equity compensation plan.
	- **Thesis**: Musk's moonshot pay plans are mostly irrelevant to a SpaceX–Tesla merger. An all-stock deal with Tesla as the acquired company would be a change in control below the first \$2T milestone, which **wipes out the entire Tesla award**. The SpaceX award would survive, with milestones the board can adjust at its discretion. The real motive for a merger is **super-voting control of Tesla**, not pay engineering.
- ## The Two Awards Compared
	- | Term | Tesla 2025 CEO Performance Award | SpaceX CEO award (as modified for xAI) |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | Size | About **423.7M shares** (roughly 12% of Tesla) in 12 tranches. Worth a bit over \$1T if fully earned at \$8.5T. | Up to about **1.3B SpaceX shares** in several tranches |
	  | Market-cap milestones | \$2T up to \$8.5T | About \$1.065T up to \$7.5T |
	  | Operational milestones | Each tranche also needs one operational goal: 8 adjusted-EBITDA thresholds (\$50B to \$400B), or 20M vehicles delivered, 10M active FSD subscriptions, 1M Optimus bots delivered, or 1M robotaxis in commercial operation | No shares at all without either a **Mars colony of at least 1M inhabitants** (most tranches) or **space-based data centers delivering 100 TW of compute per year** (some tranches). Market cap alone earns nothing. |
	  | Vesting | March 2033 if earned by 2030, otherwise September 2035. Forfeited if he leaves first. | — |
	  | Acquisition adjustment | **Mechanical**: market-cap milestones rise by the purchase price, EBITDA milestones by the target's EBITDA, and product goals are "equitably adjusted" | **Discretionary**: the board "may equitably adjust" market-cap milestones only. The Mars and space-data-center goals never adjust. |
	  | Change in control | Earned tranches vest, measured at the deal value. **All unearned tranches are forfeited.** | No general acceleration on a change in control |
	- **xAI precedent**: SpaceX was valued at a nominal \$1T and xAI at \$250B when they combined, and the board scaled every market-cap milestone up **25%**. The original top milestone of \$6T became \$7.5T. A proportional adjustment like this is stricter than adding the target's value to each milestone. On the Tesla award's additive rule, by contrast, a \$3T acquisition would satisfy only the first three tranches and forfeit the other nine.
- ## Merger Mechanics (Levine's Assumptions)
	- **Deal shape**: all-stock, with no significant premium for either side. SpaceX was worth a bit over \$2T and Tesla a bit over \$1.5T, so SpaceX holders would own the larger share of the combined company.
	- **SpaceX survives as the legal entity**, because Musk holds super-voting SpaceX stock and Tesla has none. Levine estimates Musk would end up with **about 78% of the combined company's voting power**.
	- **Outcomes**:
		- **Tesla award**: a change in control at about \$1.5T, below the \$2T first milestone, so **all 423.7M potential shares are forfeited**.
		- **SpaceX award survives**, and the board decides how to adjust its milestones:
			- | Adjustment method | Top milestone after merger |
			  | --- | --- |
			  | Additive (+\$1.5T, the way Tesla's rule works) | About \$9T |
			  | Proportional (×1.75, the xAI precedent) | **About \$13T** |
		- To earn the full SpaceX award, Musk would therefore need roughly a \$13T valuation **plus** either a million people on Mars or 100 TW of orbital compute.
	- **Existing equity dwarfs the award**: Musk owns about **46% of SpaceX's economic value** today and would own a bit over one-third of the combined company. At \$13T that stake is worth more than \$4T, against roughly \$1T for the award, and it gains the same value with or without Mars.
	- **Boards keep discretion**: either board could make Musk shrink his package as a condition of the merger, or grant him new pay for doing it.
- ## Why the Merger Is About Control, Not Pay
	- **The Tesla award was mainly a voting-control device**: more shares give Musk more say over the "robot army". A merger into super-voting SpaceX gets him that control directly and makes new Tesla shares unnecessary.
	- **The SpaceX award is partly for show**: with about 46% of the economics, Musk already keeps about half of any value he creates, so extra shares add little incentive.
	- **Implication for \$TSLA holders** (memo inference, not Levine's): a no-premium merger removes the award's potential ~12% dilution. In exchange, Tesla holders give up voting influence in a combined company Musk would control with about 78% of the votes. The governance trade-off, not the award arithmetic, is the variable to price.
