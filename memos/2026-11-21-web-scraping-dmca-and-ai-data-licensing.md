- ## Public visibility and bulk data access can have different economics
	- **Source**: Pamela Samuelson, *Web Scraping as Illegal Circumvention?*, Communications of the ACM opinion, October 14, 2026. The memo adds the November ruling in Google’s case. [ACM republication](https://theofficialacm.substack.com/p/web-scraping-as-illegal-circumvention)
	- **Thesis**: Reddit can benefit from keeping posts discoverable to individuals while charging AI and search companies for reliable access at scale. If courts allow platforms to enforce qualifying access controls, third-party data feeds become less dependable substitutes for a license. The business value depends on the rights, freshness and coverage the platform can actually deliver.

- ## Why these cases challenge the economics of search data
	- SerpApi supplies machine-readable Google search results to customers. Google and Reddit allege it bypassed Google’s SearchGuard anti-bot control, including to access search results containing Reddit material. Reddit sued Perplexity as well. The dispute matters because an AI product can depend on a third-party search feed for fresh material even when it has no direct contract with the site that hosts the content.
	- Section 1201(a)(1)(A) of the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act addresses bypassing effective controls on access to copyrighted works. Section 1201(a)(2) addresses supplying qualifying circumvention technology or services. These claims turn on how access was obtained and on the works the control protects. The New York court treated the access claim as distinct from infringement, an issue on which appellate courts have differed. [Statute](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t17/s1201/) · [Reddit ruling, pp. 45–47](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/25cv8763%2C%20Reddit%20Inc.%20v.%20SerpApi%20LLC%20et%20al.%20-%20Opinion%20%26%20Order-1.pdf)

- ## The Google and Reddit rulings turned on the pleadings
	- | Court and date | Ruling | Why it matters |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | Google v. SerpApi, N.D. California, July 20, 2026 | Dismissed claims based on results with no copyrighted content without leave to amend. Allowed amendment for claims involving protected content. | Google had to identify copyrightable material and adequately plead the copyright owners’ authority for SearchGuard. Operating the search engine alone supplied neither. |
	  | Reddit v. SerpApi and Perplexity, S.D. New York, July 31, 2026 | Allowed the access-circumvention claims to proceed against both defendants and the access-control trafficking claim against SerpApi. Dismissed the separate §1201(b) claim against SerpApi. | The court found Reddit had plausibly alleged protected material and that SearchGuard controlled automated access even when individuals could view results. |
	  | Google v. SerpApi, N.D. California, November 9, 2026 | Dismissed Google’s amended complaint with limited leave to amend. | Google’s cited licenses and privacy policy did not adequately connect copyright-owner authority and the protected content to the access control at issue. |
	- Both courts allowed the plaintiffs to assert injury without requiring copyright ownership as a threshold for suit. The cases then turned on which works and permissions each plaintiff had plausibly alleged. These orders assess pleadings and leave the underlying allegations unproven. [Google July order](https://cdn.arstechnica.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Google-v-SerpApi-Order-GRanting-Motion-to-DIsmiss-7-20-26.pdf) · [Reddit July order](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/25cv8763%2C%20Reddit%20Inc.%20v.%20SerpApi%20LLC%20et%20al.%20-%20Opinion%20%26%20Order-1.pdf) · [Google November order, ECF 62](https://serpapi.com/blog/content/files/2026/11/62.0--2026-11-09--ORDER-GRANTING-MOTION-TO-DISMISS-WITH-LEAVE-TO-AMEND-by.pdf)
	- Samuelson argues that applying a law designed around digital piracy to bulk access could also restrict archives, journalism and public-interest research. The column identifies similar claims in lawsuits about AI training data. The search-results rulings address specific access controls and allegations. They leave separate questions about training, fair use and the terms of particular licenses unresolved.

- ## Reddit sells a fresh feed while Google protects a distribution asset
	- **Reddit**: Its Google partnership supplies structured access to public posts and comments, including new material, for product display and model training. Samuelson cites an estimated **USD 60 million per year** for the deal. Reddit’s announcement confirms the arrangement but gives no price. The potential source of bargaining power is the ability to deliver a fresh, dependable feed and define permitted uses. Much of the underlying writing is authored by users, so licensing scope and authority matter. [Reddit partnership announcement](https://redditinc.com/news/reddit-and-google-expand-partnership)
	- **Alphabet**: Google is both a buyer of content access and the operator of a valuable search interface. Limiting outside reliance on its ranked results could preserve control of how that output is distributed. The repeated dismissal shows why the strength of its index and its legal authority over third-party works are separate questions.
	- **AI search and data vendors**: Licensed sources and diversified indexes can improve continuity but add cost and integration work. Exposure is greater when a product depends on a single upstream source for fresh answers. Training data and live retrieval have different economics. A training corpus can be acquired for a defined run, while retrieval may need uninterrupted access and frequent updates.
	- A licensing bull case needs evidence of renewal pricing, more buyers and limited loss of referral traffic. A bear case is that buyers turn to substitute sources or that tighter controls reduce discovery and user contributions. Treat the court decisions as evidence about bargaining leverage and data continuity. Revenue estimates still need signed contracts and disclosed economics.
