- ## Meta Class C Recapitalization and Founder Alignment Agreement

	- **Source**: Meta Platforms, Inc. preliminary Schedule 14C information statement supplied by the user in two excerpts. The filing describes the Restated Charter, Class C dividend, 2025 Equity Incentive Plan amendment, Founder Alignment Agreement, Special Committee rationale, and negotiation history through the Special Committee's formal approval on October 14, 2026. Later Board and shareholder approval dates come from the filing's notice and transaction summary.
	- **Thesis**: Meta is preparing to finance an AI investment plan requiring potentially hundreds of billions of dollars with non-voting equity rather than accept voting dilution or rely mainly on debt. The negotiated structure preserves Mark Zuckerberg's control and provides him liquidity, while stapling restrictions, leadership-linked releases, and performance gates partially constrain the resulting separation between his voting power and economic ownership.
- ## Final Decisions and Agreements

	- | Decision or agreement | Final terms | Stated rationale |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | Create Class C common stock | Amend the charter effective **January 19, 2027** to establish publicly traded, non-voting Class C shares; Meta intends to list them on Nasdaq. | Create equity currency for capital raising, employee compensation, acquisitions, and general corporate purposes without diluting existing voting power. |
	  | Initial Class C dividend | Distribute **one Class C share for every outstanding Class A or Class B share**, contingent on the Restated Charter. The record and payment dates remain to be set; payment is due no later than the fifth business day after effectiveness. | Seed a deep, liquid market, improve the prospect of index inclusion, and minimize the discount to Class A. Meta expects the market-price adjustment to resemble a two-for-one stock split; relative ownership is initially unchanged. |
	  | Future equity issuance | Meta expects to sell Class C shares from time to time, subject to market conditions. | Management forecast a need to raise **hundreds of billions of dollars over several years**, potentially beginning in fall 2026, to fund AI infrastructure and strategic plans. |
	  | Equity-plan amendment | Shares issued under the 2025 Equity Incentive Plan may be Class A or Class C; the amendment becomes effective with the Restated Charter. | Preserve employee-equity capacity without voting dilution and conserve cash for investment. |
	  | Zuckerberg's initial liquidity | **20%** of the Holder Group's initial Class C dividend is freely saleable; **80%** is restricted as Stapled Class C Shares. The freely saleable portion represents about **10% of Zuckerberg's pre-dividend economic stake**. | Provide liquidity, including for a possible California Proposition 40 wealth-tax liability, without an immediate reduction in his majority voting control. |
	  | Stapling Condition | A sale of restricted Class C generally requires a corresponding sale of Class A or Class B, conversion of Class B into Class A, or use of previously earned Stapling Credits. A non-compliant sale triggers automatic Class B-to-Class A conversion, subject to a five-trading-day cure for inadvertent errors. | Prevent Zuckerberg from monetizing most of his economic interest while retaining an unchanged amount of high-vote stock. |
	  | Leadership-linked release | Each full qualifying quarter releases shares equal to **2.5% of the initial Class C dividend** from stapling. All remaining initial shares are released after **32 qualifying quarters**, which need not be consecutive, if Zuckerberg remains full-time on the executive team or serves as executive chair. | Tie increasing founder liquidity to eight years of continued leadership rather than grant a conventional compensation package. |
	  | Headquarters-residency commitment | For eight years, while the leadership condition is met, Zuckerberg commits to reside in California or another jurisdiction containing Meta's headquarters, absent material unforeseen circumstances. | Keep him close to management and Meta Superintelligence Labs during the AI buildout; the committee cited coordination problems when he was not physically present during the pandemic. |
	  | Performance-gated additional dividends | Meta must declare another **1:1 Class C dividend** after each successive 2x, 4x, 8x, and later doubling of the adjusted **September 10, 2026 Class A reference price of \$580.60**, provided annualized shareholder return since that date is at least **10%**. No more than one dividend applies to each doubling and none can occur after Zuckerberg's death. | Permit further founder liquidity only after extraordinary value creation; every class receives the dividend pro rata. |
	  | Zuckerberg compensation | The agreement provides no new cash or equity award. Zuckerberg withdrew his request for a performance-based compensation program when he accepted the final terms, and his **\$1 annual salary** remains unchanged. | Avoid the compensation expense and possible stock-price impact of a peer-style founder award while using pro-rata dividends and performance conditions for alignment. |
	  | Equal economic treatment | Class A, B, and C generally share ratably in dividends, liquidation value, and transaction consideration. Zuckerberg and the Holder Group cannot receive superior consideration in mergers or tender offers. Meta will use best efforts to repurchase at least an equal number of Class C shares when it repurchases Class A shares. | Protect minority shareholders economically even while Zuckerberg retains voting control. |
	  | Approval process | The Special Committee unanimously recommended the package; the Board approved it with Zuckerberg abstaining. On October 20, entities holding **315,826,951 Class B shares**, or approximately **56.1% of total voting power**, approved the Restated Charter and equity-plan amendment by written consent. No Class A vote or shareholder meeting is required. | Delaware law, Meta's charter, and its bylaws permit majority written consent; the independent committee was used to negotiate a conflicted controller transaction. |
- ## Why the Parties Accepted the Package

	- **Meta management — capital access and speed**: Management said the AI plan could require hundreds of billions of dollars beginning as early as fall 2026. Class C creates an equity instrument Meta can issue quickly for financing, acquisitions, or compensation while preserving cash.
	- **Avoiding the two less-attractive financing routes**: Zuckerberg would not support a significant Class A issuance that reduced his control. Debt on the contemplated scale could pressure Meta's credit ratings, and the filing describes weakening hyperscaler credit conditions. Non-voting equity also permits participation by sovereign financial institutions that may face U.S. foreign-investment constraints when acquiring voting stock.
	- **A large initial float was necessary**: Management estimated that a limited, contingent dividend could leave Class C trading at a **5%–20% discount** and outside relevant indices. A 1:1 dividend maximizes liquidity and makes future issuance less economically dilutive than selling a thinly traded security at a larger discount.
	- **Zuckerberg — control, liquidity, and location**: Zuckerberg held approximately **60.6% of voting power but 13.4% of economic ownership** on October 1, 2026. He supported the capital raise but rejected Class A financing and Class B sunset provisions. Proposition 40 could have imposed a multibillion-dollar tax and prompted him to consider leaving California; unrestricted Class C provides liquidity without an immediate loss of control.
	- **Special Committee — preserve economic-voting alignment**: The committee accepted continued founder control because it credited Zuckerberg with Meta's long-term performance, but it would not permit an unrestricted separation of control and ownership. The 80% staple, quarterly leadership condition, performance gates, and equal-treatment provisions were the negotiated substitutes for the broader Class B sunset protections Zuckerberg rejected.
	- **No incremental founder award**: The recapitalization gives every shareholder the same dividend. The committee characterized this as preferable to a bespoke compensation plan that would create an accounting expense and dilute shareholders solely for Zuckerberg's benefit.

- ## Economic and Governance Read-Through

	- **The filing is an explicit signal of future equity financing, not merely governance housekeeping.** Meta says it currently has sufficient resources but wants Class C ready before competing AI companies absorb more available capital. Expected offerings make economic dilution a real future consideration even though voting dilution is avoided.
	- **The 1:1 dividend does not itself create value.** It initially leaves each shareholder's relative economics unchanged and mechanically divides the trading value across Class A/B and Class C. Its value to Meta comes from liquidity, index eligibility, and the ability to issue Class C later at a smaller discount.
	- **The deal prolongs founder control.** Class C issuance can finance Meta, compensate employees, and fund acquisitions without eroding Zuckerberg's voting percentage. The filing acknowledges that this may substantially extend his ability to elect directors and determine shareholder votes.
	- **The safeguards are meaningful but incomplete.** Stapling prevents immediate unrestricted monetization of most of Zuckerberg's stake, yet eight years of qualifying service can free all initial Class C shares while his high-vote holdings remain. The structure manages divergence rather than eliminating it.
	- **Performance dividends resemble founder incentives without being labeled compensation.** All shareholders benefit pro rata, and the 2x-plus-10%-return thresholds are demanding. At the same time, each additional dividend creates more non-voting liquidity and can further entrench control relative to economic ownership.
	- **Class C usefulness depends on market acceptance.** A persistent discount, exclusion from indices, employee preference for voting Class A, or sellers' need for voting stock to obtain tax-deferred acquisition treatment could force Meta to issue more shares than planned or continue using Class A and cash.
