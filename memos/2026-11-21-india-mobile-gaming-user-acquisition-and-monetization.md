- ## India Mobile Gaming: User Acquisition vs. Monetization
	- **Source**: User-provided screenshot of an X post by Roger (@luojie), recounting his team's 2021 game promotion through vivo's India distribution channel and comparing it with U.S. promotion. The game and U.S. acquisition channel were not named.
	- **Thesis**: Cheap user acquisition did not translate into comparable monetization for this game: the India campaign bought far more users, but their reported spending recovered little of the promotion budget.
- ## Campaign Economics
	- | Metric | India (vivo channel) | U.S. (channel unspecified) |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | Promotion spend | \$200 budget | \$200 budget |
	  | New users | 20,000 users | About 10 users |
	  | Reported monetization | $2 in total top-ups | Roughly broke even, per the author; exact receipts not given |
	  | Promotion cost per new user (calculated) | \$0.01 per user | About \$20 per user |
	  | Top-ups per acquired user (calculated) | $0.0001 per user | Not disclosed |
	  | Top-ups as a share of promotion spend (calculated) | 1% of spend | Not directly comparable; break-even basis unspecified |
	- The author says the team did little to no further promotion in India after the campaign.
- ## What the Comparison Measures
	- The India campaign acquired roughly 2,000 times as many users on the same budget. This is a comparison of acquisition volume, not of active or retained players.
	- The post does not report payer count, retention, the measurement window, ad revenue, or net proceeds. Top-ups are not profit or lifetime value; the anecdote cannot establish cohort profitability.
	- Geography and distribution channel are not isolated. This is evidence about one title and acquisition campaign, not a benchmark for spending by Indian gamers or for the economics of ad-supported games.
