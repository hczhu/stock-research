- ## Business Read-Through
	- **A mature game can retain blockbuster reach**: 81.5 million estimated MAU and 190.5 million annual installs indicate continued discovery and reactivation long after Candy Crush first appeared on King's website in 2011. The asset behaves more like an enduring consumer platform than a conventional hit game with a short revenue tail.
	- **Monetization does not require a high average spend per player**: The implied monthly IAP revenue per MAU is below one dollar. At this scale, a low payer conversion rate or modest average spend can still support nearly \$900 million of annual in-app purchases.
	- **The franchise compounds distribution and IP**: Five billion cumulative installs create a large pool for cross-promotion, sequels, live operations, and reactivation. The 2023 lifetime milestones imply that King's advantage is not only game design but also accumulated brand recognition and player acquisition efficiency.
	- **The economic contribution to Microsoft is likely cash-generative but not fully observable here**: The screenshot supports the durability of the King asset inside Activision Blizzard, but it provides no operating costs, platform fees, user-acquisition spend, segment margin, or share of Microsoft Gaming revenue.

- ## Evidence Boundaries
	- The 2025 numbers are external estimates, and the excerpt explicitly says King declined to provide current internal figures.
	- The 81.5 million MAU, 190.5 million installs, and \$876.5 million of IAP revenue appear to describe Candy Crush, while the \$20 billion and 5 billion milestones cover the broader Candy Crush franchise. Directly comparing the two scopes would overstate precision.
	- Installs are a flow measure and MAU is a user-stock measure; their ratio is not a retention rate. Revenue divided by installs or MAU is useful only as an order-of-magnitude monetization benchmark.
	- The excerpt says Candy Crush consistently ranks among the highest-grossing mobile games, but it supplies no ranking, market-share series, growth rate, payer count, or year-over-year comparison.
