- ## Yann LeCun: Autoregressive LLMs Are Not the Path — and the One Claim With a Price Tag
	- **Source**: Yann LeCun, social post, Sep 2026, restating and defending his standing position that "auto-regressive LLMs, in and of themselves, will not lead to human-level AI." **Attribution note**: the text was supplied without a byline. Authorship was inferred from internal evidence — first-person ownership of **JEPA** ("what I've been advocating") and of continuous-representation-space search ("what I have always advocated for"), both positions specific to LeCun — and subsequently **confirmed by the user**. This matters because the source-quality caveat below depends on the author being the person advocating JEPA.
	- **Source quality, stated plainly**: this is a partisan defending a position he has held publicly for years, against criticism, while advocating for his own research programme (**JEPA**). Treat the diagnosis as informed and the prescription as interested. His one quantitative support — "**3000 papers on JEPA in just 4 years**" — is a citation-volume argument, which evidences attention rather than validation.
	- **Thesis**: Most of this post is an argument about the definition of intelligence and has no investable content. **One claim does**: that reasoning is currently a search conducted **in token space**, that this is "limited and inefficient," and that the industry is moving toward search in **continuous representation space**. If that migration happens, **reasoning depth stops being billed as emitted tokens** — which pressures the pricing basis of the API business and degrades "tokens served" as a demand proxy. The second-most useful thing here is a set of dated, falsifiable markers.
- ## The claim with the clearest financial consequence
	- **His technical point**: today's reasoning systems already use non-autoregressive search — "which is what I have always advocated for" — but "AFAICT, they do it in **token space**, which is limited and inefficient." He argues human-like reasoning requires search in **continuous representation space**, and that "it looks like the industry is moving towards that."
	- **Why this matters commercially, which he does not say.** Chain-of-thought reasoning consumes tokens *because the thinking is emitted*. If deliberation moves into activations rather than text, the model can think longer without producing proportionally more billable output. **Compute need not fall; billed tokens would.** That decouples the cost of serving from the unit of pricing, and it is a problem for any model whose revenue line is per-token.
	- **The repo already contains an instance of the migration he predicts, from an unrelated source.** SemiAnalysis reports **looped transformers** as "effectively confirmed" in GPT-6 Astra and suspected in Anthropic's largest models — running input through the same layers repeatedly to buy **compute depth rather than parameter count** ([[2026-09-14-semianalysis-four-high-hbm-rubin-ultra-capacity-cut]]). That is latent-space computation, not token-space, and it arrived as a *capacity* story. LeCun's framing gives it a second reading.
	- **The hardware read-through runs the opposite way to the pricing one, and reinforces an existing repo conclusion.** Looped or latent reasoning re-reads the weights many times per emitted token, so **bandwidth consumed per unit of output rises**. That strengthens rather than weakens the bandwidth-over-capacity argument behind the 4-high HBM thesis: fewer tokens, each far more bandwidth-expensive.
	- **What to watch**: whether published reasoning traces stay legible. Visible chain-of-thought is token-space search; opaque deliberation with a short answer is the migration happening.
- ## The RL ceiling is a domain ceiling, and it explains where the revenue already is
	- **His claim**: self-improvement "as currently practiced, only works for domains where the quality of outputs can be **scored without human intervention** — such as mathematics, code, and scenarios that can be simulated accurately. **Not anything else.**" He adds that "humans and animals learn new skills way more efficiently than current RL methods."
	- **Set this against the measured compute mix.** SemiAnalysis has post-training and RL going from ~4% to **55% of frontier-lab compute in eleven quarters** ([[2026-08-21-semianalysis-open-vs-closed-catch-up-halves-every-era]] and [[2026-09-14-semianalysis-four-high-hbm-rubin-ultra-capacity-cut]]). LeCun's constraint implies that **majority of compute is concentrated in verifiable domains**, and that the path into non-verifiable ones is not solved.
	- **This is a better explanation of the revenue concentration than the usual one.** Coding is where frontier revenue actually is — Claude Code took Anthropic north of \$65B ARR — and the standard account is that coding was simply first. The verifiability constraint says coding is where RL *works*, which is a structural reason rather than a sequencing accident. It predicts the next verticals are the simulable ones, and that everything requiring human judgment to score scales worse.
	- **Testable form**: does RL-driven capability transfer to domains without automatic verifiers — legal reasoning, medical judgment, negotiation, taste — at anything like the rate seen in code and math? If not, the addressable surface for the current method is narrower than the capex implies.
- ## The falsification test he offers, with a date stamp
	- **"If LLMs were a path to human-level AI, we would have domestic robots and Level-4 or Level-5 self-driving cars for consumers by now. And we don't."** He sharpens it: "we certainly don't have cars that can **learn to drive in 20 hours** or practice like any teenager."
	- **This is the most useful paragraph in the post for investment purposes**, because it is checkable and dated rather than definitional. Consumer L4/L5 availability and general-purpose domestic robots are observable milestones, and the sample-efficiency framing — twenty hours, teenager-equivalent — is a specific bar that current methods miss by orders of magnitude.
	- **Read it as a marker on physical-AI timelines**, which is where the robotics capex narrative is most exposed. Relevant to [[2026-06-08-unitree-humanoid-robotics-dji-byd-playbook-semianalysis]] and [[2026-04-09-humanoid-robot-sales-china-us-estimated]].
	- The multimodal point is the weakest of the four and is mostly advocacy: current assistants use **separately-trained encoders that are not LLMs**, which he notes is what he has argued for, "except that I think the best way to do this is with **JEPA trained with self-supervised learning.**" The observation that multimodality is not coming from the LLM itself is correct and mildly relevant to architecture diversification; the prescription is his own agenda.
- ## What he concedes, and why it matters more than what he disputes
	- **The concession is explicit and is the part most likely to be misread**: "we now have computer systems that are impressive, very useful, and whose performance is **superhuman in an increasing number of domains (coding being one of them)**. But that's true of the entire history of progress in computer technology."
	- **So this is not a bearish call on AI revenue.** It is a bearish call on the **AGI framing** — and it is fully compatible with the current commercial trajectory, with the capex, and with the token-demand data in this repo. Anyone reading "LeCun says LLMs aren't the path" as a demand-side warning has substituted the headline for the argument.
	- His closing is a definitional claim, not an empirical one: intelligence "is not the mere accumulation and regurgitation of existing declarative knowledge," quoting Piaget — **"intelligence is not what you know, it is what you do when you don't know."** The ability to solve novel problems without prior training and adapt with minimal data.
	- **The honest limit of that argument**: a definition cannot be falsified by a benchmark, so the "we're still far from that" conclusion is unfalsifiable as stated. It is also unnecessary to his economically relevant claims, which stand or fall on the token-space, RL-verifiability and robotics markers above.
- ## How to hold this
	- **Separate the three layers, because they have different evidentiary status.** The **architectural observations** — token-space search, encoder-based multimodality, RL confined to verifiable domains — are descriptive and checkable. The **prescriptions** (continuous-space search, JEPA) are a research agenda with an interested advocate. The **definitional argument** about intelligence is not an investment input at all.
	- **The one position change this should prompt** is on the durability of per-token pricing. Every demand model in this repo denominates in tokens; LeCun is arguing the industry is moving toward doing more thinking per emitted token. That is bullish compute and ambiguous-to-bearish for **revenue per unit of compute** at the model layer — which compounds with the open-weight pricing pressure in [[2026-08-21-semianalysis-open-vs-closed-catch-up-halves-every-era]].
	- **What would make him wrong on the part that matters**: RL-driven gains transferring cleanly to domains with no automatic verifier, or a consumer L4 deployment and a general-purpose domestic robot arriving on current methods. Both are observable without resolving any argument about what intelligence is.
