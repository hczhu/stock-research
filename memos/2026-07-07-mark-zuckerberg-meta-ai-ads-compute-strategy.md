- ## A Script for Mark Zuckerberg: Meta's AI and Advertising Strategy
	- **Source**: Ben Thompson, ["A Script for Mark Zuckerberg"](https://stratechery.com/2026/a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg/), Stratechery, July 7, 2026.
	- **Important framing**: This article is a fictional script for what Thompson thinks Mark Zuckerberg should say on Meta's August 2026 earnings call. Historical events and business observations are separated below from Thompson's proposed commitments and predictions; none of the scripted statements should be treated as actual Meta guidance.
	- **Thesis**: Thompson believes Meta's AI spending is strategically justified because AI both threatens every digital business and directly improves Meta's core advertising engine. The missing piece is strategic discipline: Meta should stop aspiring to be a general platform, enterprise-AI vendor, or permanent cloud provider and instead use AI to deepen its advantages in entertainment, connection, commerce, and ads.

- ## Historical Company Events and Strategic Turning Points

	| Event | What Happened | Thompson's Interpretation |
	|---|---|---|
	| Facebook's initial launch | Users spent hours navigating individual profile pages | Human curiosity about other people was Facebook's first recommendation mechanism. |
	| Introduction of News Feed | Facebook consolidated updates into one feed; the launch drew protests outside its Palo Alto office | Engagement rose sharply, teaching management to trust revealed preference and product intuition over stated objections. |
	| Shift to mobile | Facebook initially retained web-centric technology and invested too late in a fully native smartphone experience | Zuckerberg's desire to preserve Facebook as a developer platform delayed the transition. Becoming an app forced Facebook to emphasize its own content and ads. |
	| Instagram evolution | Instagram became a major growth engine over approximately **15 years**, progressing from photos to Stories and then Reels as bandwidth increased | Consumer products must evolve with technology rather than preserve the use case that originally made them successful. |
	| WhatsApp acquisition | Meta secured a leading mobile messaging property | Thompson credits Zuckerberg with recognizing that messaging was mobile's killer application. |
	| TikTok disruption | TikTok recognized that public social feeds had become entertainment rather than primarily friend-to-friend connection | Meta's failure to embrace its entertainment identity created a competitive blind spot. |
	| Oculus / Reality Labs investment | Meta spent what Thompson describes in the fictional script as **twelve figures** on VR and platform development | The investment produced potentially useful technology but limited near-term market reach and consumed substantial investor credibility. |
	| Apple privacy conflict | Apple's platform changes impaired Meta's ad targeting and measurement | Thompson argues Apple framed digital advertising unfairly, but also faults Meta for failing to make an affirmative public case for its ad model. |
	| 2022 capex cycle | Meta's stock declined partly amid concern about aggressive GPU investment; ChatGPT launched approximately **one month** later | Spending that appeared excessive became strategically prescient as generative AI accelerated. |
	| Stories and Reels monetization ramps | New formats initially increased inventory and reduced price per ad before monetization caught up | Thompson identifies both transitions as exceptional stock-buying opportunities because investors focused on near-term pricing dilution instead of future inventory scale. |

- ## Thompson's Core Opinions
	- **Zuckerberg's recurring error is platform envy**: Facebook's CEO repeatedly tried to emulate prior technology titans by owning a platform, even though Meta's strongest business is advertising.
	- **Platforms and ad businesses have conflicting objectives**:
		- A platform is designed to feature and empower third-party developers.
		- An advertiser wants to capture user attention for itself.
		- Meta earns more by putting compelling content and ads at the center than by subordinating them to an external developer ecosystem.
	- **Apple accidentally improved Facebook**: Mobile reduced Facebook from a potential platform to an app, but the constraint pushed Meta toward a better consumer product and more effective native ad units.
	- **Hardware narrows Meta's natural market**: Proprietary devices restrict distribution, while an ad-supported service benefits from reaching the largest possible audience.
	- **Entertainment is the ideal advertising context**: Users voluntarily allocate attention to entertainment, creating inventory and intent that advertisers can monetize.
	- **Meta under-celebrates advertising**: Thompson thinks Zuckerberg has shown less interest in the ad business than investors and Meta's operating teams, even though it is the company's economic core.
	- **Meta ads create demand rather than tax existing demand**: Unlike search ads on Google, Amazon, or Apple's App Store, Meta often introduces consumers to products they did not know existed, connecting entrepreneurs with latent demand.
	- **Advertising funds universal access**: Better monetization allows Meta to keep consumer products free, expanding usage and avoiding the price elasticity that constrains subscriptions.

- ## Why AI Matters to Meta
	- **AI is an existential requirement**: Meta is asset-light because it is digital, but that also places its products directly in the path of AI-driven disruption. Depending on external model providers introduces strategic, pricing, access, and roadmap risk.
	- **AI already improves the core business**: Meta has used machine learning in advertising for years. Newer GPU-intensive systems improve ad targeting, content recommendations, engagement, and available ad impressions.
	- **Generative models expand the workflow**: LLMs and multimodal models can generate copy and images, predict which content and ads users want, and automate creative optimization.
	- **Each improvement can reach revenue directly**: Better recommendations increase time spent; more engagement creates inventory; better targeting and creative improve advertiser outcomes and auction demand.
	- **Meta owns consumer distribution**: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Reels, and Meta AI provide immediate surfaces for deploying models and collecting feedback.
	- **Consumer leisure is a differentiated AI market**: Meta need not win enterprise productivity. Its advantage is serving connection, entertainment, shopping, and off-the-clock experiences.
	- **Human connection may gain value as synthetic content grows**: Thompson predicts that people will become more interested in authentic human relationships and identity as AI interactions proliferate, strengthening Meta's social properties rather than making them obsolete.

- ## The Advertising Inventory Thesis
	- Thompson says the strongest leading indicator for a step-change in Meta growth is a large increase in available ad inventory.
	- Stories and Reels initially diluted ad pricing because supply expanded before demand and optimization caught up; subsequent monetization validated the inventory investment.
	- AI can make more digital objects understandable, targetable, shoppable, and sponsored, leading to Thompson's claim that **"AI makes every pixel monetizable."**
	- Potential inventory expansion includes:
		- AI-generated and personalized creative.
		- Commerce links attached to recognizable products and objects.
		- More relevant ads within generated or recommended content.
		- New AI-native interaction surfaces and hardware.
	- Thompson predicts this will be Meta's **largest inventory expansion ever**, but expects realization to take **several years**.
	- Near-term risks resemble Stories and Reels: falling price per ad, higher inference cost, and investor concern before demand and optimization absorb the new supply.

- ## Proposed Compute-Rental Framework
	- **This is Thompson's recommendation, not an announced Meta plan.**
	- Meta should rent a portion of its compute infrastructure on a short-term spot basis while retaining the right to reclaim capacity.
	- Thompson identifies two benefits:
		- Rental proceeds would help finance a still-larger infrastructure buildout that Meta expects to need later.
		- External rental pricing would establish an observable hurdle rate for every internal AI project.
	- Meta should reclaim rented compute only when expected internal returns exceed the spot-market rental income.
	- This would impose market discipline on capital allocation and steer capacity toward Meta's highest-return capabilities, particularly advertising and recommendations.
	- Thompson does **not** recommend building a permanent hyperscale cloud business. He expects Meta's internal ad opportunity eventually to earn more than long-term third-party compute rental.

- ## Strategic Prescription
	- Meta should explicitly define itself as an entertainment, connection, commerce, and advertising company rather than a general technology platform.
	- It should invest aggressively in AI talent and infrastructure because both defense and growth require it.
	- AI projects should be evaluated against measurable internal returns or the external market value of the compute they consume.
	- Meta should avoid centering its strategy on chatbots, business productivity, frontier-model prestige, or direct competition with OpenAI and Anthropic.
	- Reality Labs should be reframed as groundwork for AI-native access devices, not as a standalone attempt to force mass adoption of virtual reality.
	- Management should connect capex explicitly to ads, content recommendation, engagement, commerce, and new inventory to rebuild investor trust.

- ## Predictions
	- **Meta's AI capex will prove economically valuable if routed into ads**: Its existing monetization engine gives Meta a more direct return path than frontier labs that must establish new subscription or enterprise businesses.
	- **Inventory growth will precede monetization**: AI-generated and AI-understood surfaces may pressure near-term ad pricing before advertiser demand catches up, repeating the Stories and Reels pattern.
	- **Consumer AI will diverge from productivity AI**: OpenAI and Anthropic can compete for work and enterprise use cases, while Meta can dominate leisure, social connection, entertainment, and discovery.
	- **Human-centered networks become more important in an AI-saturated world**: Verified identity, relationships, creators, and social context should become scarcer and more valuable as synthetic content expands.
	- **Meta will eventually need most of the compute it builds**: Temporary excess capacity may be rentable, but Thompson expects advertising and recommendation returns to outbid permanent cloud customers.
	- **AI may validate selected Reality Labs assets**: Glasses or other ambient devices could become more useful when users need continuous access to AI rather than immersive VR.
	- **Management narrative will affect the valuation multiple**: Investors are more likely to support tens of billions of dollars in AI capex when management ties spending to Meta's proven ad economics instead of abstract superintelligence ambitions.

- ## Investment Implications
	- **The core underwriting case is operating leverage from AI-enhanced ads**: The most important outputs are higher engagement, more inventory, better conversion, stronger advertiser demand, and lower creative friction.
	- **Capex should be compared with incremental ad gross profit**: Spending is not justified merely because compute is scarce or AI is strategic; the internal return must eventually exceed a market rental hurdle.
	- **Meta has a monetization advantage over model labs**: It can deploy AI into products with billions of existing users and a mature auction rather than waiting to build a new customer base.
	- **Third-party model dependence is a real strategic risk**: Internal model and infrastructure investment protects Meta from changes in vendor pricing, availability, safety policy, and competitive access.
	- **Reality Labs remains the credibility overhang**: Thompson's fictional admission of twelve-figure spending underscores why investors demand clearer capital discipline before crediting a new infrastructure cycle.
	- **The largest risk is strategic drift**: AI capex becomes less attractive if Zuckerberg pursues platform status, general-purpose productivity, permanent cloud services, or hardware for its own sake.
	- **The strongest variant view is that AI expands Meta's moat**: Rather than displacing social apps, AI could increase the value of human identity and relationships while making every content surface more monetizable.
	- **The bear case is delayed monetization**: Large compute and talent commitments could precede revenue by years, while inference costs, inventory dilution, and non-core projects suppress free cash flow.

- ## What to Monitor
	- Whether Zuckerberg's August 2026 commentary frames AI primarily around ads and consumer experiences or around superintelligence and productivity.
	- Growth in ad impressions, price per ad, conversion, return on ad spend, and advertiser adoption of generative creative.
	- Evidence that AI expands inventory without reducing user experience or overwhelming demand.
	- AI capex and depreciation relative to incremental ad revenue and operating income.
	- Any actual Meta program to rent surplus compute, its contract duration, clawback rights, customers, and realized pricing.
	- Reliance on third-party models and how quickly internal models replace external services in production.
	- Reality Labs spending and whether products are repositioned around practical AI access rather than immersive VR.
	- User engagement with AI-generated versus human-created content across Facebook, Instagram, Reels, and WhatsApp.
	- Whether Meta's social graph and identity signals become more valuable as synthetic content increases.
	- Management's willingness to stop projects whose internal returns do not exceed an external compute-market hurdle.
