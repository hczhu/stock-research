- **Source**: Samuel K. Moore, "The Next 15 Years of Moore's Law, According to Imec," *IEEE Spectrum*, May 19 2026 (July 2026 print issue), plus the printed roadmap chart from imec's ITF forum, OCR'd from a photograph of the print edition. Comments from imec CTO Paul Heremans. **Imec is a de-risking body, not a manufacturer** — Heremans describes the job as exploring cost/benefit to narrow the field for chipmakers, years before anything reaches production. Read the dates as research-program targets, not commitments.

- **Thesis**: The roadmap's real message is what is *absent* from the next seven years. **CFET does not arrive until ~2033 and the channel-material change waits until ~2041**, so every density and efficiency gain available across the entire current AI investment cycle has to come from packaging, 3D integration and architecture rather than from devices. The chart adds the sharper point the article text leaves out: **imec's own node cadence has stretched to roughly 2.5 years**, and the per-node shrink is being made larger to compensate — fewer, harder steps rather than the historical rhythm.

- ## The roadmap as a timeline

	- | Node | Year | Logic cell height | Shrink vs prior | Device architecture |
	  |---|---|---|---|---|
	  | **N2** | 2026 | ~132 nm | — | Nanosheet |
	  | **A14** | 2028 | ~115 nm | −13% | Nanosheet |
	  | **A10** | 2031 | ~98 nm | −15% | Nanosheet |
	  | **A7** | 2033 | ~80 nm | −18% | Nanosheet → **CFET transition begins** |
	  | **A5** | 2036 | ~64 nm | −20% | Sequential CFET |
	  | **A3** | 2038 | ~50 nm | −22% | "Bonded CFET?" |
	  | **A2** | 2041 | not shown | — | "Thin channel (2D?) CFET?" |

	- **The cadence is the finding.** Node years run 2026, 2028, 2031, 2033, 2036, 2038, 2041 — a 2-3-2-3-2-3 pattern averaging **~2.5 years per node through 2041**. Imec is publishing a roadmap in which Moore's Law is explicitly slower in time than the two-year rhythm the industry priced for decades.

	- **Per-node shrink accelerates to offset it**, from −13% at A14 to −22% at A3. Net, cell height falls **132 nm → 50 nm, a 62% reduction across twelve years, or about −7.8% annually** on cell height alone. Cell height is one dimension of two, so this is not an area figure — but it is the dimension imec chose to publish, and the deceleration in *steps per decade* is unambiguous.

	- **Two of the three CFET generations carry imec's own question marks.** Only "Sequential CFET" is unqualified; "Bonded CFET?" and "Thin channel (2D?) CFET?" are printed with interrogatives on imec's chart. The uncertainty is not a reader's inference — it is the roadmap's own notation, and it lands on exactly the two variants that matter most commercially.

	- **A tension worth carrying**: the article body says the likely path "builds both transistors at once, instead of one after another or building them on separate wafers," which describes a monolithic CFET — yet the chart labels the first CFET generation **Sequential**, meaning one after another. Either the text and chart disagree, or the near-term production path differs from the one imec is de-risking. Flagged rather than resolved; the chart was read from a photograph of the print page.

	- **EUV stays the exposure tool through the 2030s**, with 0.55NA carrying the load and no post-EUV patterning transition anywhere on the roadmap. That is the cleanest read-through in the piece: **[[$ASML]]'s platform is the assumed constant for fifteen years**, and High-NA is the upgrade path rather than a bridge to something else.

- ## What CFET is, and why the date matters more than the device

	- CFET stacks the PMOS and NMOS of a CMOS pair **on top of each other** instead of side by side — **as good as halving the area of some circuits**, per proponents. The monolithic path deposits alternating silicon and silicon-germanium layers, trenches them, then etches with a chemistry that destroys SiGe and spares Si, leaving suspended nanosheet ribbons: top set one polarity, bottom set the other.

	- **All three leading-edge manufacturers already have prototypes.** TSMC built a compact memory cell and a ring oscillator, reported at IEDM in December 2025. Samsung will detail the smallest CFET yet, with **six nanosheet layers**, at the VLSI Symposium in June 2026. Intel reports a variant at the same event.

	- **The integration method is unsettled** — Heremans: *"It is very clear that there are many versions still open."* Imec's own approach builds the top transistor's layers on a **separate wafer** and bonds them so only the Si/SiGe stack transfers, leaving an insulating layer that isolates top from bottom. The side benefit is the interesting part: today's wafers are cut on a crystal plane favoring NMOS conduction, so a separately-made PMOS wafer **can be cut on a plane favoring PMOS** — addressing a mobility mismatch accepted as a constant for decades. Intel is testing exactly this.

	- Imec expects CFET to follow the FinFET and nanosheet pattern — launch, then a density and performance push, then a final efficiency squeeze. **The 2033 date is the start of a multi-node arc, not the moment the benefit lands**, which the A5/A3/A2 rows make concrete.

- ## 2D semiconductors at ~2041 are a power story, not a density story

	- Around 2041 imec expects the silicon channel inside the CFET to give way to a 2D semiconductor such as molybdenum disulfide. Heremans frames the objective as **operations per watt**, noting that at advanced nodes a small voltage reduction has an outsized effect on power.

	- The physical argument is thickness: **2D channels are under 1 nm against roughly 3 nm for a future silicon nanosheet**, so the wrapping gate needs less voltage to control current through it. A further gain is available if the industry picks a 2D material with higher carrier velocity.

	- This is the same tokens-per-watt objective the repo tracks on the datacenter side, arriving at the device layer — **fifteen years out**, which is the point. Nothing in the channel-material path helps the current buildout.

- ## Hybrid bonding is where density actually comes from before 2033

	- **AMD's MI300 stacks compute tiles on a base die** built on an older process handling memory and communications, with vertical connections as close as **9 µm**.

	- Imec's most advanced wafer-to-wafer bonding in development reaches **about 200 nm pitch**, which Heremans puts at **over 25 million interconnects per square millimetre**. The arithmetic checks: 1 mm ÷ 200 nm = 5,000 per side, squared = 25M. Against MI300's 9 µm that is **45× finer linearly and roughly 2,000× denser by area.**

	- At that density the die boundary stops being an architectural constraint, which is what imec calls **CMOS 2.0**: not merely stacking chips made on different processes, but **fusing layers of transistors within one chip, each optimized for a different job** — memory density on one, drive current on another. Heremans: *"That gives you an enormous boost in what you can expect from this kind of fused chip."*

	- **This is the investable half of the roadmap.** A ~2,000× areal interconnect gain available through the 2020s dwarfs the one-time area halving CFET offers in 2033, and it accrues to bonding equipment and to whichever foundry industrializes it first rather than to transistor innovation.

- ## Read-throughs

	- **Backside power becomes a prerequisite, not a differentiator.** Intel has already moved power delivery beneath the transistor layer, and CFET's connection complexity may force **some data signals** down there too. That converts [[$INTC]]'s early backside-power work from a node-specific advantage into table stakes everyone must reach before CFET — one of the few places where Intel's roadmap choices are structurally ahead rather than behind.

	- **SRAM relief is seven years away at best.** [[2026-07-03-sram-renewed-life-ai-memory-architecture]] records CFET SRAM delivering up to **55% bitcell area scaling versus A14 nanosheet** and about **40% versus A10 forksheet**. Imec now dates the enabling device to A7/~2033 — and the table above dates A14 to 2028 and A10 to 2031, so those comparisons are against nodes that do not exist yet either. **The most promising answer to the SRAM scaling wall cannot ship inside this cycle**, which strengthens the case for cache chiplets, packaging and architecture as the near-term levers.

	- **Treat the dates as soft in both directions.** Fifteen-year semiconductor projections have a poor record, imec's role is to narrow options rather than commit to them, and the organization prints question marks on two of its own three CFET generations. What is firm is the *ordering* — packaging first, transistor structure second, channel material last — and the *cadence*, which has visibly stretched. The investment case should rest on those two, not on any single year.
