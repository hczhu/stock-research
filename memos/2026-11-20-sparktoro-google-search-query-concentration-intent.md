- ## How concentrated Google searches are, and what people search for (SparkToro and Datos)
	- **Source**: Danny Goodwin, "Surprising data: 15% of Google searches are driven by only 148 terms," Search Engine Land, December 3, 2024 (user-provided PDF), summarizing Rand Fishkin's SparkToro analysis of Datos clickstream data. Datos and Search Engine Land are both Semrush companies.
	- **Data**: about **332M queries** across more than **320,000 unique terms**, from about **130K US devices** (mobile and desktop) using Google continuously from **January 2023 to September 2024**. Only terms with 100+ searches over the 21 months are included. This is **pre-AI Mode data**, so it is a baseline for how search was used before chatbots took share.
	- **Thesis**: Google search demand is highly concentrated. **148 terms make up 15%** of searches and a few thousand make up a quarter, mostly **navigational** queries for big brands and Google's own products. Only about **15% of searches are commercial or transactional**, the queries that monetize best. Fishkin's read: "Monopolies are winning. Big brands and topics are winning."

- ## Concentration
	- **The head**: **148 terms = 15%** of all Google searches. A few thousand terms are about 25%, and Fishkin says the share "seems to be increasing all the time."
	- **The long tail**: terms with fewer than 11 searches a month are only **3.6% of demand**, despite the huge number of unique queries.
	- **Ten of the most-searched terms**: YouTube, Gmail, Amazon, Facebook, **ChatGPT**, Google Translate, WhatsApp Web, Google Maps, Pornhub and Google Docs. Five are Google's own products, and ChatGPT already ranked among them in 2023–24.

- ## What the author recommends
	- Organic search traffic goes to fewer websites over time, and visibility is harder against Google's AI Overviews and other search features.
	- Brands should be discoverable beyond Google (YouTube, TikTok, podcasts, LinkedIn, newsletters). On Google, they should focus on capturing existing demand rather than hoping to be discovered.
