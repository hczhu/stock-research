- ## NVIDIA invests \$3.5B in MediaTek convertibles and pulls it onto NVLink Fusion
	- **Source**: NVIDIA press release via GLOBE NEWSWIRE, Santa Clara, **August 31, 2026**. **First-party announcement** — no financial terms beyond the headline number, no volumes, no revenue figures, and the strategic framing is the company's own.
	- **Thesis**: The \$3.5B is the smaller half of the story. **NVLink Fusion is NVIDIA's answer to the custom-XPU threat: if hyperscalers are going to build their own accelerators regardless, make sure those accelerators plug into an NVIDIA rack.** Pulling MediaTek — one of the two merchant design houses that actually build those XPUs — onto that fabric is a move aimed less at MediaTek's customers than at **Broadcom**.
- ## What was announced
	- **NVIDIA invested \$3.5 billion in convertible bonds issued by MediaTek** — note the instrument: **convertibles, not equity.**
	- **MediaTek will adopt the NVIDIA NVLink Fusion platform**, described as giving hyperscalers, cloud service providers, and frontier model developers a **"prevalidated path"** to develop **custom XPUs** and bring them into NVLink-connected, rack-scale AI factories.
	- **Three collaboration areas**:
	  | Area | Content |
	  |---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
	  | **AI infrastructure** | MediaTek works with the NVLink Fusion ecosystem so customers can build custom AI infrastructure that integrates with NVIDIA rack-scale systems and AI factories |
	  | **Local AI computing** | Multiple generations of **RTX Spark and DGX Spark PC chips** for consumer PCs, AI developer supercomputers, and enterprise workstations — **NVIDIA GPUs integrated with MediaTek SoCs** |
	  | **Automotive** | Continued platforms for AI-powered, software-defined vehicles |
	- MediaTek's contributed capabilities are listed as **custom silicon, HPC, power-efficient SoC design, advanced packaging, interconnects and connectivity** — i.e. precisely the skill set required to build a competitor's accelerator.
- ## The strategic logic: co-opting the threat rather than fighting it
	- **The structural problem NVIDIA faces is documented in [[2026-08-28-aws-15gw-base-capacity-doubling-by-2027]]**: across OpenAI's and Anthropic's disclosed commitments, **NVIDIA holds roughly 34% of ~44.5 GW**, leaving about two-thirds committed to non-NVIDIA silicon. Custom XPUs are not a hypothetical threat; they are the majority of contracted capacity at the two largest buyers.
	- **NVLink Fusion concedes the chip and defends the rack.** The offer is: build whatever accelerator you want, but connect it through NVLink into an NVIDIA-architected rack-scale system. **That moves the moat from the die to the fabric** — which is where NVIDIA's advantage is most durable and least replicable.
	- **This is consistent with what practitioners say about NVLink's actual role.** Per [[2026-08-29-neil-movva-sail-token-factory-throughput-inference]], *"NVLink is mandatory for low-latency inference"* — sharding a matmul across GPUs is what NVLink is genuinely irreplaceable for. **Selling the interconnect to people building their own compute is selling them the part they cannot easily build.**
	- **The competing standard is the thing to watch.** [[2026-08-28-semi-doped-openai-jalapeno-hot-chips-teardown]] records that OpenAI's Jalapeño uses **ESUN**, the scale-up Ethernet effort Broadcom spearheaded — explicitly **not** NVLink or UALink. So the scale-up fabric is now a live standards contest, and this announcement is NVIDIA buying a major design house onto its side of it.
	- **The "prevalidated path" language is the commercial mechanism**: lower the engineering cost of going custom, but only along a route that terminates in an NVIDIA rack. Classic platform play — reduce the barrier to entering your ecosystem so customers never build the exit.
- ## Why MediaTek specifically — read this as aimed at Broadcom
	- MediaTek is one of the few merchant houses capable of designing frontier-class custom accelerators, and it is already inside the threat: [[2026-07-01-mediatek-meta-asic-second-customer-after-google-tpu]] records MediaTek taking **Meta's ASIC** after Google's TPU work.
	- **Broadcom is the incumbent in this role and the clear target.** On the commitment ledger, **Broadcom-linked programs account for ~13.5 GW across OpenAI and Anthropic — comparable to NVIDIA's own ~15 GW** — and Broadcom is the named partner on OpenAI's Jalapeño as well as the sponsor of ESUN.
	- **So the move has three simultaneous effects**: it gives MediaTek capital and a differentiated platform story against Broadcom; it puts NVIDIA's interconnect inside programs it would otherwise lose entirely; and it **keeps the custom-ASIC design market contested rather than consolidated** under one supplier.
	- **That last effect is the one most consistent with how NVIDIA is described as operating.** Per the Movva account, Jensen's approach is to cultivate a diverse ecosystem — *"it's great to have competition amongst his buyers"* — funding multiple counterparties so no single one accumulates leverage. **Funding the number-two ASIC house is the same policy applied to the supply side of the threat.**
- ## The instrument matters: convertibles, not equity
	- **\$3.5B in convertible bonds** rather than a straight equity stake gives NVIDIA **downside protection with equity upside**, avoids immediate dilution for MediaTek, and stops well short of control or consolidation.
	- **Context on scale**: [[2026-08-24-wsj-usvyatsky-big-tech-off-balance-sheet-commitments]] records NVIDIA committing **\$27B of equity investments** between late April and its January 2027 fiscal year end. **This deal is roughly 13% of that program** — which reframes it as one line item in a systematic capital-deployment strategy rather than a one-off.
	- **How to account for it, per the framework already on file**: [[2026-08-21-ben-thompson-ilb-risk-relocation-commodity-logic]] argues NVIDIA's investments and backstops function as **an off-margin price cut** — the counterparty gets cheaper capital because NVIDIA absorbs risk, and the expected cost of that risk never appears in gross margin. **The same treatment applies here.** A convertible is a cleaner case than a backstop, since the downside is capped and the instrument is a real asset, but the strategic subsidy is the same in kind.
	- **The second reading from that memo also fits**: NVIDIA is *"buying time before the CUDA moat is gone completely and supply chains stabilize for would-be challengers."* Locking a major design house into NVLink is precisely a time-buying move against supply-chain maturation.
- ## The Spark line — the quieter but concrete part
	- **Multiple generations of RTX Spark and DGX Spark PC chips**, integrating NVIDIA GPUs with MediaTek SoCs, across consumer PCs, developer supercomputers, and enterprise workstations.
	- **This addresses a real product weakness.** The DGX Spark's specification recorded in [[2026-08-28-stratechery-mac-refresh-memory-pricing-and-jalapeno]] — **128 GB unified memory at only 273 GB/s** — was criticized precisely for bandwidth, against a Mac Studio at 512 GB and 1,200 GB/s. NVIDIA's gap in this segment is **SoC and memory-system integration**, not GPU capability, and MediaTek is a credible answer to exactly that.
	- **The unstated competitive target is x86.** An NVIDIA GPU paired with a MediaTek Arm SoC across consumer and workstation PCs is a platform play against **Intel and AMD** in a segment where NVIDIA has historically supplied only the discrete GPU. "Multiple generations" implies a roadmap commitment rather than a single product.
- ## What is not disclosed, and what to watch
	- **No terms on the convertible**: coupon, maturity, conversion price, conversion trigger, or resulting potential ownership. Without conversion terms the economic stake is unknowable, and **the size of NVIDIA's eventual position is the single most useful missing number.**
	- **No governance detail** — board representation, information rights, or any exclusivity provision. **Whether MediaTek is now restricted from ESUN or UALink work is the question that determines whether this is a partnership or a lock-up**, and the release is silent.
	- **No volumes, revenue, or timing.** "Multigenerational" and "continue to collaborate" are directional, not contractual.
	- **What would confirm the thesis**: a named hyperscaler taking a MediaTek-designed XPU to market **on NVLink Fusion** rather than on an Ethernet-based scale-up fabric. Absent that, this is an option NVIDIA has purchased, not a position it has won. **Watch also whether Broadcom responds with a comparable capital arrangement of its own** — the mechanism is now established, and the ASIC design houses have become assets worth financing rather than merely suppliers.
