- ## Sam Altman on Cheeky Pint: Agent Teams, Compute Cost per Gigawatt and Why Token Pricing Is Fake
	- **Source**: *Cheeky Pint* podcast, "Why Sam Altman is against a single superintelligence," John Collison (Stripe) with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, published Oct 29, 2026. The transcript was supplied by the user. All figures are Altman's or Collison's spoken claims, not disclosures.
	- **Thesis (Altman's view)**: how much compute can usefully go into a single problem keeps rising exponentially. Chain-of-thought made it possible, and teams of agents now push it further. The gap between typical AI users and the most productive ones keeps widening. Compute demand "only goes one way," and Altman says the market is failing to add supply. Cost per gigawatt should fall from about \$70B to \$20B (five-year TCO), which requires compressing the gross margins stacked along the supply chain. Altman prefers "a trillion AIs of slightly superhuman intelligence" to "one AI of 1 trillion IQ."

- ## Cyber: PHP Week and the Limits of Sandboxing
	- **PHP vulnerabilities**: models uncovered critical PHP vulnerabilities ("PHP week"). Asked why it took so long to find them, Altman says the models keep improving fast and people "just hadn't really used them to go look at everything out there." Altman expects "many more serious vulnerabilities than we expect." On defense, Altman says "the first day... went much better than I expected." Companies used the models to build patches, and some took their applications offline for an hour to scan and redeploy.
	- **Re-architecture**: Altman expects "a completely new sort of model of how we make systems and networks." New startups plan whole new stacks for routers, operating systems and applications, which "would have seemed insane two years ago." Collison asked whether this means more consulting work for firms like Infosys; Altman didn't answer directly.
	- **Threat models**: cyber experts "no longer are confident that we have the right assumptions baked into our threat model," for example if AI finds deep, novel hardware bugs.
	- **Sandboxing**: in Altman's ordering, sandboxing works to a point and monitoring (chain-of-thought and activation monitoring) works further. Beyond that you need high confidence in alignment. Altman doubts a sandbox can hold a model "a million times smarter than the smartest security researcher." The near-term priority is "a better security model."
	- **Iterative deployment**: Altman says "building up a capability overhang is a thing to fight against." Models must be made safe first, but should get into people's hands.
- ## Agent Teams and Test-Time Compute
	- **Compute per problem**: "a few years ago... it was impossible to throw more than a dollar of compute at a problem and have it help you at all." Chain-of-thought changed that. "Now, you can throw a whole team at something. And you can do that 10,000 times in parallel." The most advanced math and hacking work already uses agent teams; ordinary corporate use does not.
	- **Budgeted agents**: an agent told to "do the best job you can with \$10,000 of compute" understands the instruction.
	- **Scaling and safety**: Altman suspects teams of agents "have very interesting scaling law properties, but also variance in alignment properties." Altman likes agent teams on safety grounds, because they resemble society's resilience through checks and pluralism.
	- **AI-driven AI research**: today you can say "Find me a 5% compute efficiency win." Nobody can yet say "go discover a new architecture" as big as the transformer. Altman thinks applying OpenAI's math pipeline to AI research "will probably work." Any lack of progress, Altman says, will come from deciding to limit how smart single models get, not from AI being unable to do research. Altman expects to "learn a lot about [agent use] in the next three months."
- ## Math: Navier-Stokes and the Cost of Discovery
	- **Cost**: the Navier-Stokes proof cost "order \$10 million... maybe a little more" of compute. OpenAI "did let it get out of control for one week on the math," intentionally. The average proof after that cost "way less." Total math work ran to "100s of millions of dollars," including unpublished discoveries.
	- **Cost decline**: "I bet we could redo Navier-Stokes at 1/100th of the cost a year from when it was done," through smarter models and better approaches. In every new paradigm, Altman expects OpenAI to find "our 10x improvements per year" in turning compute into useful results.
	- **Unpublished results**: OpenAI has more than the ~700 published proofs. Altman is working through how to release discoveries while respecting scientists' careers, after the backlash from mathematicians.
	- **"Hold My Beer" weekend**: when a new model arrived, staff threw their hardest academic research problems at it in a channel named "Model Name-Hold My Beer," and it was "knocking down one after the other" across many fields.
	- **Internal compute allocation**: OpenAI allocates "a lot of compute to internal productivity" and gives people wide latitude. It has "no token leaderboards," and being high on one is "slightly bad" for promotion. During an outage of OpenAI's Codex coding agent, staff had a "what if you couldn't [code]?" moment.
- ## Compute Supply: "The Invisible Hand Is Failing"
	- **Demand**: "The demand for sufficiently capable models at a sufficiently low price, I think that trend only goes one way." Altman expects swings between exuberance, bust, constraint and glut.
	- **OpenAI's supply moves**:
		- A chip with "incredible performance at... units of intelligence per watt." Altman floated "If OpenAI sells our chip" as one way supply could respond.
		- Learning to build its own data centers.
		- A robotics program aimed at "making lots of data centers," which Altman calls "self-replicating data centers": AI controlling robots that run fabs, build turbines or solar panels, and build data centers. "We have that within grasp."
	- **Memory**: asked how the supply response to high prices is going, Altman said "Bad." For a long time, DRAM makers would "invest \$20 billion of CapEx to make \$19 billion"; now they are "each going to make like \$100 billion of free cash flow in a year." Their history of near-bankruptcy from overbuilding keeps their capex conservative.
	- **Local inference uses scarce DRAM badly**: memory sitting in personal computers is used "5% of the time," while the cloud can run at maximum utilization. Altman says this is the opposite of many people's intuition.
	- **Where supply is responding**: turbines, batteries, concrete, steel, and components for industrial robots (not "the humanoid fake demos").
	- **Chip design with AI**: lithography is limited to "one company on Earth," but producing a design ready for tape-out "is a great problem for AI." OpenAI also uses AI internally for research, software engineering and procurement.
- ## Revenue and Cost per Gigawatt
	- **Five-year TCO**: Altman picks **~\$70B per GW** as today's cost to build a gigawatt and run it for five years. Altman would be "much happier" at **\$20B**, while still demanding 10x better performance from smarter models each year. Altman can't imagine \$2B.
	- **What it takes**: reaching \$20B means "energy's got to get cheaper," and "all of the gross margin stacking" between "melting sand" and getting the token back "has got to get compressed." "If you assumed all materials were free and no one made a profit, maybe you could get there in the current energy envelope. But only just."
	- **Dollars per GW**: Altman says the price per GW of leading-edge AI "should go down." It could rise only if AI's value per watt grows so fast, amid shortage, that buyers pay more. Altman would read that as "compute not keeping up."
- ## Enterprise AI Spend
	- **Stripe's token spend**: Collison says tokens are "1%-ish order of magnitude" of Stripe's opex, heading to 5%, 10% and 20%. Altman: "That feels too low." Both expect tokens to reach tens of percent of opex for many companies. Altman says "there are more companies with the problem of not using enough AI rather than using too much AI."
	- **AI is not the waste to cut**: Altman compares it to meetings that cost a fortune and add no value, at companies that police \$17 of snack spending. AI use "is not a company's biggest spend problem," Altman says, acknowledging a bias.
- ## Personal Agents and Agentic Commerce
	- **No "Macintosh moment" yet**: Dots, Muse and Grokbot are liked, but not yet "I cannot imagine my life without this thing," the way people talk about coding agents or ChatGPT. Altman says the category is "a small number of ideas away from a super mainstream thing." The winner will look much like today's agents but "will just work," reliably and on more complex tasks. The ingredients are "intelligence, context, tools."
	- **Computer use**: Altman expects the world to converge on "You're allowed to tell an AI to use your computer on your behalf," though Altman's confidence is low.
	- **Brand beats UI**: Collison argues that businesses wrongly treat their HTML and mobile apps as a moat, when their defensibility comes from brand and selection. Altman agrees, while admitting a bias: when ChatGPT shopping offers a choice, "10 out of 10 times I click Amazon. Pay a higher price." That preference has "nothing to do with my experience of clicking around amazon.com." Altman thinks Amazon is "in a great place if commerce moves much more agentic," and concedes OpenAI hasn't yet offered partners a good enough platform inside ChatGPT. Altman compares it to the App Store, which large companies joined reluctantly for fear of handing customer relationships to Apple, and which was "great for mobile."
	- **Web forms are "fake work"**: Collison says filling any web form is below a human's pay grade, because the information already exists. Altman: "I would be so happy to never do fake work at a computer again."
- ## Pricing: Tokens Are a Fake Unit
	- **Outcome pricing rejected**: OpenAI "almost made a very... bad call" to stop pricing its next model in tokens and simply quote a price per prompt, "like AWS." Customer response was "so negative." Customers said they would rather pay more, even for inflated tokens, "because I know there's a real thing there."
	- **The unit problem**: Altman calls tokens "a kind of fake unit" and rejects inventing an "intelligence-equivalent token," comparing it to chip-node "nanometers," which are "totally fake." Choosing between "10-cent-per-million tokens out of a small model" and expensive tokens from a big model, and guessing chain-of-thought length, is "cognitive overhead that I don't think actually adds any value." Most providers include chain-of-thought tokens in what they charge.
- ## Models and Benchmarks
	- **Benchmaxxing**: the two things that matter are scientific discovery and being enjoyable to work with, and neither has great benchmarks. Altman believes OpenAI is better than other labs at not benchmaxxing "right now," though not always historically.
	- **Model personality**: the latest models are "much better than we've had in a long time," after a glazing era and an "ornery" anti-glazing era. The target is Emmett Shear's "really thoughtful and challenging, but still respectful, senior coworker."
