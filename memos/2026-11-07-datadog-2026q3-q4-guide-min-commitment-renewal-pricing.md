- ## Datadog Q3 2026: a strong quarter, a Q4 guide that is low by design
	- **Source**: FUNDA research note on Datadog's Q3 2026 results (excerpt: executive summary, results and the start of the guidance section). Growth rates and estimates are FUNDA's. The excerpt stops before listing the CFO's three factors in full, so the list below combines the summary with the section heading. Related: [[DDOG-2026-Q2]], [[DDOG-2026-Q1]], [[2026-09-16-repvue-observability-pricing-power]], [[2026-06-08-datadog-state-of-ai-engineering]].
	- **Thesis**: Q3 revenue grew **35.2% YoY**, about 6 points above the 28–29% guide and close to what the buy side expected. The issue is Q4: the **25–26%** guide is in line with consensus (~26%) but well below the buy-side high end (30–31%). FUNDA estimates Q4 will actually come in near **34%**. The gap comes from how the guide is built, not from weaker demand. The largest customer is guided only at its **minimum commitment**, although it spent well above that in Q3, and several large customers **renewed early at lower prices**. Non-AI growth is still accelerating. The cost is that quarterly revenue has become harder to forecast, and the stock trades on the guide.
- ## Q3 numbers
	- | Metric | Q3'26 | Comment |

	  | Revenue growth | +35.2% YoY | ~6pt above the guided rate; FUNDA's preview had 36% |
	  | Beat vs prior guide midpoint | \$57.1M | Full-year guide midpoint raised \$60M, so the implied Q4 guide is almost unchanged since August |
	  | Non-AI growth | High-20s, up ~1pt | Fifth straight quarter of acceleration per FUNDA |
	  | Infrastructure monitoring (~half of ARR) | Mid-30s growth | The core product is growing as fast as the company |

	- **Beat passed through, not added to**: raising the full-year midpoint by about the Q3 beat means management made no change to its Q4 assumptions. After a quarter 6 points above guide, that is a deliberate choice to stay conservative.
- ## Why Q4 is guided at 25–26%
	- **Largest customer at its minimum commitment**: this is the AI lab whose usage reduction was fully removed from guidance in [[DDOG-2026-Q2]]. It spent well above its minimum in Q3, but the guide assumes it falls back to the floor. Any spending above the floor is upside, and that is most of the gap to FUNDA's ~34%.
	- **Early renewals at lower prices**: several large customers renewed ahead of schedule at lower unit prices. Normally this trades price for a longer commitment and more volume. Revenue dips first, and the volume shows up later, if it does.
	- **Seasonality**: the third factor in the CFO's sequential bridge, per the section heading. The excerpt gives no detail.
	- FUNDA's view is that none of the three signals weaker demand by itself. Billings, RPO and non-AI acceleration support that.
- ## What it means for the stock
	- **The guide no longer tracks the business closely**: with the largest account floored at its commitment, every quarter mixes a conservative base with uncertain upside from one AI customer. Beats of 5–6 points become the norm, but the guide always looks like a sharp slowdown. Investors who anchor on the guide will keep seeing deceleration that doesn't happen.
	- **Renewal pricing is the more important signal**: price cuts at early renewals fit RepVue's evidence of buyers pushing back on observability costs ([[2026-09-16-repvue-observability-pricing-power]]). If they are volume-for-price trades, they are healthy. If competitors (Grafana, open-source OpenTelemetry stacks, cloud-native tools) are forcing them, they are an early sign of pricing pressure on Datadog's largest accounts. The excerpt doesn't say which.
	- **Concentration is the real risk**: Datadog's fastest growth and its biggest single risk are the same AI-native customers. Non-AI growth in the high 20s is the floor investors can count on. The AI lab's usage above its commitment is the upside that sits outside the guide.
	- **What to check next quarter**: whether Q4 lands near FUNDA's ~34% or near the 25–26% guide, whether the largest customer's spend stays above its minimum, and whether net retention holds up after the lower-priced renewals.
