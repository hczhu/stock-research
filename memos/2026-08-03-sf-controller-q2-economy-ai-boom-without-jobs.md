- ## SF Controller Q2 2026: The AI Boom Shows Up in Rents, Not in Jobs
	- **Source**: **"Status of the San Francisco Economy: 2026 Second Quarter,"** City and County of San Francisco, **Office of the Controller, Office of Economic Analysis, Aug 3 2026.** First-party municipal data compiled from EDD, JLL, Kastle, Indeed, Placer.ai, STR, ApartmentList and Zillow. Published quarterly — a recurring, free, primary-source series worth tracking rather than a one-off.
	- **Thesis**: The Controller's own framing is that the city is "entering an accelerating period of economic growth, **primarily driven by the AI investment boom.**" But the boom is landing in **asset prices and physical congestion, not in employment** — and their own summary concedes it: "although AI is leading the office recovery, **overall tech employment is only modestly growing**," with the year's job gains in health care and tourism. A capital inflow large enough to move rents double-digit while adding almost no tech headcount is the cleanest real-economy corroboration of the labor-substitution thesis in [[2026-04-09-ai-labor-substitution-elasticity-demand-argument]] and [[2026-06-01-labor-market-impacts-of-china-shock-vs-ai-shock]].

- ## The three tensions worth carrying
	- **Leasing without attendance.** Vacancy fell 3.7 points while office attendance sat near **45%** — the lowest in the metro comparison. **AI companies are absorbing space they are not filling with commuters.** For office real estate that is a better signal than attendance data implies: absorption is driven by headcount plans and prestige footprints, not desk utilization. It also means an attendance-led recovery thesis and a leasing-led one are different trades.
	- **Capital without payroll.** Rents +14% in four months and double-digit home-price growth *while the state declines* is a large, local, income-driven shock. Yet Information employment is roughly flat and total metro employment is still ~62k below February 2020. **The money is arriving as compensation to a small headcount and as capex, not as broad hiring.** Job postings at 68 versus a 2020 baseline of 100 say the same thing from the demand side.
	- **A bifurcated recovery.** SF leads on AI-linked asset prices and trails every peer metro on normal-economy measures — last on office attendance, last on job postings, last on hotel RevPAR. The city is not recovering; **one sector is booming inside a city that has not.**
- ## What this is useful for
	- **A free, quarterly, primary-source read on whether AI capex converts to employment**, in the one municipality where the effect should be most visible. The next release is the test: if the AI boom is real and broad, Information employment and job postings should start moving; if it stays a capex-and-compensation story, rents keep rising while the payroll lines do not.
