- ## Google’s AI Search shift through advertisers’ eyes
	- **Thesis**: Some longtime Google advertisers are cutting or reallocating Search budgets as familiar placements become less visible. Others are testing alternatives while staying on Google. Alphabet says AI is driving more commercial searches, but acknowledges lower monetization in AI Mode.
	- **Source**: Kate Conger, [The New York Times, November 19, 2026](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/19/technology/google-search-ads-ai.html). Advertiser accounts are from the supplied article; company context comes from Alphabet’s Q3 call and Google Ads documentation.

- ## Firsthand accounts: placement, cost, and budget moves
	- | Advertiser or marketer | What they observed | How they responded |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | **Marshall Evans**, small-business marketer | AI answers were taking up prominent space, often pushing traditional ads farther down the page. | Cut one trophy seller’s monthly Google Search allocation from **\$2,000 to \$500** over roughly a year, a **75% reduction**. Over the prior six months he shifted ad dollars toward Tubi, Bing, Reddit, and Nextdoor. |
	  | **Max Anderson**, OnlyFinder CEO; buying Search ads for the business since 2022 | His ads sometimes appeared below an AI Overview or did not appear at all. He estimated his costs **roughly tripled or quadrupled over three years**; the article does not specify a cost metric, such as CPC or cost per acquisition. | Now spends **about half** what he spent on Google ads in the early days of OnlyFinder. He plans to keep using Google but has not found a predictable way to get the business mentioned in AI Overviews. He did not disclose dollar amounts. |
	  | **Valerie Schlosser**, VML marketing executive | Observed users spending more time in chatbot conversations and less time browsing websites. | Clients are interested in testing ChatGPT and Perplexity, but she said they are not pulling out of Google Search. She did not quantify a budget shift. |
	- Some advertisers told the Times that users kept chatting rather than clicking through to their websites, even when an ad appeared. These are individual accounts, not a measured change in click-through or conversion rates across Google.

- ## What buyers can and cannot control
	- **Eligibility is not placement.** Google says eligible Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns can enter the standard ad auction for AI placements, but buyers cannot target AI Overviews or AI Mode exclusively. A paid ad in an AI surface is separate from an organic mention in the generated answer; buying ads does not influence that answer.
	- **Higher costs are what buyers reported, not a separate AI surcharge.** The article says some advertisers found the additional features used to reach AI Mode more expensive. Google says those placements use the usual auction and billing models, with no extra charge to participate. [Google Ads Help: ads in AI-powered Search](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/18826097)

- ## Alphabet: query growth and Search monetization diverged
	- **Q3 2026 Search & other advertising revenue rose 12% year over year to \$63.3B**, down from **17% growth in Q2** and below the **15% Wall Street expectation** cited by the Times. It represented about **49.6%** of Alphabet’s \$127.6B in consolidated revenue; the Times identified the quarter as the first below half.
	- Google said query growth was the fastest in five years and included growth in commercial searches. Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler said **lower AI Mode monetization** contributed to the Search slowdown, but named **foreign exchange as the largest driver** and also cited retail and media-specific effects. Google is expanding ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode, including multi-turn ads in the U.S. [Alphabet Q3 earnings call](https://abc.xyz/investor/events/event-details/2026/2026-Q3-Earnings-Call-2026-Ujou16CTw9/default.aspx) · [[GOOG-2026-Q3]]
