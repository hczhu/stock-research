- ## SpaceX Acquisition Deal Structure

	- **Acquisition price**: \$60B
	- **Termination fee**: \$1.5B cash paid by SpaceX if either party walks; plus \$8.5B in free computing power — so Cursor's downside floor is effectively \$10B in value even if the deal falls apart
	- **Computing resource**: access to Colossus supercomputer (hundreds of thousands of top-of-the-line Nvidia AI chips)
	- **Strategic rationale for SpaceX/xAI**: Grok described by an xAI contractor as not the "best at coding"; SpaceX needs a leg up in AI coding. Musk has since posted that Grok improved significantly after training on "a lot" of Cursor data.
	- **Cursor's upside**: removes dependence on Anthropic; access to massive compute to train Composer models independently

- ## Anthropic–Cursor Relationship (and Breakdown)

	- At peak, Cursor accounted for **~40–50% of Anthropic's revenue** — a level of concentration that created mutual dependency and fragility.
	- Anthropic privately told Cursor leadership before Claude Code launched that it was "more of a research effort than a major commercial push." Claude Code quickly became a direct competitor.
	- By Feb 2026, Claude Code run-rate (\$2.5B) had **surpassed Cursor's** (~\$2B) — developers began publicly posting that they were canceling Cursor for Claude Code.
	- Windsurf precedent: Anthropic cut off a rival coding startup (Windsurf) during its acquisition talks with OpenAI — this raised the stakes for Cursor's exposure to a single model provider.
	- Jan 5, 2026 emergency all-hands: Truell told the team Claude Code was an existential threat, canceled unnecessary meetings, and directed teams to build Cursor's own model.

- ## Composer — Cursor's Own Model

	- Built on open-source base from **Moonshot** (Chinese AI lab), heavily modified.
	- **Composer 2.5** (May 2026): described as ">85% Cursor's own work" — the Moonshot base is now a small slice of the final product.
	- Competitive on **price and speed**, which matters as AI compute costs hit engineering budgets across tech.
	- Early traction with developers; Cursor says feedback has been "insanely positive."

- ## Michael Truell — Founder Profile

	- Age 25 (born ~2001); raised in NYC; parents are journalists.
	- **Horace Mann prep school**: built Halite (coding game teaching programming via territory conquest on a grid) at age 15 — attracted thousands of users, won \$10K from a top math association.
	- **MIT**: double major in CS and mathematics.
	- Described as quiet, humble, monk-like; didn't pay himself for the first few years of Cursor's existence.
	- Deeply involved in hiring: personally trawls GitHub and X to identify engineers; candidates undergo unpaid multi-day (sometimes month-long) work trials at HQ before hire/no-hire decisions.
	- Investor Ali Partovi (wrote one of Cursor's first checks, co-founder of Code.org): "He has a level of ambition, self-confidence, and drive that pushes him toward staying independent."

- ## Investment / Strategic Implications

	- **\$60B for a \$4B ARR business = 15× ARR** — a rich but defensible multiple given growth rate (doubling in ~3 months) and Fortune 500 penetration.
	- The deal structure (with \$10B downside protection) is unusual — signals SpaceX/xAI is willing to pay significantly just for the data, technology transfer, and competitive advantage even if the full acquisition closes.
	- Cursor's Composer strategy (own model on Moonshot base) parallels what Apple did with Silicon: reduce dependency on a critical supplier by internalizing the capability.
	- The Anthropic revenue concentration story (40–50% of Anthropic revenue from one customer) is a reminder that AI infrastructure providers can be dangerously exposed to single distribution partners even at multi-billion-dollar scale.
	- SpaceX's S-1 filing (referenced in article) — first public signal SpaceX is moving toward a public market event.
