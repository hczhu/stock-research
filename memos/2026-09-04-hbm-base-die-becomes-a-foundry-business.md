- ## The HBM Base Die Becomes a Foundry Business
	- **Source**: Chinese-language industry analysis, "HBM，生变！" (Sep 2026), citing Korean media, DIGITIMES, TrendForce, SK hynix's own site, TSMC Technology Symposium disclosures, and Nvidia's Aug 26 NVHBM announcement
	- **Thesis**: The competitive question in HBM is moving off the DRAM stack. From HBM4 onward the bottom die is a **logic** die on a leading-edge foundry node, and the logic inside it — memory controller, DFT, and increasingly compute — is where the differentiation and the margin now sit. That converts a share of every HBM stack from a memory business into a **foundry business**, and turns the three-way DRAM race into a contest between *coalitions*: memory maker + foundry + AI chip designer + advanced packaging. This is the physical mechanism behind the de-commoditization argued in [[2026-07-04-dram-commodity-vs-decommoditization-synthesis]] and [[2026-06-05-memory-de-commoditization-permanent-plateau-geninnov]].
- ## Why HBM4 forces the base die off a DRAM node
	- Through HBM3E the base die did three simple jobs — mechanical support, TSV routing, and basic signal routing — which the memory makers handled on their own 10nm-class DRAM processes. Two changes break that.
	- **Interface density doubled.** HBM4 widens the bus from **1,024-bit to 2,048-bit**, and Nvidia pushes per-pin speed past **11 Gbps** on Rubin. Fitting twice the TSVs and PHY circuitry into the same tiny die area exceeds what DRAM-node wiring density and metal-layer counts can deliver.
	- **Logic moved downward.** To chase efficiency and latency, GPU and ASIC vendors now require the memory controller, DFT circuitry, and in some cases algorithm-specific compute blocks to sit in the base die itself.
	- The two manufacturing disciplines do not overlap. DRAM process technology optimizes cell density and cost per bit; complex logic needs transistor performance, wiring density, and advanced EDA flows. **Memory fabs simply do not run 12nm, 5nm or finer logic lines.**
	- SK hynix says it plainly on its own site: in HBM4 the logic die's role grows, and development is aimed at raising base-die performance, strengthening the connection between the HBM stack and the logic die, and lowering power.
	- The bill of materials changes accordingly — from **DRAM manufacturing + TSV + stack packaging** to **DRAM core die + logic base die + foundry + TSV + advanced packaging**. An HBM stack is becoming a small chiplet system rather than a special kind of memory.
	- The 2024 SK hynix–TSMC HBM4 agreement already described this as a tripartite model of IC design house, foundry, and memory maker. Its first deliverable was the base die.
- ## NVHBM: Nvidia moves its memory controller into someone else's die
	- Announced Aug 26. In a conventional accelerator the memory controller occupies expensive leading-edge area inside the GPU or XPU. NVHBM lifts it out of the compute die and places it in the HBM base die.
	- | NVHBM vs standard HBM4E | Nvidia's claim |
	  |---|---|
	  | Memory bandwidth | **up to +30%** |
	  | Power | **up to −15%** |
	  | XPU compute-die area freed | **up to 25%** |
	- The area figure is the commercially important one. Freeing a quarter of the most expensive silicon in the system, by relocating a function into a die someone else pays to fabricate, is a direct transfer of cost and a direct gain in usable compute per reticle.
	- **Amazon's Annapurna Labs is among the first design teams working on the architecture** — so the co-design model is already extending past Nvidia to the hyperscaler ASIC programs.
	- The boundary between GPU and HBM is dissolving. Memory controller is the first function to migrate; address mapping, cache management, data-movement logic and targeted compute are the plausible next ones. Consistent with the base-die roadmap Samsung laid out at Hot Chips, captured in [[2026-08-24-dram-feed-nand-price-vs-volume-hbm4-yield-convergence]].
- ## Three memory makers, three foundry strategies
	- | | Base die strategy | HBM4 | HBM4E | Roadmap |
	  |---|---|---|---|---|
	  | **SK hynix** | Specialize and outsource | TSMC **12nm-class** | Migrating to a finer node; **evaluating Intel Foundry as second source** | Custom HBM via TSMC advanced logic; iHBM thermal solution |
	  | **Samsung** | Full vertical integration | 1c DRAM core + **own 4nm foundry** base die | Same 1c + 4nm pairing | Base die to **2nm** in later products |
	  | **Micron** | Reversed course to outsourcing | In-house CMOS logic base die | **TSMC for both standard and custom** logic base dies | Expects custom base logic die HBM4E to carry **higher gross margin than standard** |
	- **Micron's margin statement is the most revealing line in the source.** A memory maker is saying, in investor materials, that the incremental value in HBM4E lies in the customized logic rather than the DRAM. That is the de-commoditization thesis conceded by one of its subjects.
	- **SK hynix is staffing like an ASIC company, not a memory company.** Its new Silicon Valley HBM architecture team posts roles requiring digital design, power delivery network (PDN) experience, and **sub-3nm foundry process** background — capabilities no DRAM maker previously needed.
	- Samsung's long-criticized structure inverts here. Running memory, foundry and advanced packaging under one roof was treated as conglomerate sprawl; in a custom HBM regime it means Samsung can quote an entire stack — DRAM, logic, packaging — without a partner, and without sharing margin.
- ## The business model shifts from JEDEC catalog to ASIC engagement
	- The historical memory model was standardization: build to a JEDEC spec, compete on process, capacity, yield and cost, sell the same part to everyone. Scale drove cost down and standardization drove scale.
	- AI buyers break that. Nvidia, AMD, Google, Amazon, Microsoft and the growing set of in-house ASIC programs each want different bandwidth, power, interface and package-area tradeoffs. One standard part cannot satisfy all of them.
	- So HBM is converging on the ASIC commercial sequence: **co-define the spec → co-design → foundry tapeout → advanced packaging → customer qualification.** Every step of that sequence except the first is billable by someone other than the memory maker.
	- The competitive frame changes with it. It is no longer SK hynix vs Samsung vs Micron. It is coalition vs coalition, and the memory maker is only one member.
- ## Where the value lands, company by company
	- **[[$TSM]] — the largest and most direct beneficiary.** It already owns the two richest sockets in the AI server chain: GPU/ASIC logic foundry and CoWoS advanced packaging. The logic base die is a **third socket inside the same package**, and TSMC is currently incumbent at SK hynix (12nm-class HBM4) and now Micron (HBM4E, standard and custom). The die-count math is the point: a high-end accelerator carries **6 to 8 HBM stacks**, so one package that used to represent a single leading-edge logic die now represents **seven to nine** of them. Wafer *area* remains far smaller than the GPU — the base die is not a reticle-sized part — but the count, the node migration toward 5nm and finer, and the growing die size all compound TSMC's share of the value in an AI memory system.
	- **[[$005930.KS]] Samsung — the structural re-rating candidate, and the cleanest expression of the thesis.** It is the only company that can build a custom HBM end to end: 1c DRAM, 4nm logic base die on its own foundry with a 2nm roadmap, and its own advanced packaging. Two second-order effects matter as much as the first. Captive HBM4 base-die demand **fills SF4 capacity**, which is precisely the utilization problem that has kept Samsung Foundry lossmaking — and it is why Samsung was able to raise 4nm/5nm prices up to 15%. And in a world where the customer wants to co-design, single-vendor accountability is a genuine sales advantage over a two-company handoff.
	- **[[$INTC]] Intel — the highest-optionality name here.** Winning a Nvidia or AMD compute die is close to unwinnable near term. The base die is a materially lower bar: it needs credible advanced logic manufacturing, but it does not carry whole-system compute risk, the volumes are large, and **the customer is actively motivated** — SK hynix wants to break TSMC concentration and cap rising base-die cost. This is the most realistic incremental foundry market available to Intel, and it is worth more to the equity than its size suggests because it would be Intel Foundry's first external leading-edge design win of consequence.
	- **[[$NVDA]] — captures value without manufacturing any of it.** NVHBM converts up to 25% of Nvidia's own most expensive silicon area into usable compute by relocating a function into a die its suppliers fabricate, while extending Nvidia's spec authority down into the memory stack. It also deepens memory-vendor dependence on Nvidia's architecture rather than JEDEC's.
	- **[[$MU]] Micron — benefits, with the margin mix shifting toward logic it does not own.** Outsourcing HBM4E base dies to TSMC buys access to nodes it cannot run, and management expects higher gross margin on custom versions. The tradeoff is that the differentiated content increasingly sits in a die a foundry makes.
	- **[[$000660.KS]] SK hynix — retains the HBM lead but acquires a dependency it did not have.** It keeps the DRAM core die and stacking expertise where its moat actually lives (see [[2026-08-21-sk-hynix-hbm-history-mr-muf-moat]]), but must now share stack economics with a foundry and build ASIC-grade design capability from scratch. Adding Intel as a second source is a margin and resilience move, not a technology one.
	- **[[$AMZN]] and the hyperscaler ASIC programs — quiet winners.** Annapurna's early NVHBM involvement means Trainium-class silicon gets memory-controller-level customization without owning a fab or a DRAM line. The same logic extends to [[$AVGO]] and [[$GOOGL]] custom accelerator programs, which now have a memory-side lever they previously did not.
	- **Who this hurts: China's memory entrants.** CXMT's commodity-DRAM breakout — 7–10% of Q2 DRAM revenue, per the conflicting Counterpoint prints noted in [[2026-09-04-dram-feed-cxmt-share-break-and-lta-floor-arithmetic]] — does not translate upward. From HBM4 the binding constraint stops being DRAM process and becomes **access to leading-edge logic foundry capacity and EDA**, which is exactly where export controls bite hardest. The catch-up gap in commodity DRAM narrowed to roughly a node; the gap in custom HBM is now a foundry gap on top of a DRAM gap. Extends [[2026-06-05-cxmt-korea-china-hbm-gap-narrows-three-years]].
	- The qualification to hold on all of this: base-die wafer volume is small relative to GPU wafer volume, so for TSMC this is a margin-mix and package-attach story rather than a capacity story. And custom base dies work against multi-sourcing — a stack designed around one vendor's protocol is harder to second-source, which is a commercial obstacle rather than an engineering one, and the reason phase-one integration is likelier to ship than the more aggressive in-base-die compute concepts.
