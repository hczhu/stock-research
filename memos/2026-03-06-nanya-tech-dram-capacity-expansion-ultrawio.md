- ## Capacity Expansion Insights
	- **The new fab is a long-cycle supply response, not near-term relief**: equipment installation begins only in early 2027 and mass production is planned for H2 2027, so the expansion does not materially address 2026 supply conditions.
	- **Peak capacity matters, but ramp timing is the missing variable**: 45,000 wafers per month is a clear headline capacity target, but the article does not disclose current Nanya wafer capacity, the phase-by-phase ramp curve, or expected yield progression on 1B/1C/1D.
	- **Capex discipline is still explicit**: a 2026 capex ceiling of NTD 52B suggests management is staging the buildout rather than committing unlimited spending upfront, despite expecting tight DRAM supply through 2028.
	- **Process migration is central to the expansion**: the new fab is tied to Nanya's self-developed advanced 10nm-class roadmap, including 1B, 1C, and 1D nodes. This means the expansion is not simply adding legacy capacity; it is also an attempt to close the technology gap versus leading DRAM suppliers.
	- **The plan potentially changes Nanya's role in the DRAM cycle**: Nanya has historically been more exposed to commodity/legacy DRAM. The new fab plus UltraWIO points to a strategy of moving toward advanced and customized memory, where product differentiation and customer co-development matter more.

- ## UltraWIO / Custom AI Memory Insights
	- **UltraWIO is positioned as custom AI memory, not standard DRAM**: the article explicitly contrasts UltraWIO with JEDEC-standard DRAM, describing it as a customized architecture built around a super-wide I/O interface.
	- **The architecture mirrors the HBM logic, but targets a different integration path**: UltraWIO is described as conceptually similar to HBM's tight GPU-memory coupling, but the reported design emphasizes customer-specific AI-engine integration and possibly WoW packaging.
	- **Edge inference is the stated demand driver**: Commercial Times frames the effort around AI inference shifting from cloud to edge and the rise of decentralized computing, which implies Nanya is looking beyond cloud GPU/HBM demand.
	- **Partner ecosystem is a gating factor**: Nanya is reportedly working with multiple logic IC makers. UltraWIO success depends not only on DRAM process execution, but also on logic partner design wins, package integration, and customer architecture adoption.
	- **Trial production is an early validation point, not a commercial ramp**: some products have entered trial production, but the source only points to more tangible results in H2 2026. Volume, customers, pricing, and qualification status remain undisclosed.

- ## What to Monitor
	- Whether H2 2026 UltraWIO milestones include named customers, sample qualification, design wins, or actual revenue contribution.
	- Whether Nanya gives a detailed phase ramp for the new fab, especially the path from initial H2 2027 mass production to peak 45kwpm output.
	- Whether the 2026 NTD 52B capex budget changes if DRAM tightness persists or customer prepayments / co-development funding emerge.
	- Yield and cost progress on 1B / 1C / 1D nodes, since advanced-node execution is the key difference between adding capacity and adding competitive capacity.
	- Packaging details around UltraWIO, especially whether WoW becomes the chosen architecture and which partner handles logic-die design and integration.
	- Any evidence that edge-AI inference customers require non-HBM custom memory architectures rather than standard LPDDR / DDR / HBM solutions.
