- ## Expert call: how AWS sells Bedrock, and what Claude is worth to Amazon
	- **Source**: User-provided screenshots of an expert-network transcript (excerpts from about 00:03 to 00:32). The expert is a former AWS AI and Bedrock go-to-market specialist who mostly covered highly regulated industries and has since left AWS. The interviewer's highlights were kept. Statements about the Anthropic contract are the expert's own understanding, which they say they never verified. Related: [[2026-05-27-aws-bedrock-anthropic-hyperscaler-margins]], [[AMZN-2026-Q2]], [[2026-10-21-hard-budget-caps-aws-gcp-agent-era-consumption-billing]].
	- **Thesis**: According to the expert, enterprises no longer choose a cloud AI platform on model quality, because model access has become **commoditized across AWS and Azure**. They choose on **where their data and adjacent workloads already sit**, followed by compliance, GPU capacity, cost, and deployment flexibility. That makes Bedrock mainly a way to **defend and extend AWS's existing installed base**, with Claude as the lead offering. Per token, the expert thinks Amazon earns **more on open-weight models than on Claude**, and accepts thinner Claude economics to win the overall workload.
- ## How the sales motion evolved
	- | Period | What the expert describes |
	  |---|---|
	  | Before Anthropic | AWS was "caught a little bit flat-footed" by ChatGPT and Microsoft. It was seen as "an infrastructure provider, but not an AI player"; prospects compared it to OpenAI and "we didn't have a competitive offering." Sales were outbound and relied on data gravity and compliance, sometimes winning "every part of an AI workload except the final call to a large language model" |
	  | After Anthropic joined Bedrock | AWS reached "basically parity" on models and then competed on its usual strengths: compliance, reliability, scalability, and GPU availability. "Once we added Anthropic, we were able to compete much more successfully" |
	  | 2024–2025 | Mostly pilots, with customers testing whether large language models could handle mission-critical work. Bedrock expanded from model hosting into a toolkit (guardrails, retrieval, context engineering) |
	  | 2025 | "A lot of the revenue was just coding assistance. It was **Claude Code running on AWS**" |
	  | 2026 | "Meaningful production workloads begin to scale up": agentic and other workloads reaching production "because they've proven they're good enough," with "other significant revenue" now coming from them |
	- The 2025-to-2026 shift matches the broader agent-adoption story: AWS's first large source of AI revenue was one developer tool, and broader enterprise use is only now arriving. It also implies Bedrock revenue in 2025 depended heavily on Anthropic's own product success.
- ## How enterprises choose between Bedrock, Azure Foundry, Vertex, and going direct
	- | Factor | What the expert said | Who benefits |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Model quality | Early evaluations asked "Is Claude on Amazon Bedrock as good as OpenAI on Azure?" Since then, "That went away… got commoditized" | No one; it stopped deciding deals |
	  | **1. Data and adjacent workloads** | "Where is my data now? Where are my workloads?" When AWS already owned the workload, that was "a huge strength… against Microsoft or Google"; when Microsoft owned it, "it was hard for us to win" | The incumbent cloud. This is a defensive moat, not a way to take share |
	  | 2. Compliance and privacy | Regulated buyers worried that providers train on their data or leak intellectual property; the expert cited Samsung's leak | AWS, which is traditionally strong here |
	  | 3. Capacity | GPU supply has not caught up with demand, and hyperscalers "can sell those GPUs 24/7, 365 days a year." AWS's pitch: "we can support you at scale without throttling you," for example for Claude in Europe, at a time when Claude or OpenAI would sometimes stop working under load | Whoever has the most installed capacity in the region |
	  | 4. Cost | Inference cost is falling and "AI performance you can get for a dollar… is going way up." AWS pitched Haiku for scale and open-weight models for "better price performance" | Cheaper models, including open-weight ones |
	  | 5. Flexibility | Pay per token for Claude, or run an open-weight model on your own instances (paying for servers rather than tokens). "Fairly complicated token versus instance-based pricing conversations" | AWS's breadth of deployment options |
	- **Open-weight models are now credible for some use cases**: earlier, frontier Claude and GPT models were "way ahead of open source," though Llama was good enough for some customers. Now there are "all these Chinese models" plus **Muse**, which the expert called Meta's latest offering, that customers "can literally run… on their own GPUs."
- ## Data residency: the main reason to buy Claude through AWS
	- The interviewer asked whether, on Bedrock, customer data never goes to Anthropic or OpenAI. The expert said: "Correct. That's a major advantage. That's why lots of customers buy Claude through Amazon instead of direct from Anthropic." AWS runs Claude on its own GPUs on Anthropic's behalf, so "my data does not leave the AWS environment."
	- **One qualification**: in multi-tenant setups, data "leaves my VPC or my private AWS environment" and goes to shared Bedrock servers. It stays inside AWS but not inside the customer's own network. "Lots of Bedrock activity was because people never wanted their data to leave the AWS infrastructure data estate."
	- **What this means for Anthropic**: its direct enterprise sales compete with its largest channel partner on exactly this point. For regulated buyers, Bedrock is the default way to buy, which limits how much of that segment Anthropic can serve directly.
- ## Anthropic economics: revenue share, price parity, margin per token
	- **Revenue share**: the expert's understanding was that the early revenue share was "basically… at cost. Amazon was not making a margin on a token of Claude in the early days"; "Anthropic was making all the money on that." They believe this has changed with Claude's growth on AWS and now assume "some sort of percentage-based revenue share," probably varying by region. The expert stressed: "I can't say that as I never verified that."
	- **Price parity**: "Claude tokens are at parity": Amazon charges whatever Anthropic charges direct. The expert guesses that "ultimately… Anthropic sets it," but that pricing is worked out together, because the cost of serving a model is mostly AWS compute ("here's how much GPU cost there's going to be under the hood").
	- **Margin per token**: "they're making more margin on the open-weight tokens," because AWS keeps all the revenue from open-weight tokens but only a revenue share on Claude. The strategy was "playing the long game… How do we win the AI workload versus Microsoft or Google? Let's do whatever we need to do to win the workload," and that win "often would be driven by Anthropic."
	- **How this fits the SemiAnalysis view**: [[2026-05-27-aws-bedrock-anthropic-hyperscaler-margins]] attributes AWS's rising margins to Bedrock's token-as-a-service model, with a distribution fee on top of the infrastructure fee. The expert's "at cost" account describes the early deal and is unverified. Their belief that it has since become a percentage share is consistent with SemiAnalysis. Both views can hold: Claude may be lower-margin per token than open-weight models yet still be accretive to AWS overall, because of the volume it brings and the adjacent workloads it pulls in.
- ## Analysis
	- **Price parity removes price as a reason to choose a channel.** Since a Claude token costs the same on Bedrock and direct, the choice is decided by data location, compliance, and capacity. AWS's large installed base wins all three by default. The share of Anthropic's revenue that runs through cloud channels is therefore likely to follow the enterprise cloud market, not Anthropic's direct sales effort.
	- **The defensive moat also limits AWS's reach.** "When Microsoft owned it, it was hard for us to win" is the mirror image. AI workloads mostly follow existing cloud share, which makes AI more likely to entrench cloud market shares than to reshuffle them. Capacity is the one factor that can move deals between clouds, which is why it was the expert's main competitive lever.
	- **AWS has a mild incentive to steer customers toward open-weight models.** Higher margins there, plus Haiku and open-weight price-performance pitches, give AWS reason to push cost-sensitive workloads off frontier Claude. Strong production demand for Claude Code and agents works against that. As production agent workloads scale, the shift between frontier and open-weight models is a key variable for Bedrock's blended margin.
	- **Throttling as a sales argument**: AWS pitched "no throttling" against periods when "Claude would stop working or OpenAI would stop working" under load. If regional capacity really is better on AWS, that explains part of Bedrock's Claude volume beyond compliance. The expert gave no numbers, so this is a sales claim, not measured evidence.
