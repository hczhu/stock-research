- ## Jira in academic papers — issue-tracking & agent-platform survey
	- **Source**: 62 arXiv papers that mention "Jira", collected under `tmp-files/jira-pdfs/`. Text extracted via `pdftotext` + keyword scan for "jira". Links are arXiv abstract pages (version-stripped).
	- **Thesis**: Across a decade of software-engineering research, **Jira is the de-facto system-of-record** — the default issue tracker researchers mine for bugs, technical debt, requirements, and workflow data. The 2019–2023 papers use Jira as a *data source / benchmark*; the 2024–2026 papers increasingly use Jira as the *integration target and control plane for LLM/agentic tooling* (Text-to-JQL benchmarks, GPT project-management assistants, agentic backlog orchestration, RAG over tickets, agent red-teaming). That shift is the read-through for [[$TEAM]]: deep entrenchment as infrastructure (a moat), plus a rapidly expanding agent surface — with the caveat that much of the new tooling is third-party and sits *on top of* Jira, which is both validation and a disintermediation risk.
-
- ## Bottom line
	- **Jira is the standard substrate of SE research.** ~55 of these 62 papers treat Jira as *the* issue tracker to study or build on — technical-debt datasets, bug-report corpora, requirements mining, DORA/productivity telemetry. That ubiquity is a durable, hard-to-displace moat for [[$TEAM]].
	- **A dedicated open dataset exists purely to study public Jira** ([2201.08368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08368)) and a full **PhD dissertation** is devoted to issue-tracking ecosystems with a "Why Jira" section ([2507.06704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06704)) — Jira is important enough to be its own research object, not just a tool.
	- **The agent wave is anchoring on Jira/JQL.** Multiple 2025–2026 papers build Text-to-JQL benchmarks and agents (Jackal, Agentic Jackal), GPT PM assistants (JiraGPT Next), Jira-integrated autonomous backlog orchestration, RAG over Jira tickets, and treat Jira as a canonical SaaS target for agent red-teaming — corroborating the Rovo/Teamwork-Graph thesis (see [[2026-07-02-atlassian-team26-ai-products-rovo-teamwork-graph]] and [[2026-07-04-atlassian-academic-papers-ai-sdlc-survey]]).
	- **Risk framing**: (1) research ubiquity ≠ revenue — many papers just use free/public Jira data. (2) Third-party agents *on top of* Jira could commoditize the UI and push value to the agent layer; Atlassian's own agent push (Rovo) is the defense. (3) **One false positive**: [2102.07412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07412) "Jira" is a *Kurdish speech-recognition system*, unrelated to Atlassian.

- ## Read-through for $TEAM
	- **Entrenchment / moat**: Jira is the default issue tracker of software-engineering research — used as *the* corpus for bugs, technical debt, requirements, architecture knowledge, and productivity/DORA telemetry. This ubiquity is exactly the kind of switching-cost, system-of-record moat that supports the [[$TEAM]] durability case.
	- **Agent surface expansion**: the 2024–2026 cluster shows the market building agents *on and around* Jira — Text-to-JQL (Jackal, Agentic Jackal), GPT PM assistants (JiraGPT Next), Jira-integrated autonomous backlog orchestration, RAG over Jira tickets, and Jira as a canonical SaaS action-surface for agents. This validates Atlassian's Rovo / Teamwork Graph bet that Jira's structured work context is the anchor for enterprise AI.
	- **Two-edged signal**: third-party agents that sit on top of Jira could commoditize the Jira UI and shift value to the agent layer. Whether Atlassian captures that value (Rovo, Teamwork Graph, Forge marketplace, API monetization) vs. being reduced to a data backend is the key competitive question. The JI-RADAR/Forge and Centific/RLVR papers show both dynamics: ecosystem lock-in *and* outsiders optimizing agents for Jira workflows.
	- **Caveats**: (1) academic ubiquity ≠ paid seats — much research uses free/public Jira data. (2) The exact production metrics that are favorable (HULA 59% merged-PR rate; see the sibling Atlassian memo) come from Atlassian-affiliated studies. (3) [2102.07412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07412) is a homonym ("Jira" Kurdish ASR) and carries no signal.
