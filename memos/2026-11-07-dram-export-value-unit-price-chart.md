- ## What the Price–Volume Arithmetic Actually Says
	- Relative to the mid-2025 reference range, the endpoint shows approximately **5–6x export value and 5–6x value per kilogram**. Their similar scale suggests the increase in value per unit of weight explains much of the export-value surge, rather than requiring a comparable increase in physical weight shipped.
	- At the last two points, export value rises roughly **26%**, while unit value rises roughly **5%**. The identity is **exported weight = export value ÷ value per kilogram**. Using the rounded readings gives approximately **160,000kg → 192,000kg**, or **20% higher weight**.
	- This is a conditional decomposition, not a measured monthly shipment-growth statistic: it requires identical product coverage, valuation definitions, and reporting-period lengths. If the red bar is a partial-period estimate or projection, direct comparison may be invalid.
	- Conversely, the penultimate observation has higher value per kilogram without higher total value, implying lower weight under the same assumptions. This illustrates why neither the bars nor the line alone can establish volume strength.
	- **Kilograms are not bits**: more memory per package/module, a different mix of chips versus modules, product specifications, and packaging weight can change this ratio. A 5x increase in dollars per kilogram is not evidence that the same DRAM chip or gigabyte costs 5x as much.
