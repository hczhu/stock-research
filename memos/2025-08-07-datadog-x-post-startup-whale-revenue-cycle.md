- ## Datadog — Successive Waves of Startup Winners
	- **Source** — Hongcheng (@hzhu_), August 7, 2025, user-supplied X-post screenshot. The embedded research exhibit is titled “Datadog New ARR Base Case”; its author and firm are not visible. The filename follows the post date. [Original X post](https://x.com/hzhu_/status/1953678411802788028?a=XzWrRwAYZciM_5QftK8&s=20)
	- **User's thesis** — Datadog attracts startups through product quality and ease of use. A small subset becomes very large customers, producing substantial consumption revenue before optimizing spending or adopting alternatives. Successive startup cohorts can replenish that growth, while established non-tech enterprises provide a more stable base.

- ## The Customer Lifecycle Described by the User
	- | Stage | Customer behavior | Datadog revenue effect |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Early adoption | Small technical teams choose a capable, easy-to-use platform. | Low initial spend with expansion potential. |
	  | Hypergrowth | More workloads and telemetry rapidly increase consumption. | A few customers can become outsized contributors. |
	  | Budget scrutiny | The observability bill becomes large enough to draw management attention. | More cost controls and price negotiations. |
	  | Optimization or substitution | Technically strong customers consider open-source or internally built alternatives. | Lower consumption, partial migration, or potential churn. |
	  | New cohort emergence | New startup winners scale into large accounts. | Fresh expansion can offset maturing or shrinking accounts. |
	- The user compares this pattern to venture investing: a broad early customer pool produces a small number of exceptional revenue contributors. Datadog captures their software consumption rather than equity returns.
	- The post also argues that large non-tech organizations, including financial institutions, tend to stay longer. This is the user's segmentation thesis; the post contains no cohort retention data proving the difference.

- ## What the Thesis Requires
	- **Analytical inference** — New-customer expansion must replenish reductions from existing large accounts. Continued startup formation alone does not establish the size or timing of replacement revenue.
	- **Analytical inference** — High product satisfaction can coexist with cost-driven migration. Strong engineering teams can appreciate the product while finding the economics of internal alternatives increasingly attractive.
