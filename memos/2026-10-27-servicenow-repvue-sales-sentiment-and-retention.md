- ## ServiceNow: Resilient Sales Metrics, Deteriorating Seller Sentiment
	- **Source**: User-provided commentary citing RepVue survey results for Q3 2026. Original post URL, author, and publication date were not supplied; the cashtag search link is not a source permalink. Figures below are survey-reported, not company financial disclosures.
	- **Thesis**: ServiceNow's near-term selling performance can remain healthy while confidence in its products, leadership, and compensation deteriorates. The potential transmission mechanism is seller attrition and lost productivity—not evidence that revenue has already weakened.
	- **Sample**: **171 current-employee sales reps in Q3 2026**; **1,896 reps surveyed over several years**, not in the latest quarter. The excerpt does not establish that the same respondents were tracked over time.

- ## How the Divergence Could Reach Revenue
	- **Capacity and deal economics can offset dissatisfaction for a time.** More reps, larger contracts, and faster sales cycles help explain why revenue could still accelerate. The survey cannot establish that acceleration, and these indicators should not be multiplied into a revenue forecast: ramp status, productivity, attainment distribution, and contract duration are missing.
	- **The author's prediction is a two-to-four-quarter lag.** The proposed sequence is worsening morale and compensation dissatisfaction → experienced sellers leave → vacancies, disrupted customer relationships, and slower replacement-rep ramp → weaker sales productivity. The excerpt offers a plausible mechanism, not a demonstrated predictive relationship or quantified revenue impact.
	- **Net hiring can mask loss of experience.** Rising headcount and rising attrition are compatible. A larger organization can still lose account knowledge and subject-matter expertise if experienced sellers are replaced with new hires; the survey does not disclose workforce tenure or fully ramped capacity.
	- **Competition for sellers may compound AI competition for customers.** The post points to Anthropic and OpenAI recruiting sales talent, but supplies no hiring-flow data. Treat a specific ServiceNow-to-AI-lab exodus as unverified, not a measured trend.

- ## Seller Accounts: AI Opportunity, Product Gaps, and Uneven Management
	- **AI is both an opportunity and a threat.** One seller reports “Strong momentum with AI and specifically the Moveworks acquisition,” while saying ITSM and HRSD face AI-native challengers. These observations can coexist: acquisition-led momentum does not establish defensibility across the installed product portfolio.
	- **Execution complaints center on organizational strain.** Sellers describe unclear product strategy, regional leadership problems, pressure on experienced staff, and weaker expansion beyond established customers. Some distinguish attractive account-executive roles from less attractive solution-sales roles, suggesting experience varies by role and account assignment.
	- **The positive evidence is substantive too.** Another seller praises leadership, training, and career development while acknowledging intense performance expectations. A critical respondent still calls ServiceNow a market leader with strong products. The selected comments are not uniformly negative.
	- **Reading the evidence correctly:** These are seller opinions, not customer surveys or verified accounts of acquisition performance. Selected quotations include someone who says they already left, so they should not all be assumed to belong to the 171 current-employee Q3 sample. Respondent selection, peer composition, scoring scale, geographic mix, and statistical significance are undisclosed; percentile ranks are not percentages of satisfied employees.
