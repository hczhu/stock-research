- ## Intuitive Surgical: dominant and recurring, but priced for almost nothing to go wrong (MBI)
	- **Source**: MBI Deep Dives, "Intuitive Surgical: The Long Road to Everyday Surgery," October 26, 2026 (user-provided PDF). The author's reverse-DCF model targets a ~10% IRR. The author says he is staying away from the stock at about \$452. Additional history from scuttleblurb, "[ISRG – Intuitive Surgical] the robot is the easy part," October 22, 2026 (user-provided excerpt), is in its own section below.
	- **Thesis**: Intuitive is a near-monopoly razor-blade business. About \$12B of 2026 revenue is 84% recurring at a ~40% non-GAAP operating margin, and procedure growth drives every revenue line. The runway is benign general surgery and international markets. Those are also the procedures with the weakest clinical case, the lowest prices, and the first real competitors in two decades. MBI's "priced-in" case, the operating path today's price needs to earn ~10%, looks like its own bull case.

- ## Scale and history
	- **Scale**: over **10,000 procedures a day** on **12,000+ systems** (12,094 at September 2026). da Vinci procedures grew from about 1.0M in 2018 to **3.15M in 2025**, a 17% CAGR, while the installed base went from about 4,990 to 11,106 systems.
	- **Shareholder returns**: the split-adjusted IPO price was \$2 in 2000. At \$452 that is about **23% a year for 26 years**.
	- **How it became a monopoly**:
		- **2003**: merged with Computer Motion, its only robotic rival, for about \$148.5M after a 2002 patent verdict. This put the field's foundational patents in one company.
		- **2000s**: urologists took the robot's share of prostatectomies from near zero to over 80% in a decade.
		- **2013**: a JAMA study, an ACOG statement and an FDA warning letter stalled benign gynecology. Intuitive answered with new products: Xi (2014), SP (2018) and Ion (2019).
		- **2016**: the feared "patent cliff" never hurt, because the moat outlasted the patents.
	- **Leadership**: three CEOs since 1997. Gary Guthart and Dave Rosa were both 1996 engineering hires. Rosa, the ninth employee, became CEO in July 2025, and Guthart stayed on as full-time Executive Chair.

- ## Early product-procedure fit and the razor-blade model (scuttleblurb)
	- **An improbable origin**: SRI International's late-1980s vision was a remote-controlled robot surgeon operating on soldiers on distant battlefields. Re-pitched as a fix for laparoscopy's ergonomics (laparoscopy's small incisions plus open surgery's wrist control and depth perception), it still failed to win support inside Guidant. Fred Moll, then running Guidant's surgical device division, founded Intuitive in 1995 with John Freund and Robert Younge on an **exclusive, royalty-free license** to some SRI IP.
	- **A product in search of a procedure**: the prototypes were named Lenny (for Leonardo) and then Mona (for Mona Lisa). European clearance came in **1999** for general and cardiac surgery. At the June 2000 IPO, da Vinci still had **no FDA clearance**, was in patent litigation with Computer Motion, and had shown proof of concept in cholecystectomy and some cardiac procedures without a clear procedure fit.
	- **Fit came from open surgery, not laparoscopy**: the robot's real advantage was less trauma than open surgery (smaller incisions, less pain, shorter recovery, lower inpatient costs), not dexterity over laparoscopy.
		- | Procedure | Adoption milestones |
		  |---|---|
		  | Radical prostatectomy (US) | Share rose from **10% in 2004 to 20% in 2005** and about **two-thirds by 2010**. MBI puts it above 80% within a decade |
		  | Hysterectomy for cancer | **More than 80%** of the addressable market by 2014 |
		  | Benign hysterectomy | Resisted adoption: most cases were already minimally invasive, and the market was shrinking by 2014 as payers pushed conservative treatment of fibroids and other benign conditions |
		- **Concentration**: from 2008 to 2013, prostatectomies and hysterectomies made up about **60% of procedure volume**.
	- **The razor-blade model**: hospitals justify the robot (\$600K–3.1M plus \$95–225K a year of service) through extra operating-room throughput and beds freed by shorter recoveries. Each installed robot then throws off a **roughly 7-year instrument and service annuity**.
		- Many instruments last only a dozen or so procedures before an embedded chip retires them.
		- The instrument catalog keeps expanding, so da Vinci can complete more of each complex procedure without separate laparoscopic tools. That captures more recurring revenue per operation.
	- **Resilience in a downturn**: as the installed base grew, instruments and accessories rose from **27% of revenue to more than 40%**. In 2009, system unit growth collapsed from 39% to 1%, yet total revenue still grew **20%** on recurring instruments and service. Hospitals are capital-constrained in recessions, but surgeries still have to be done.

- ## Business model: one robot, three revenue streams
	- **2025 revenue of \$10.1B**:
		- | Line | 2025 revenue | Share |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | Instruments and accessories (I&A) | \$6.0B | 60% |
		  | Systems | \$2.5B | 25% |
		  | Service | \$1.6B | 16% |
	- **Unit economics per purchased system**:
		- | Item | Approximate value | Basis |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | System price | \$1.6M | 2025 ASP; range \$0.6–3.1M by model |
		  | Procedures per system per year | About 300 | US about 330, outside the US about 260 |
		  | I&A revenue per procedure | About \$1,850 | US about \$1,940, outside the US about \$1,580, Ion about \$2,200 |
		  | Service contract per year | About \$145K | Contracts run \$95–225K, typically five years |
		  | **Recurring revenue per system per year** | **About \$700K** | **About \$4.9M over a 7-year life**, almost 3× the up-front price |
	- **The chip, not wear, decides when an instrument is replaced**: each EndoWrist instrument has a chip that locks it out after a set number of uses, historically 10. One remanufacturer's testing found no degradation through 29 uses.
	- **Procedures are the key metric**: one point of procedure growth is about 36,000 procedures, or the annual work of about 120 robots. That is 5–6% of the ~2,100 systems placed each year, so small swings in procedure growth produce large swings in system sales.
	- **Leasing**: about half of 2025 placements were leased, and about 70% in the US.
		- The leased fleet is **3,210 robots**, which earned \$874M of lease revenue in 2025. Usage-based contracts cover 1,810 of those robots, and usage-based placements rose from 355 in 2023 to 496 in 2025.
		- More leasing makes year-one systems revenue about 20% lower than under 2019's leasing mix, but six-year cash collected is about the same.
		- Usage-based contracts turn the robot into a variable cost for the hospital, so a second vendor's robot has to take volume away from da Vinci to justify itself.
	- **The hospital's cost**: about **\$3,100 per robotic procedure** all-in: about \$760 of system depreciation, \$480 of service and \$1,850 of instruments. A laparoscopic cholecystectomy uses a few hundred dollars of disposables.
		- **Complex cancer surgery**: an easy choice, because the robot saves days of inpatient stay.
		- **Routine benign surgery**: costs \$2,000–3,000 more. A 2025 JAMA Surgery study found ~15% higher total costs for non-complex cholecystectomies.
		- **Intuitive's evidence**: its funded "Benign COMPARE" meta-analysis covered 366 studies. It found **54% lower odds of conversion to open surgery** than laparoscopy and shorter stays, with comparable complications and about 24 minutes of extra operating time.
	- **Why adoption happens anyway** (per Scuttleblurb): residents train on the robot, it is easier on surgeons' bodies, and hospitals want to keep a bought robot busy. A JAMA study of 169,000 Michigan patients found laparoscopy's share went from gaining 1.3 points a year to losing 0.3 points a year after a hospital adopted robotic surgery.
	- **Pricing strategy**: charge for differentiation, and give cost savings back where buyers are price-sensitive.
		- **2027 instrument cuts**: an expanded Extended Use program plus a China price cut will lower I&A revenue per procedure by **3–5%** at constant mix, and much more on benign procedures.
		- **Rollout**: country by country from 1H 2027. The US version needs a new 510(k).
		- **Precedent**: the 2020 Extended Use program cut I&A revenue per procedure by about 7%.
	- **Margins**: gross margin is in the mid-to-high 60s (69% in 2021, 66% in 2025), with R&D at 13–14% and SG&A at 24–28% of revenue. Management targets a **35–40% non-GAAP operating margin** and treats anything above it, now 41–42%, as capacity to invest. FCF margin recovered to about 25% in 2025 as factory capex fell back to about 5% of revenue.

- ## Market size: Intuitive's "23M, 20M, 9M" funnel
	- | Funnel layer | Procedures a year | da Vinci 2025 share |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Soft-tissue procedures in da Vinci specialties | About 23M | About 14% |
	  | Of those, can be done minimally invasively | About 20M | About 16% |
	  | Of those, "line of sight" (product, clearance and economics in place) | **About 9M** | **About 35%** |
	- **The line of sight keeps widening**: it has grown 1M a year, from 6M in January 2023 to 9M in January 2026. da Vinci's share of it only moved from 31% to 35%. MBI doubts a series that grows exactly 1M a year, and notes that most of the 9M-to-20M gap is about price, mainly **benign procedures outside the US**.
	- **MBI's US bottom-up pool**: about **4.6M procedures**, of which da Vinci does **44%** (2.01M).
		- | Category | US pool | da Vinci share |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | General surgery | About 3.3M (cholecystectomy ~1.0M, hernia ~1.1M, appendectomy ~0.5M, colectomy ~0.3M, bariatrics ~0.23M) | 38% |
		  | Gynecology | About 0.8M | 58% |
		  | Urology | About 0.25M | 81% (prostatectomy about 90%) |
		  | Thoracic, cardiac, head and neck | Remainder | 35% |
		- Cholecystectomy is 22–33% robotic. US growth is now almost entirely in price-sensitive benign general surgery, and US procedure growth has slowed from the mid-teens to about 10%.
	- **Outside the US**: 1.14M procedures in 2025, 36% of the total and growing nearly twice as fast as the US, led by the UK, Germany, Italy, India and Japan.
		- Benign procedures abroad grew **41% in Q3 2026**, up from 34% in Q1.
		- The implied line of sight outside the US is about 4.4M, of which da Vinci does about 26%.
		- Revenue per procedure is lower abroad, but margins have held as the mix shifted. From 2015 to 2025, revenue per procedure fell from about \$3,660 to \$3,190 while gross margin stayed at 66%.
	- **Newer growth areas**:
		- **Cardiac**: grew 40% in Q3, with a 300–400K line of sight against about 17K procedures in 2025. Breast surgery grew 42%.
		- **Ion** (lung biopsy): 144K procedures in 2025, about 45% of US lung biopsies and about 5% of revenue.
		- **GI endoscopy**: about 15M US procedures a year. A flexible robotic endoscope got FDA 510(k) clearance in August 2026; MBI treats it as an option.

- ## Competition
	- **The moat today is more than patents**: 5,600+ patents still matter, but so do regulation (rivals get cleared one indication at a time), training (88,000+ surgeons trained on da Vinci), evidence (48,000 publications), the instrument ecosystem, leases and five-year service contracts, force-feedback data, and the "da Vinci" brand.
	- **Many challengers already failed**: Senhance was sold for \$0.35 a share and wound down, Titan Medical, Medrobotics and avatera are gone, and J&J's Ottava took seven years to reach the FDA.
	- | Competitor | Status | Angle |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Medtronic Hugo | FDA urology clearance in 2025; general surgery and gynecology filed June 2026 | Largest surgical sales force; cost per use. Targets about 50K cumulative cases and 250 units by April 2027, roughly five days of da Vinci volume. A \$700M Cornerstone Robotics deal gives it a cheaper Chinese-built system for 50 countries |
	  | J&J Ottava | FDA De Novo in July 2026 for upper-abdominal general surgery | Bundled with Ethicon staplers and energy devices. A "financially material program by the end of the decade" |
	  | Stryker / CMR Versius | Stryker invested about \$400M in October 2026, with an option to buy the rest | Modular and portable; targets hospitals and ambulatory surgery centers (ASCs) |
	  | China (MicroPort Toumai and 4+ others) | MicroPort revenue RMB 551M in 2025, +114% | Xi-like designs, local pricing and provincial preference |
	- **By market**:
		- **China**: competition is clearly rising. Provincial tenders favor local suppliers, a national cap on robotic procedure charges forces a significant I&A price cut from November 2026, and tariffs on imported Xi reached 125% in early 2025.
		- **Price-sensitive segments**: Intuitive is pre-empting rivals. Installations of the cheaper Xi-R (~\$1.2M) and X more than doubled, and 15 of 18 Q3 ASC placements were Xi-Rs.
		- **Core US hospitals**: competition is so far flat. ASP held at \$1.6M and selling cycles are unchanged since Hugo launched.
	- **Three holes in the moat** (MBI):
		- 1. **Instruments**: remanufacturers reset the chip and sell for about 40% less. An appeals court sent the SIS tying case to a jury, and Intuitive settled a hospital class action in October 2026 with changes to how it deals with remanufacturers. Restore Robotics has FDA clearance to remanufacture the most-used Xi instrument and says 300+ hospitals have joined. If hospitals can use remanufactured instruments without risking their service contract, I&A (about 60% of revenue) faces price competition.
		  2. **Cholecystectomy safety**: a 2023 JAMA Surgery Medicare study found bile-duct injury in 0.7% of robotic cases vs. 0.2% laparoscopic. A 2026 Mayo study found lower injury rates with the robot. A safety signal falls on surgeons, who choose the approach, so it matters more than a cost gap.
		  3. **Price**: more than half of da Vinci procedures are now benign. By 2028–30 there could be four cleared multi-port platforms in US general surgery, three from companies whose instrument businesses let them price the robot as a loss leader.

- ## Management and capital allocation
	- **Cash use, 2013–2025**: \$19.6B of operating cash flow, \$5B of capex, \$0.3B of acquisitions, and **\$10.3B of buybacks, 71% of FCF**. Cash was \$9.0B at end-2025, with no debt and no dividend.
	- **Buybacks have mostly offset stock compensation**: diluted shares were about 361M in 2013 and 363M in 2025, as SBC totaled \$4.9B (25% of operating cash flow). 2026 is the exception: \$4.45B bought back so far, including a record \$2.94B quarter at an average of \$377. The diluted count is now about 352M vs. about 362M a year ago.
	- **Incentives**: 2025 PSUs are two-thirds combined da Vinci and Ion procedure growth and one-third adjusted operating margin vs. 20 med-tech peers. MBI notes a tension: management cut instrument prices in the same year it is paid on procedure counts. MBI still calls it "a well-run, engineering-led, patient company."

- ## Valuation (MBI's reverse DCF, ~10% IRR target)
	- | Case | 2032 revenue | Key assumptions | IRR at 35× terminal FCF |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | **Priced-in** (what the current price requires) | **\$28.2B**, a 15.9% seven-year CAGR | Procedures grow about 15% a year to 8.4M; installed base about 26,000; I&A per procedure absorbs the 3–5% cut then holds flat; non-GAAP operating margin **about 48.5%**; recurring revenue 89%, outside-US 40% | About 10% by construction |
	  | Bear | About \$18.8B | Hugo, Ottava and Versius take benign share; the cholecystectomy evidence stays contested; China losses persist; I&A per procedure keeps falling; growth fades to the mid-to-high single digits | **About 0.2%** |
	  | Bull | About \$31.1B | Price elasticity pays off; international markets and new countries add about 1.5 points a year; force-feedback and SP instruments win back the price cut; GI endoscopy at 1.5× priced-in volume | **About 12%** |
	- **What "priced-in" requires**: da Vinci would do 56% of a 15M line of sight by 2032 and **69% of the US pool, up from 44%**, which means cholecystectomy well past half robotic. Operating margin would reach about 48.5%, well above management's 35–40% target. Even then, the terminal multiple needed is about **35× FCF**, roughly 27× GAAP and 24× non-GAAP earnings.
	- **MBI's conclusion**: the bull case is "tantalizingly close" to what is priced in. Today's price is "a bet that almost nothing goes wrong," so MBI is "going to keep a comfortable distance" for now.
