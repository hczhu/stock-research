- ## HBM4 Pricing — Nvidia's Strategic Discount and Samsung's Margin Catch-Up
	- **Source**: User-provided excerpt from SemiAnalysis commentary linked to its *Accelerator and HBM Model*; the excerpt does not include the article title or publication date. All 2027 prices, increases and margin comparisons are the author's estimates rather than disclosed contracts.
	- **Thesis**: HBM4 is a seller's market, but pricing is segmented by strategic dependence rather than volume alone. SK hynix appears to be giving Nvidia its lowest price because SK Group needs priority access to Nvidia GPUs for its own AI data-center plans. Samsung is expected to charge about 15% more on average, narrowing SK hynix's historical HBM margin lead despite Samsung's more expensive process. The incremental 2027 earnings setup therefore favors Samsung, while Nvidia gains a cost advantage over competing GPU and custom-ASIC buyers.
- ## 2027 HBM4 Price Map
	- | Supplier / buyer | Estimated 2027 price | YoY increase | Interpretation |
	  |---|---:|---:|---|
	  | SK hynix → Nvidia | **\$3.0–3.3/Gb** | **~70%** | Lowest quoted band; strategic and volume discount |
	  | SK hynix → AMD, Broadcom, AWS, Google | **\$3.7–4.1/Gb** | Not stated | Other Tier-1 buyers pay materially more despite large volumes |
	  | Samsung → blended HBM4 customers | **\$3.5–3.9/Gb** | Not stated | Approximately 15% midpoint premium to SK hynix's blended range |
	  | SK hynix → blended HBM4 customers | **\$3.0–3.4/Gb** | Not stated | Lower pricing reflects Nvidia concentration and product positioning |
	  | Samsung → Nvidia | Absolute price not isolated | **~98%** | Samsung concedes less than SK hynix |
	  | Micron → Nvidia | Absolute price not isolated | **~89%** | Large increase, but insufficient cost data for margin comparison |
	- At the midpoint, Nvidia pays SK hynix about **\$3.15/Gb** versus **\$3.90/Gb** for other Tier-1 customers—a **~19% discount**, or roughly \$0.75/Gb.
	- Samsung's midpoint of **\$3.70/Gb** is about **16% above** SK hynix's \$3.20/Gb blended midpoint, consistent with the source's “~15% premium” framing.
	- The ~70% Nvidia increase implies an SK hynix 2026 starting point near **\$1.85/Gb**. The important change is not simply inflation; it is a widening dispersion by buyer and supplier.
- ## Why Nvidia Gets the Best Price
	- **Volume certainty** matters: Nvidia remains the largest HBM buyer and an important co-development partner, lowering demand and qualification risk for SK hynix.
	- **But volume alone is not a sufficient explanation.** In a shortage, SK hynix could redirect supply to AMD, Broadcom, Google or Amazon if Nvidia resisted price increases. The existence of ready substitute buyers should normally eliminate a large discount.
	- The author's explanation is an implicit group-level exchange: **SK hynix gives Nvidia favorable HBM economics; Nvidia gives SK Group priority GPU allocation** for its AI data-center initiative.
	- The benefits and costs fall on different constituencies. SK Group captures strategic access to scarce accelerators, while SK hynix minority shareholders absorb lower HBM revenue and margin.
	- This changes how customer concentration should be modeled. Nvidia is simultaneously SK hynix's largest customer, technology partner and a critical supplier to its parent group's downstream ambitions.
- ## Seller Power Still Dominates the Market
	- Nvidia's discount does not mean buyers broadly control pricing. The source expects every major buyer to face substantial increases, with purchase volume and incumbent relationships affecting only the degree.
	- HBM qualification and co-development reduce short-term fungibility, but demand from GPU vendors and hyperscaler ASIC programs creates multiple credible buyers for every available unit.
	- **Allocation is more valuable than the lowest price.** Buyers accept sharp increases to secure capacity and avoid delaying accelerators whose economics depend on complete HBM-equipped packages.
	- Nvidia's advantage is relative: its HBM cost rises ~70%, but competitors buying from SK hynix pay a higher absolute price, while Nvidia's increases from Samsung and Micron are estimated at ~98% and ~89%.
	- This can reinforce Nvidia's platform advantage. AMD and custom-ASIC programs may avoid Nvidia's GPU markup, but they do not automatically obtain Nvidia's memory terms.
- ## Process, Performance and Cost Structure
	- | Vendor | DRAM / base-die process | Relative cost | Product implication |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | Samsung | **1c DRAM + Samsung SF4** | Higher | Better HBM4 performance and stronger pricing |
	  | SK hynix | **1b DRAM + TSMC N12** | Lower | Cost advantage, but the source says performance trails Samsung |
	- Historically, SK hynix earned much higher HBM margins because its front-end and back-end yields were superior. Its cheaper 1b/N12 design adds another cost advantage.
	- Samsung is attacking from both sides: its HBM4 product is more competitive, and its front-end yield has improved materially from the lagging HBM3E 12-high generation.
	- A more expensive process can therefore produce better economics when the performance premium and yield improvement support a sufficiently higher selling price.
	- The source expects the **Nvidia-bound HBM margin gap** between SK hynix and Samsung to fall from roughly the **teens in percentage points during 2026 to single digits in 2027**. Pricing, rather than a reversal in process cost, drives most of the convergence.
- ## Equity Lens
	- **[[$005930.KS]]**: the cleanest positive revision. Higher HBM4 pricing, better performance, improving yields and strength in commodity DRAM support a more favorable incremental earnings and free-cash-flow trajectory than SK hynix under the source's assumptions.
	- **[[$000660.KS]]**: remains a high-margin HBM leader, but the Nvidia discount leaves money on the table and caps upside precisely when investors expect exceptional cash generation. The group-level bargain also raises a governance question for minority shareholders.
	- **[[$NVDA]]**: HBM becomes a materially larger platform BOM item, pressuring gross margin or requiring higher system prices. Its preferential SK hynix terms soften the shock and may widen its cost advantage over competing accelerators.
	- **[[$MU]]**: an estimated ~89% Nvidia price increase is directionally attractive, but the excerpt provides no absolute price, yield or process-cost figures. It supports pricing power, not a precise Micron margin forecast.
	- **[[$AMD]], [[$AVGO]], [[$AMZN]], [[$GOOGL]]**: alternative accelerator programs deepen supplier power but appear to pay more for SK hynix HBM4 than Nvidia. Their silicon may be cheaper than Nvidia's, yet memory procurement partially offsets that advantage.
- ## Cash Returns and Capital Allocation
	- SK hynix raised its shareholder-return target from **within 50% to over 50% of cumulative free cash flow**, using treasury-share repurchases and cancellations plus fixed, special and ordinary dividends. The scale and mix are due with Q3 2026 earnings.
	- Samsung's estimated **KRW 90–110T** 2026 return program includes about KRW 30T of dividends, a KRW 15T employee-compensation buyback, and additional dividends or repurchases to be finalized in early 2027.
	- The pricing divergence matters because foregone SK hynix HBM margin reduces the pool available for memory investment and distributions, while Samsung's improving HBM economics expand both strategic and shareholder-return capacity.
- ## Evidence Limits
	- None of the contract prices, the SK–Nvidia reciprocal arrangement or customer-specific margin estimates is disclosed by the companies. Treat them as a coherent analyst model, not confirmed terms.
	- The excerpt does not provide volumes, stack heights, contract duration, packaging terms, yield assumptions or total cost per good stack. Dollar-per-gigabit comparisons cannot by themselves establish gross margin.
	- Samsung's 15% price premium may partly compensate for its more expensive 1c/SF4 design rather than representing pure incremental margin.
	- Nvidia's apparent discount may reflect co-development obligations, qualification timing, mix or take-or-pay certainty in addition to SK Group's GPU needs.
	- The thesis would weaken if Samsung's yield ramp stalls, SK hynix closes the HBM4 performance gap, additional capacity reduces seller power, or buyer-specific pricing converges after 2027 allocations are finalized.
