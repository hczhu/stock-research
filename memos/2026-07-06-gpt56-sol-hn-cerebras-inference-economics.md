- **Thesis frame**: The most investable thread signal is that **inference speed (tokens/sec) is becoming a first-class product axis**, and the whole stack is **memory-bandwidth-bound**. GPT-5.6 Sol will launch **on Cerebras at "up to 750 tok/s"** — the first frontier closed model on Cerebras' wafer-scale hardware — which the market read as a **Cerebras validation but with hard economic caveats**. Secondary signals: OpenAI's **pricing/version strategy** (deprecating cheap models, Anthropic-style cache-write fees), the **weights-in-silicon** (Taalas) tail, and **open-weight Chinese models** eating the cheap-tier.

- ## Cerebras ($CBRS) — headline customer, but caveats stack up
	- **The launch fact**: "We're launching GPT‑5.6 Sol **on Cerebras at up to 750 tokens/second** in July… access initially limited to select customers as we expand capacity." This is (per commenters) **the largest/first frontier closed-source model Cerebras has served** (they already run OpenAI's smaller **Codex-Spark** models).
	- **Reality-check on "up to 750"**: users report **GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark on Cerebras delivering ~150 t/s in practice** vs the "1,000 t/s" launch claim — i.e., **"up to" is doing heavy lifting**; real throughput is oversubscribed/variable. One noted 150 t/s is **"barely faster than Nvidia GPUs that batch a lot more and are more cost-effective."**
	- **Capacity/economics constraints (the bear texture)**:
		- Cerebras is **wafer-scale SRAM** (a giant on-chip cache), **no meaningful KV-cache offload**, **wildly high on-wafer bandwidth** but **hard/expensive to scale** and **supply-constrained** with **"prohibitive" production costs**.
		- To serve **Kimi K2.6 they must do aggressive REAP pruning**; one commenter pegs **~750B parameters as the economic upper limit** Cerebras can serve — implying **Sol is either smaller than assumed, or priced high enough to justify an inefficient serving regime**.
		- Reference point: **Llama 405B runs ~900 t/s** on Cerebras with an estimated **~13.5K concurrent streaming clients** capacity — useful for sizing how few users a wafer supports at frontier scale.
	- **Sentiment/positioning**: at least one commenter is **openly short CBRS** ("burned 10 years… struck accidental gold with the giant wafer… valued like they broke the grail"). Combined with the [[2026-07-06-openai-broadcom-jalapeno-hn-cerebras-hbm-readthrough]] read (OpenAI building its own Broadcom inference ASIC, CBRS −~20% that week), the picture is **Cerebras as a validated-but-fragile single-customer-concentrated inference play**. The GPT-5.6 deal is a positive data point *against* that concentration worry, but "select customers / expanding capacity" signals they can't yet serve it at scale.

- ## Inference is memory-bound — the DRAM/HBM read-through
	- Repeated first-principles point: **auto-regressive decoding is memory-bandwidth-bound**, not compute-bound. This is the same wall as [[2026-07-06-carmack-nand-flash-vs-hbm-ai-inference-memory]] and the core of [[DRAM-memory-ssd-index-thesis]].
	- **Nvidia ($NVDA) numbers cited**: **Blackwell + TensorRT-LLM + speculative decoding** consistently hits the **~1,000 TPS/user barrier** on **Maverick 400B**, vs **~250 TPS/user** normally, out of **10K+ TPS aggregate/server**. Speculative decoding (**draft model 10–20× faster than target, ~60–80% acceptance**) is how the big speedups are achieved — **2–4× free**, MoA-style up to 10× with some quality loss.
	- **Consumer/edge memory wall**: Nvidia **Spark = 256-bit LPDDR5** (bandwidth-limited); **Apple M3 Ultra = 1024-bit / 819 GB/s at $3,999**. "Replace the SoC with an ASIC but keep the same memory → same performance." **Read-through: memory width/bandwidth is the binding spec everywhere**, reinforcing HBM/high-bandwidth-memory scarcity value.
	- **Local-inference substitution signal**: multiple shops **buying Mac Studios / DGX Sparks** to migrate agentic workflows off cloud APIs, citing **"break even in <1 year"** vs Claude subscriptions. A slow structural pull toward on-device once open models are "good enough" — bearish long-run for per-token API pricing, bullish for high-memory edge silicon.

- ## Weights-in-silicon (Taalas) — recurring, with hard numbers this time
	- **Taalas** (Toronto) demo (chatjimmy.ai) again cited at **~13–17K tok/s**, but the thread finally has **die economics**: **HC1 = Llama 3.1 8B (3–6-bit quant), 53B transistors, 815mm² on TSMC N6, ~250W**, near the **858mm² reticle limit**.
		- Scaling math from a commenter: on **N2** that's still **~272mm²** at **~$30K/wafer** — bigger than a 9950X or RTX 5070. **Frontier models (5–10T params ≈ 1000×) are infeasible to bake**; you'd need **300–1,000 chips** and **hundreds of thousands of units per model** to get **<$100K/board**.
		- **Verdict**: not a frontier-serving threat; **viable only for stable, small (≤~30B) models** — edge, voice, vision, tool-routing coprocessors — and possibly **cheap fallback models** for labs. Proposed fix: pair a frozen Taalas core with an **FPGA/LoRA "update" chip** for continual learning.
	- **Tenstorrent** (Jim Keller) cited as the dataflow-architecture alternative. **Read-through**: the "specialized inference silicon" theme is broad (Cerebras, Groq, Taalas, Tenstorrent, plus hyperscaler ASICs), all attacking Nvidia's inference share while **still depending on advanced-node TSMC capacity and memory**.

- ## OpenAI business-model / pricing signals
	- **Pricing**: **Sol $5in/$30out, Terra $2.50/$15, Luna $1/$6.** Sol = **same price as GPT-5.5** (so likely **same-size base model + more post-training**, hence "5.6" not "6"); **Terra ≈ half-price GPT-5.5 at claimed parity**.
	- **Margin creep**: **"For GPT-5.6 and later, cache writes billed at 1.25× uncached input rate"** — copying Anthropic; effectively a **stealth ~25% input price increase**. Prior ChatGPT caching was free/automatic.
	- **Forced-upgrade ladder (a recurring gripe)**: **GPT-5 mini ($0.25/$2) being discontinued Dec**, replacement **5.4 mini ($0.75/$4.5) is 3× the price**; **5.4 nano benchmarks higher but tests ~30% worse in real enterprise workflows**. Labs are **abandoning the cheap tier** (Anthropic Haiku, Gemini Flash-lite prices rising too) — framed as **SaaS-style "deprecate cheap, force upgrades, keep revenue up"** ahead of the **OpenAI IPO**.
	- **"ultra" mode = subagents** (orchestrator + subagents burning tokens), a **billable harness feature**, not a new model — same as Claude Code's ultra mode. **Read-through: token consumption per task is being engineered upward** even as per-token prices nominally fall.

- ## Open-weight / Chinese-model competition (the cheap-tier capture)
	- Concrete migration signal: shops **shifting workloads from Haiku/Sonnet/GPT-5-mini to DeepSeek V4 Flash/Pro, Kimi 2.6, GLM 5.2, Qwen 3.5/3.6** for cost. **DeepSeek V4 Flash trades blows with Aug-2025 GPT-5-high** per Artificial Analysis; **GLM-5.2 API output ~1/3 of Gemini-3.1-Pro** and (being open-weight) **provably servable profitably at that price**.
	- **Xiaomi MiMo 2.5 Pro UltraSpeed**: **~1,000 tok/s**, **~3× DeepSeek V4 Pro price**, 1M context — a **Chinese entrant directly competing on the speed axis** OpenAI/Cerebras are touting.
	- Caveats aired: open models need **xhigh/max reasoning effort** to shine (long reasoning chains), **DeepSeek trains on API data**, and **US-gov export/serving restrictions on Chinese models** are a live risk. **Read-through**: the cheap/mid tier is **commoditizing fast toward open-weight**, compressing frontier-lab margins there and reinforcing why labs chase frontier + custom silicon.

- ## Regulatory / model-gating overhang (cross-cutting)
	- GPT-5.6 preview is **US-government-gated** ("limited preview for trusted partners whose participation has been shared with the government") over **cyber/bio misuse** concerns — same regime that **held back Anthropic's Fable/Mythos** (Fable public only ~3 days). Commenters allege **corruption ($25M to Trump)**; regardless, **model availability is now a policy variable**, and **geographic gating (EU/rest-of-world getting weaker models)** is an emerging structural risk to the whole sector's TAM assumptions. Also validates **open-weight models as the un-gateable fallback**.

- ## Bottom Line (for the names)
	- **Cerebras ($CBRS)**: GPT-5.6 Sol is a **marquee validation** but the **"up to 750 / select customers / ~150 t/s real / ~750B param ceiling / prohibitive cost / short interest"** cluster says the **wafer-scale economics remain the constraint**. Pairs with the Jalapeño memo's read that **its anchor customer (OpenAI) is simultaneously building its own inference ASIC** — durable single-customer risk.
	- **Nvidia ($NVDA)**: still sets the **real throughput bar** (Blackwell ~1,000 TPS/user via spec-decoding) at better cost/batching; specialized-silicon entrants nibble at inference but **haven't broken the memory-bandwidth economics** it also rides.
	- **HBM/DRAM ($MU, SK Hynix, Samsung)**: every thread — Cerebras SRAM, Nvidia HBM, edge LPDDR, Taalas — **returns to memory bandwidth as the binding constraint**; structurally supportive of [[DRAM-memory-ssd-index-thesis]].
	- **Frontier labs (OpenAI/Anthropic)**: **defending margins via cheap-tier deprecation + stealth cache fees + subagent token inflation**, while **open-weight Chinese models capture the low end** and **regulation gates the high end** — a **two-sided squeeze** worth tracking into the OpenAI IPO.
