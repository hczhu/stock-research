- ## Qualcomm’s China Android business bottomed as the global handset market contracted
	- **Thesis**: Memory shortages and higher prices cut handset build plans and prompted OEMs to draw down inventory. Leaner inventory gives Qualcomm’s Android chip sales room to recover sequentially in a weak global market, while Apple’s shift to its own modem continues to weigh on total handset revenue.
	- **Source**: Qualcomm’s July 29 FY2026 Q3 earnings presentation (slides 5, 7–8, including both supplied screenshots) and November 4 FY2026 Q4 earnings call (pp. 3–5, 7–8, 10). Fiscal Q3 is the June quarter and fiscal Q4 is the September quarter. [Qualcomm earnings materials](https://investor.qualcomm.com/financial-info-sec-filings/historical-financial-results/default.aspx)
	- | Market or revenue measure | Period | Qualcomm disclosure |
	  | --- | --- | --- |
	  | QCT Android handset revenue, historical | FY2020–FY2025 | **12% CAGR**. The chart shows roughly \$12B rising to \$21B, read from the bar heights. |
	  | QCT Android handset revenue, July forecast | FY2026 vs. FY2025 | **~20% decline**, with an estimated **annual EPS hit greater than $1.50** attributed to memory pricing and supply constraints. |
	  | QCT handset revenue from China OEMs | FY2026 Q3 to Q4 | July outlook called for a June-quarter bottom and **double-digit sequential growth** in the September quarter. In November, management confirmed the trough and sequential growth for China Android and indicated further growth into December. The call gave no actual growth percentage for that cohort. |
	  | Global handset units, November outlook | Calendar 2026 and 2027 | Qualcomm projects **low- to mid-teens contraction in 2026** and assumes **flat to slightly down in 2027**. |
	- The FY2026 10-K attributes the decline in QCT handset shipments chiefly to major OEMs cutting builds to lower inventories as memory became more expensive and harder to source. By November, management said channel inventory remained thin and assumed the FY2026 drawdown would not repeat in FY2027. If inventories stay lean, Qualcomm’s Android chip shipments can track handset production more closely even with muted end demand. [FY2026 Form 10-K, pp. 40–42](https://investor.qualcomm.com/files/content_files/QCOM-09-27-26-10-K_Final-v2-IR.pdf)

- ## Reported handset results and the Apple modem transition
	- | Reported metric | FY2026 Q3 | FY2026 Q4 | FY2026 |
	  | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
	  | QCT handset revenue, including Apple | \$5.086B vs. \$6.328B a year earlier (−20%) | \$5.137B vs. \$6.961B a year earlier (−26%, +1% sequentially) | \$24.072B vs. \$27.793B a year earlier (−13%) |
	  | QCT earnings before tax as % of revenue | 26% | 24%, down 5 points YoY | 27%, down 3 points YoY |
	- The July forecast covered Android handset revenue. The reported handset line includes Apple. [FY2026 Q4 earnings release, pp. 3–4](https://investor.qualcomm.com/files/content_files/FY2026-4th-Quarter-Earnings-Release.pdf)
	- **Apple modem share.** In July, Qualcomm said its own supply constraints accelerated the expected decline in Apple product revenue starting in fiscal Q4. It expected its share of modems for the upcoming iPhone launch to be **materially below its prior 20% estimate**. Apple’s transition to in-house modems changes Qualcomm’s chip content per iPhone and makes the supplier’s revenue a poor proxy for iPhone unit demand. [FY2026 Form 10-K, p. 42](https://investor.qualcomm.com/files/content_files/QCOM-09-27-26-10-K_Final-v2-IR.pdf)
	- **December-quarter guidance.** Qualcomm expects total QCT handset revenue to decline **mid-single digits sequentially** in FY2027 Q1 as Apple’s ramp-down outweighs Android growth. The separate Apple patent license was renewed, and management said the QTL revenue run rate remained unchanged after renewals with Apple, Huawei and Transsion.

- ## Higher semiconductor costs reach consumer products and squeeze margins
	- **Two cost pressures.** OEMs face higher memory prices and limited availability when planning device production. Qualcomm also faces higher input costs across wafer fabrication, assembly, test, advanced packaging, memory and other materials. The 10-K links broader cost and capacity pressure partly to demand for leading-edge technology, AI and data center applications.
	- **Chip price increases take time to reach margins.** Qualcomm started pricing newer platforms and phone models at higher levels. The CFO expects the benefit to begin in fiscal Q2 FY2027 and be fully reflected in the second half. He expects baseline gross margin to recover to the low end of its historical **48%–50%** range, before the dilution from data center custom silicon. The timing makes a sequential Android recovery an earlier signal than a gross-margin recovery.
	- **Memory constraints extend beyond handsets.** In fiscal Q4, QCT IoT revenue grew **4% YoY to $1.888B**. Industrial, networking and robotics products grew **20%**, while memory constraints weighed on consumer products. Qualcomm’s IoT segment growth combines these different trends and gives limited visibility into the size of the consumer headwind. [FY2026 Q4 earnings release, p. 4](https://investor.qualcomm.com/files/content_files/FY2026-4th-Quarter-Earnings-Release.pdf)
	- **More chip content per premium phone.** Management expects agent features on flagship Android devices to raise compute content per device. That could support revenue per handset even if the replacement cycle stays weak. The November unit outlook remains the baseline for modeling industry volume.
