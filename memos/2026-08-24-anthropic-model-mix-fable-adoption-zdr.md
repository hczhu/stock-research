- ## Anthropic's model mix: Opus 5 drove July growth, not Fable 5 — and the crowd blames data retention
	- **Source**: Newsletter commentary on an *FT* chart showing the spend split across Anthropic models, **indexed to June**. Chart: [FT spend-split by Anthropic model](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VTvm!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8b720a6f-4d4e-48c4-bdb3-2349005e0cf9_1600x1097.jpeg). Includes a **Martin Casado** thread (August 24, 2026) canvassing explanations.
	- **The observation**: July growth was **driven by Opus 5, not Fable 5**, prompting the question of why Fable's adoption has been lacklustre.
	- **The crowd's answer**: **zero data retention (ZDR)**. Casado's own follow-up — *"Consensus on this seems to be ZDR which tracks to my own experience as well. If that really turns out to be the case, it's an incredibly strong and clear message to the labs on the impact of data retention policies."*
	- **Thesis**: If ZDR is genuinely the binding constraint, it is **the cheapest problem in the industry to fix** — a policy decision rather than a training run or a margin concession — which makes the share loss self-inflicted and reversible. But it is not free, because **retention is where the scarce training data now comes from.** That trade-off is the real content and nobody in the thread names it.
- ## Handle the attribution carefully — it is crowd sourcing, not analysis
	- **Casado himself said the cause was not obvious**, and listed the competing candidates before the consensus formed: **cost, speed, aggressive moves from OpenAI, ZDR, open source, bad press or vibes, refusal to work in some domains, and biased data.** None of these has been ruled out.
	- **The sample is small and self-selected**: a tweet with ~13.4K views and ~15 replies, drawn from the enterprise-adjacent AI Twitter population — precisely the group most likely to encounter procurement blockers, and therefore most likely to name one.
	- **The chart is indexed, which hides levels.** A model can drive growth off a small base while remaining a minor share.
	- **Timing matters here specifically**: per [[2026-08-28-ft-anthropic-openai-run-rates-ramp-ai-index]], **Opus 5 shipped on July 24**, so "Opus 5 drove July growth" describes roughly one week of a launch ramp. **That is a launch spike, not demonstrated durable share.**
- ## This updates what is already on file, and possibly corrects it
	- **The Ramp AI Index for July** put **Fable 5 at 8.0%** of Anthropic model spend against **Opus 5 at 3.5%**. So Fable has *more share* but *less growth* — the phenomenon is **lacklustre adoption, not absent adoption**, and the two datasets are consistent.
	- **But the attribution differs, and mine may have been wrong.** In that memo I recorded Simon Willison's reading that **Fable's cost** explained its placement, and added only the methodological caveat that a spend-denominated index inflates high-priced models. **This piece points at data retention instead** — a completely different mechanism with completely different implications. A cost problem is solved with price; a retention problem is solved with a contract term.
	- **Third independent datapoint on the same thread**: [[2026-08-26-ainews-nvidia-q2-hf-confirmed-glm53-flash-economics]] recorded practitioners citing **the absence of zero data retention as a hard enterprise blocker**, with one stating their company avoids the product for that reason alone.
	- **So three separate sources now converge on the same segment-level friction.** That is meaningfully stronger than any one of them, and it shifts the weight of evidence away from the cost explanation I filed earlier.
- ## Why ZDR is a segment lock rather than a preference
	- Zero data retention means the provider does not keep prompts or completions after processing. **For regulated verticals — financial services, healthcare, legal, government — it is frequently a contractual precondition, not a nice-to-have.**
	- **The consequence is binary, not marginal**: without it, a model is **procurement-ineligible regardless of capability or price**. That is why the effect can show up as a flat adoption curve for an otherwise strong model, and why it would not respond to discounting.
	- **It also explains the shape of the data.** A capability gap produces gradual share loss; a price gap produces migration to cheaper tiers. **A compliance blocker produces exactly what the chart shows — a model that is used, but not adopted by the segment that would scale it.**
	- OpenAI publishing on the topic weeks earlier suggests **this is being contested as a competitive axis**, not merely a policy detail.
- ## The trade-off the thread does not name
	- **Retention is where the valuable training data now comes from.** The scaling memos already on file establish that **random human preference data has lost its value** because the median served model exceeds the median rater, and that what labs now need is **expert signal and RL environments** rather than more internet text.
	- **Enterprise usage traces are exactly that expert signal** — lawyers, analysts, engineers, and clinicians solving real problems, with implicit correctness feedback. **A blanket ZDR commitment forecloses the highest-quality data source a lab has access to.**
	- **So the decision is enterprise revenue today versus training data tomorrow**, and it is genuinely hard rather than an oversight. The likely resolution is tiering — ZDR as a paid enterprise entitlement, retention as the default elsewhere — which is roughly where the industry already sits, with the fight being over which tier gets it and at what price.
	- **The contrast at the other end of the spectrum is instructive**: [[2026-07-16-kimi-k3-open-weight-frontier-commoditization]] records **Moonshot training on API content by default** with enterprise opt-out. **Western labs' ability to offer credible ZDR is a differentiator against open-weight Chinese models in regulated verticals** — if they choose to use it rather than trade it away for data.
- ## What would settle it, and what to watch
	- **The decisive test is a policy change with an observable response.** If Anthropic extends ZDR to Fable-class access and the adoption curve inflects, the hypothesis is confirmed cleanly. **This is unusually falsifiable for a market-share question**, because the intervention is a single, dated, announceable decision.
	- **What would falsify it**: Fable adoption remaining flat after a retention change, which would point back to Casado's other candidates — most plausibly cost, domain refusals, or simple displacement by Opus 5 within the same vendor.
	- **The confound to keep in view**: Fable and Opus 5 are **both Anthropic products**. Share moving between them is not necessarily lost business — it may be internal cannibalization on a launch, which is a different and much less concerning fact than losing enterprise seats to a competitor.
	- **The datapoint I would most want** is Fable adoption split by **regulated versus unregulated verticals**. If the shortfall concentrates in finance, healthcare, and legal, ZDR is almost certainly the cause; if it is uniform across sectors, it is not.
