- **Source**: Exponential View, *The State of the AI Economy*, June 25, 2026, by Azeem Azhar, William Gildea, Hannah Petrovic, Nathan Warren, and Marija Gavrilov. Full 66-page PDF supplied by the user; an earlier version of this memo used selected Exponential View / Ramp charts and a supplementary Reddit screenshot.
- **Thesis**: Realized GenAI revenue and highly elastic token demand validate the AI buildout more strongly than adoption proxies alone, but returns are not yet secure. Infrastructure revenue only recently cleared depreciation, external financing is rising, and falling token prices must continue to stimulate more-than-proportional usage. Near-term value remains concentrated in scarce compute, memory, power, and cloud capacity; over time, value should migrate toward differentiated applications while yesterday's frontier models commoditize.

- ## Key Takeaways
	- **Demand is real and accelerating**: Exponential View estimates **\$110B of deduplicated trailing-12-month GenAI revenue** and a **\$175B annualized run rate** by June 2026. The industry now adds another \$1B of cumulative revenue in under two days, versus 180 days in 2023.
	- **Growth has survived multiple product eras**: quarterly revenue growth remained about **35% in the latest period**, equivalent to **3.2x annualized**, as usage shifted from chatbot subscriptions toward coding agents.
	- **The market is simultaneously large and early**: AI revenue is only **0.42% of US GDP**, **0.8% of labor costs**, and **3.0% of corporate profits**. Enterprise AI can grow for years before becoming a large P&L line item.
	- **The capex test has only just begun**: hyperscalers and neoclouds will have invested about **\$2T cumulatively through 2026E**, including **\$535B above the pre-AI trend**. Q1 2026 infrastructure revenue had only **19% headroom after depreciation**, before other operating costs.
	- **Token deflation is expanding rather than shrinking the revenue pool**: global token volume exceeds **30 quadrillion per month** and grew **14x YoY**. The report estimates demand elasticity of roughly **1.2-1.8**, meaning a 10% price cut has historically coincided with 12-18% more usage and higher total spend.
	- **Agents intensify compute consumption**: the report's workload comparison estimates about **4.17M tokens per agentic coding task**, roughly **1,200x** a multi-turn coding chat. The mix shift from chat to agents can offset rapid hardware and serving efficiency gains.
	- **Value is beginning to move up the stack**: hosting still captured **82%** of deduplicated GenAI revenue in Q1 2026, but foundation models reached **11%** and apps **7%**; the combined model-and-app layer expanded **2.95x in one year**.
	- **Model pricing power is temporary**: closed labs earn premium prices only while they hold the capability frontier. Open-weight models rapidly commoditize prior-generation quality, pushing labs into applications and infrastructure to defend economics.

- ## Demand and Adoption Trends
	- **Contracted demand is visible but not purely AI**: combined reported backlog reached about **\$2.0T**, led by Microsoft at \$633B, Oracle at \$553B, Google at \$468B, and Amazon at roughly \$364B. Definitions differ and include non-AI obligations, so backlog is directional evidence rather than a clean AI revenue forecast.
	- **Power demand has broken a long stagnation**: US electricity generation was roughly flat from 2008-2024 but is now increasing by about **9 TWh per month on an annual-growth basis**, versus about 6 TWh per month during the 1950-2008 expansion.
	- **The build is becoming more silicon-intensive**: chips rose from **40% to 60%** of data-center build cost between 2021 and 2026E. Memory alone rose from **2% to about 18%**, the largest mix shift in the stack.
	- **Consumer value exceeds measured revenue**: survey-implied US consumer surplus from GenAI was **\$3-4B per month**, about **30% above monthly revenue** in the report's comparison. Free and bundled usage may create real welfare before appearing in GDP or vendor revenue.
	- **Corporate claims remain efficiency-led**: 70% of quantified S&P 500 GenAI claims concerned cost reduction, time savings, or throughput. Only 6% claimed direct revenue gains and 7% cited conversion improvements.
	- **AI impact reporting is broadening**: companies claiming AI impact on earnings calls increased **3-4x since 2023**, and 50-60% of claims are now quantified, yet most S&P 500 firms still report no quantified bottom-line effect.
	- **Heavy adopters are growing faster, with causality unresolved**: among 70,000 Ramp customers, top-quartile AI spenders by revenue share outgrew firms with no AI usage by **92 percentage points** since November 2022. Faster-growing firms may also be more willing to adopt AI, so the result should not be read as a clean treatment effect.

- ## Capex Payback and Unit Economics
- | Indicator | Report evidence | Investment interpretation |
  |---|---|---|
  | Quarterly payback crossover | Quarterly GenAI infrastructure revenue first exceeded depreciation in Q4 2025 | The build is no longer entirely pre-revenue, but the margin of safety is new and thin |
  | Q1 2026 headroom | 19% for hyperscaler/neocloud revenue; 32% for all GenAI revenue | Depreciation absorbs 81% of infrastructure revenue and 68% of total ecosystem revenue before other costs |
  | Cumulative payback | Cumulative revenue has covered only about half the depreciation plus expected headroom | Current-period profitability does not mean the historical build has earned its cost of capital |
  | H100 one-year rental price | \$3.05/hour at launch, \$1.70 trough, \$2.40 latest | Reacceleration suggests inference demand is absorbing merchant supply despite newer GPUs arriving |
  | One GW annual ownership cost | \$7.9B: 89% capital cost and 11% opex | Capital structure and utilization dominate economics; power is material but not the largest cost |
  | One GW hardware footprint | 480,000 GPUs in 6,700 GB200 NVL72 systems | A single gigawatt represents an enormous procurement and operating commitment |
  | Open-weight inference cost | About \$0.10 per million tokens | Requires roughly \$0.20-\$0.40 selling price for 50-75% gross margin |
  | Closed-model inference cost | About \$0.42 per million tokens including an illustrative \$0.32 license fee | Required price rises to \$0.84-\$1.68, exposing the economic burden of model royalties |
  | GPU useful life | Older accelerators continue generating rental yields after six years | Longer economic lives can lift reported infrastructure headroom from 19% at six years to 36% at eight years |
	- **Central tension**: extending useful lives improves near-term accounting returns, but only if old GPUs remain economically productive. Shorter obsolescence cycles would reverse the reported headroom and expose overbuild.
	- **Financing risk is migrating**: hyperscalers still fund mainly from operating cash flow, while neoclouds rely more heavily on leases and debt. The capacity risk moves from equity holders of cash-rich platforms toward lenders, lessors, and specialized infrastructure investors.

- ## Token Economics and Agentic Demand
	- **Usage is expanding faster than price declines**: the report's cross-provider elasticity range of **1.2-1.8** implies more-than-unit elasticity. Google's time series produced a coefficient near **-1.7 with R² of 0.93**, though common time trends may overstate causal price sensitivity.
	- **Provider examples reinforce the direction**: Google reported token volume increasing from 9.7T to more than 480T per month as price fell 97%; ByteDance's Doubao rose from 4T to more than 50T daily tokens as price fell 50%.
	- **Reasoning raises the cost per visible answer**: processed tokens per output token increased from 12 to 36. Output-token pricing alone can obscure the growing input and hidden-reasoning workload required for better answers.
	- **Quality-adjusted output is compounding**: from January 2025 to April 2026, total tokens grew 39x, output tokens 30x, and three quality-adjusted measures grew 33-59x. Even conservative adjustment suggests useful output is rising, not merely machine-to-machine verbosity.
	- **Token pricing enables attribution but is not value itself**: tokens resemble early pageviews or megabytes—a measurable billing unit, not the economic outcome. The report proposes capability-adjusted output tokens as a better interim metric, while task completion, retention, and customer ROI remain the ultimate tests.
	- **Prediction**: as agents take on multi-step work, token governance, model routing, caching, and cost attribution will become core enterprise infrastructure. Vendors that reduce cost per completed task can win even if they do not minimize token count.

- ## Value Capture Across the Stack
	- **Hosting remains dominant but is losing share**: the deduplicated layer mix shifted from approximately 89% hosting / 8% models / 3% apps, to 82% / 11% / 7% by Q1 2026.
	- **Compute scarcity supports near-term upstream margins**: Nvidia remains the largest compute provider, but custom silicon from Google and AWS increases vertical integration and may reduce the merchant GPU share over time.
	- **Foundation-model concentration is fragile**: OpenAI and Anthropic lead closed-model revenue, yet open-weight competition offers low-cost quality tokens. Among self-selecting OpenRouter users, Google/OpenAI/Anthropic token share fell from roughly 72% to 33%; this is directional rather than market-wide.
	- **Labs are moving vertically to defend margins**: application products such as coding and legal agents capture workflow value, while infrastructure ownership can reduce hosting leakage. Vertical expansion also puts labs into direct competition with customers and partners.
	- **Application differentiation must deepen**: generic wrappers face the risk that general-purpose frontier models absorb integration work. Durable apps need proprietary data, domain workflows, distribution, trust, compliance, or their own specialized models.
	- **Long-run beneficiary may be the customer**: if open weights compress model royalties, efficient models reduce compute needs, and general-purpose agents compress application margins simultaneously, much of the surplus flows to users rather than public vendors.

- ## Report-Derived Predictions
	- **Revenue growth should remain infrastructure-supportive through the agent transition**: if token elasticity stays above one and agentic workloads remain orders of magnitude more token-intensive than chat, falling unit prices can coexist with rising aggregate inference revenue.
	- **The bottleneck should broaden from GPUs to the full data-center system**: memory, networking, power generation, grid equipment, cooling, land, and interconnection become binding as campuses scale from hundreds of megawatts to gigawatts. GPU availability alone will no longer determine deployment speed.
	- **Depreciation becomes the decisive hyperscaler KPI**: revenue growth currently covers new depreciation with little headroom. Investors should increasingly distinguish cash capex, assets placed in service, depreciation schedules, utilization, and AI-attributable revenue rather than treating capex guidance as a single demand signal.
	- **Neocloud outcomes should bifurcate**: sustained rental-rate recovery and high utilization can create operating leverage, while lower pricing, customer concentration, refinancing pressure, or shorter GPU lives can impair equity quickly because debt and leases fix the cost base.
	- **Memory should retain more data-center wallet share**: reasoning, KV cache, larger context windows, and agentic workloads increase memory intensity even when models become more efficient. The report's 2%-to-18% build-cost shift is unlikely to reverse rapidly if inference remains the dominant workload.
	- **Foundation-model margins should compress outside the frontier**: open weights turn prior-generation capability into a commodity. Closed labs must repeatedly advance the frontier, lower serving costs, or capture workflow revenue through applications to preserve premium pricing.
	- **Applications should gain revenue share but not uniformly**: domain-specific products with proprietary context and measurable outcomes can capture more value, while generic wrappers are vulnerable to model vendors integrating the same features.
	- **AI's measured macro impact should lag its economic impact**: free consumer tools, bundled software features, and internal productivity gains create surplus before appearing as direct AI revenue. Revenue-to-GDP comparisons may understate adoption even as they correctly show low current penetration.

- ## Stock Read-Throughs
	- **[[$NVDA]] — strongest near-term demand validation, with longer-term share and replacement-cycle questions**: the move from \$31B to \$95B of supply commitments, improving H100 rental rates, and 50x data-center power scaling support continued accelerator demand. The offsets are hyperscaler custom silicon, open-model efficiency, and evidence that older GPUs can remain productive well beyond six years, which could lengthen replacement cycles.
	- **[[$AMD]] — expanding inference demand creates room for a second merchant supplier**: a 14x token-volume growth rate and more-than-unit price elasticity enlarge the accelerator market faster than one vendor may be able to serve. Realized upside still depends on software maturity, system availability, networking, and customer willingness to qualify an alternative platform.
	- **[[$AVGO]] — custom compute and networking gain strategic importance**: Google and AWS vertical integration reduces dependence on merchant GPUs, while gigawatt clusters require high-value switching and interconnect. This broadens AI semiconductor value capture beyond accelerators, even if custom silicon moderates Nvidia's share.
	- **[[$MU]] and global memory suppliers — clearest mix-share beneficiary**: memory rose from 2% to about 18% of data-center build cost, while chips as a whole rose from 40% to 60%. Agentic and reasoning workloads reinforce HBM and server-memory intensity; cyclicality and supply additions still determine how much demand becomes durable pricing.
	- **[[$MSFT]], [[$AMZN]], [[$GOOGL]], and [[$ORCL]] — backlog validates demand, depreciation determines returns**: cloud contracts and AI revenue are catching up with the build, but infrastructure revenue had only 19% post-depreciation headroom in Q1 2026. Custom silicon, utilization, asset life, energy procurement, and pricing discipline will separate attractive platforms from low-return capacity.
	- **[[$META]] — report economics understate its internal AI return path**: Exponential View counts Meta capex but excludes AI-driven advertising uplift and minimally monetized assistants from GenAI revenue. Meta therefore depresses the report's industry payback ratio even if recommendation and advertising gains generate material internal returns.
	- **[[$CRWV]] and [[$NBIS]] — high-beta exposure to utilization and financing conditions**: rising merchant GPU rates are constructive, but neocloud capex is disproportionately debt- and lease-funded. Their equity value is especially sensitive to contracted demand quality, refinancing, residual GPU values, customer concentration, and useful-life assumptions.
	- **Power and electrical-equipment suppliers — demand is shifting from forecast to grid requirement**: expected US load growth through 2030 rose from 24 GW to 166 GW, with data centers representing about 55%. The opportunity spans generation, transmission, transformers, switchgear, backup power, and cooling, but interconnection delays can defer revenue timing.
	- **AI application vendors — favorable share trend, harsher differentiation test**: apps reached 7% of deduplicated revenue and are gaining share, yet foundation labs are entering coding, legal, and other vertical workflows. Public software beneficiaries need observable retention, expansion, or margin improvement rather than an undifferentiated AI feature bundle.

- ## Evidence Boundaries
	- The report's core revenue estimate is a proprietary bottom-up model covering more than 1,000 firms. It combines filings and executive disclosures with leaks, specialist research, proxy metrics, and modeled cloud attribution; not every line item is audited or directly disclosed.
	- Deduplicated revenue assigns value added across apps, models, and hosting so a customer's dollar is counted once. Layer-specific gross revenues should not be summed because the same spend flows down the stack.
	- Revenue scope is global excluding China and excludes chips, financing, AI-driven advertising uplift, AI features embedded in legacy software, and most internally consumed AI. Token-volume scope includes China, so revenue and token totals are not geographically identical.
	- Capex is the report's estimated AI-attributable share of seven hyperscaler and neocloud builders, while disclosed total capex also supports legacy cloud, SaaS, logistics, metaverse, and other businesses.
	- Backlog is not the same as revenue. Timing, cancellation terms, capacity availability, and customer concentration matter.
	- Company definitions are not identical, so cross-company comparisons are directionally useful but not perfectly apples-to-apples.
	- The chart does not isolate AI-only backlog. Some Microsoft, Amazon, and Google obligations include non-AI cloud or software commitments.
	- The historical trend table is visually estimated from the chart, while the latest company values are explicit chart labels.
	- The data-center power table mixes completed systems, announced data centers, and targets under construction. It is best used to understand order-of-magnitude scaling, not as a like-for-like efficiency comparison.
	- Build-cost shares do not indicate absolute dollars. A component can lose share and still grow revenue if total AI data-center capex expands fast enough.
