- ## TPU resale guarantees: financing aid with residual-value risk
	- **Thesis**: Google is using conditional repurchase rights to make TPU system sales more financeable for customers while retaining a potential route to recover and redeploy hardware. The arrangement can support adoption of Google’s custom silicon, but leaves Google exposed if hardware resale values fall faster than the contracted buyback prices imply.
	- **Source**: Alphabet Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, quarter ended September 30, 2026 ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm); [filing text mirror](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/GOOG/10-q-alphabet-inc-quarterly-earnings-report-9e9e43ebd2ea.html)).
- ## Key disclosures
	- Alphabet says Google Cloud product sales primarily consist of TPU systems—hardware, software, installation, support, and extended warranty. Some customer arrangements include multi-year options requiring Google to repurchase hardware and embedded software at pre-set prices; these are generally accounted for as rights of return. If a customer assigns systems to another party but retains the repurchase right, Google accounts for it as a financial guarantee.
	- | Disclosure | Amount / timing | Context |
	  |---|---:|---|
	  | Maximum potential future payments under TPU repurchase guarantees | $6.6B at Sep. 30, 2026 | Gross maximum exposure, conditional on the contract terms and assets being returned in working order; not a forecast of expected cash payments |
	  | Fair value of guarantee obligations | Not material | Accounting estimate of the obligations’ value, not proof that the maximum exposure is risk-free or impossible to trigger |
	  | TPU systems supplied under signed customer agreements | Limited number of agreements | Customers require or provide on-premises infrastructure for specialized, high-scale workloads |
	  | Revenue timing for those agreements | Began in Q2 2026; significant majority expected in 2027 | Commercial contribution is weighted toward a future period rather than already fully reflected in current sales |
	- Alphabet states that the repurchase prices are below original cost in its risk disclosure. It also says that, given the size of the discount and other factors, counterparties have no economic incentive to exercise the repurchase option. The filing does not disclose individual contract prices, underlying hardware, or customer names.
- ## Commercial and investment read-through
	- **Customer financing and adoption**: A contractual resale floor can reduce a buyer’s exposure to the hardware’s residual value and make a large, specialized system purchase easier to underwrite. This financing benefit is an economic inference; Alphabet does not quantify how much the guarantees changed customer purchasing decisions or financing costs.
	- **Google’s asymmetry versus a merchant chip supplier**: If systems are returned, Google may be able to use them in its own data centers or otherwise remarket them. That is a potential strategic advantage over a chip vendor without a large cloud operation, but redeployment is not automatic: usefulness depends on the TPU generation, software support, power/network configuration, remaining life, and Google’s capacity needs.
	- **Residual-value risk**: The contract puts a floor under the customer’s resale proceeds at a pre-set amount, but that floor is below Google’s original cost. If market value falls below the contractual repurchase price, customers may have more reason to exercise the option and Google may take an economic loss; if systems retain value and Google can put them to work, the realized loss could be smaller than the headline exposure.
	- **Exposure is not a $6.6B expected loss**: Maximum potential payments are the ceiling disclosed for these guarantees. They are neither a recognized expense nor evidence Google expects to pay the full amount. The “not material” fair value and management’s view that exercising is not economically attractive are mitigating signals, while the risk-factor language acknowledges that exercise and redeployment constraints could make costs significant depending on market conditions.
	- **Scale-up signal, not yet proof of TPU economics**: Alphabet began recognizing revenue from a limited set of external TPU-system agreements in Q2 2026 and expects most of that revenue in 2027. The guarantees may help Google extend TPU demand beyond its own cloud, but investors still need evidence of repeat customer demand, profitable system sales after returns/support costs, and durable residual values across hardware generations.
- ## Evidence that would change the read
	- The balance and movement in maximum TPU repurchase exposure, fair value recognized, or any actual repurchases.
	- Whether Alphabet reports more customers or repeat deployments, and whether TPU system revenue becomes material relative to Google Cloud.
	- Any disclosure of repurchase exercise rates, realized recovery values, returned-system redeployment, or hardware impairment / inventory charges.
	- Whether contracted buyback prices remain comfortably below secondary-market values as new TPU generations arrive and older systems depreciate.
