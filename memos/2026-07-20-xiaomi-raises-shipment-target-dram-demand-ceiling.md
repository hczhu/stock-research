- **Source**: 界面新闻 / InterfaceX (36Kr, Jul 20 2026), "Xiaomi raises phone shipment target" (author 李家琦). Supply-chain reporting on Xiaomi lifting its 2026 handset target, framed against the DRAM/NAND super-cycle. Investment framing is mine. Companion to [[DRAM-memory-ssd-index-thesis]], [[2026-07-16-dylan-patel-podcast-ai-infra-memory-cpu-optics-power]], [[Nanya-2026-Q2]], [[2026-07-03-dram-hbm-supercycle-news-roundup-jun-jul-2026]].

- **Thesis frame**: Xiaomi **raising** its 2026 shipment target — after cutting it twice — is the first concrete **demand-side signal** in the memory super-cycle: handset makers "are no longer willing to keep eating rising memory costs." The claim is that **when demand-side tolerance hits the ceiling, the price-increase momentum can inflect (拐点)**. But the same report stresses the **near-term shortage is unchanged** — AI-datacenter HBM/enterprise DRAM demand still surging, and the Big 3 keep cutting consumer-memory output to defend high prices. Net: a **potential top-signal for consumer-memory pricing, not yet a reversal** — and a real-world confirmation of the "inelastic buyers drop out" mechanism the [[2026-07-16-dylan-patel-podcast-ai-infra-memory-cpu-optics-power]] memo predicted.

- ## The Xiaomi Signal
	- **Raised 2026 full-year smartphone target from ~90M → 110M units (+~16%)**; the increment is **mainly low-end models**. Rationale (per a person close to Xiaomi): Xiaomi internally believes **the memory market may be near a reversal**.
	- **Target trajectory this year** (memory-cost driven):
	  
	  | Date | 2026 target | vs prior |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Initial | ~170M (= 2025 actual level) | — |
	  | Jan 2026 | ~135M | cut >20% (memory price hikes) |
	  | Jun 30 (Nikkei) | ~95M | further cut ~30%; **>40% below initial** |
	  | **Jul 2026 (now)** | **110M** | **raised ~16%** off the trough |
	- **Demand rebellion**: OPPO and vivo **recently rejected Samsung's Q3 quote** — even though the increase over the prior two quarters was modest. Read: **downstream tolerance for memory-cost pass-through is topping out.**

- ## The Memory Super-Cycle Backdrop (the data)
	- **Price increases (TrendForce)**:
	  
	  | Period | DRAM | NAND Flash |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Q3 2025 | +171.8% YoY | — |
	  | Q4 2025 | +45–50% (QoQ, on top) | — |
	  | Q1 2026 | +90–95% contract (record single-Q) | +55–60% (record) |
	  | Q2 2026 | +58–64% QoQ | +54–75% QoQ |
	- **The squeeze mechanism**: Samsung / SK Hynix / Micron shifted large advanced-process capacity to higher-margin **HBM + server DRAM**; **~70% of global memory capacity is consumed by data centers**, leaving phones/PCs as the sacrificed residual.
	- **Cheap memory is disappearing**: **Apr 2026 Samsung stopped taking new LPDDR4 orders**; Micron and SK Hynix stopped by end-2025 — the core "cheap memory" for sub-\$1,000 phones is exiting the market.
	- **BOM asymmetry (why low-end gets crushed)**: storage is **>30% of BOM on sub-\$200 phones** vs **<10% on \$800+ phones**. For the same DRAM increase, **low-end retail prices need +40–50% to hold margin; high-end only +5–8%.**

	  | Counterpoint (early Jun) | ~1.08B units | **−13.9%** (from −2.1% initial); **lowest since 2013** |

- ## Why It May *Not* Be a Reversal Yet (supply discipline persists)
	- **Ceiling on tolerance ≠ immediate price drop.** Shortage fact unchanged: (1) AI-datacenter HBM/enterprise-storage demand still surging, Big 3 keep steering advanced capacity to high-margin lines; (2) Samsung, SK Hynix, Kioxia **continue cutting consumer-memory output to defend high prices.**
	- **NAND wafer-output cuts (Omdia, 2026 vs 2025)**:
	  
	  | Maker | 2025 | 2026 |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Samsung | 4.9M wafers | 4.68M |
	  | SK Hynix | ~1.9M | 1.7M |
	  | Kioxia | 4.8M | 4.69M |
	- Big-3 capex is **structurally shifting to HBM + server DRAM**; consumer-memory capacity is further compressed.

- ## Investment Read-Throughs
	- **Memory makers ([[$MU]], SK Hynix, Samsung; [[DRAM-memory-ssd-index-thesis]]) — two-sided, still net-bullish near-term**: continued production cuts + capex-to-HBM + 70%-to-datacenter = **shortage and high prices persist**. But the OPPO/vivo quote rejection and Xiaomi's cut-then-raise are the **first cracks in the "elasticity ceiling"** — exactly the [[2026-07-16-dylan-patel-podcast-ai-infra-memory-cpu-optics-power]] prediction that **inelastic consumer buyers (budget phones) drop out first** as prices rise until "AI gets its fill." This is the demand-destruction pressure valve becoming visible; it's a **top-watch signal for consumer-DRAM pricing momentum**, not (yet) a price reversal.
	- **The Xiaomi trajectory quantifies the "Xiaomi −40%" datapoint** cited in [[2026-07-16-dylan-patel-podcast-ai-infra-memory-cpu-optics-power]] and [[Nanya-2026-Q2]]: 170M → ~95M was the ~−40% cut; **now +16% back to 110M** — a partial recovery premised on a *hoped-for* price inflection, weighted to low-end (the most memory-cost-sensitive tier), which is a **bet, not a confirmation**.
	- **Smartphone volumes / budget OEMs — bearish**: global 2026 units revised to **−13% to −14% (lowest since 2013)**; sub-\$1,000 handsets structurally squeezed, low-end retail must rise 40–50% to hold margin → **unit destruction + forced premium mix-shift**. Bearish budget-phone OEM margins (Xiaomi, Transsion, OPPO/vivo low-end) and the Android budget ecosystem.
	- **Premium relatively insulated ($AAPL / high-end)**: high-end needs only +5–8% price to absorb the same DRAM increase — the **mix shifts toward premium**, and premium OEMs have far more room. Consistent with the "iPhone/MacBook prices up, but they can pass it" read in the Dylan Patel memo.
	- **HBM demand is the root cause and stays intact**: the whole consumer squeeze exists *because* the Big 3 divert advanced capacity to HBM/server DRAM — so this news is, indirectly, **another confirmation of durable AI-memory demand** (bullish HBM/AI-infra leg) even as it flags consumer demand destruction.

- ## What to Monitor
	- Whether more OEMs reject quotes (OPPO/vivo already did) and whether Q3/Q4 contract DRAM/NAND price increases decelerate — the actual inflection tell.
	- Xiaomi's realized shipments vs the raised 110M target (did the price-reversal bet pay off, or does it re-cut a third time?).
	- Big-3 consumer-memory output and any easing of the LPDDR4/low-end allocation freeze.
	- Continued strength of AI-datacenter HBM/enterprise-DRAM demand (the supply-diversion driver) — the swing factor for how long consumer memory stays starved.
