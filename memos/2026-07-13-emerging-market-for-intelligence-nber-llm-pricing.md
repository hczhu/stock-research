- **Source**: Demirer (MIT), Fradkin (BU), Tadelis & Peng (Microsoft Research), "The Emerging Market for Intelligence: Pricing, Supply, and Demand for LLMs," NBER Working Paper 34608, December 2025. Data: scraped OpenRouter (Jul 2023–Dec 2025; >100T tokens served in 2025) + Microsoft Azure AI Foundry firm-level data (Jul 2023–Jun 2025; 100T tokens in 2Q25 alone) + Artificial Analysis benchmarks. The first comprehensive non-survey dataset on enterprise LLM demand. (This is ref. 7 in [[2026-07-12-tsmc-manufacturing-innovation-platform-cusumano]].)

- **Thesis frame**: Six empirical facts about the LLM market with direct read-throughs for AI-lab economics, hyperscaler capex, and the open-vs-closed debate. The two most investable: (1) **open-source models are ~90% cheaper per unit of intelligence yet hold <30% share** — brand/trust/support differentiation is real and defends closed-lab pricing; (2) **short-run price elasticity ≈ −1.1** — near the Jevons-paradox threshold but, since aggregate elasticities are smaller, **price cuts do NOT self-fund via volume in the short run**. Token demand growth comes from capability diffusion and long-run adoption, not price stimulation.

- ## The Six Facts (paper's own structure)
	- **1. Supply is exploding, driven by open source.** Models on OpenRouter: 253 → 651+ during 2025 (434 open vs 217 closed ever listed); creators 43 → 85 (75 open-source vs **only 10 closed-source — unchanged since April 2025**, i.e. the closed frontier is a mature 10-firm club with diminishing returns to entry); inference providers 27 → 90 in one year (DeepInfra, Fireworks, Groq, Cerebras…). 14 open models served by ≥10 providers each; most closed models by a single provider.
	- **2. Prices collapse; dispersion persists.** **GPT-4-class intelligence fell ~1000× in price in ~2 years**; reasoning-class (o1) fell even faster. Bottom-decile models are **50–150× cheaper** than top-decile *at the same point in time*. Median price per unit of intelligence fell from >100 (2023) to <1 (Dec 2025), with per-unit range 0.01–20. Yet the **average price paid per token stayed roughly constant** (prompt ~\$1, completion ~\$2–4) — users continually upgrade to smarter models, spending the deflation dividend on quality.
	- **3. Leadership churns violently.** Top-10 models today = ~60% of usage but had **zero share 10 months ago**; Anthropic, Google, and xAI **each held the #1 creator spot during 2025** (Anthropic peaked ~50% early-2025; Google >40% mid-year via Gemini 2.0/2.5; xAI surged late-August via Grok Code Fast 1; DeepSeek peaked ~20%). Largest creator holds 25–50%, #2 15–30% — concentrated but perpetually reshuffled.
	- **4. Differentiation is both vertical and horizontal.** No model dominates across use cases (Google leads ~half of categories; Anthropic owned programming until Grok Code Fast 1). Demand intelligence varies by category: programming highest (0.51 index), translation lowest (0.32). **Willingness to pay varies 9×**: SEO pays \$1.40/M prompt tokens, programming \$0.84, translation \$0.17, legal \$0.16. **90th-percentile token intelligence (~0.6) sits well below the frontier (>0.7)** — "the premium charged for frontier models may not be justified for many applications."
	- **5. Short-run price elasticity ≈ 1.** Preferred within-model, across-provider estimate: **−1.08 to −1.11**. Naive spec gives −0.55. Since model/provider-level elasticities overstate aggregate elasticity (substitution), the authors read this as **against short-run Jevons paradox**: cutting price does not increase total spend. Long-run Jevons effects remain possible (the industry's token growth is secular adoption, not price response).
	- **6. Multihoming is experimentation, not architecture.** Firms using multiple models rose 25% → 50% (mid-2023 → mid-2025), **but most multihomers still put >90% of usage on one model**. Single-model concentration is the norm — switching costs and workflow integration bind.

- ## Substitution Case Studies (who cannibalizes whom)
	- **Anthropic = brand-centric, within-family substitution**: Claude 3.7 Sonnet's launch cannibalized Claude 3.5 nearly 1-for-1 **with minimal impact on GPT-4o/Gemini** — loyal user base, low intra-Claude switching costs, quality not captured by benchmarks (Claude 3.7 demand persisted even after Claude 4). Bull for Anthropic pricing power; also why its releases don't dent rivals.
	- **Gemini Flash & Grok Code Fast 1 = market expansion**: entered at previously unoccupied price-performance points and gained huge share **without reducing other models' demand**. Grok Code Fast 1 went from 0 to top model within a week via aggressive coding-app partnerships (free on Roo Code) and subsidized pricing — while Claude 4 Sonnet's trend was unaffected.
	- **GPT-4o (Azure) = classic displacement** (steep decline in other models' firm share) vs **DeepSeek R1 (Azure) = no displacement** of top Microsoft-hosted models.
	- **Coding-app channel risk**: between Jul and Oct 2025 the top model on Cline/Kilo/Roo flipped from Claude 4 Sonnet to Grok Code Fast 1 — but all three apps featured Grok **as a free default**, so "some of the switching may reflect default settings or promotional placement rather than informed user choice." **Distribution/default placement is becoming a market-share weapon.**

- ## Investment Read-Throughs
	- **Closed-lab pricing power is real but narrow.** The 90%-cheaper open alternative caps closed pricing *in theory*, yet <30% share and rigid closed prices (0.9% weekly change) show enterprises pay for brand, support, privacy, and unbenchmarked quality. Supports the "labs defend margins" leg of [[2026-07-06-gpt56-sol-hn-cerebras-inference-economics]] — but the moat is *differentiation*, not intelligence per se.
	- **The deflation dividend gets re-spent, not banked**: constant average price paid + 1000× cheaper intelligence = users perpetually upgrade quality. Token revenue for the ecosystem grows with adoption and quality mix, which is the more durable version of the token-demand thesis than "price cuts stimulate volume" (elasticity ~1 says they don't, short-run).
	- **Frontier ≠ where the tokens are.** Most usage sits well below the frontier, and mid-tier models are top choices in many categories — validates the mid/cheap-tier commoditization worry for labs, the opportunity for open-weight/Chinese entrants ([[2026-07-06-gpt56-sol-hn-cerebras-inference-economics]]), and the "inference-optimized silicon for good-enough models" theme ([[2026-07-07-interconnect-first-inference-latency-over-bandwidth-lpddr]], Taalas).
	- **Only 10 closed-source creators, static since April 2025** — the frontier is an oligopoly with a hard entry wall (compute/data/talent), even as open-source entry explodes below it. Consistent with capex-as-moat.
	- **Inference-provider proliferation (27→90) is margin-destructive for commodity hosting** but validates the aggregator/router layer (OpenRouter: 5–5.5% take rate) and speed/reliability differentiation (Cerebras, Groq) — price is *not* the only axis (throughput correlates positively with price; 14% of providers achieve >5× throughput).
	- **Leadership churn is the norm** — 3 different #1 creators in one year cautions against extrapolating any lab's current share (relevant to OpenAI-IPO-era narratives and to Anthropic/Google positioning alike). Category dominance is what persists (Google in ~half of categories; translation near-duopoly HHI ~8,000).
	- **Watch items**: whether open-source share recovers from ~25% (or Grok-style closed value models keep squeezing it); whether default/free placement in coding apps (the Grok playbook) becomes standard — it converts distribution spend directly into token share; long-run elasticity estimates (the actual Jevons test); Azure Foundry token growth as the enterprise proxy.
