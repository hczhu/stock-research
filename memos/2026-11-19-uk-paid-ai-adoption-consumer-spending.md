- ## UK paid AI adoption: monthly payers and consumer spending

	- **Source**: User-provided 12-page PDF, *Who Pays for AI? Paid AI adoption, measured monthly*, release 2026-07, data through July 2026; Nikhil Datta, Peter J. Lambert and Patrick Schneider, supported by Warwick's CAGE and ESRC. Exact monthly figures and intervals checked against the [companion dashboard](https://whopaysfor.ai/). This memo summarizes the dashboard, not the separately linked 64-page working paper.
	- **Coverage**: De-identified records from **14 million UK bank and card accounts**, weighted to represent adults aged 18+. Accounts are the input sample, not 14 million distinct AI users.

- ## Monthly paid-user numbers

	- | Month | Paid users per 1,000 adults | Share of UK adults | Published 95% interval, per 1,000 |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | June 2025 | 23.1 | 2.31% | 18.6-26.1 |
	  | January 2026 | 36.4 | 3.64% | 28.7-40.1 |
	  | June 2026 | 50.6 | 5.06% | 38.0-55.6 |
	  | July 2026 | 52.0 | 5.20% | 39.9-57.4 |
	- The report translates the **June 2026** estimate into **about 2.8 million people**. Its headline rounds that month's adoption to 5.1%; the chart endpoint is July, not June.
	- **Consumer spending**: July 2026 spending was **£1,600 per 1,000 adults**, equivalent to **£1.07 billion annually at that month's rate**. This is an annualized run rate, not realized full-year spending.
	- Paid-user estimates and total spending include direct provider payments plus estimated Apple/Google app-store subscriptions. These are payment-based measures, not active-use telemetry or free-user counts.

- ## Claude gains share of direct subscription spending

	- | Provider | December 2025 spending share | July 2026 spending share |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | ChatGPT | 73.5% | 52.5% |
	  | Claude | 7.4% | 29.4% |
	  | Gemini | 5.8% | 5.1% |
	  | Other | 13.3% | 13.0% |
	- Exact companion-table values; the PDF chart rounds the percentages. These are **spending shares, not paid-user shares**. App-store payments are excluded because bank statements do not identify the product. Accounts from card issuers that hide Claude's name are also excluded, with the remainder reweighted.

- ## Who pays: distinguish current payments from ever having paid

	- | Segment | Reported rate | Definition and period |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Ages 20-29 / 30-39 | 17.9% / 17.5% | Ever paid through July 2026 |
	  | Ages 40-49 / 50-59 / 60+ | 14.1% / 8.1% / 3.0% | Ever paid through July 2026 |
	  | Monthly income under £1,500 / over £4,000 | 10.0% / 18.8% | Ever paid through July 2026 |
	  | Men / women | 6.22% / 4.86% | Paid in July 2026 |
	  | London / North East | 7.85% / 3.76% | Paid in June 2026; highest/lowest regions shown |
	- The age and income measures use accounts active in June 2026; income bands are those recorded **before November 2022**, not current earnings. The youngest displayed age group was **5.9 times** as likely as ages 60+ to have ever paid, or **4.8 times** after adjustment for income, sex and region.

	- | Selected occupation or benchmark | Share paying directly in June 2026 |
	  |---|---|
	  | IT professionals | 20.3% |
	  | University and college lecturers | 16.0% |
	  | Finance and insurance professionals | 15.8% |

	  | All open-banking accounts, chart benchmark | 3.6% |
	  | Social care workers | 1.7% |
	- The report describes a twelvefold occupational spread. Occupations are inferred from professional-body, regulator and union payments plus employer records. Only sufficiently represented occupations are shown. This direct-payment measure excludes estimated app-store subscriptions and is **not comparable on the same basis** with the national best estimate above.

- ## How payments are identified

	- Direct transactions are matched to a registry of **200+ AI products** using statement descriptions, exact prices and launch dates. App-store payments conceal product names, so recurring charges at AI subscription prices are estimated rather than directly identified.
	- The figures measure consumer-paid adoption, not all workplace access or enterprise seats. Provider spending shares cannot be used to allocate the national paid-person count among individual services.
