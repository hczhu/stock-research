- ## Stratechery on Nvidia's Quarter — The First-Party Dollars-Per-Gigawatt Numbers, and Avoiding a Consolidated World
	- **Source**: Ben Thompson, "Nvidia Earnings, Dollars Per Gigawatt, Open and Hugging Face," Stratechery Update, Sep 1 2026, quoting Bloomberg and the earnings call (CFO Colette Kress, CEO Jensen Huang).
	- **Why this one matters to the repo**: it supplies **Nvidia's own per-gigawatt revenue figures**, which reconcile against three conflicting third-party estimates already on file. There is no NVDA earnings note in `earnings/`; this is analysis of the call, not the call itself.
	- **Thesis**: Thompson's organizing line is that **everything Nvidia does is aimed at avoiding a consolidated world** — because a fragmented market of buyers who lack the scale to disaggregate the stack is precisely where Nvidia's margin survives. The quarter itself is a supply disclosure, not a demand disclosure. And the per-GW numbers show the real growth mechanism is **Nvidia capturing a rising share of a gigawatt**, not gigawatts alone.
- ## The forecast is a statement about supply
	- | Metric | Figure |
	  |---|---|
	  | FY guidance, next fiscal year | **+70% sales growth** |
	  | Average analyst estimate | **+45%** |
	  | Prior-year growth | **100%** |
	  | Share reaction | **+8.7% to \$227.98**, biggest one-day rally since April 2025 |
	  | Market value added in a day | **~\$442 billion** |
	  | Stock, year to date | **+22%** |
	- **The guide is capped by supply, and Huang says so outright**: "even though our demand is much greater than 70%, our supply allows us to confidently deliver 70%." Kress adds that customer forecasts "point to our growth doubling next year."
	- **Nvidia has never guided a full year before.** Huang's stated reason is coordination, not confidence: "we wanted to be consistent with everybody, from our customers, our shareholders, our supply chain, everybody sees the same view... because everybody is putting a lot of resources at play." Read it as a **supply-chain signalling mechanism** — Nvidia publishing a commitment so that memory, power, land and shell vendors size their own capex against a shared number.
	- **Thompson's framing is the durable one**: the top line is a function of how much Nvidia can build, so **"we won't know what demand truly is until supply is abundant."** His analogy is early iPhone — forecasting was easy and reliable for years because revenue was a function of units producible and carriers supported, and true demand was unknown until the 6S generation when supply was sufficient and every carrier had it.
	- **The self-aware caveat is worth preserving.** Thompson notes he keeps assuming he is insufficiently bullish, then quotes his own **February 2024** piece making this identical supply-constraint argument — two and a half years earlier. The observation has been continuously true and continuously under-priced, which is either strong evidence for the thesis or evidence that the framing is unfalsifiable. Consistent with the supply-constraint conclusion in [[2026-09-05-circuit-broadcom-custom-asic-unmodelable-2027-peak-constraint]].
- ## Dollars per gigawatt — the first-party numbers, and what they settle
	- Kress, on the call: "our revenue opportunity has grown from roughly **\$18 billion per gigawatt** [Hopper] to **\$25 billion with Blackwell**, to **\$40 billion with Vera Rubin**."
	- Vera Rubin's scope now spans Vera CPU, Rubin GPU, NVLink, InfiniBand or Ethernet, **and a "Groq LPU announced earlier this week"** — recorded as stated; this memo does not establish the deal structure behind that inclusion, and it is worth chasing separately.
	- Claimed generational gain: **30× higher throughput per megawatt and 35× lower token costs** relative to Grace Blackwell Ultra.
	- **The derived number is the point, and Nvidia does not state it**: per-GW capture is **+39% Hopper→Blackwell, +60% Blackwell→Vera Rubin, and +122% Hopper→Vera Rubin.** Growth is not primarily a gigawatt story; it is Nvidia taking a larger slice of each one.
	- **This reconciles the conflicting estimates already in the repo, and the reconciliation is clean**:
		- Balaban's **\$35–45B/GW for compute servers** ([[2026-06-18-mad-podcast-stephen-balaban-lambda-ai-compute-neoclouds]]) brackets Vera Rubin's \$40B almost exactly — so **Nvidia's figure is its capture, not the build cost.**
		- Balaban's **total AI factory at \$47–63B/GW** (servers plus shell plus generation) and WSJ's **\$80–100B/GW** for a complete deployment ([[2026-07-13-wsj-data-center-builders-stake-sales]]) are total-project numbers and are not comparable to Kress's.
		- Against a ~\$50–60B/GW total build, Nvidia's share moves from roughly **30–36% under Hopper to 67–80% under Vera Rubin.** That is what "expanding our share of the data center TAM" means quantitatively.
		- **It also corrects a figure in this repo.** The Circuit hosts cited Nvidia at "\$40–50B per GW going to \$90–100B." The lower bound matches Vera Rubin; **the upper figure is roughly 2.3–2.5× Nvidia's own total-platform disclosure** and appears to conflate Nvidia capture with total deployment cost — exactly the confusion those hosts themselves warned about when they said they would "love to see the spreadsheet behind both those math."
	- **Practical consequence for modelling**: gigawatt counts and Nvidia revenue are not linearly related across generations, and per-GW capture has a ceiling — it cannot exceed the total build. A capture ratio already near two-thirds of a full deployment means **this particular growth lever is closer to exhausted than the +122% trend implies.**
- ## Where the margin actually breaks
	- Huang's moat argument is that integration wins when everything else is hard: "you can no longer procure technology per se and stand up this infrastructure. You've got to go secure the land, power and shell, which oftentimes is a couple 2, 3 years out," plus construction, power, cooling and labor. Nvidia works upstream with memory suppliers, power generators and land-power-shell developers.
	- Thompson's gloss is the cleanest statement of the moat: **"when you have so many other problems to solve, worrying about Nvidia's margins is often at the bottom of the list... when there are many difficult pieces, the integrated piece that solves multiple problems at once is particularly valuable."**
	- **Then he states the exception precisely, and it is the most useful sentence in the piece**: that holds "unless you have the sort of scale that makes it possible and profitable to solve every problem you have — **including Nvidia's margins. That's the hyperscalers.** Or, in the case of the frontier labs, have so much demand and such high costs that you both have to figure out a way to capture more margin and have the potential payoff to justify it."
	- **So the margin is safe with everyone except the two buyer classes where volume concentrates.** That is the entire custom-silicon thesis restated from Nvidia's side, and it is why the Broadcom and Jalapeño work in this repo is the relevant counterweight — see [[2026-08-25-semianalysis-jalapeno-specs-tco-vs-rubin]].
- ## The non-hyperscaler half, which is the answer to that exception
	- Huang's counter is that the exception does not cover the market. The non-hyperscaler segment — **sovereign AI, regional AI, neoclouds, AI start-ups and enterprises — "represents about half of our business, and that's growing 100% a year."**
	- The forward claim is larger: **"that part of the world's computing is likely to be larger over time than even what we're currently experiencing in the cloud."**
	- The mechanism is capability, not preference: "customers can still mix and match. However, **most companies just don't have the skills to do that or desire to do that.**"
	- **This is the structurally important disclosure in the quarter.** If half of revenue sits with buyers who cannot disaggregate the stack and that half is compounding at 100%, then hyperscaler and frontier-lab custom silicon compresses Nvidia's *share of a segment* rather than its blended margin. The number to track is whether the non-hyperscaler half holds its growth rate, because it is the load-bearing assumption under the bull case.
- ## The OpenAI chip answer, and what Huang reached for instead
	- Asked about OpenAI's own chip, Huang gave a layered answer: those XPUs are "inference-specific chips for one cloud or one service," Nvidia is "a platform... that spans the entire AI life cycle that you can use in any cloud," AI services "at some point are going to want to go around the world," and "I have 100% confidence."
	- **Thompson does not buy it**: "That's about five different answers, none of them particularly compelling in a potential world where the frontier labs just win everything; perhaps that is why Huang followed up his **answer salad** by reminding investors that **Nvidia holds huge stakes in both companies.** And then he went back to talking about \$/GW."
	- **That is the read worth keeping.** When the strategic answer is weak, the equity stake is the hedge — Nvidia participates in the outcome even where it loses the socket. It is a real mitigant and it is also an admission about the socket.
- ## Open weights as the fragmentation strategy, and why Hugging Face follows
	- Huang on open models: "the world will need both closed models and open models," and "**nearly all open models run on Nvidia**... because Nvidia's footprint around the world is the highest, and our architecture is the most fungible" — PCs, DGX Spark, robots, workstations, on-prem. "The CUDA ecosystem is literally everywhere."
	- **Thompson's framing is the thesis of the whole piece**: "Open weights are the model equivalent to Nvidia's ACIE segment. **As long as the world is fragmented Nvidia wins**; the only way its margins come under pressure is if there are a limited number of buyers who have the means to take only part of Nvidia's stack or replace them completely. In a world of choices, the most fungible architecture with the deepest developer pool has a huge advantage."
	- **On Hugging Face, note the timing carefully.** As of this Sep 1 piece the deal **had not been officially announced** and was reported by The Information. Later repo entries treat it as done at ~\$13B — see [[2026-08-28-ainews-nvidia-huggingface-open-model-specs-agent-security]] and [[2026-09-05-circuit-broadcom-custom-asic-unmodelable-2027-peak-constraint]]. Worth resolving which entries describe a report and which describe a closed transaction.
	- Thompson dismisses the elaborate rationales — reviving DGX Cloud, or absorbing spare compute — as **"overthinking things."** His read: Hugging Face is "a critical Schelling point for open source models, much like GitHub was for open source software a decade ago," and the Microsoft–GitHub precedent applies. **"Nvidia doesn't need to make money from Hugging Face, or mandate that Hugging Face-hosted models use Nvidia chips... simply ensuring that open weights have a meaningful future structurally ensures Nvidia's long-term margin profile."**
	- **The converse is the testable version**: if open weights consolidate onto a small number of closed frontier providers, Nvidia loses the fragmentation that protects its pricing. Watching open-weight share of production inference is therefore a direct read on Nvidia's long-run margin, not merely on model competition.
- ## Two obligations disclosed in passing
	- **\$36 billion in cloud services contracts** committed as part of **credit support agreements with customers**. The Information's framing of the risk: if market conditions shift and customers cannot lease that capacity to third parties, **Nvidia ends up responsible for some of that compute.**
	- The earlier precedent for scale: Nvidia had committed **\$13 billion** to rent back its own chips from cloud providers including AWS for DGX Cloud, a business it stepped away from a year ago, having believed cloud could eventually generate **\$150 billion** in revenue.
	- **These belong in the off-balance-sheet work, not the product story.** A vendor extending credit support against its own customers' ability to sublease capacity is demand-side financing with recourse, and it is the same structure tracked in [[2026-06-11-nvidia-coreweave-nebius-circular-financing]] and [[2026-07-21-bloomberg-big-tech-ai-off-balance-sheet-financing]]. Thompson does not dwell on it; the figure deserves more attention than he gives it.
