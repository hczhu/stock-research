- ## The OPC / Maker Economy — Barbell World Where Model Companies Take Everything (podcast)
	- **Source**: Chinese-language founder/VC podcast (unnamed hosts), recorded shortly after **Claude Opus 4.6** shipped. Speaker runs an ~8-person startup pivoting from a "one-person company (OPC)" thesis into building a **Maker community** product. All facts/arguments below are from this conversation only; no external data.
	- **Thesis**: As frontier coding models improve every ~2-3 months, the software industry collapses into a **barbell (哑铃) structure** — nearly all economic value at the top with **model companies**, a thin sliver at the bottom with **OPCs / Makers** (2-5 person teams doing the work of a 30-50 person org), and the **mid-size SaaS layer squeezed out of existence**. Surviving bottom-tier value is **emotional / experiential**, not utility. Clearest near-term monetization is **AI + crypto / prediction markets** (shortest path from tokens → economic value).

- ## The Mood: "Steamroller Ahead, Cutting Rebar" (压路机前剪钢筋)
	- Ecosystem sentiment is **despair** ("哀鸿遍野") — not just startups, also mature companies. Consensus fear: **"align with the model company's roadmap, or step aside and get eliminated."** Value ultimately sits with the model companies; they capture the vast majority of it.
	- **Cadence problem**: after Opus 4.6, a small team ships ~**1-2 products per person per week**. Each model jump (4.5 → 4.6 → next-gen "客户" rumored) vaporizes the last batch. One month you find a great opportunity; a month later the model is strong enough that your product "有没有那么大价值" and payback keeps sliding.
	- **Key metaphor**: startup value creation is *cutting rebar in front of an oncoming steamroller* — you *can* cut it, but it's still just a piece of rebar the roller flattens anyway.
	- **Counterpoint (the optimism)**: the same force gives a tiny team a **sustained ~100× productivity multiple**. Speaker's own reported example: spun up a brand-new project, "coded it out overnight, finished at 3am, and it just works" once the internal infra is ready.

- ## Why Nobody Ships What They Build
	- Over ~2 months the team built dozens of products; **shipped revenue = zero**. Most are **self-use tools**, never released.
	- **Why build**: obvious — solve your own daily friction, proof productivity.
	- **Why not ship as a company**: serving external market users means solving **operations + growth** — hard. Internal tools are **hyper-personalized** (e.g. one colleague's version-management workflow tool is useless to a teammate with different habits), and **value density is too low** to be worth productizing. Anyone with their own taste/workflow just codes their own.
	- **"Future unevenly distributed"**: tool-enthusiasts already self-build many small tools; most people still buy **mature, standardized products** because they don't have that many painful, individualized problems. Building from scratch is now faster than forking/tweaking someone else's — once **infra is in place** (they unified backend deploy: was self-hosted Supabase + manual backend/data/deploy → now **one command** onto the company's backend).

- ## The Barbell (哑铃) Structure
	- Analogy floated: **software will look like hardware** — ~10 brands / ~10 dominant models, extremely concentrated at the top. Everyday use collapses to a handful of coding agents; most standalone tools (PS, IDEs, vertical tools) **cease to exist**.

	  | Layer | Who | Value capture |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | **Top** | Model companies (Anthropic, OpenAI…) | **Vast majority.** Coding tools ("扣代也") are just the model's tool container. Ship many agent surfaces — a canvas for creation, a terminal for general domains, a design tool, etc. |
	  | **Middle** | Mid-size SaaS / vertical software | **Squeezed to nothing.** No model-scale value; their old moats (software category + cost) evaporate as users self-customize. "很尴尬." |
	  | **Bottom** | **OPCs / Makers** (not literally 1 person — 2-5 people, efficiency of a 30-50 person team) | A **thin but real** sliver. Enabled because model power is so high one coding agent does the work of many, so every long-tail / individualized need becomes buildable cheaply. |

- ## Where Bottom-Tier Value Survives: Emotional / Experiential, Not Utility
	- **Utility value** is captured by "空间" at the top; commoditized. What differentiates at the bottom is **emotional / experience value**.
	- Traditional software historically had **no moat, no brand, no network effect**. But with enough **diversity + differentiated experience**, products can carry **brand and fan loyalty** — even influence the user's *mood*.
	  - Example: a colleague who loves a certain aesthetic built a **note-taking app skinned in cat shoes ("数的鞋子")** — nobody "needs" it, but he loves it → differentiation.
	  - Compared to **art / performance art**: outsiders say "anyone could do that," yet a specific creator's work is trusted and valued because it's *theirs*. **Emotional value can be very high.**
	- Image/design is called out as the **rare domain whose "leaderboard keeps changing"** as base tech shifts (early graphics → early generation → GAN → Diffusion) — each tech step unlocks genuinely new outputs and playstyles.

- ## Distribution: Model-Company App Store? — Speaker Says No
	- Model companies control the **"publisher" (发布器)** — the creation tool is theirs (the coding agent) — but they **do not control the "container" (容器)**: the OS, mobile app, web page, PC software where content is *consumed*.
	- To build a distribution platform you'd have to **price-war the coding agents** ("怎么可能") — creation side must be subsidized, only the **consumption** side monetizes. So generic App-Store plays are unattractive.
	- New content forms demand **new containers** (Douyin defined a container for short video; new AI games define their own). Winning = **correctly judging the new content form and building the container for it.**
	- Today's spillover: **Skills → GitHub**; **new products → Xiaohongshu**. But if a genuinely **new creator group + new culture** emerges, old platforms won't serve it — a **new platform** must appear.

- ## The Speaker's Bet: a Maker Community (Connection Value)
	- Product: ~**8 people, ~1 month**, most-invested effort so far; "改了很多版" and still not right. Core value = **connection (连接价值)** for the new creator group ("真来 vibe-coding 的人" / Makers) — *not* the money-only "岗位价值" crowd.
	- **Creator lifecycle — 3 R's** (they optimize for the middle one):

	  | Stage | Chinese | Meaning |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | 1 | **回应** (response) | People see it; numeric feedback (likes) — table stakes |
	  | 2 | **回想** (recognition / resonance) | **Who** recognizes you — this is *community*; the target |
	  | 3 | **回报** (reward) | Money |
	- Shift from **business model → impact model**: an individual doesn't *have* to monetize (cost of building is near-zero). Companies must monetize (shareholder duty, "资本主义…无限增长"); individuals with taste create **impact**, and impact → network effect → eventually value. Explicit analogy: **early GitHub**, where people simply open-sourced their code and a community formed.
	- Post-scarcity framing: speaker guesses "**within ~10 years most people won't need to work**" (calls it optimistic); people will need **meaning / impact**, and productivity ("very 功利") is only valuable while you occupy a *job position (岗位)*. Off the job, creation is emotional/meaning value.

- ## AI vs Douyin (short-video incumbent)
	- Prior view: Douyin's supply is **already saturated** (users can't finish the feed), so AI-lowered video-creation cost has **limited near-term platform impact** — going from "80 → 85-90," not "20 → 70."
	- But from the **consumer** view: greater diversity/personalization **improves retention** for previously under-served users (content they wanted now exists). Long-run, more native creators using the new tools drive **continuous content evolution** (DV/YouTube-era creators ≠ short-video-era creators; skills and outputs differ entirely).
	- App-Store caveat: distribution still obeys **power-law concentration** — users converge on a few head products — but new tools still surface **new native creators** who make genuinely different things.

- ## Clearest Monetization: AI + Crypto / Prediction Markets
	- The **shortest path from tokens → economic value** is **trading**: consume X tokens, produce Y dollars, directly, no "岗位" required. Speaker argues AI's impact here is **under-exploited** relative to its potential.
	- **One-person hedge funds**: put an AI-built strategy on a platform; others bet / invest on its historical track record. Referenced platform name (unclear) "**Ola / Oraa**"; it's **crypto**, because doing this in regulated (法币) markets raises compliance issues — prediction markets are crypto-native too.
	- **Polymarket** cited: a popular skill helps you trade Polymarket; weather traders ("掏地" ≈ weather-betting) already earn "不错的交易回报" using AI to process large information sets. Speaker suspects such AI strategies are also **Polymarket's own user-growth mechanism** (seed a tool that lets users earn small money).
	- Signals in use: AI analyzing **on-chain data + Twitter/X**. A quant-trader friend claims prediction-market strategies can currently yield "**级别**"-scale, **stable** returns.
	- Blunt conclusion: **"Web3 is the ultimate 归宿" (endgame)** — token-native from the start — but it's a **chaos era (乱纪元), all noise**. What sustains it: **meaning / hope** ("为梦想窒息").

- ## Investment Implications (for VC / public markets)
	- If **mid-size companies disappear**, the middle of the software market **hollows out** → weak SaaS pricing/compounding (echoes the SaaS-tollbooth bear case, see [[2026-06-05-software-tollbooths-saas-pricing-bear-geninnov]]).
	- **Model companies** remain the concentrated value sink — they own "普遍性" (generality); building *around* their generality = "完蛋" (doomed). Correct play at the bottom is **特殊性 / customization** and betting an idea *grows*.
	- **VC re-shapes**: with few mid-size targets, primary VC drifts toward **secondary markets** or toward **many small OPC bets** — likened to **franchise/chain-store investing (连锁创业)**: give an operator capital, take **cashflow-based** revenue share ("Cashflow-based"). Speaker: software industry starts to **resemble the cultural / content industry**.
	- **Independent-developer income ladder** (rough, speaker's read):

	  | Market | Typical outcome | Top outcomes |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | **US** | avg indie dev ≈ hundreds of thousands USD/yr | "不少" reaching **several million USD**; a few tens of millions |
	  | **China** | equivalent ≈ a few million RMB/yr | more dispersed |
	- **AGI framing tie-in**: model labs explicitly target replacing most economic-value-creating *job positions*; if achieved, the durable human activity migrates to **meaning-driven creation** (emotional/experiential products) and **direct token→dollar** loops (crypto/prediction markets) — the two poles that survive the barbell.
