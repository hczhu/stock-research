- ## Gartner's updated forecast: memory becomes over half of semiconductor revenue, peaks in 2027 and dips in 2028
	- **Source**: two versions of a chart titled "In Demand: Memory chips are surging in revenue, outpacing growth for other types, including those that handle processing and power," source **Gartner**. The chart style suggests the Wall Street Journal (user-provided screenshots, November 2026). Values are as labeled on the charts, in trillions of dollars. 2026–2028 are forecasts.
	- **Thesis**: Gartner now expects worldwide semiconductor revenue to roughly **triple from about \$0.81T in 2025 to about \$2.48T in 2028**, driven by memory.
		- **Memory**: revenue grows **more than 4× in 2026**, to \$0.93T. It peaks at **\$1.34T in 2027**, then **declines about 8% in 2028**, to \$1.23T.
		- **Non-memory**: grows steadily, **25–33% a year**.
		- **The mix**: memory becomes the **majority** of semiconductor revenue in 2026–27 (55–57%), then slips back below half in 2028.

- ## Cross-checks against company results (memo's reading)
	- **Run-rates already support \$0.93T for 2026**:
		- Micron's fiscal 2026 revenue was \$133.2B, and its FQ1-27 guide of \$61.5B annualizes to about **\$246B** ([[MU-2026-Q3]]).
		- SK hynix's Q3 2026 revenue of about \$77B annualizes to about **\$309B** ([[SKHY-2026-Q3]]).
		- Samsung's memory business and the rest of the industry (Kioxia, SanDisk, CXMT, YMTC, Nanya) make up the remainder.
	- **Supply discipline in 2027–28**: Micron and SK hynix both see 2027–28 supply **tighter** than 2026. SK hynix expects demand to exceed supply **through 2030** ([[MU-2026-Q3]], [[SKHY-2026-Q3]]). Gartner's 2028 dip is therefore more cautious than the vendors' outlooks. It is closer to the memory-cycle-top bear arguments ([[2026-11-25-memory-cycle-top-three-consensus-blindspots-bear-case]]), though far milder than a typical bust.
	- **Valuation**: if memory revenue falls 8% in 2028 after a 4× rise, earnings at that point are near-peak. That is the same point as the "P/E of 3 isn't cheap" argument ([[2026-11-05-veteran-investor-memory-pe3-not-cheap-lta-skepticism]]).
	- **Non-memory's steady growth**: 25–33% a year is driven by AI logic (Nvidia, custom ASICs, TSMC). Memory rising above half of industry revenue is historically unusual. It reflects memory pricing more than unit volume.
