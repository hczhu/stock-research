- ## November Ramp AI Index: more tokens, slower spending growth
	- **Source**: Ara Kharazian, Ramp Economics Lab, November 11, 2026. The November update covers October activity. [New Fronts in the AI Trade](https://ramp.com/data/ai-index-nov-2026)
	- **Thesis**: In Ramp's panel, token usage is growing much faster than spending. Customer support is emerging as a source of demand beyond coding, while SpaceXAI is gaining paying businesses. The effect on model-provider revenue depends on how quickly these workloads scale and what customers pay to run them.

- ## Spending growth is concentrated
	- | October indicator | Reading | Change or context |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Total AI spending | New high in the Ramp panel | **+1.65% MoM** |
	  | Token volume | Usage rose much faster than spending | **+36%** in the latest comparison shown in Ramp's indexed chart |
	  | Average price per million tokens | Realized average across the tracked mix | **−21%** over the prior month |
	  | Top 1% by AI spend per employee | Median **$10.2K per employee per month** within the group | **+9% MoM**, a record |
	  | Top 10% by AI spend per employee | **$801 per employee per month** | Roughly flat MoM |
	  | Median business | **\$16.32 per employee per month** | **Up \$0.93 MoM**, about 6% from the implied prior value |
	  | Concentration of OpenAI and Anthropic spend | **Top 1% of businesses account for about 80%** of observed spend with the two providers | The largest spenders are concentrated in VC-backed tech, finance, and technical services firms |
	- **Why the concentration matters.** A small group of businesses with similar funding exposure can drive the aggregate higher even when spending barely rises elsewhere. Slower funding or tighter budgets in that group could weigh on spend despite a rising count of paying businesses.
	- **Price and volume are separate inputs.** A falling average price can reflect cheaper models, lower quoted prices, or a change in token mix. Ramp gives the token-volume and price changes alongside a broader spending headline, without a common-denominator reconciliation. Use them as directional evidence when modeling revenue.

- ## Customer support gains share as engineering spend stays below its peak
	- | Spending measure | October | What changed |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Engineering | **72% of department-classified token spend** | Still the largest department. The four-week spending series remains below its summer peak |
	  | Customer success and support | **13.3% of classified token spend** | Up from **0.8% in June**, a gain of **12.5 percentage points**. The indexed series rose sharply in late summer, then eased from its peak |
	  | Executive leadership | Small relative to engineering and support | Ramp noted modest gains |
	  | Frontier model mix | **69.5% of model spend** across Opus, Fable, Sol, and Astra | Up from **67.7%**, or **1.8 percentage points** |
	- **The next workloads may monetize differently.** Kharazian suggests customer-service automation could drive demand for faster, cheaper models than coding agents require. Chatbots are a possible explanation for support spending. Ramp identifies the department through API-key labels, which provide limited evidence about the exact workflow. The rise in frontier dollar share shows premium models still capture most of the measured spend while use cases broaden.
	- **Denominator.** The department breakdown covers token spending Ramp could classify using normalized names from connected API keys. The plotted series uses four-week moving averages. Spend share measures dollars and can differ substantially from token share.

- ## SpaceXAI gains paying customers as OpenAI and Anthropic token spending slows
	- | Paid adoption among Ramp businesses | September 2026 | October 2026 | Monthly move |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | Any paid AI product or service | 55.8% | **56.8%** | **+1.0 pp**, versus +0.66 pp in September |
	  | Anthropic | 43.7% | **44.8%** | **+1.1 pp** |
	  | OpenAI | 40.6% | **40.6%** | Flat |
	  | SpaceXAI | 4.1% | **6.3%** | **+2.2 pp**, the biggest gain among these providers |
	- September levels are calculated from Ramp's reported October levels and changes. Adoption measures companies paying each provider, and a company can pay several. If both provider cohorts are included in the overall measure, the October percentages imply **at least about 28.6% of Ramp businesses paid both OpenAI and Anthropic** (44.8% + 40.6% − 56.8%). This is a lower bound from rounded figures.

	- **Three-provider competition is the author's thesis.** Kharazian points to coding adoption, Grokbot's consumer reach, and compute capacity as possible advantages for SpaceXAI. Its low starting base and growing paid reach support watching it as a competitor. Material pricing pressure depends on customers routing sustained production volume to it.

- ## Revenue and margin implications
	- **Model providers.** More tokens can be positive for adoption while falling revenue per token limits sales growth. The profit effect depends on whether inference cost per unit falls with the realized price. If support becomes a large workload, per-resolution economics and the share routed to cheaper models will matter alongside total volumes.
	- **Application vendors.** Cheaper inference can lower the cost of adding AI to customer support software. How much of that benefit vendors keep depends on their ability to charge for the workflow or its results, and on whether customers can choose a different model or tool.
	- **Scope of the panel.** Ramp sees spend captured through its own platform. Payment channels outside Ramp can change the picture for large contracts. The figures describe the observed sample and cannot be converted into provider-wide revenue, global market share, or infrastructure spending.
