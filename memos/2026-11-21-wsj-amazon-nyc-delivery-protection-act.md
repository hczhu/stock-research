- ## Amazon’s dispute with New York City over last-mile delivery
	- **Source**: Isabella Simonetti, [“Amazon Urges NYC Customers to Oppose Mamdani-Backed Delivery Bill”](https://www.wsj.com/business/logistics/amazon-urges-nyc-customers-to-oppose-mamdani-backed-delivery-bill-0461ea54), The Wall Street Journal, updated November 20, 2026 (supplied PDF, pp. 1–4); [NYC Council bill text and legislative history](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=BDDD32F1-1178-492D-80F2-205845356FD2&ID=7879110); reader comment by Katherine Gray supplied separately.
	- The dispute centers on who employs the workers who deliver Amazon’s packages. Amazon uses independent Delivery Service Partners (DSPs); the proposed NYC rules would generally make covered facility operators the direct employers of workers performing core delivery and warehouse services.

- ## What the Delivery Protection Act would change
	- Councilmember Tiffany Cabán introduced **Intro 518**, backed by Mayor Zohran Mamdani. The measure would require covered last-mile warehouses and storage facilities to obtain a city license and meet safety, training, and employment rules.
	- As introduced, the bill generally bars third-party contracting for covered core services, subject to exceptions and transition provisions. The Council record lists the bill as **laid over in committee**, not enacted.

- ## Amazon’s customer campaign and City Hall’s response
	- About a week before Black Friday, Amazon used a pop-up on its app and website to direct New York City shoppers to a form that drafted an email to their council member opposing the bill.
	- | Figure | What it measures |
	  |---|---|
	  | Up to $5 per order | Amazon’s warning about a possible fee if the bill passes as written, not a charge prescribed by the bill |
	  | Up to $664 per year | Potential increase cited in Amazon’s prewritten message to council members, not an observed household cost |
	  | About 60,000 emails in 36 hours | Amazon’s count of messages sent to council members after the campaign launched; not a measure of citywide opinion |
	- Mamdani said any price increase would be Amazon’s choice and defended the bill on worker rights and street safety grounds. Council Speaker Julie Menin said the Council aims to protect workers without affecting prices or jobs. Neither position establishes what Amazon would actually charge.
	- **Separate enforcement disputes**: Mamdani previously announced that the city collected more than $9 million in idling fines involving Amazon delivery vehicles. Amazon said tracking gaps meant many fines had not reached its DSPs. The city also sent Amazon and other retailers cease-and-desist orders over e-bikes it said were illegal; Amazon said it requires e-bikes sold through its store to meet applicable safety standards.

- ## Firsthand account of curbside package sorting
	- **Katherine Gray, reader comment**: Gray says she regularly sees Amazon delivery partners use sidewalks and parking lanes in her neighborhood to sort packages. She contrasts this with FedEx and UPS, which she says sort packages at facilities as trucks are loaded.
	- Gray supports the legislation. In her view, sorting in public space shifts part of the cost of private distribution onto the neighborhood and disadvantages competitors that pay for facilities. This is one resident’s account and interpretation, not a verified citywide comparison or a measure of delivery costs.
