- ## Gruber on the iPhone Duo: a great tablet in disguise, and the worst iPhone Apple has made
	- **Source**: John Gruber, "The iPhone Duo," *Daring Fireball*, Wednesday, Oct 21, 2026 (user-provided text). It is a review after a week with a review unit, published two days before the first customer deliveries. The review includes a one-day-later update.
	- **Thesis**: Gruber's overall view is that the Duo is "two devices in one," and only one of them is good.
		- **Inner display**: "a splendid, joyful, beautiful tablet… that out-iPads the iPad," and Gruber's "favorite tablet experience ever."
		- **Outer display**: "by far, the worst iPhone Apple has ever made… a miserable fat little thing."
		- **Missing Face ID**: Gruber calls this "a dealbreaker," says Apple "should [not] have released the Duo before they figured this out," and has no plans to buy this model.
		- **Bottom line**: "an extraordinarily polished prototype — not a finished product that I'd want to buy and carry."
	- Related: [[2026-11-10-circuit-nov-6-semi-selloff-2028-capacity-amd-arm-qcom-sitime]] (Ben Bajarin, a full-time Duo user, says "there's no going back"), [[2026-10-28-wsj-flash-makers-revenge-on-apple-storage-upgrade-margins]], [[AAPL-2026-Q2]].
- ## The author's overall opinions
	- **The foldable's core problems are unsolved.** Gruber asks how Apple solved the obvious foldable problems: thickness and weight, a cramped outer screen, misplaced buttons when open, Touch ID instead of Face ID, and apps not yet updated. The answer: "They didn't." Some were mitigated, some may be fixed in later versions, and some "are endemic to the very idea of a foldable, and cannot be solved."
	- **"It's a tablet in disguise," like a Transformer.** Transformers were compromised in both states but mostly played with as robots. Likewise "the Duo-as-phone is just not as good a phone as any regular iPhone. Honestly it's not even close." Snapped in half, the outer half is an iPhone shape "zero people would want." Even the iPhone Mini, which Apple discontinued, was "a way better shape."
	- **Every pickup means "The Decision"**: open it, or "suffer with the outside display." Opening it is enough effort that Gruber often just puts it back in a pocket. "Perhaps for some people looking to be more mindful of their screen time, this is actually a selling point."
	- **Who it's for**: people who mainly want "a pocketable small tablet" and are "ambivalent about your iPhone." It will frustrate people who love their iPhone and use it for quick interactions all day.
	- **The steelman**: an iPad Mini you can fold in half to fit in your pants, with a small outer screen for the time, notifications and Live Activities. "Well, that's the iPhone Duo." Even for that buyer, this first model's irritations may be too much, and it costs \$2,000 against \$800 for an iPad Mini.
- ## What Gruber liked
	- **The tablet experience**: Gruber prefers the open Duo to the iPad Mini "in every single regard other than hardware button placement." The open Duo is 15% lighter and feels "featherweight." It is great for reading in portrait (Kindle-sized) and for video in landscape. The speakers are "loud and separated."
	- **The nano-texture matte inner display solves the crease.** It is the one foldable problem Apple did solve, with no glare even in sunlight, and Gruber wants it on the regular iPhone 18 Pro. **Prediction**: "No foldable phone on the market had a matte inner display until the Duo. I suspect most of them will next year, and by 2028 all of them will."
	- **Split-screen multitasking is "brilliant"**:
		- It shows two apps side by side, 50-50, in landscape.
		- You drag the home indicator up and drop the app on a shelf, and each half behaves like its own Duo.
		- It "puts every idea Apple has tried on iPadOS for multitasking to shame." Gruber dates iPad multitasking's wrong turn to iOS 9 in 2015 and says Apple "should throw out iPadOS's multitasking interface."
		- Gruber goes further: "The Duo flavor of iOS is a better tablet OS than iPadOS," and "because iPhone Duo is not called an 'iPad', it gets to be what the iPad should've been all along."
	- **Build quality is "nothing short of excellent."** It stays perfectly flat when open, closes with a "pleasing snap," and leaves no gap when shut.
	- **Battery life is "very good."** It gets warm but not hot.
	- **Thoughtful touches**: Standby (tent) mode, laptop (L-shape) mode for video, fold-aware setup, and the Dock animation on rotation.
	- **Developer support is arriving fast**: "Apple has clearly done a great job evangelizing developers." Netflix is fully optimized. NYT Games fixed its crossword keyboard within a day of the review.
	- **Camera**: "terrific." Close to the year-old iPhone Air's, with the newer A20 Pro image processing and an ultrawide lens that adds 2cm macro. It is not Pro grade: no Pro controls, ProRes, Log or spatial video.
- ## What Gruber disliked
	- **No Face ID is the dealbreaker.**
		- Touch ID prompts came so often that Gruber's tally ran out of room on a sheet of notepaper.
		- Apple's Watch-unlock workaround is "a crutch." It's the "only tolerable way" to use the Duo, but it means wearing an Apple Watch all day, only one iPhone can be paired, and once it unlocked the phone in a child's hands.
		- Resting a finger to unlock is inconsistent and causes about one accidental unlock a day.
		- Gruber will reconsider "a future model that at least has Face ID on the front outer display," but won't truly enjoy one "unless it has Face ID on both sides."
	- **The outer display feels cramped and its layout breaks habits**:
		- Moving the Dock to the right "breaks 19 years of spatial memory," and Dock icons are smaller than the rest.
		- Putting the "❮" back button on the right "just feels wrong," like a turn signal that moves the opposite way.
		- In Unforgetful, a Live Activity collapsed the Close and Done buttons into a "···" menu.
		- With the keyboard open, Messages shows barely one prior bubble, "like using an ancient BlackBerry."
	- **Ergonomics when folded**: "twice as thick and twice as heavy." It feels denser than the 18 Pro Max despite a similar weight. It slides awkwardly in and out of a pocket and sometimes rotates there. It needs two hands even when folded, and the D-shaped frame's sharp corner digs into the left palm.
	- **Button and camera layout when open**:
		- The Touch ID button sits in the opposite position from a Touch ID iPad, a game of "Where Are the Buttons?"
		- The camera is aligned with the outer display, so the large inner viewfinder can never be filled. Gruber considers this "a mistake."
		- The side and Camera Control buttons get pressed while opening or closing the Duo, causing four accidental photos in a week.
	- **Apps not updated for the Duo are "terrible"**: they show as "little virtual iPhone Minis floating in a sea of black." Examples are Signal, NBC Sports and Ivory, and "your bank probably won't support the Duo until the Duo 2 is out next year."
	- **Accessories**: the \$80 silicone case is "ridiculous": it needs glue and a setting that shrinks the display. The \$130 folio is better but expensive.
	- **Software rough edges**: iOS 27.1 is "a little buggy," with a zoom glitch on rotation and a one-time rendering smear on the crease. Command-Tab and Command-H don't work with a hardware keyboard.

		- The Duo is \$800 more than the 18 Pro and \$700 more than the 18 Pro Max at every tier. A 256 GB iPad Mini is \$800.
	- **Security (Apple Platform Security whitepaper)**: the chance a random person unlocks a device is under 1 in 1 million for Face ID and under 1 in 50,000 for Touch ID, so Face ID is about 20× more secure. With five enrolled fingerprints, Touch ID rises to 1 in 10,000.
	- **Displays and camera**: the outer display's aspect ratio is 1.44:1, against the sensor's 1.33:1. The main camera's minimum focus distance is 15cm (the 17 and 18 Pro's is 20cm), with 2cm macro through the ultrawide.
	- **Colors**: star light (off-white) and night sky (very dark blue).
- ## My notes
	- **Pricing and volume**: at \$2,000 and up, with \$800 over the 18 Pro, the Duo raises iPhone average selling price for every unit sold. But Gruber's review points to a niche first generation, bought by tablet-first users and enthusiasts, not mainstream iPhone upgraders.
		- The reviewers disagree. Bajarin, after initial skepticism, is now a full-time user with "no going back." Gruber is switching back to the 18 Pro immediately.
		- For the stock, the Duo looks more like Apple claiming the foldable category and gaining margin on a high-end product than like an iPhone unit catalyst in FY27. *My note*: this is my inference; the review gives no sales data.
	- **Generation 2 is the real test**: Gruber's fixes are specific. Face ID on the outer display at minimum, ideally on both, plus camera alignment with the inner display.
		- Gruber expects "Duo 2… next year." If the Face ID gap closes, the strongest objection from the most influential Apple reviewer goes away.
	- **The iPad is affected more than the iPhone**: Gruber prefers the Duo to the iPad Mini and calls iOS Duo a better tablet OS than iPadOS.
		- At \$2,000 against \$800, Duo sales that replace an iPad Mini purchase still raise revenue per customer.
		- But it also suggests Apple's best tablet software now ships on the iPhone. The next post on Daring Fireball, "Apple Announces New A20 Pro iPad Mini," shows the iPad Mini line continuing for now.
	- **Watch attach as a side effect**: Watch unlock is the "only tolerable way" to use the Duo, which nudges Duo buyers to wear an Apple Watch. It is a small attach tailwind, and it comes from a design weakness.
	- **Storage and flash costs**: the 2TB Duo at \$3,200 continues Apple's response to higher flash costs, which is to raise storage-step prices rather than absorb the cost ([[2026-10-28-wsj-flash-makers-revenge-on-apple-storage-upgrade-margins]]).
	- **Ecosystem advantage**: developers are shipping Duo-optimized apps within days of launch, and NYT Games fixed its keyboard overnight. Android foldables have long struggled with app support, so that pace is a real platform advantage for Apple.
