- **Source**: X post by **Evanbear1 / @EvanZir** (Jun. 2026), a Roblox developer/studio owner — "The End of Cash Grab Roblox Games." Firsthand developer account; data points are from his own portfolio (single-source, perspective skewed toward the old short-term meta).
- **Thesis**: Roblox re-weighted its **"Recommended For You"** discovery algorithm toward **long-term retention (28-day player value)**, killing the "win the click" cash-grab meta. Healthier ecosystem long-term, but the dev argues **monetization is still a hidden major gatekeeper**, and the abrupt rollout is a platform-risk/trust problem for creators.
- ## The Algorithm Change (facts)
	- "Recommended For You" expanded from **primarily short-term signals → player value over a 28-day window**.
	- Now emphasizes: **Day 1 return**, continued return **Days 2–7**, and **meaningful engagement through Days 8–28**.
	- Old **qualified play-through rate (QPTR)** signal **broken apart** into more specific measures: **play-through behavior, session quality, and spend**.
	- Goal: separate games that *grab attention* from games that *keep players invested*.
- ## Old Meta vs New Meta
	- **Old**: strong thumbnail + readable title + on-trend concept + decent first session → algorithmic push. Rewarded fast, trend-driven games buildable in 1–2 weeks, monetized early, then abandoned.
	- **New**: "Can you earn the return visit (and get them to spend)?" Packaging still gets the first click but no longer carries Day 7 / Day 28.
	- Retention is **hard to fake**: "you can trick someone to play a game once, but you cannot trick them into caring for a month."

- ## New Algorithm Priorities (dev's read, in order)
	- 1) **Retention** (the foundation) → 2) **Monetization** → 3) **Playtime** (only when it reflects genuine engagement, not chores/artificial friction).
	- Weak retention = "refilling a leaking bucket" by churning new users — eventually runs out.
- ## The Monetization-Gatekeeper Critique
	- Despite the "retention-first" public messaging, **monetization still appears to be a much larger factor than Roblox admits**.
	- Evidence: 90th-pctile retention/playtime games with only ~40th-pctile monetization are **not getting picked up / tested with larger audiences**.
	- Real message ≈ "Build games players come back to **that also monetize well enough for Roblox to keep distributing them**."
	- Ask: not the full formula, but **honest weighting** — strong retention may be *necessary but not sufficient*.
- ## Winners and Losers
	- **Helps**: large studios, experienced devs, teams with resources for deeper systems, content pipelines, communities, higher quality.
	- **Hurts**: new devs and those surviving on two-week projects to "pay rent." Raises both the **quality bar and the execution bar**.
	- **Monetization ≠ enemy**; *monetization-first before gameplay/retention* is what's penalized. Best games monetize off players caring (progression, identity, friends, status, community).
	- **Simple ≠ shallow**: simple-but-deep games thrive; lazy versions of traditional categories (tycoons, simulators, roleplay) decline.
- ## Examples Cited
	- Strong long-term retention done well: **Twin Atlas** — Creatures of Sonaria, Dragon Adventures, World Zero, Horse Life.
	- Simple-surface, deep-underneath: **Grow a Garden**, **Steal a Brainrot**.
	- Category evolution: **Sell Lemons** (personality + structure + stronger loop).

- ## Investment Implications ($RBLX)
	- **Quality/LTV pivot**: rewarding 28-day retention should raise content quality, player LTV, and engagement durability — structurally bullish for DAUs/hours/bookings *quality* vs churn-driven spikes.
	- **Monetization baked into distribution**: if discovery favors monetizing games, Roblox is effectively optimizing distribution for **bookings/its own take-rate** — supports near-term monetization but invites "platform self-dealing" scrutiny.
	- **Ecosystem professionalization / consolidation**: power shifts to larger, well-resourced studios → fewer but better titles; potential risk to long-tail creator supply.
	- **Platform/developer-trust risk**: abrupt, unannounced discovery changes can reshape creator businesses overnight (−50% revenue here). If devs lose confidence in stability, long-term content investment suffers — a key dependency for the UGC flywheel.
	- **Redistribution, not necessarily decline**: one portfolio's −50%/−70% is *reallocation* of discovery, not proof of aggregate platform weakness — watch Roblox's reported engagement/bookings for the net effect.
- ## Caveats
	- Single developer's account; portfolio over-indexed to the old short-term meta (so impact likely worse than platform median).
	- The "monetization is the real gatekeeper" claim is **inference from his own games**, not disclosed by Roblox.
	- Revenue/percentile figures are self-reported and unaudited.
