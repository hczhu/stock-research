- ## Are Samsung's and SK hynix's LTAs really more binding? Their balance sheets say less than the claims
	- **Source**: a Korean media report (user-provided English summary, November 2026), based on Samsung's and SK hynix's Q3 2026 quarterly reports.
	- **Thesis (the report's)**: Samsung and SK hynix both say their long-term agreements (LTAs) are **more binding than before**, but the **advance payments on their books** don't clearly support that.
		- **SK hynix**: contract liabilities have barely moved in a year, at about **KRW 500B**.
		- **Samsung**: contract liabilities jumped by **KRW 8.5T** in Q2 as it signed LTAs, but rose only **KRW 550B** in Q3. The industry suspects later contracts traded **smaller upfront payments for higher price caps**.
		- **Why it matters**: smaller deposits make it easier for customers to walk away if supply and demand reverse.

- ## The data
	- **Contract liabilities**: the obligation to deliver product after a customer has paid in advance.
		- | Company | Contract liabilities | Change | Memo calculation (USD) |
		  |---|---|---|---|
		  | **SK hynix** | About **KRW 500B**, barely changed over the past year | About flat | About \$0.37B at the September 30 rate of KRW 1,355.7 per USD |
		  | **Samsung**, Q2 2026 | **KRW 23.6T** | **+KRW 8.5T** QoQ, after years in a set range, as it signed LTAs with large customers | About \$15.2B, with the increase about \$5.5B at the June 30 rate of KRW 1,549.4 |
		  | **Samsung**, Q3 2026 | — | Only about **+KRW 550B** in advance-payment-related contract liabilities | About \$0.4B |
	- **What SK hynix says**:
		- Because transactions use "various methods such as **deposits**," it is hard to judge LTAs from contract liabilities or advance payments alone.
		- The report's reading is that other arrangements such as **escrow, guarantees and collateral** may be in use.
	- **The report's objection to escrow and guarantees**:
		- With escrow, guarantees or collateral, the money can be recovered only through set procedures if a contract is broken.
		- If the contracts were truly stronger, there should be a reason to choose these over taking the cash directly.
		- An accountant notes escrow is uncommon, since a company "can earn at least interest simply by receiving the money directly and putting it to work."
	- **What Samsung says**: it said advance payments would grow as more contracts were signed. They barely grew in Q3. The industry's reading is that Samsung **cut upfront payments in later contracts in exchange for a higher price cap**. If so, that doesn't match its description of "sufficiently binding LTAs."

- ## How the three vendors compare (memo's reading)
	- | Vendor | What the company says | What's on the books | Size relative to revenue (memo calculation) |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | **Micron** | 26 SCAs; \$32B of customer financial commitments, "the vast majority" cash deposits | **\$12.7B of deposits on the balance sheet**; long-term customer contract liabilities rose from \$0.6B to **\$12.9B** in one quarter ([[MU-2026-Q3]]) | About **24%** of FQ4-26 quarterly revenue (\$54.2B) |
	  | **Samsung** | LTAs with large customers, "sufficiently binding" | Contract liabilities of KRW 23.6T after Q2's +KRW 8.5T; only +KRW 550B in Q3 | The Q2 increase alone is comparable in size to Micron's deposits |
	  | **SK hynix** | LTAs with 14 customers cover over 50% of 2027–28 capacity, with "safeguards such as deposits" ([[SKHY-2026-Q3]]) | Contract liabilities of about KRW 500B, flat | About **0.5%** of Q3 revenue (KRW 104.6T) |
	- **SK hynix has the widest gap**: the most ambitious coverage claim, more than half of 2027–28 capacity contracted and DRAM and HBM "effectively sold out" through 2029, sits next to almost no visible advance payments.
	- **A caveat on the accounting**: contract liabilities may not capture every deposit.
		- Micron books its customer deposits **as** contract liabilities and reports the cash under financing activities.
		- A vendor could instead record refundable deposits elsewhere, such as "deposits received" or other financial liabilities, or hold them off balance sheet as guarantees or escrow.
		- So SK hynix's KRW 500B doesn't prove there are no deposits, but SK hynix hasn't shown where they are. Its other liability lines and its notes on commitments are where to check.

- ## Why it matters for the cycle debate
	- **The bear case on LTAs**: weaker upfront cash supports it.
		- A veteran investor says customers will "gladly forfeit their deposits" when spot falls far enough ([[2026-11-05-veteran-investor-memory-pe3-not-cheap-lta-skepticism]]).
		- The "LTA price floor fiction" post says customers push out deliveries and renegotiate in a downturn ([[2026-11-25-memory-cycle-top-three-consensus-blindspots-bear-case]]).
		- The smaller the cash at risk, the cheaper it is for a buyer to walk away.
	- **The trade-off Samsung may be making**: accepting smaller deposits for a **higher price cap** raises upside if tightness persists but weakens protection in a downturn. That is a bet that the shortage lasts.
	- **Volume vs. price**: Korean buyers reportedly lock **volume but not price** ([[2026-06-01-memory-super-cycle-x-thread-and-memo]]). Samsung's Apple deal also sets a minimum volume and leaves price to the market ([[2026-11-17-samsung-apple-four-year-dram-lta]]). Contracts that float price and carry little cash protect allocation more than they protect margins.
	- **Micron stands out**: of the three, only Micron shows large, visible cash deposits tied to take-or-pay terms. Even so, they are only about 9% of its roughly \$150B of remaining performance obligations ([[MU-2026-Q3]]).
