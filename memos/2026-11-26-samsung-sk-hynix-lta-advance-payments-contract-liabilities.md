- ## Are Samsung's and SK hynix's LTAs really more binding? Their balance sheets say less than the claims
	- **Source**: a Korean media report (user-provided English summary, November 2026), based on Samsung's and SK hynix's Q3 2026 quarterly reports.
	- **Thesis (the report's)**: Samsung and SK hynix both say their long-term agreements (LTAs) are **more binding than before**, but the **advance payments on their books** don't clearly support that.
		- **SK hynix**: contract liabilities have barely moved in a year, at about **\$0.37B**.
		- **Samsung**: contract liabilities jumped by about **\$5.5B** in Q2 as it signed LTAs, but rose only about **\$0.4B** in Q3. The industry suspects later contracts traded **smaller upfront payments for higher price caps**.
		- **Why it matters**: smaller deposits make it easier for customers to walk away if supply and demand reverse.

- ## The data
	- **Currency**: all won amounts are converted to USD at the period-end close for each balance or quarterly change: KRW 1,439.0 per USD (December 30, 2025), 1,530.1 (March 31, 2026), 1,549.4 (June 30, 2026) and 1,355.7 (September 30, 2026). The won strengthened about 14% in Q3, which inflates USD balances at September 30 compared with June 30 even when the won amount barely moved. Changes below are therefore the won change converted at the quarter-end rate, not the difference between USD balances.
	- **Contract liabilities**: the obligation to deliver product after a customer has paid in advance.
		- | Company | Contract liabilities | Change |
		  |---|---|---|
		  | **SK hynix** | About **\$0.37B**, barely changed over the past year | About flat |
		  | **Samsung**, Q2 2026 | **\$15.2B** | **+\$5.5B** QoQ, after years in a set range, as it signed LTAs with large customers |
		  | **Samsung**, Q3 2026 | — | Only about **+\$0.4B** in advance-payment-related contract liabilities |
	- **What SK hynix says**:
		- Because transactions use "various methods such as **deposits**," it is hard to judge LTAs from contract liabilities or advance payments alone.
		- The report's reading is that other arrangements such as **escrow, guarantees and collateral** may be in use.
	- **The report's objection to escrow and guarantees**:
		- With escrow, guarantees or collateral, the money can be recovered only through set procedures if a contract is broken.
		- If the contracts were truly stronger, there should be a reason to choose these over taking the cash directly.
		- An accountant notes escrow is uncommon, since a company "can earn at least interest simply by receiving the money directly and putting it to work."
	- **What Samsung says**: it said advance payments would grow as more contracts were signed. They barely grew in Q3. The industry's reading is that Samsung **cut upfront payments in later contracts in exchange for a higher price cap**. If so, that doesn't match its description of "sufficiently binding LTAs."

- ## Fact check against the filings (added 2026-11-26)
	- **Sources**:
		- Samsung's reviewed **quarterly consolidated financial statements** for Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026, from Samsung IR (also filed on DART). The statements are in KRW millions; converted to USD below.
		- Korean articles quoting SK hynix's **H1 2026 half-year report** and statements from both companies: [MTN, August 19](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026081913303555011) and [Green Economy, November 16](https://www.greened.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=354721).
		- I couldn't retrieve SK hynix's Q3 quarterly report itself.
	- ### Samsung: the report's numbers are accurate
		- | Balance at | Contract liabilities (Note 14) | Change | "Other" financial liabilities at amortized cost (Note 3) | Other non-current liabilities (balance sheet) |
		  |---|---|---|---|---|
		  | Dec 31, 2025 | \$9.71B | — | \$9.92B | \$5.53B |
		  | Mar 31, 2026 | \$9.86B | +\$0.72B | \$10.11B | \$5.77B |
		  | **Jun 30, 2026** | **\$15.23B** | **+\$5.49B** | **\$15.49B (+\$5.51B)** | **\$15.79B (+\$10.09B)** |
		  | **Sep 30, 2026** | **\$17.81B** (FX-inflated) | **+\$0.41B** | \$17.66B (flat in won) | \$20.26B (+\$2.22B) |
		- **Confirmed**:
			- Contract liabilities rose about **\$5.5B in Q2 to \$15.2B**, then only about **\$0.4B in Q3**, as reported.
			- In H1 2025 they were about \$9–10B (the H1 2026 figure is up 68.8% YoY in won, per MTN). Q1 2026 was \$9.9B, consistent with a stable range before Q2.
		- **Samsung's own description** (Q2 call, via MTN): it included "a substantial amount of **deposit-like advance payments held for multiple years**" in LTA terms to raise enforceability. It had received about **one-quarter of the total agreed advance payments**, with more to come as additional contracts are signed.
			- MTN's rough extrapolation is about **\$26B** of total agreed prepayments. That is an assumption, not a disclosure.
		- **New finding not in the report** (memo's reading):
			- In the same Q2 quarter, Samsung's "other" financial liabilities at amortized cost also rose about **\$5.5B**. Other non-current liabilities rose about \$10.1B.
			- That pattern fits LTA cash split between **advance payments** (contract liabilities) and **refundable deposit-like balances** (financial liabilities), both held long-term. Samsung doesn't label the financial-liability increase as LTA deposits, so this is an inference.
			- If correct, Samsung's Q2 LTA-related cash was closer to **about \$11B** than \$5.5B. Neither line grew in Q3, which still supports the report's point that new Q3 contracts brought little upfront cash.
			- The 9-month cash flow statement shows about **\$9.8B** of "other" working-capital inflows in operating cash flow, consistent with these receipts.
		- **A note on the definition**: Samsung's contract liabilities sit inside advance receipts, accrued expenses and other liability lines, not only advance receipts. The advance-receipts line itself was only about \$2.0B at September 30.
		- **An alternative reading of Q3**: having received only one-quarter of the agreed prepayments by Q2, Samsung may simply have **later tranches not yet due**. Smaller upfront payments traded for a higher price cap is the industry's interpretation, not a disclosure.
	- ### SK hynix: confirmed in substance, with an explanation the report understates
		- **Contract liabilities plus deferred revenue totaled about \$0.37B at June 30, 2026**, only slightly above year-end (Green Economy, citing the H1 report). That is consistent with the report's "barely moved from about \$0.37B."
		- **The H1 report stated there was no order backlog under long-term supply agreements** (MTN). That is notable given SK hynix's claims about LTAs.
		- **SK hynix's explanation**: customers' deposits are **entrusted to a third party**, not received directly. SK hynix can recover them if the customer breaks the contract. Its words: "customer deposits are managed separately, so not all are reflected in the financial statements." It didn't disclose the size of the deposits or the return and forfeiture terms.
		- **The report's objection**: an accountant notes escrow forgoes interest income. That is valid economically, but **third-party deposits and letters of credit are standard ways to secure performance**. Even **Micron** discloses that some customers provided **letters of credit from financial institutions** alongside cash deposits (Micron FY2026 10-K, per Green Economy).
		- **Pricing**: SK hynix describes LTA pricing as "adjusted to market conditions within a price range agreed with the customer." That protects volume more than price.
	- ### Verdict
		- **Samsung**:
			- **True**: contract liabilities stood at 23.6T after Q2's +8.5T jump, and grew only +0.55T in Q3.
			- **Inference**: that additional contracts traded smaller upfront payments for higher price caps. Scheduled later tranches are a possible alternative.
			- **What the report misses**: a matching +8.5T in other financial liabilities in Q2, which suggests more LTA cash on the books than contract liabilities alone show.
		- **SK hynix**:
			- **True**: contract liabilities are flat at about \$0.37–0.44B.
			- **Explanation not addressed by the report**: SK hynix says its deposits are held by a third party.
			- **Not verifiable**: the deposits' size and terms are undisclosed, so how binding SK hynix's LTAs are can't be checked from its filings. Its H1 report showing no LTA order backlog makes the gap between claims and disclosure wider than at Samsung or Micron.

- ## How the three vendors compare (memo's reading)
	- | Vendor | What the company says | What's on the books | Size relative to revenue (memo calculation) |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | **Micron** | 26 SCAs; \$32B of customer financial commitments, "the vast majority" cash deposits | **\$12.7B of deposits on the balance sheet**; long-term customer contract liabilities rose from \$0.6B to **\$12.9B** in one quarter ([[MU-2026-Q3]]) | About **24%** of FQ4-26 quarterly revenue (\$54.2B) |
	  | **Samsung** | LTAs with large customers, "sufficiently binding" | Contract liabilities of \$15.2B after Q2's +\$5.5B; only +\$0.4B in Q3 | The Q2 increase was about \$5.5B, or about \$11B including the parallel rise in financial liabilities, against Micron's \$12.7B of deposits |
	  | **SK hynix** | LTAs with 14 customers cover over 50% of 2027–28 capacity, with "safeguards such as deposits" ([[SKHY-2026-Q3]]) | Contract liabilities of about \$0.37B, flat | About **0.5%** of Q3 revenue (\$77.2B) |
	- **SK hynix has the widest gap**: the most ambitious coverage claim, more than half of 2027–28 capacity contracted and DRAM and HBM "effectively sold out" through 2029, sits next to almost no visible advance payments.
	- **A caveat on the accounting**: contract liabilities may not capture every deposit.
		- Micron books its customer deposits **as** contract liabilities and reports the cash under financing activities.
		- A vendor could instead record refundable deposits elsewhere, such as "deposits received" or other financial liabilities, or hold them off balance sheet as guarantees or escrow.
		- So SK hynix's \$0.37B doesn't prove there are no deposits, but SK hynix hasn't shown where they are. Its other liability lines and its notes on commitments are where to check.

- ## Why it matters for the cycle debate
	- **The bear case on LTAs**: weaker upfront cash supports it.
		- A veteran investor says customers will "gladly forfeit their deposits" when spot falls far enough ([[2026-11-05-veteran-investor-memory-pe3-not-cheap-lta-skepticism]]).
		- The "LTA price floor fiction" post says customers push out deliveries and renegotiate in a downturn ([[2026-11-25-memory-cycle-top-three-consensus-blindspots-bear-case]]).
		- The smaller the cash at risk, the cheaper it is for a buyer to walk away.
	- **The trade-off Samsung may be making**: accepting smaller deposits for a **higher price cap** raises upside if tightness persists but weakens protection in a downturn. That is a bet that the shortage lasts.
	- **Volume vs. price**: Korean buyers reportedly lock **volume but not price** ([[2026-06-01-memory-super-cycle-x-thread-and-memo]]). Samsung's Apple deal also sets a minimum volume and leaves price to the market ([[2026-11-17-samsung-apple-four-year-dram-lta]]). Contracts that float price and carry little cash protect allocation more than they protect margins.
	- **Micron stands out**: of the three, only Micron shows large, visible cash deposits tied to take-or-pay terms. Even so, they are only about 9% of its roughly \$150B of remaining performance obligations ([[MU-2026-Q3]]).
