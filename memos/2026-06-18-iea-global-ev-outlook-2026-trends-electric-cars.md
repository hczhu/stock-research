- ## China Deep Dive

	- Monthly EV sales share exceeded 50% in 11 out of 12 months in 2025 (vs only 5 months in 2024), lifting annual share to almost 55%.
	- 44 million electric cars on Chinese roads at end-2025, ~13% of total car stock (up from 1 in 10 in 2024).
	- China growth decelerated sharply: 2020–24 annual growth >75%, sales share rising ~10pp/year; 2025 growth <20%, share rose only ~6pp.
	- **Trade-in scheme**: CNY 20,000 (\$2,750 USD) subsidy for replacing old car with EV; CNY 15,000 (\$2,050) for conventional. Temporarily halted July 2025 (funding exhausted at provincial level), causing EV sales to dip ~10%. Attracted 11.5 million applications over 2025; by November, nearly 60% of applications were for NEVs.
	- **Zero-mileage cars** (purchased new, immediately resold to capture subsidy): ~2% of conventional car sales, ~4% of EV sales — disrupt dealer pricing and inflate sales figures slightly.

- ## Europe Deep Dive

	- **Germany** (largest EU EV market): +50% in 2025 to record 850,000 sales; ~30% of cars sold were electric. Average BEV price fell ~6% in 2025 on more affordable models. France: BEV +15%, PHEV -25%, share ~25% stable.
	- **Italy** (+65%), **Poland** (+125%), **Spain** (+80%): surge driven by reinstated/continued EV purchase subsidies.
	- **UK**: +25%; 1 in 3 new cars electric in 2025 (up from 1 in 4 in 2024). BEV share 23% of all new cars. Fell short of VETS 28% ZEV target; manufacturers complied via credit borrowing flexibilities. July 2025: new subsidy for BEVs priced below GBP 37,000 (\$47,400); 25%+ of BEV sales qualified.
	- **Norway**: 97% of new car sales electric in 2025 — world's highest share, nearly all BEV, <2% PHEV. Tax exemption for BEVs will be tightened in 2026 (applies only to NOK 300,000 / \$28,000 and below, phased out by 2028).
	- **Türkiye**: Sales more than doubled to ~240,000; EV share rose from 1% in 2022 to 20%+ in 2025 — 4th-largest European EV market. Driven by registration tax (ÖTV) advantage for BEVs (10% vs 45–80% for ICE); ÖTV raised to 25% in July 2025 for models meeting price criteria, causing brief dip before rebounding.
	- EU BEV share of EV sales: 55% in 2020 → nearly 70% in 2025; Europe and China reached similar BEV-in-EV shares, Europe slightly higher at year-end.
	- European Commission proposed the **Automotive Package** toward end-2025: increased regulatory flexibility for 2030/2035 targets, promotes EU-made vehicles, electrifying company fleets.

- ## United States — Policy-Driven Collapse in Q4

	- Full-year 2025: ~1.5M sales, slightly below 2024 — only major market to stagnate.
	- **Policy sequence**:
		- Jan 2025: Executive Order 14154 directed the government to end EV support (financial incentives + fuel economy standards).
		- Jul 2025: One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) eliminated financial penalties for non-compliance with fuel economy standards; terminated EV tax credits (new and used) after September 2025.
	- **Result**: Q4 2025 EV sales were 45% lower than Q4 2024; Q1–Q3 had been ~15% higher vs prior year. Full-year roughly flat because of pull-forward buying ahead of credit expiry.
	- Q4 2025 EV share: only 6–7% of car sales.

- ## Emerging Markets (EMDEs ex-China)

	- Sales reached ~1.2 million in 2025 (+80% YoY), a record. Chinese-made EVs account for 60% of sales in these markets.
	- Several markets doubled in size; Southeast Asia saw the largest absolute increase.
	- BEV share in EMDEs (80%) is higher than major markets (65%) — but heterogeneous: Southeast Asia >90% BEV, Brazil 45%, Uzbekistan 33%.
	- More than half of the 2 million sales outside the three major markets came from Latin America, Asia-Pacific, and Middle East countries that crossed the 10% EV sales share threshold.

- ## Investment Implications

	- **China market deceleration is structural, not temporary**: the 75%+ CAGR era is over; 2025's <20% growth likely the new norm as the market matures and penetration approaches saturation in urban areas. Subsidy-pull volume inflation via zero-mileage cars adds noise.
	- **Europe is the cleanest policy-driven growth story in 2025**: EU CO2 standards are the binding constraint, not consumer preference. Regulatory risk (Automotive Package flexibility) could soften the 2030/2035 targets and reduce urgency for OEMs.
	- **US EV market is structurally impaired for 2026+**: OBBBA removal of both consumer credits and OEM compliance incentives removes the two pillars of US EV demand. Q4 2025 run-rate (~6–7% share) is the likely floor.
	- **Chinese EV export machine is the primary growth vector for EMDEs**: ~60% of emerging-market EV sales are Chinese-made. Any tariff escalation in Europe or additional markets directly hits Chinese OEM export revenue.
	- **Norway subsidy phase-out (2028)** is a useful leading indicator: the highest-penetration market globally is beginning to remove support. As the EV stock share (>33%) makes fiscal subsidies less defensible, this pattern will recur in other leading markets.
