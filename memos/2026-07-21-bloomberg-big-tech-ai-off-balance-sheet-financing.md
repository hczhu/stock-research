- ## Big Tech AI Financing Reopens Off-Balance-Sheet Accounting Questions
	- **Source**: Amanda Iacone, “Big Tech AI Spree Revives Accounting Devices That Toppled Enron,” Bloomberg Tax deep dive, July 21, 2026; graphics by Irfan Uraizee. User-provided 14-page PDF.
	- **Thesis**: The AI buildout is moving a growing share of infrastructure financing into variable interest entities, special-purpose vehicles, future leases, purchase commitments, guarantees, and credit backstops. These structures can be legitimate risk-sharing tools, but they make conventional leverage, EBITDA, return-on-capital, and free-cash-flow comparisons less reliable because the project debt and interest expense can remain outside the hyperscaler's consolidated statements while the economic dependence persists through tenancy, guarantees, equity exposure, and future cash payments.

- ## How the Structures Change Reported Economics
	- A VIE or SPV can own the data center and raise the construction debt while the hyperscaler contributes minority equity, signs a long-term lease or capacity agreement, provides guarantees, or becomes the principal tenant.
	- If the hyperscaler does not control the entity and is not deemed its primary beneficiary, the project company's assets, debt, and interest expense can remain outside the parent's consolidated financial statements.
	- This can improve the parent's reported optics:
		- **Leverage** looks lower because the project debt belongs to another legal entity.
		- **EBITDA** looks higher because interest on the SPV's debt is not a parent-company expense.
		- **Free cash flow** looks stronger during construction because external lenders fund more of the upfront build.
		- **Return on capital** can look better because fewer infrastructure assets appear in the parent's invested-capital base.
	- The economics do not disappear. Future lease or service payments consume cash, guarantees can be called, equity can be impaired, and a sole tenant may still depend operationally on the asset.
	- Moody's incorporates future lease commitments when assessing debt because the payments drain cash even before they become recognized lease liabilities.

- ## Meta Hyperion Is the Clearest Consolidation Test
	- Meta and Blue Owl contributed equity to a separate structure financing the Louisiana Hyperion data-center campus; the entity raised **\$27B** of debt.
	- Another related entity is the landlord, while a Meta subsidiary is the sole tenant. Meta also provides construction management plus administrative and property-management services.
	- Meta says it should not consolidate the debt because it is not responsible for finding replacement or additional tenants, which it identifies as the activity most important to the entity's economic performance.
	- Ernst & Young identified Meta's consolidation assessment as challenging and involving significant judgment.
	- Veritas Investment Research analyst Ben Butler argues Meta should consolidate Hyperion because it is the sole tenant, an equity investor, the property manager, and subject to billions of disclosed obligations.
	- The disagreement captures the core analytical problem: legal control can be separated from economic reliance, leaving investors to decide whether reported accounting or contractual substance is the better leverage measure.

- ## Company Accounting Signals
	- **[[$META]]**: the highest-profile judgment call. The **\$46B** maximum exposure, sole-tenancy position, equity stake, and management responsibilities make Hyperion economically important even without consolidation.
	- **[[$GOOGL]]**: keeps VIE arrangements involving data-center leases, credit backstops, and power-infrastructure guarantees off balance sheet because it says it does not direct the structures and is not the primary beneficiary.
	- **[[$MSFT]]**: discloses little beyond stating that it does not consolidate its VIEs, leaving investors with less detail about the structures and risk allocation.
	- **[[$ORCL]]**: combines **\$260B** of future leases with stretched leverage and a **\$3.3B** lease backstop. S&P Global downgraded its credit partly because of leverage, making Oracle the clearest case where contractual commitments are already affecting external credit assessment.
	- **[[$NVDA]]**: purchase and optics commitments improve supply visibility but lock in future cash outlays before goods and services are received. Vendor arrangements can transmit an AI-demand slowdown upstream even when hyperscaler project debt remains isolated.

- ## The Enron Analogy Has Limits
	- Enron used off-balance-sheet entities to hide debt and exploit rules that allowed nonconsolidation when a third party supplied a small equity stake.
	- Modern VIE and lease rules are stricter, and companies disclose more about unconsolidated entities, future leases, guarantees, and maximum loss exposure in financial-statement footnotes.
	- An SPV is not evidence of fraud. It can allocate construction, financing, and residual-value risk to parties willing to hold it.
	- The relevant parallel is opacity: if investors focus only on consolidated debt and headline cash flow, they can miss obligations and dependencies that better represent the economics.
	- Rapid chip obsolescence and uncertain long-run utilization make that allocation consequential. The option to abandon capacity has value to a hyperscaler only if lenders, landlords, developers, or vendors can absorb the residual loss.

- ## Public-Market Interpretation
	- Compare companies using both recorded liabilities and disclosed commitments; the report's **\$1.8T versus \$1.4T** aggregate gap shows how rankings can change when future leases and purchases are included.
	- Track VIE maximum-loss exposure, project-company debt, guarantees, credit backstops, sole-tenant status, lease commencements, and ratings adjustments alongside capex and net debt.
	- For hyperscalers, off-balance-sheet financing preserves near-term flexibility but can create a delayed cash-flow and margin burden when lease payments begin or capacity proves underutilized.
	- For [[$NVDA]] and infrastructure suppliers, commitments support near-term demand visibility; the second-order risk is that customers renegotiate, defer, or transfer stress when project returns disappoint.
	- For private-credit providers such as [[$OWL]], the opportunity is long-duration infrastructure income backed by large tenants. The risk is correlated exposure to the same AI utilization assumptions, technology cycle, and refinancing market.

- ## Evidence Limits
	- Bloomberg's chart combines future leases and purchase commitments, which are not economically identical to interest-bearing debt and should not be added to liabilities without considering timing, discounting, cancellation rights, and associated assets.
	- The PDF's chart clips some company labels at the right margin; this memo uses only company-level figures that were legible in the chart or stated explicitly in the article.
	- The report identifies accounting and disclosure risk but does not establish that any named company violated consolidation rules or concealed required information.
