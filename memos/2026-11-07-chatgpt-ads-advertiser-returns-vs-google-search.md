- ## ChatGPT Ads: Advertiser Returns Lag Google Search in an Early Study
	- **Sources**: Danny Goodwin, Search Engine Land, “ChatGPT ads generate 64% less revenue per visit than Google Search: Study,” November 2, 2026, supplied by the user; Maximilian Kaiser and Christian Schulze, [“Can Advertising Fund Free AI? Evidence from ChatGPT’s International Advertising Rollout”](https://ecommerceresearch.org/chatgpt-ads/), October 2026 working paper, under review.
	- **Thesis**: The early evidence favors Google Search’s ability to deliver valuable commercial traffic. ChatGPT needs better conversion, lower ad prices, or additional demonstrable advertiser value to compete on returns. This is a warning about current advertiser economics, not proof that conversational advertising cannot mature or that Google’s own AI interfaces will monetize like traditional Search.
	- **Critical definition**: “Revenue per visit” is revenue attributed to visits on advertisers’ websites—not OpenAI’s or Google’s advertising revenue per user, query, or session.

- ## Paid Advertising: Results and Sample Size
	- | Metric | ChatGPT paid traffic | Google Search paid traffic / comparison |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | U.S. advertiser revenue per referred session | **\$0.89** | **\$2.45**; ChatGPT is about **64% lower** |
	  | U.S. conversion rate | **0.37%** | **1.17%**; gap of **0.80 percentage points**, approximately **68% lower** on a relative basis |
	  | Within-advertiser comparison | **97%** earned less revenue per ChatGPT visit | Comparison covers **87 advertisers** buying both channels in U.S. traffic |
	  | CPC estimated to match Search ROAS | About **$0.26** | A relative-return benchmark, not a break-even CPC |
	  | Publicly disclosed ChatGPT CPC | **$1.72** for one advertiser | About **6.6x** the estimated parity price; not a platform-wide average |
	- The full dataset covers nearly **10B sessions across 1,898 websites**, June 2025–August 2026. That headline sample must not be mistaken for the sample supporting paid-ad revenue conclusions.
	- **Primary-paper clarification**: the paid panel contains **387 websites**, including **102 with complete financial tracking**; **31** have linked Google Ads cost records. U.S. dual-channel comparisons use the smaller group in the table. These are nested analytical samples, not contradictory sample sizes. [Study and methods](https://ecommerceresearch.org/chatgpt-ads/).

- ## What the Pricing Comparison Means
	- The paper uses a **$0.72 median Google Search CPC** in its cost sample. The parity calculation is **0.72 × (0.89 ÷ 2.45) ≈ 0.26 dollars per ChatGPT click**, assuming comparable margins and tracked-visit economics.
	- At the reported outcomes, the disclosed ChatGPT CPC would need to fall roughly **85%** to reach that benchmark. Alternatively, attributable value per click would need to improve substantially. This is conditional arithmetic, not a forecast of future auction prices.
	- Do not combine the study’s average revenue per session with the disclosed advertiser’s CPC and describe the result as that advertiser’s actual ROAS: the inputs come from different samples. A click and an attributed website session are also not guaranteed to match one-for-one.
	- Matching Search’s revenue-based ROAS is not the same as making the advertiser profitable. Product gross margins, customer lifetime value, refunds, and fulfillment costs still matter; platform inference costs are an entirely separate calculation.

- ## Organic Referrals: Suggestive Decline, Not a Proven User Backlash
	- | Estimate | Finding | Interpretation |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Baseline comparison after ads launched | Organic ChatGPT referrals down **5.9%** over the first **13 weeks** | Not statistically significant; the primary study reports a **95% interval of −12.6% to +1.2%** |
	  | Regional-growth-adjusted estimates | Down **7.2%–8.4%** | Statistically significant under assumed regional ChatGPT growth differences; not unconditional confirmation of the baseline result |
	- Fewer unpaid outbound referrals do not necessarily mean fewer ChatGPT users, lower retention, or damaged trust. Ads could change the allocation of outbound clicks; regional growth and other unobserved differences can also affect comparisons.
	- These results should not be added mechanically to the paid-conversion shortfall. One concerns organic traffic volume after ad availability changes; the other compares outcomes among visitors arriving from paid channels.

- ## What the Study Can and Cannot Establish
	- The paper is observational, not a randomized advertising experiment. Advertisers select into channels; campaign targeting, maturity, creative quality, audience mix, and intent can differ. The comparisons describe observed outcomes, not the guaranteed incremental return from moving a dollar of budget.
	- Attribution is last-click and website-based. It may miss later or indirect purchases; it also does not establish that every tracked purchase was caused by the ad. Any argument that missing purchases close the gap needs evidence that they add disproportionately more value to ChatGPT than to Search.
	- **[[$GOOGL]]**: the findings support a near-term commercial-intent and performance-marketing advantage. They do not establish that Google AI Mode has the same economics as conventional paid Search or that Search is immune to agents taking over discovery.
	- **OpenAI**: advertiser retention creates pressure to improve matching and conversion or reduce effective prices. Lower prices would require more monetizable engagement to generate the same platform revenue; raising ad load could create a separate user-experience trade-off.
	- The key distinction is audience scale versus commercial yield. Large ChatGPT usage alone does not establish an advertising business with Search-like returns. Equally, a young ad product’s weak early results do not establish its long-run ceiling.
