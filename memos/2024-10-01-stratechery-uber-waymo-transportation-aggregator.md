- ## Uber and Waymo: The Transportation Aggregator Thesis
	- **Source**: Ben Thompson, Stratechery, “Taking Waymo, Uber and Waymo,” October 1, 2024 — https://stratechery.com/2024/taking-waymo-uber-and-waymo/
	- **Thesis**: Thompson predicts that autonomous vehicles will strengthen rather than displace Uber if the market separates into specialized layers. Uber can own demand, dispatch, utilization, human surge capacity, and physical fleet operations; Waymo can supply high-margin autonomy software; and OEMs or third parties can finance and manufacture the vehicles. Uber's greatest threat is not autonomy itself, but Waymo becoming an independent, vertically integrated operator willing to accept the capital intensity and lower returns required to own the customer.

- ## The 2024 Waymo-Uber Partnership
	- Waymo and Uber announced that autonomous rides in Austin and Atlanta would launch in early 2025 **only through the Uber app**.
	- The fleet was expected to grow to **hundreds of Waymo Jaguar I-PACE vehicles** across the two cities.
	- The operating responsibilities reveal the likely industry split:
		- **Uber**: customer demand, dispatch, cleaning, repair, and general depot operations.
		- **Waymo**: the autonomous-driving system, testing, roadside assistance, and selected rider-support functions.
	- The expansion followed **tens of thousands of autonomous trips** served through the companies' existing Phoenix partnership.
	- Thompson sees this structure as strategically logical: Uber should not bear the cost and technical risk of developing autonomy, while Alphabet should avoid recreating a ride-hailing network and labor-intensive fleet operation.

- ## Why Uber Remains Useful in an Autonomous Market
	- **Demand aggregation**: Expensive autonomous vehicles need high utilization. Uber's installed rider base and marketplace liquidity can keep more vehicles occupied than a new single-supplier network.
	- **Hybrid dispatch**: Autonomy will initially work only for selected routes, weather, and operating conditions. Uber can assign each trip to either an autonomous vehicle or a human driver.
	- **Surge capacity**: Concerts, sporting events, and demand peaks can exceed the capacity of a fixed AV fleet. Human drivers provide variable supply without requiring idle vehicle capex during normal periods.
	- **Fleet operations**: Cleaning, charging, repairs, and depot management are local, labor-intensive, and relatively low-margin. Thompson argues that these tasks fit Uber's operating model better than Alphabet's high-margin cost structure.
	- **Customer ownership**: If riders request transportation through Uber rather than choosing a specific AV provider, autonomy becomes one supply type within Uber's marketplace.

- ## Thompson's Predictions for Uber
	- **Autonomous vehicles will reach consumers primarily through ride-hailing networks**, not replace those networks with wholly new standalone services.
	- **A long hybrid period is more likely than rapid full autonomy**: human drivers remain essential for complex trips and peak demand even as AV coverage expands.
	- **Uber can become the transportation Aggregator** by matching heterogeneous supply to demand and maximizing the utilization of costly vehicles.
	- **Waymo remaining inside Alphabet favors Uber** because Alphabet is structurally less suited to low-margin vehicle ownership, depot operations, and city-by-city service management.
	- **Waymo would need to become a standalone company to maximize its chance of owning the market**. A spinout could accept lower returns on capital and build the operational capabilities required for vertical integration.
	- Thompson's most likely long-run structure:
		- **Uber owns the customer and marketplace.**
		- **OEMs, potentially including Chinese manufacturers, build the vehicles.**
		- **Waymo supplies the high-margin autonomous-driving software**, earning less revenue than a vertically integrated fleet owner but retaining a more attractive return profile.

- ## Bargaining Power and Value Capture
	- Uber's advantage rises with the number of AV suppliers: fragmented vehicle and autonomy supply makes a neutral demand aggregator more valuable.
	- Waymo gains leverage if its driving system is uniquely capable and riders actively seek the Waymo brand rather than a generic autonomous ride.
	- The partnership is complementary today because each party contributes a scarce capability: Waymo provides credible autonomy; Uber supplies customers, utilization, and physical operations.
	- Over time, the strategic contest is over the consumer relationship. An Uber-only launch in Austin and Atlanta supports Thompson's aggregation thesis, while a growing Waymo-owned app and fleet would support vertical integration.

- ## Consumer Experience as Strategic Evidence
	- Thompson found the Waymo ride at least as competent as a human-driven trip and unusually emotionally engaging because the vehicle greeted and spoke to him.
	- The experience increased his affinity for Google, suggesting autonomous vehicles can humanize an otherwise utilitarian technology brand.
	- However, the novelty faded by the second ride as he returned to his phone. This rapid normalization suggests safety and availability may become expected features, shifting differentiation toward distribution, price, utilization, and service reliability.

- ## Forecast Scorecard
	- Evidence supporting **Uber as aggregator** would include additional AV providers joining Uber, Uber-exclusive AV launches, improving AV utilization through the network, and growth in Uber-operated fleet services.
	- Evidence supporting **Waymo vertical integration** would include rapid adoption of the Waymo app, direct launches without Uber, large-scale Waymo-controlled fleet financing, and internalization of depot operations.
	- Evidence supporting the **hybrid network** would include AV concentration in predictable routes and geographies while human drivers continue to cover weather, complex trips, and demand spikes.
	- The central economic question is whether Uber's utilization and demand advantages remain large enough to justify its marketplace take rate once AV supply consolidates into a smaller number of sophisticated counterparties.
