- ## "Thoughts About Scaling Law" — the GLM-5.3 release note as a scaling argument
	- **Source**: First-party blog post accompanying the **GLM-5.3** release (the GLM line is Z.ai's; GLM-5.x is also the benchmark model in [[2026-08-09-semianalysis-tilert-inferencex-gpu-vs-dataflow-asic]]). **Read it as a vendor argument, not neutral analysis** — the piece exists to explain why a release with *no increase in parameters* should be taken seriously. The literature it cites is real and checkable; the conclusion it draws about its own product is not independently verified, and the post reports **no benchmark numbers at all**.
	- **Thesis**: Parameter count is not a standalone quantity — it is only interpretable alongside data volume, where compute is spent, and who runs the model under what conditions. The argument's payload is that **total parameters matter only up to a threshold** ("enough to hold the world"), after which capability comes from **effective depth per forward pass and, above all, post-training**. GLM-5.3 is offered as the controlled experiment: same base, same architecture, same total *and* activated parameters as GLM-5.2, plus one month of RL on long-horizon environments.
- ## The correction history — the same law revised three times
	- | Regime | Prescription | What broke it |
	  |----------------------|-----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
	  | **Kaplan et al. (2020)** | Grow parameters faster than data, roughly **2.7:1** | The fit's error **compounded with every order of magnitude of compute**, so the largest models of that generation were the **most** misallocated |
	  | **Hoffmann et al. (2022)** — Chinchilla | ~**20 tokens per parameter**; with sufficient compute both should grow **at the same rate**. Fit across **400 models** | Optimised **training** compute only, for a model trained once and evaluated once |
	  | **Inference-aware** | Smaller models trained **far longer** — deliberate over-training | — |
	  | **Sparsity / MoE** | Total and activated parameters must be treated as **separate quantities**; the dense 20:1 ratio does not transfer | — |
	- **The industry followed Kaplan and had to reverse**: GPT-3, Gopher, and MT-NLG are named as the compliant generation, and the post's verdict is that the trillion-parameter round was *"a detour the whole field took together and then reversed."*
	- **The over-training numbers are the concrete part**: Llama-2-7B at roughly **290 tokens per parameter** and Gemma-2-9B at roughly **889** — versus Chinchilla's 20. That is **~14× and ~44× past compute-optimal**, deliberately, because inference dominates lifetime cost once a model is called billions of times a day. This is the mechanical version of the claim recorded in [[2026-08-15-beren-scaling-is-subtler-than-it-seems]] that **nobody does Chinchilla-style pretraining anymore**.
- ## The MoE decomposition — the most investable idea in the post
	- The claim is that in a sparse model two quantities do different jobs and must not be summed:
		- **Total parameters** → roughly **how much the model can hold**: knowledge, facts, the long tail.
		- **Activated parameters and effective depth** → roughly **how far it can think**: how many steps of a causal chain it can carry before it comes apart.
	- Supporting citations: **Roberts et al. (2025)** find optimal tokens-per-parameter is **task-dependent** — **memorization favours more parameters, reasoning favours more data**. Follow-up MoE work is stronger still: at fixed TPP, **raising total parameters actually degrades reasoning**, while **activating more experts reliably helps it**.
	- **Why this matters beyond model design — it splits the hardware demand vector in two.** Total parameters drive **memory capacity** (you must store the weights). Activated parameters and depth drive **compute and memory bandwidth per token**. If reasoning capability comes from activation and depth rather than from total parameter count, then the capacity-led path is **self-limiting rather than merely expensive**.
	- **This is a direct tension with what I recorded in [[2026-08-23-beren-architecture-research-as-scaling-bottlenecks]]** — that MoE extends total parameters ~2 OOMs (toward 10T) and therefore drives storage demand faster than active compute. That framing assumes bigger total is always better. This post says at fixed TPP it is actively harmful to reasoning. **Both cannot be right about where the next order of magnitude goes**, and the resolution determines whether incremental memory demand is capacity-shaped or bandwidth-shaped. Worth tracking as an open question rather than assuming the capacity story.
	- The applied example is pointed and reveals the intended market: *"Finding a vulnerability is not a retrieval problem. It doesn't come from having memorized more CVEs; it comes from carrying a twenty-step chain of inference to the end without losing the thread."* **Reasoning depth is being positioned as the product**, for agentic and security work.
- ## GLM-5.3 as the stated experiment
	- The design is genuinely clean **if the description is accurate**: same base, same architecture, same total and activated parameters as GLM-5.2, with the only variable being **one month of scaling long-horizon environments and RL**. Any gain is then attributable to post-training alone.
	- **What is missing is the entire result.** The post says only that *"the gains are not marginal"* — no benchmark, no metric, no magnitude. **A controlled experiment reported without its measurement is a claim, not evidence**, and this is the load-bearing gap in the piece.
	- If the claim survives independent benchmarking, the read-through is significant: **a month of RL on environments produced a step change at constant parameters and constant pretraining compute.** That is direct support for the structural spend identified in [[2026-08-15-beren-scaling-is-subtler-than-it-seems]] — that the challenge is shifting from sourcing data toward **constructing environments and verifiers** — and it is a data point for capability gains **decoupling from pretraining capex**.
	- It also sits against the observation in that same memo that **there is still no publicly accepted RL scaling law**. This post is effectively arguing from a scaling relationship it cannot yet name.
- ## The multi-dial framing, and where I would push back
	- The closing frame is the reusable part: scaling has **more than one dial**, they turned post-training because it *"had the most slack left in it — not because the others are finished,"* and **"the one worth turning next is rarely the one that was worth turning last."** Base model size, pretraining data, and compute per forward pass are all described as still available, with mid-training and pre-training named as future candidates.
	- **Three reasons to discount the framing, none of which the post addresses**:
		- **"Most slack" and "cheapest" are not distinguished.** One month of RL is vastly cheaper than a new pretraining run. A lab that turns the cheap dial and reports success has not demonstrated it was the highest-return dial, only the accessible one.
		- **There is an alternative explanation for which dial got turned.** A China-based lab operating under compute export restrictions has structural reasons to extract gains from post-training rather than from a larger base model. "We chose not to scale parameters" and "we could not scale parameters" produce the same release note.
		- **The threshold claim is unfalsifiable as written.** "Enough to hold the world" has no value attached, so the assertion that total parameters stop mattering above it cannot be checked, and conveniently places the current model above the line.
	- **What survives the discount** is still worth holding: the citation trail (Kaplan → Chinchilla → inference-aware over-training → task-dependent TPP → MoE reasoning degradation) is a real and legible progression, and the total-versus-activated distinction is the correct way to read any MoE parameter announcement. Combined with [[2025-03-01-beren-scaling-laws-are-a-property-of-data]], the picture is consistent: **the headline parameter number has been the least informative figure in a model release for several generations now**, and this post is a vendor conceding as much about its own product.
