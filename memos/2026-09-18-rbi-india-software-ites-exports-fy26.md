- ## RBI Survey: India's Software and ITES Exports Reach US\$221.4B, +8.2% — the Base Rate for the "AI Eats IT Services" Thesis
	- **Source**: Reserve Bank of India press release, **Sep 18 2026** — annual survey on computer software and ITES exports, **2025-26 round**. **7,569** companies contacted, **2,363** responded, covering **~89%** of estimated total software services exports. Official statistical survey, not a vendor or brokerage estimate.
	- **Why this belongs in the repo**: it is the **only high-coverage, first-party measure of what the global IT-services base actually did**, and the repo had no India IT coverage at all. Every argument about AI compressing outsourced software and business process work should be tested against this series first.
	- **Thesis**: Through FY2025-26 the sector grew **+8.2%** in dollar terms — the disruption has not shown up in aggregate revenue yet. The exposure is visible in the **composition** rather than the growth rate: **91.7% of delivery is off-site**, **25.3% is BPO**, and only **2.9% is software product development**. That is a revenue base built almost entirely on remotely-delivered labour arbitrage with almost no product IP, which is precisely the shape most exposed to automation.
- ## Headline figures
	- | Metric | FY2025-26 | Growth |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Software services exports, excl. overseas commercial presence | **US\$221.4B** | **+8.2%** |
	  | Total incl. foreign affiliate services | **US\$239.3B** | **+9.5%** |
	  | Foreign affiliates' local business | **US\$17.9B** (from US\$13.9B) | **+28.8%** |
	- **The fastest-growing line is the one that is not offshore.** Foreign affiliates' local business grew **3.5× faster than core exports** — India's IT majors are increasingly delivering through local presence in client markets rather than cross-border. Read as client-proximity demand, visa/immigration adaptation, or both; either way it is a **structurally lower-margin, higher-cost** delivery mode than the offshore model the sector was built on.
- ## Composition, which is where the exposure sits
	- | Segment | Value | Share of US\$221.4B |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | **IT Services** | US\$147.0B | **66.4%** |
	  | **BPO Services** | US\$56.0B | **25.3%** |
	  | Engineering Services | US\$12.0B | 5.4% |
	  | **Software Product Development** | **US\$6.4B** | **2.9%** |
	- **BPO at a quarter of the base is the single most automatable exposure in the table.** Rules-based business-process work is the clearest near-term target for agentic automation, and it is US\$56B of annual export revenue.
	- **Software product development at 2.9% is the structural weakness.** After three decades, India's software exports remain overwhelmingly *services*, not *products*. A services business scales with headcount; a product business scales with AI leverage. The sector has almost none of the latter to fall back on.
- ## Concentration and delivery
	- | Dimension | Reading |
	  |---|---|
	  | **USA** | **54.1%** — US\$119.7B |
	  | Europe | 31.8% — US\$70.3B, of which **UK 15.4%** (US\$34.0B) |
	  | Asia | 6.3% — US\$13.9B |
	  | Australia & NZ | 2.5% — US\$5.5B |
	  | **Off-site delivery** | **91.7%** — US\$203.0B |
	  | On-site delivery | 8.3% — US\$18.4B |
	  | Private limited companies | US\$134.6B, **60.8%**, +9.3% |
	  | Public limited companies | US\$81.7B, 36.9% |
	- **US plus UK is 69.5% of exports.** A single-country tech-spending slowdown, a tariff regime, or a visa-policy change transmits almost directly. This is not a diversified export base.
	- **Off-site at 91.7% cuts both ways and is the crux.** It is why the margin model works, and it is why the revenue is exposed: work that never required physical presence is work an agent can plausibly take. The 8.3% delivered on-site is the hardest to automate and the least profitable.
	- **GATS mode split** (of the US\$239.3B total): Mode 1 cross-border **US\$202.7B (84.7%)**, Mode 4 natural-person presence US\$18.4B (7.7%), Mode 3 commercial presence US\$17.9B (7.5%), Mode 2 consumption abroad US\$0.3B (0.1%).
- ## Currency exposure
	- Invoicing: **USD 72.6%**, EUR 9.6%, **INR 6.3%**, GBP 6.0%, AUD 2.0%.
	- **93.7% of revenue is invoiced in foreign currency** against a largely rupee cost base, so INR moves pass almost undamped into reported margins. Any model of Indian IT earnings is partly an FX model, and USD at 72.6% means dollar strength is a first-order earnings variable, not a footnote.
- ## How to use this
	- **Treat +8.2% as the base rate.** Any claim that AI is compressing IT services has to explain why a sector with 89%-coverage official data grew high single digits through FY26. On this evidence, disruption is a forward thesis, not an observed one.
	- **The markers that would confirm it are compositional, not top-line.** Watch **BPO's 25.3% share** for the first decline, the **off-site 91.7%** for erosion, and whether **foreign-affiliate local business keeps growing at 3.5× the core** — the last being a sign that value is migrating to on-shore presence where automation bites less but margins are thinner.
	- **Next release is the test.** This is an annual survey; FY2026-27 is the first year in which agentic coding and process automation could plausibly register in aggregate export revenue.
