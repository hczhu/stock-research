- **Thesis**: Rare dual-insider view of the two best-executing Chinese internet orgs of the last decade. Core mental model: **business model dictates org design**, and in the AI era the classic product/eng/test/algo division collapses into coding-capable generalists — smaller, faster teams. Investment-relevant readthroughs on Meituan/PDD's retail moat, China AI talent flows, and software productivity.

- ## Business Model → Org Structure (the core framework)
	- In China internet, only **Meituan and Pinduoduo (PDD)** truly understand *retail*; **Alibaba and ByteDance** understand *advertising*. Retail is a "bitter/hard business" (苦活) — Meituan spent the last ~decade crossing that moat.
	| | ByteDance (ads) | Meituan (retail + tech) |
	|---|---|---|
	| Chain / cycle | **Short chain, short cycle** | **Long chain, long cycle** |
	| Margin | **High margin** | **Low margin** |
	| Org implication | Hire the best people, place flexibly, move fast | Persistent iteration; deep operating plans, coordinate thousands |
	| Recruiting analog | **Google** model | **Amazon** model |
	- Both founders' (Zhang Yiming / Wang Xing) underlying beliefs are the *same*; surface practices look opposite because the two businesses have opposite economics.
	- 2021–2026: China mobile internet growth slowed → this is precisely when **org capability** gets revealed (who can execute in a down market).

- ## Founder / Top-Talent Traits (what to look for)
	- **Strong learning ability + high curiosity** — learning speed past a threshold is enough; curiosity is what makes them dig to first principles (cites Bezos, Musk "first-principles").
	- **IQ: top ~1% is plenty**, not Olympiad-level. "We're not doing physics." Top 1% of an industry ≈ already rare enough; the US-VC-era obsession with elite pedigree is overstated — many A-share founders aren't from top schools.
	- **Low ego = confident *and* humble** — can't be knocked down / holds own judgment, yet genuinely hears others and knows what they don't know. VCs favor founders who've been "forged" by setbacks.
	- **Very strong motivation** — money, mission, or achievement; must be strong enough to survive brutal competition, being betrayed by rivals/reports. Note: it's *not* about being from a poor background — Zhang Yiming / Wang Xing weren't poor. The hard part is *spiritual* endurance, not material.
	- **High energy / physical stamina** — cites Lei Jun (Xiaomi) running all-day meetings.
	- **Ability to build the org** — the single most important thing for whether a company scales; can't rely on one person.

- ## Org-Building Playbook (startup stage)
	- Split org work into two big time sinks: **(1) People** (select/use/develop/motivate/retain) and **(2) Business-people fit** (define → decompose to individuals → form合力 → identify & reward performance).
	- **~80–90% of people-time should go to recruiting.**
	- **Culture**: high-leverage, low-time. All top companies' cultures are near-identical ("be candid," "seek truth / face reality," "pursue excellence") because the scientific way to win is the same. What matters: the **top ~20 people actually behave this way**, and founders publicly state what they're *against*. (ByteDance calls it culture; Meituan calls it "leadership principles.")
	- **Comp / leveling**: at startup stage, **skip 360 reviews, job-leveling, competency ladders** — founders should see the front line and judge fast; fast-growing startups' valuation jumps (5–10x) swamp small comp unfairness. These systems only matter once large.
	- **Training barely works**: Meituan's **70-20-10** — growth is 70% from doing/battle, 20% from good seniors/peers, 10% from formal training. "People aren't trained; they're forged in battle and selected." Best lever = **give people the battlefield**; the good ones surface. Especially true in AI, where 15-yr vets and 3–5-yr people understand the new paradigm about equally.

- ## Recruiting Deep-Dive (the highest-leverage work)
	- Five stages: **(1) clarify need → (2) source supply (扩供给) → (3) judge → (4) negotiate → (5) close/onboard.** Most people over-invest in interviewing (stage 3) and under-invest in **1 & 2 — which should get 50–80% of time.**
	- **Sourcing supply is the single most important thing** in company-building — directly determines what org you can build and whether you win. Three sub-questions: what is a "good supply," what good supply *I* need, and how to reach/close them. You often only need **top-of-mid-tier** talent for the current stage, not top 0.1%.
	- **Pre-stock talent**: think monthly about (a) business problems, (b) who you'll need, how many, when — then pre-stock/pre-meet candidates. Waiting until a problem appears is too late.
	- **Reference checks (背调) > interviews.** Even elite execs' interview judgments are **wrong ~20%**, and for startups he'd assume **~1/3 wrong**. Interviews confuse 90+ people with 10–20 people ("brilliant vs. terrible look alike after prep"). Trust people you know who've *worked with* the candidate.
	- **Negotiate expectations, not money.** Good candidates care about *what they'll get to do in 3–5 years* and their deepest driver (achievement / impact / recognition / money). Most people (60–80% even at big firms) haven't thought clearly about why they'd leave — help them figure it out; money is the easy part.
	- **Comp timing view: "every day from now is the best day to hire."** AI tightens supply/demand → hiring only gets harder → whatever you pay today is a bargain. (His stage-specific tactic vs peers: pay a premium + longer vesting to lock people, cheaper than churn.)
	- A capable AI/tech founder should **do sourcing themselves** (search Xiaohongshu, comments, profiles); the HR/"H2" role is mainly the **AI-to-real-world connection layer** (finding contact info, outreach) — like an investment associate, while the founder acts as the partner making the judgment.

- ## Google vs Amazon Recruiting Models (the two archetypes)
	- **Google (short-cycle / high-margin)**: dedicated **sourcing team**, a generic **interview pool** that interviews *without a specific role* (assess general quality), hire-committee decides against a "Google bar," then place internally. Fits high-margin / ByteDance-like orgs. Google now runs a **multi-thousand-person recruiting org** (ByteDance too, at peak).
	- **Amazon (long-cycle / low-margin)**: heavy annual **operating plan** → derive headcount/roles/cost → hire against a **bar-raiser**, must be **above the 50th percentile of the current team**, plus **~10% forced attrition/yr** → keeps the team at "immortal legion" quality over 3–4 years. Fits low-margin, slow-changing industries. Too heavy for startups, but the **bar-raiser** concept is worth borrowing.

- ## AI-Era Org Changes (most forward-looking)
	- **The product/frontend/backend/test/algo split collapses**: one person owns a business slice end-to-end (product→test) → **drastically less coordination overhead**.
	- **Coding-capable non-eng teams** (sales, growth, success) see **5–10x efficiency**, and no longer need dedicated eng support — "a huge change in operating cadence."
	- Argues **against one-person / solo AI companies (OPC)** — "a waste of this era." Every direction should have **one good person + AI**; that combination can build something far greater. Analogy: it's at least as big as early PC internet / ~2007 — do the big thing, not a personal project.
	- New hires must be **AI-native**; for engineers, coding with AI is a **hard requirement (100%)**.

- ## Founder Demographics (00后 vs 90后)
	- Current AI wave started **~Nov 30, 2022** (ChatGPT); ~3.5 years in. It's **unfair for post-2000 (00后) founders to compete now** — too few years out of school, no network, can't staff a good person per direction, and face brutal (fair + foul) competition + fundraising pressure. He backs **90后** founders who've already explored directions, built a decent team, and raised money.
	- Prefers founders who are **"mature but retain a youthful edge (少年感)"** and remain **pure/on the front line** — genuinely wanting to change the industry, not "executive-polished" and detached.

- ## China AI Talent Flows (investment signal)
	- Career-path choices over the last few years diverged massively: joining **ByteDance / Doubao** vs. **big-model startups (e.g. MiniMax, Zhipu)** vs. **AI unicorns** produced **10–100x return differences in just 2–3 years**.
	- **High churn back to incumbents**: many who joined MiniMax / Zhipu left and returned to big companies. Signal: intense but unstable China AI labor market; incumbents (esp. ByteDance/Doubao) retain gravitational pull.

- ## Investment Implications
	- **Meituan ([[$3690.HK]]) & PDD ([[$PDD]]) retail moat**: insider frames them as the *only* China internet players who truly master retail ops — a durable, hard-won moat (low-margin/long-chain execution) that ad-first peers (Alibaba, ByteDance) structurally lack. Supports quality-of-execution premium for Meituan/PDD.
	- **ByteDance org strength** (private): high-margin ad model + Google-style "hire-the-best, place-flexibly" recruiting machine → readthrough to its ability to keep winning talent and launch products (Doubao) fast.
	- **Software productivity / margins**: AI collapsing specialized eng roles into generalists → **smaller teams, 5–10x on non-eng functions** → structurally supports software operating-margin expansion and lower headcount growth (relevant to SaaS names broadly).
	- **AI talent = wage inflation**: "every day is the best day to hire" → rising comp for scarce AI talent is a cost tailwind for labor-market/recruiting tech and a cost headwind for AI-builders.
	- **AI recruiting TAM**: guest's startup targets AI-driven supply/demand matching, especially **blue-collar** hiring where he claims **~99% of Chinese blue-collar workers have been scammed** — large, low-trust market ripe for disruption.
