- ## Atlassian in academic papers — AI/SDLC research survey
	- **Source**: 9 arXiv papers that mention Atlassian, collected under `tmp-files/atlassian-pdfs/`. Extracted via `pdftotext` + keyword scan for "Atlassian". Links are the arXiv abstract/PDF pages.
	- **Thesis**: Atlassian is one of the most prolific *industrial* publishers of applied "LLM agents inside the SDLC" research, mostly through a standing Monash University collaboration. The papers show Atlassian deploying LLM agents and code-review automation into its own Jira/Bitbucket/Confluence products at production scale with measured results, while third-party researchers increasingly treat Jira/Confluence APIs as the default enterprise-workflow substrate to build on. Net incrementally bullish on [[$TEAM]]'s AI-native product depth; the recurring gating risk across every paper is output reliability (code quality, hallucination).

- ## Bottom line
	- **Atlassian is dogfooding LLM agents in production, not just prototyping.** HULA (coding agents in Jira) and RovoDev Code Reviewer (code review in Bitbucket) are both deployed internally/at scale with real telemetry — this is the applied-research backbone behind the Rovo product story.
	- **The published production numbers are concrete and favorable**: RovoDev Code Reviewer cut PR cycle time 30.8% and human-written review comments 35.6% over a one-year online evaluation; HULA reached a 59% merged-PR rate on real Jira issues.
	- **Platform-centrality signal**: outside researchers (Centific, JKU Linz) build directly on Jira REST / Confluence / Forge APIs as *the* enterprise workflow layer — evidence of ecosystem gravity/moat even when Atlassian is not the author.
	- **Consistent caveat = the risk to watch**: every deployment paper flags code quality, readability, or hallucination as the adoption bottleneck. Atlassian's own follow-up work (readability study, HalluJudge) is aimed squarely at de-risking that — a tell that reliability, not capability, is the thing standing between demos and monetizable trust.

- ## Read-through for $TEAM
	- **Cluster pattern**: 6 of 9 papers are Atlassian-authored or Atlassian-collaborated (mostly the Monash / Chakkrit Tantithamthavorn group). Two product lines dominate — **HULA** (coding agents → Rovo Dev) and **RovoDev Code Reviewer** (code review) — plus supporting reliability work (readability, HalluJudge) and infrastructure telemetry (CI build prediction). This is a coherent, multi-year applied-AI program feeding the Rovo/Teamwork Graph product narrative (see [[2026-07-02-atlassian-team26-ai-products-rovo-teamwork-graph]]).
	- **Why it matters for the thesis**: the strongest bull case for [[$TEAM]] is that it converts proprietary work context + SDLC surface area into agents that do real engineering work. These papers are the receipts — deployed, measured, with double-digit-percent cycle-time and reviewer-load reductions.
	- **The 3 non-Atlassian papers** (Centific RLVR, JKU JI-RADAR) matter as an *ecosystem* signal: when third parties benchmark and build against Jira/Confluence/Forge APIs, it corroborates the platform-moat argument independent of Atlassian's own marketing.
	- **Risk framing for monitoring**: watch whether the reliability gap (code quality / hallucination) narrows in subsequent releases. Atlassian publishing its own guardrail research (HalluJudge) is a positive tell but also confirms the problem is live. Adoption/monetization of Rovo Dev is the metric that turns this research into revenue.
	- **Caveat on the papers themselves**: production percentages come from Atlassian-affiliated studies on Atlassian's own tools — favorable framing is expected; treat the direction as more reliable than the exact magnitudes.
