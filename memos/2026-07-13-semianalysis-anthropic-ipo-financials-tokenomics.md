- ## SemiAnalysis: Anthropic 3Q26 profit over $1B — the Anthropic IPO financials sneak peek
	- **Source**: SemiAnalysis paid article (Brookhart, Huang, Patel; 2026-07-07), 66-page PDF. Bottom-up "Tokenomics Model" reconstruction of Anthropic's financials ahead of its confidential IPO filing (June 1, 2026). WSJ reporting cited as independently confirming the model's accuracy. Full quarterly model data (4 blocks: API revenue by model family, Consumer Subscription Build, Inference Margin Build, Consolidated Income Statement) extracted to the **"SemiAnalysis estimates" sheet of the "Anthropic" spreadsheet** (ID `13sc6xFEOlZDUZggU8ddwYeyJxycOr0xMljj_AhN7x_U`).
	- **Thesis (theirs)**: Anthropic is the clear B2B AI-lab winner — profitable, faster-growing, and structurally higher-margin than OpenAI thanks to a 75–85% usage-based (API) revenue mix levered to Claude Code and agentic workloads. The high-margin API flywheel funds outsized training reinvestment, widening the capability lead. Base case: **$300B ending ARR in 2027 at 20x = $6T EV**, "the world's largest company." They argue Anthropic should IPO first to weaponize its capital access against OpenAI.
	- **My read**: extraordinary claims on the growth data (largely corroborated by WSJ and disclosed NDR), but the valuation math is promotional — even the article's own comment section calls 20x on $300B "fan math." The durable takeaways for the portfolio are structural: hyperscaler TaaS distribution economics, the compute/capital supercycle, and the "labs eat the software multiple" risk to SaaS holdings.

- ## Anthropic data points
	- **ARR trajectory**: $9B (end-2025) → **$30B (end-1Q26)** → **$60B+ (Jul 2026)**. Monthly net-new ARR in 1Q26: Jan +$3B, Feb +$7B, Mar +$11B; now running **$10B+/month NNARR**. Anthropic + OpenAI combined ≈ **$100B ARR**.
	- **Net Dollar Retention 500%** (CFO Krishna Rao, Invest Like the Best, May 2026): the $2B cohort of 1Q25 ARR became $12B of 1Q26's $30B — meaning $18B of the past year's adds are *new* customers still ramping.
	- **Revenue mix**: API/usage = 75–85% (Claude Code supports usage billing); subscriptions only ~15% of ARR; consumer subs just **5%** (vs OpenAI ~40% consumer, ~65% subscription in 1Q26). Claude Code = **>7% of all GitHub commits**; >65% of total lab ARR industry-wide is coding use cases.
	- **Margins**: blended GM **mid-60s%** today vs **−94% in 2024**; API GM **80%+**; consumer margins structurally worse. "EBTIT" (EBIT before training) margin **36% in 2Q26**. GAAP EBIT turns positive 3Q26 at **$1B+ (6% margin)** — the article's title stat. Long-run steady state: GM mid-70s, training ~25% of revenue (48% of compute), other opex 20% of revenue → **30–40% EBIT/FCF margins**.
	- **Efficiency**: ARR per MW of inference compute $16M (≈Oct 2025) → **$60M (later 2026)**. Inference cost per M tokens down ~50x since 1Q24 (model: $10.86 → $0.23) while revenue per M token fell only ~8.5x — the whole margin story.
	- **Reinvestment war chest**: cumulative training spend since 1Q24 of just **$8B** produced today's $60B-ARR business. 2027 post-COGS capital: Anthropic **$160B vs OpenAI $92B**; cumulative EBTIT advantage vs OpenAI ≈ **$250B through 2028**.
	- **Customers**: >$100k/yr customers up **7x y/y**; >$1M/yr up **42x in 2 years**. [[$META]] is the **largest customer** ("tokenmaxxing", 9-figure spend) yet only 3–5% of revenue. Average enterprise Claude Code user spends $150–250/month; 90% of users <$30/day (their anti-"tokenbudgeting" argument).
	- **Consumer**: free MAU 55–60M, 9% paying attach; avg paying US consumer ~$45/mo (card panel). vs OpenAI: ~950M WAU **stuck since Jan**, ~6% attach, ~900M free users costing <$0.70/mo each ≈ 20–25pt gross-margin drag (at equal $100B ARR, ~$25B less gross profit than Anthropic).
	- **Compute constraint**: OpenAI+Anthropic have ~**6GW** today vs **>100GW** unconstrained demand by 2030 (needs >90GW net adds; 2.5GW added 2025, 5GW in 2026). 1Q26 rate-limits/throttling on Claude were the demand>supply symptom. Anthropic renting new compute at **>2x market rates** (SpaceX deal cited).
	- **Growth upside claimed**: Fable release lifts pricing + use cases; **Mythos + cybersecurity** framed as the next Claude-Code-style vertical, ramping faster than coding; then healthcare/finance/biotech s-curves. Sonnet 5 cited as evidence lower-tier closed models can command *higher* pricing than prior gens.

- ## Risks (theirs) + critique (mine)
	- **Their risk list**: tokenbudgeting; OSS + rumored OpenAI price cuts; Google/Meta making coding a 4-horse race (pricing/GM down); US regulatory model blockades ("Fable-style government delays" — called Anthropic's biggest risk being its own government); compute shortages; TaaS mix diluting margins (offset: API GM 80%+ leaves room).
	- **Critique — internal tensions worth keeping**:
		- The **$6T base case (20x $300B 2027 ARR)** assumes NNARR *accelerating* to $15B/month and a multiple no company has held at any comparable scale. The article's own comments flag this ("fan math"; "no company in history has held that multiple at a fraction of that scale").
		- **Switching-cost contradiction**: a whole section praises Bedrock/Foundry because enterprises can *swap models instantly* — then claims durable pricing power. Both can't be fully true; pricing power rests entirely on staying SOTA.
		- **EBTIT** excludes training — for a frontier lab that's excluding "the cost of staying alive" (commenter's phrase, and fair). GAAP EBIT positive 3Q26 is the more honest milestone.
		- The model's own artifacts (see spreadsheet: negative implied per-MW metrics in 3Q26–4Q26) show the MW-denominator build straining at the projection edge.
	- **Independent corroboration**: WSJ article on Anthropic financials matched the model; NDR 500% is a first-party CFO disclosure; ARR milestones consistent with press reporting. The *history* is solid; the *2027 extrapolation* is the speculative part.

- ## Portfolio read-through
	- **Semi/memory longs ([[$DRAM]], [[$TSM]], 2454, [[$SOXX]]-adjacent)**: >90GW of net compute additions needed through 2030 vs 2.5GW added in 2025 — if even half-right, the memory/foundry demand runway extends well past current estimates. Consistent with [[2026-07-07-hyperscaler-capex-goldman-bofa-cy27-28e]] and the DRAM-supercycle thesis; compute renting at >2x market rates says scarcity persists.
	- **Hyperscalers held ([[$GOOG]], [[$META]], [[$AMZN]])**: the TaaS toll-booth (85% share of $28B, 20–30% take rate) is a genuinely new high-margin revenue line — but it comes bundled with equity dilution ($84.75B GOOG raise; META rumored) and FCF-negative 2027. The market has to decide whether to price the toll booth or the dilution first.
	- **SaaS holdings ([[$DDOG]], [[$NET]], [[$SNOW]], [[$TEAM]])**: two-sided. More AI workloads → more to observe/secure/store. But the article's own SaaS-2022 analogy is the warning: when the token-optimization phase eventually arrives, it will look exactly like the cloud-optimization drawdown these names lived through. Also note labs' revenue quality (500% NDR, 80% GM) makes SaaS multiples look expensive by comparison when Anthropic lists.
