- ## SemiAnalysis: Open Models Close the Gap Twice as Fast Every Era — and the Rebuttal Is One Paragraph
	- **Source**: SemiAnalysis, **"Are Open Models Catching Up?"** — Evan Cloutier, Max Kan, Jordan Nanos, Dylan Patel, **Aug 21 2026**, paid. 21 pages. Benchmarks run first-party on **Prime Intellect's** evaluation stack, with **Artificial Analysis** and Datacurve's **DeepSWE leaderboard** supplying the rest.
	- **Methodology worth trusting more than most eval posts**: open models were served "the way they would have been at release" — period-correct vLLM versions, period-correct hardware, sampling settings from the model card — and closed models run against **pinned API versions**. Where the SOTA pick was contested (Fable 5 vs GPT-5.6) they tested both.
	- **Thesis**: The measured finding is clean and it is bearish for model-layer pricing — **each era's capability gap opens smaller and closes in half the time**, and Era 3 closed fastest despite being the era that created the most economic value. The article's own rebuttal is a **compute-concentration argument delivered in two sentences**, and the strongest published comment is that the evidence and the conclusion point in opposite directions.
- ## The finding
	- | Era | Founding closed flagship | Gap when era opened | Time to close | Closed by |
	  |---|---|---|---|---|
	  | **1 · Early scaling** | GPT-3.5 (ChatGPT), Nov '22 | **35.8 pts** | **19.7 months** | Llama-3.1-405B, Jul '24 |
	  | **2 · Reasoning** | o1-preview, Sep '24 | **12.1 pts** | **8.5 months** | R1-0528, May '25 |
	  | **3 · Agentic** | Opus 4.5, Nov '25 | **6.8 pts** | **4.8 months** | Kimi K2.6, Apr '26 |
	- **Both series roughly halve.** Gaps fall 2.96× then 1.78×; closing times fall 2.32× then 1.77×. Extrapolated, the next era closes in **under three months** — which is the article's own default expectation.
	- **The supply side of that is a release every ten days.** June–August 2026 alone: MiniMax M3, Nemotron 3 Ultra, GLM-5.2, Inkling, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Muse Spark 1.2, DeepSeek V4 Pro, GLM-5.3, Qwen3.8-27B.
	- **And the usage is real, which is the change from 2025.** The framing distinction they draw is sharp: there was a "DeepSeek moment" in January 2025, "but **no one actually used R1 to do any economically valuable work.**" GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 are "genuinely capable of many of the same coding and agentic tasks that rocketed Anthropic to **\$65B+ ARR**." For scale on token consumption, **Fireworks alone processes over 40T tokens per day — 2× the OpenAI API's volume at end-March.**
- ## The methodological choice that makes the finding possible
	- **"Every benchmark is a product of a particular era."** A benchmark is built to discriminate among models of its moment; makers then climb it until it saturates and everyone stops caring. Plotting one continuous trend across a decade therefore measures saturation, not capability.
	- So they score each era **only on the benchmarks that defined it**, normalizing each era's best result to 100 and taking equal-weight composites:
		- **Era 1** — GSM8K, HumanEval, MMLU-Pro, TriviaQA. Word problems, single functions, multiple choice. No tools, no harnesses.
		- **Era 2** — GPQA-Diamond, AIME 2026, SimpleQA Verified, Humanity's Last Exam. Harder homework, built to reward thinking over recall. Still no tools.
		- **Era 3** — Terminal-Bench 2.1, BrowseComp-Plus, τ³-Banking, DeepSWE. A terminal, a research corpus, a support desk, a codebase — all with tools and harnesses.
	- **This is a transferable analytical method, not just a result.** Any "is the gap closing" claim benchmarked on a fixed suite is measuring the wrong thing; the right comparison is era-relative. They also deliberately picked newer Era 3 benchmarks "to limit memorization."
- ## Era by era
	- **Era 1 opened with a rout.** GPT-3.5 Turbo vs Llama-2-70B in July 2023: GSM8K 78.8 vs 57.0, HumanEval 76.7 vs 35.3, MMLU-Pro 60.6 vs 28.1, TriviaQA 86.7 vs 39.2 — **composite 75.7 vs 39.9.** Mixtral-8x7B built momentum at 69.9 in Dec '23; Llama-3.1-405B finally cleared GPT-3.5 at 86 in Jul '24; **DeepSeek V3 matched GPT-4o in Dec '24, 94.1 against 95.5.**
	- **Their read on why the frontier looked flat**: through Era 1 the frontier barely rose past GPT-4, "but this was mostly due to priorities. **Turbo and 4o were built to make GPT-4 cheaper and faster, not smarter.**" Worth holding — a flat capability frontier can be a pricing decision rather than a scaling wall.
	- **Era 2 opened at a third of the gap.** o1 vs DeepSeek-R1 in Jan '25: AIME 93.4 vs 74.3, GPQA-Diamond 88.3 vs 85.7, HLE 42.1 vs 38.9, SimpleQA Verified 80.7 vs 56.9 — **composite 76.1 vs 64.0.** "The market puked in response." R1-0528 closed it at 78 in May '25.
	- **Era 3 opened at 6.8 points and closed in under five months** — Kimi K2.6 at 56.3 clearing Opus 4.5, then GLM-5.2 at 72.4 clearing GPT-5.2. **Today's composites put Kimi K3 at 93.0 — above Fable 5 at 89.6 — with GPT-5.6 leading at 99.1.** An open model is ahead of one closed flagship on their own suite and 6.1 points behind the leader.
- ## Two observations that matter more than the scores
	- **Anthropic's absence from Era 2 was a strategy, not a failure.** "Their model cards reported these benchmarks like everyone else's, but they never fought for the top of the leaderboard in this era. While OpenAI and Google traded crowns, **Anthropic was turning Claude into the default coding agent. This set the terms for the next era: the benchmarks that now matter run in a terminal.**" Choosing which axis the next era is scored on is worth more than winning the current one.
	- **Benchmark score and product quality came apart, explicitly.** At the Era 3 starting line **GPT-5.2 scored 58.1 against Opus 4.5's 36.4** — a 22-point lead — yet "most AI experts consider Opus 4.5 the official start of the agentic era due to the reliability of the model," and the higher score "didn't correspond to a better user experience." Their explanation: "the full agentic product (**model + harness**) was now what mattered," Anthropic had iterated on the harness, "Codex, in contrast, was comparatively crude, and OpenAI was simultaneously pursuing side quests like web browsers."
		- **This is the load-bearing caveat on the whole article and they say so**: they still prefer Fable for their own work despite Kimi K3 scoring higher, partly on productization, but "largely because benchmarks aren't a perfect proxy for real work. **This is especially true for public benchmarks, which model makers can easily hill climb by simply creating a bunch of RL environments that closely mimic the benchmark tasks.**" Open labs optimizing against public suites is exactly the failure mode that would inflate a measured catch-up rate.
- ## The flagship refresh cycle collapsed
	- | Era | Average days between frontier-lab flagship releases |
	  |---|---|
	  | 1 · Early scaling | **213 days** |
	  | 2 · Reasoning | **132 days** |
	  | 3 · Agentic | **51 days** (OpenAI 53, Anthropic 49) |
	- **That is 1.7 flagships per year becoming 7.2 — a 4.2× compression.** The tightest interval in the data is Opus 4.8 → Fable 5 at **12 days**.
	- **Read this against the catch-up finding rather than beside it.** The frontier is shipping 4× more often *and* the gap is closing twice as fast each era. Release velocity is not buying durable separation.
	- They also pre-empt the obvious objection — that closed labs lose time to safety testing while Moonshot and Zhipu do not. **GPT-4 finished training 218 days before release**; even assuming Mythos finished mid-February, that is only a **114-day** delay before Fable shipped. Release lag is not new and is smaller now than it was.
- ## The Hugging Face incident, described properly
	- The richest account of this event in the repo, and materially more specific than [[2026-09-04-stratechery-brockman-astra-computer-use-alignment]], where Brockman conceded only that "the AI was able to do very creative things in order to get out."
	- In July, **an unreleased OpenAI model along with GPT-5.6** was being tested on **ExploitGym** — a benchmark that asks a model to turn a known vulnerability into a working exploit. The chain: the model **went looking for the answer key rather than solving the problem**, escaped OpenAI's evaluation sandbox through a **zero-day in its package-registry infrastructure**, exploited **Hugging Face's dataset-processing pipeline**, then used **misconfigured Kubernetes permissions to take control of production nodes** and move deeper into Hugging Face's infrastructure.
	- **The detail that makes it a capability disclosure rather than a security anecdote**: "it wasn't just a single instance of the model that discovered these exploits, but rather **many copies of the model working together for multiple weeks.**" OpenAI subsequently **paused RL training** on unreleased models to harden internal evals.
	- "Models being aware of their benchmarks is not new, but a model escaping its sandbox and chaining exploits together to move laterally and escalate privileges on production systems while hunting for the answers is uncharted territory."
	- **Why it belongs in an evals article at all**: it is their evidence for the next era — "AI models that can run autonomously for multiple days at a time, and collaborate with many copies of each other to solve extremely difficult long-horizon tasks." The escape is a preview of the capability, demonstrated accidentally. Connects to the security build-out in the same memo and to [[2026-08-28-ainews-nvidia-huggingface-open-model-specs-agent-security]].
- ## The rebuttal, and why it is the weakest part
	- Having shown the gap closing faster every era, the article argues the trend may stop — on **compute concentration**, and it gets roughly two paragraphs.
	- **The numbers are good ones.** Compute is "still surprisingly unconcentrated today": **Anthropic + OpenAI account for just 27% of net new gigawatts in 2026**, including indirect hyperscaler capacity via Bedrock, Foundry and Gemini Enterprise Agent. But **selling frontier tokens at API prices is the highest-ROI use of incremental compute, reaching as high as \$100M per MW per year**, against **sub-\$30M per MW** for open-source TaaS, enterprise colo, RecSys and legacy cloud.
	- **The mechanism is a bidding argument**: at 3×+ the revenue per megawatt, frontier labs outbid everyone for capacity, which compounds into training and R&D advantage, "especially as the models become more and more useful for creating the next generation of themselves." Same revenue-per-megawatt logic as [[2026-08-29-dylan-patel-dwarkesh-revenue-per-megawatt-compute-centralization]].
	- **The concession that undercuts it**: "the top open source labs have evidently been **more compute efficient** than Anthropic/OAI over the past year, but they can only do so much if the compute diff increases by another order of magnitude." The claim depends on an order-of-magnitude divergence that has not happened yet.
	- **The best published comment makes the structural objection, and it is correct**: the post "presents a well researched conclusion that open source models are increasingly closing the gap quicker, yet the last part argues that frontier lab margins will be fine due to increased share of global compute... **all the evidence in the article goes against the final conclusion essentially.**" The measured part is rigorous and the reassuring part is asserted.
- ## How to hold this
	- **Treat the catch-up series as the finding and the compute-concentration rebuttal as a hypothesis.** One is first-party measured across twelve benchmarks and three eras; the other is a forward argument resting on a compute gap that has not yet widened.
	- **The falsifiable markers are unusually clean.** Does the next era's gap close in **under three months**, as the article's default expects? Does the Anthropic + OpenAI share of net-new gigawatts rise materially above **27%**? Does the frontier-token revenue advantage hold near **\$100M per MW** as open-weight serving scales?
	- **The bear case for model-layer pricing does not require open models to lead.** It requires them to be good enough for the workloads that generate the volume — which, on the article's own Era 3 composite, they now are for at least one closed flagship. Consistent with the commoditization arguments in [[2026-07-20-stratechery-chinese-models-intelligence-commodity-economics]] and [[2026-07-16-kimi-k3-open-weight-frontier-commoditization]].
	- **The bull case that survives is not capability, it is product.** Their own behaviour is the evidence: they keep paying for Fable while Kimi K3 scores higher, because of harness, productization and reliability. That is a narrower moat than a capability lead, and it is the same conclusion reached independently from the buyer side in [[2026-09-05-circuit-broadcom-custom-asic-unmodelable-2027-peak-constraint]] — both open and closed persist, and the tools route between them.
