- ## Meta's teen-safety agreement with 52 state attorneys general
	- **Source**: Meta newsroom post, August 26, 2026, updated August 27 to note the judge's approval. **This is the company's own account** — it is framed throughout as an "agreement" and a "new industry standard" rather than a settlement, no underlying litigation is described, and every figure carries an "approximately." Quote from **C.J. Mahoney, Chief Legal Officer**.
	- **Thesis**: The product concessions are real but bounded; the interesting engineering is financial and competitive. Meta has structured the deal so that **30% of the money is released only if TikTok and YouTube both adopt the same rules and pay matching amounts**, and so that the harshest engagement limits only bind if peers accept them too. That converts a company-specific regulatory cost into an **industry-wide cost**, and hands the attorneys general a \$5.3bn financial incentive to pursue Meta's two largest attention competitors.
- ## The money, and why the accrual is smaller than the headline
	- | Item | Amount |
	  |----------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
	  | Total payment | **~\$18bn**, in annual installments over **10 years** |
	  | Non-contingent portion to participating states | **70%, ~\$12.7bn** |
	  | **Contingent portion** | **30%, ~\$5.3bn** — released only on two conditions |
	  | **Q3'26 legal expense accrual** | **~\$10bn** |
	- **The two conditions on the contingent 30%**: (1) YouTube and TikTok implement a **one-hour daily limit, Night Mode, and age assurance**; and (2) **YouTube and TikTok each pay an amount matching the 30% figure**, with half of the remaining funds tied to each company's payment.
	- **The \$10bn accrual reconciles as the present value of the certain portion, not as a haircut on the total.** Discounting \$12.7bn paid evenly over ten years gives **\$10.3bn at 4%** and **\$9.8bn at 5%** — bracketing the disclosed figure. The implication is that **the \$5.3bn contingent tranche is not being accrued at all**, consistent with it being neither probable nor reasonably estimable while it depends on two third parties acting. **If TikTok and YouTube ever sign, expect a second charge.**
	- **The guidance point is stated explicitly and matters**: the charge *"was not contemplated in the expense range that was provided in the Q2 earnings call,"* while all other July guidance is unchanged. So this lands **on top of** the FY26 expense guide of \$165–169bn recorded in [[META-2026-Q2]] — roughly **6% of the full-year expense guide, and about 24% of Q2's entire \$42bn quarterly expense base**, in a single quarter.
	- **This confirms a warning already on file.** Q2 carried **\$2.4bn of legal charges**, and Ben Thompson's read in [[2026-08-03-stratechery-meta-timing-problems-financial-tail]] was that the expense growth included legal charges and *"those might not be a one-off!"* A \$10bn follow-on within a month settles that: **legal is behaving as a recurring line, not a discrete event.** Given Q2 free cash flow of \$784M, the timing of the first installments against an already-strained cash conversion is worth watching.
- ## What actually changes in the product
	- **Applies automatically to under-18s on Instagram and Facebook** in participating states and territories. **Most terms run 10 years**; Time Limit and Night Mode start as **5-year** commitments.
	  | Control | Default |
	  |------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
	  | **Time Limit** | **2 hours/day**, **cumulative across Facebook and Instagram**, including detected multiple accounts; only a parent can disable |
	  | **Night Mode** | Blocked **midnight–6am** — no posting or viewing Feed, Stories, Explore, or Reels |
	  | **School Mode** | Notifications muted **8am–3pm**, except DMs and account security alerts |
	  | **Prompts** | Every **15 minutes** of continuous use, plus at **60 and 90 minutes** cumulative |
	  | **Algorithmic feed** | Teens may select a **non-personalized feed as default**; parents can force it |
	  | **Autoplay** | Teens may disable; parents can force it |
	  | **Hidden likes** | Like and reaction counts hidden by default |
	  | Filters | Cosmetic surgery **and now extreme makeup** filters blocked |
	- **The most important line in the product list is the exclusion**: direct messaging is **exempt from Night Mode, Time Limit, and School Mode**. Meta conceded feed time and protected messaging — the retention anchor and the hardest thing for a competitor to replace. **That is the correct trade if you are defending the long-run user relationship rather than near-term engagement**, and it tells you how Meta ranks its own surfaces.
	- **The structurally deepest concession is the non-algorithmic feed option**, since recommendation quality is the product moat and the mechanism behind the impressions-plus-pricing dynamic in [[META-2026-Q2]]. Its impact is limited by being **opt-in rather than default** — but note that **parents can make it mandatory**, which converts it from a teen choice into a household setting.
	- Also included: 13+ content settings by default (movie-ratings-inspired), private accounts by default, restrictions on suspicious adults, improved reporting response times, and expanded parental controls — notification when a teen **links a secondary account**, alerts on interactions with suspicious accounts, and periodic reports on usage and **attempted changes to protective settings**.
- ## The competitive engineering, which is the real story
	- **Meta has priced peer compliance into the settlement.** If TikTok and YouTube sign on, the Time Limit and Night Mode commitments **extend from 5 to 10 years** *and* tighten — daily limit drops to **one hour per app** and Night Mode expands to **10pm–7am**. So Meta accepts the severe version **only if its competitors accept it too**.
	- **Read the incentive structure from the AGs' side**: \$5.3bn of their award is unfunded until TikTok and YouTube comply *and* pay. Meta has effectively **given 52 attorneys general a direct financial stake in prosecuting its two biggest competitors for teen attention.** The public letter urging TikTok and YouTube to join is the visible half of a mechanism whose enforcement half is financial.
	- **The logic Meta offers is genuinely sound on its own terms** — teens "move fluidly between dozens of apps," and restricting one app pushes usage to another, so unilateral limits transfer engagement rather than reduce harm. That argument is also **exactly what a firm facing asymmetric restriction would say**, and both readings are true simultaneously. The correct inference is not cynicism but structure: **Meta is converting a unilateral cost into a mutualized one**, and has bought a 5-year option on the worst provisions in case it fails.
	- **A separate lever aimed at a different target**: Meta says it will continue advocating for legislation requiring **app stores to verify age and obtain parental approval before a teen downloads an app**. That relocates the compliance burden and liability onto **Apple and Google**, and this settlement strengthens the lobbying position — Meta can now argue it has done its part. Adds a front to the regulatory pressure already tracked in [[2026-08-19-stratechery-apple-app-store-fee-settlements-att-germany]].
	- **Scope makes this a de facto national standard**: **52 attorneys general** across states, territories, and DC. There is no meaningful jurisdictional gap left, so this is a US-wide regime in all but name — comparable in direction to the UK measures in [[2026-05-21-ofcom-uk-children-online-services-passive-measurement]].
- ## Ongoing obligations, and what is not disclosed
	- An **independent auditor** tests and reports compliance to the states **annually for five years**. An **independent social media research foundation** will be established, with Meta sharing **consented user data** to support research into teen well-being.
		- **The research foundation is a two-sided commitment.** It is a credible-sounding concession, but it institutionalizes an outside party producing findings on teen well-being using Meta's own data. **Adverse published findings become a recurring headline and litigation risk**, on a five-to-ten-year horizon, and Meta does not control the output.
	- **What the post does not provide, and would be needed to size the impact**: teen share of US users, teen share of US time spent, and teen share of ad revenue. Without those, the engagement hit cannot be quantified — and their absence from a document that quantifies everything else is itself informative.
	- The reasonable prior is that under-18s are a **small share of ad revenue** (advertisers pay for purchasing power) but a **meaningful share of the habit-formation pipeline** and of Reels engagement. So the near-term revenue impact is likely modest relative to a \$10bn charge, while the long-term cost — a cohort trained on time limits, muted notifications, hidden likes, and optional non-algorithmic feeds — is unquantifiable but plausibly larger than the cash.
