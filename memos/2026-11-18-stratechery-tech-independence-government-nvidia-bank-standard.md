- ## The end of tech's independence from the US government (Stratechery, Nov 2026)
	- **Source**: Stratechery, Ben Thompson, "Tech Company Independence, 1968–2026," written in obituary form. It quotes the Financial Times story "Bessent says Nvidia needs 'shake-up' with external CEO."
	- **Thesis**: For 58 years US tech sold to the whole world on its own terms, and treated "we didn't know" as an acceptable defense when products were misused. Thompson argues that for AI this model is over: Treasury now expects Nvidia to police its customers the way banks must, and Huang lost his job for running Nvidia as a normal tech company.
	- Related: [[2026-11-12-nvidia-huang-exit-hbm-vendor-agreements]], [[2026-11-18-nvidia-vanishing-moats-after-huang]].

- ## The independence model, 1968–2026
	- **Origin**: Robert Noyce and his co-founders left Fairchild Semiconductor, frustrated by its conservatism and their lack of upside, and founded **Intel**. Arthur Rock's financing of Intel effectively created Silicon Valley venture capital.
	- **Noyce's insight**: the US government was then the largest single buyer of chips. Noyce decided the fastest way to progress was to make chips **for everyone, including the government, on Intel's terms rather than the government's**, and to make it up in volume.
	- **The industry's assumptions from then on**:
		- The world is the customer, and company executives define the route to progress.
		- Government may buy products but not define them. If it wants the best products, it should not restrict whom they can be sold to, because volume and ecosystems are what make them the best.
		- Misuse by bad actors is an acceptable trade-off: most users are good, and the benefits of scale outweigh the friction of tracking billions of people.

- ## Treasury's new standard: run Nvidia like a bank
	- | Issue | Tech's traditional standard (Huang) | Bank standard (Bessent) |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Who enforces the rules | The government. If Commerce allows a sale, the sale is allowed | **The company**, following the spirit of the law and not just the letter |
	  | Knowledge of end customers | Ignorance is a defense | Know your customer and analyze every dollar. "Ignorance is not a defense" |
	  | Customer attestations | A customer's statement that chips will not go to China is enough | Worthless without "boots on the ground" verifying compliance |
	  | Misuse by bad actors | An acceptable trade-off for the benefits of scale | A compliance failure. If Nvidia "were a bank," the Comptroller of the Currency "would be in there right now, and a lot of the executives would be removed" |
	- **Bessent's demands** (via the FT):
		- He wants to know whether Nvidia had "a culture of compliance" around export restrictions, and whether the C-suite knew Singapore-based shell companies were being used to get chips to China.
		- "Were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?"
		- He wants answers from the board and outside counsel **within 10 days**, and says Nvidia needs a "shake-up" with an **external CEO**.
	- **Megaspeed**: a Singapore company that builds AI data centers in Asia, linked to the alleged Chinese spy who had an affair with Huang. US authorities are investigating whether it smuggled high-end Nvidia chips into China.
		- Bessent blames Howard Lutnick's Commerce Department for delaying Megaspeed's addition to the US blacklist: "Many other departments were pushing for this, and commerce had the pen… We have all the rules we need. We just need them enforced."
		- Lutnick is under pressure from inside the administration and from Congress, and lawmakers are speeding up efforts to stop advanced AI chips from reaching China.
	- **Why Treasury's view matters**: Thompson describes Treasury as an apparatus arguably as powerful as the US military. It controls global money flows, and banks are its enforcers.

- ## Thompson's read on Huang and the precedent
	- **Huang as a businessman**: Thompson sees Huang as "just a businessman who cares about nothing else other than Nvidia." That worked when GPUs were gaming products no one in government cared about. Once AI was deemed "anything but normal," business as usual "wasn't just over: it was a crime."
	- **Washington's hostility**: Huang "aggressively belittled" critics of selling chips abroad. Thompson calls the "giddiness and bloodlust" in Washington over his fall extraordinary. Many in Washington and in finance now call him a traitor, a label Thompson rejects.
	- **The Anthropic precedent**: Anthropic's dispute with the US government earlier in 2026 raised the same question, in Thompson's earlier article "Anthropic and Alignment." Should an unelected CEO, Dario Amodei, decide how AI is used, including by the Department of War, or should elected officials and the laws Congress passes decide?
	- **Thompson's conclusion**: if AI is as exceptional as claimed, the government will demand a say in how it is used whether executives agree or not, and that is "a good thing, or at least a least-bad thing." Huang may have disagreed with the chip ban, but it was his duty to accept it as the law of the land.
