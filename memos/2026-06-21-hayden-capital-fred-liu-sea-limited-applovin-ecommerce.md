- ## Investment Philosophy

	- ### Portfolio Construction
		- Concentrated portfolio of **5–15 names** — all in tech, invested globally.
		- Target companies that can **3x–5x earnings over a decade or more**; buy at fair-to-cheap valuation and compound alongside growth.
		- Portfolio follows a **power law**: top 20% of ideas generates all of the alpha; the other 80% roughly nets out. Losers are small from a NAV perspective because early-stage positions start small.
		- Position sizing framework:
			- | Stage | Position Size | Rationale |
			  |---|---|---|
			  | Tracker / early conviction | <2–5% | Wide range of outcomes; thesis not yet proven |
			  | Core | 5–10% | KPIs proving thesis direction; narrowing range of outcomes |
			  | Mature / grown-in | Mid-teens to ~20% | Allowed to compound; range of outcomes has narrowed substantially |
		- As KPIs confirm thesis, add: even if stock is up 10%, expected value may have risen 25% because downside case is gone.

	- ### What to Look For in Founders
		- Owner-operators willing to make contrarian decisions that professional boards would avoid — launch new businesses even if it temporarily depresses cash flow for years.
		- Rational capital allocators: not empire builders. Willingness to shut down businesses that aren't working is a positive signal, not a negative.
		- Especially valuable in conservative cultures (parts of Asia) where disrupting the status quo is against the grain — if you find someone doing it, higher probability of genuine differentiation.
		- Historically: best entrepreneurs found in US and Asia; expanding to Japan and Latin America.

	- ### Core Investment Pattern: Core Subsidizing Nascent
		- The most recurring pattern in the portfolio: a profitable core business is reinvesting cash into a new business — making the stock screen at a high multiple on apparent earnings.
		- If you understand the unit economics of the core and can underwrite the probability the new business works, the downside is protected by the core; the upside is a multiple of the whole company's value.
		- **Sea Limited 2018 example**: gaming EBITDA of \$400M; paid \~4x GMV (\$4B market cap); street hated reinvestment into Shopee. If e-commerce failed, gaming alone was still growing and the price was defensible.
		- **Afterpay 2019 example**: Australian business had ~30% margins, growing mid-20s%. US market is 10x the size. Channel checks showed US and Australian merchant commentary on BNPL conversion was identical — confirmed US demand existed. Stock priced at ~20x Australian earnings alone with US optionality essentially free.

- ## Sea Limited ([[$SE]]) / Shopee

	- ### Original Thesis (2018)
		- Hayden first invested in 2018. Position grew from low single digits to ~20% (largest position) over several years.
		- At time of investment: Garena gaming doing ~\$400M EBITDA; Sea was trading at ~\$4B market cap (~10x EBITDA). Street hated reinvestment into Shopee (number two in SEA e-commerce behind Lazada/Alibaba).
		- Key question at the time: "Does a gaming company have a right to win in e-commerce?" Liu's answer: even if e-commerce failed, core gaming value was protected; team had already proven rational capital allocation (later evidenced by quick exit from India and Eastern Europe).

	- ### Why Shopee Won — Structural Advantages
		- **Mobile-first architecture**: Lazada and Tokopedia were desktop-era companies (Tokopedia founded 2009; Lazada's first product was a laptop). Their UX was optimized for desktop, not lightweight mobile. Shopee was built mobile-first from day one.
		- **Long-tail / unbranded goods focus**: Electronics (Lazada/Tokopedia) are commoditized and price-transparent. Fashion, beauty, and home goods are difficult to price-compare, unique to the platform, and create repeat purchasing behavior.
			- Unbranded/fashion → lower AOV → more frequent orders → more time spent → higher platform stickiness
		- **Female shopper skew**: Shopee deliberately targeted female shoppers over male. Female shoppers leave longer, more detailed product reviews (anecdote: at a Shopee Seller Day, they literally showed a slide on why female shoppers are valuable — they write two-paragraph reviews vs. "A+ great product"). More reviews → better trust → stronger flywheel on an early platform.
		- **Shopping as entertainment**: Users in emerging markets spending 30–60 minutes per day on the Shopee app. On cheap Android phones with limited storage, you typically have only 2–3 apps — if Shopee is one of them, it becomes the default entry point for both browsing and buying.
		- **Operational speed**: A Lazada employee (met in a Kuala Lumpur mall cafe) told Liu: "Whenever Lazada launches a new feature, Shopee will copy it within two weeks and do it three times better." Lazada's cultural dysfunction post-Alibaba acquisition (Mandarin emails being Google Translated by local employees) compounded the execution gap.

	- ### KPIs That Built Conviction
		- **Supplier density**: Hayden web-scraped product and merchant counts per category across Shopee, Lazada, and Tokopedia. When Shopee exceeded the incumbents in category coverage, conviction increased.
		- **Order frequency**: The key metric. Started at ~2x/month (vs. Lazada at ~1x per couple months). Grew to ~4x/month by late 2019 — pre-COVID. When order frequency approached China-level benchmarks, the platform's addictiveness was confirmed.

	- ### Current Valuation Framework (circa podcast date, ~\$50B market cap)
		- Sum-of-parts liquidation floor:
			- | Component | Estimated Value |
			  |---|---|
			  | Cash and investments | ~\$12B |
			  | SeaMoney (lending business) | ~\$8B |
			  | Garena gaming (1.8B EBITDA × 5x, Krafton/PUBG comp) | ~\$9B |
			  | **Total ex-Shopee** | **~\$29B** |
			  | Implied Shopee value | **~\$18–20B** |
		- Shopee GMV: \$127B in 2024, growing 25%+; expected to exceed \$160B in 2025.
		- Implied Shopee valuation at current price: ~1/8 of GMV.
		- Terminal margin debate: management guides 2–3% EBITDA/GMV medium-term (currently ~50bps); China evidence (most competitive e-commerce market globally, 4 major players) shows every player achieves 2%+ EBITDA/GMV even in a bloodbath.
		- Volatility precedent: During TikTok/Tokopedia investment cycle (2023 reinvestment), stock fell ~60%; once Sea signaled profit-maximization, stock rose ~5x. Demonstrates multiple sensitivity to margin guidance.
		- Secular tailwind: E-commerce penetration has never declined as countries become wealthier — history is on their side.

	- ### Brazil vs. Mercado Libre ([[$MELI]])
		- Shopee entered Brazil 2019–2020 after proving model in Southeast Asia. Brazil had ~\$10–12K GDP per capita, better credit infrastructure, and Mercado had already educated consumers on online shopping.
		- **Why Mercado is structurally upper-income**: Founded post-dot-com bubble (2002–2012) with no competition; had to be profitable quickly → served top 5–10% of households (high AOV, branded goods, quality logistics).
		- **Why Shopee targeted the other 95%**: Serving lower-income households at low AOV requires order density to justify logistics routes. Shopee spent ~\$4B to jumpstart the chicken-and-egg problem (only Sea and Amazon had the capital; Amazon never focused on it).
		- **Shopee's logistics progress in Brazil**:
			- Now the **2nd largest owner of warehouse space in Brazil**, right behind Mercado.
			- Recently signed the **largest single warehouse lease in Brazilian history** (building not yet complete).
			- Ships **more packages per day** than Mercado.
			- **70% of packages fulfilled by Shopee Express** (own logistics network).
		- Liu's conclusion: Mercado's logistics moat was not as durable as the market believed — it can be replicated with capital in 5 years. "Good logistics is now table stakes."
		- Coexistence thesis: Mercado and Shopee will both succeed by serving different income segments. Mercado = top 10–20% households; Shopee = bottom 80%. Business models and consumer priorities (quality/speed vs. price/entertainment) are different enough to support co-existence.

- ## Pinduoduo ([[$PDD]])

	- **Thesis**: Invested 2022 when China tech was globally hated and Western funds were forced to divest China holdings. Bought at ~\$35–40B market cap with business growing ~65% YoY; sell-side consensus had flat \$7B profit estimates. Free cash flow was expected to explode; PDD now has **>$50B in cash on the balance sheet** — exceeding the original entry market cap. Exited when thesis played out and better opportunities emerged elsewhere.

	- ### Fast-Follower Model
		- PDD has **never been a pioneer** — it is always the fast follower and copier.
		- China produces dozens of VC-backed startups burning cash to find working business models. PDD lets them bear all the R&D and testing cost, learns from their mistakes, then enters with capital deployed more efficiently.
		- At 1% market penetration, catching up with capital is easy. At 50%, nearly impossible. PDD entered when category penetration was still low.
		- PDD is a **"Gen 2"** Chinese internet company: born in mobile (vs. Gen 1 Alibaba/Baidu/JD/Tencent born in desktop, ~10 years earlier).
		- Tencent invested in PDD and fed WeChat traffic to the app. Alibaba's links were **blocked and unclickable on WeChat** — structural advantage that could not be replicated.

	- ### Rural China Wedge (C-to-M Model)
		- Attacked the **~600 million people in lower-tier cities and rural China** — effectively a different country with GDP per capita of ~\$5K vs. Shanghai's ~\$20K (4x gap).
		- This segment was ignored by Alibaba and JD.com, which were urban/tier-1 focused.
		- Rural users: first mobile internet experience, limited entertainment → shopping as entertainment + infinite scroll.
		- Did not care about 2-day shipping; willing to wait 1–2 weeks → enabled PDD to use categories that worked with slower logistics.
		- **C-to-M (Consumer-to-Manufacturer) model**:
			- Aggregate demand from consumers → present bulk order to factory → factory makes to order, eliminating inventory risk.
			- Factories at 50% capacity accept prices above marginal cost but below average total cost → structural price advantage.
			- Sushi conveyor belt UI: items available for limited time only → urgency, "if it's gone, it's gone" → encourages immediate ordering.
			- Original group buying mechanic: required e.g., 100 people to co-purchase before factory produces.
		- WeChat virality: hundreds of millions of Chinese experiencing mobile internet for the first time; PDD deal-sharing was a natural viral loop that cannot be replicated today.
		- Now attacking Alibaba/JD directly — only in the last couple of years, after building a large enough base in the rural segment.

- ## Applovin ([[$APP]])

	- ### Original Thesis (Spring 2022)
		- Studied the full mobile gaming ecosystem in 2018 — every part of the stack from studios to AdTech networks to gaming engines to outsourced tech. Conclusion: best position is either the **AdTech network** (rake on all money flows) or the **gaming engine** (Unity). Applovin IPO'd a couple years later as the AdTech network opportunity.
		- In Spring 2022, market still viewed Applovin as a mobile gaming studio (~70% of revenues). But the **AdTech network segment was 30% of revenues and growing 100%+ YoY** — and was the actual business.
		- Applovin was trading at ~10x FCF at time of initial investment; doubled down at lower prices when the market continued to misunderstand the business.

	- ### Why the AdTech Network Wins

		- #### The Casino Analogy
			- Mobile gaming is a circular economy: studios make revenue → reinvest all of it into user acquisition ads → try to make more revenue. Like a slot machine: win some, plow it all back in.
			- Applovin sits in the **middle of this loop** and takes **25–30% rake on every spin** — vs. a casino's 1–5% edge. It extracts most of the value from the ecosystem without bearing the volatility of individual game success or failure.
			- Total mobile app economy: ~\$120B. Mobile gaming: >$60B (majority of app economy). Applovin currently serving ~\$15B on the demand side.

		- #### The ATT Wedge
			- Apple's App Tracking Transparency (2021): ~51% of iPhone users opted out of data sharing with third-party apps → competitors lost signal on a massive portion of the addressable audience.
			- Applovin had the **anticipatory hedge**: in 2018, spent ~\$1B acquiring mobile gaming studios to own **200 million first-party users** whose behavioral data it fully controls.
			- These 200M users feed the ad targeting model; extrapolated to 1B+ total players worldwide, the model can infer preferences with much higher accuracy than ad networks that lost ATT access.

		- #### Full-Stack Supply + Demand Visibility
			- **Demand side** (~30% market share): Applovin's ad network buys ad slots on behalf of advertisers — behind only Facebook and Google in mobile.
			- **Supply side** (~70% market share): MoPub acquisition (~\$1B) — one of the largest mediation platforms. MoPub represents publishers (game developers) selling their ad inventory.
			- Combined: Applovin has full-stack visibility into both what advertisers will pay AND what supply costs — pricing power no single-sided competitor can match.

		- #### Algorithm as Moat
			- Black-box algorithm — even CEO Adam Foroughi cannot fully explain what it predicts. Analogous to Renaissance Technologies or Citadel Securities: investors don't need to understand the model, just the outputs.
			- More data → better model → higher conversion → more advertiser ROI → higher advertiser spend → even more data. Self-reinforcing.
			- All ads are **performance-based** (no brand advertising): advertisers get real-time ROI feedback within seconds → they self-select, keep spending if it works, stop if it doesn't. Organic signal quality.

	- ### E-Commerce Expansion Thesis
		- Current conversion rate: **~1.3%** (i.e., 98.7% of served ads are unmonetized). Management targets **5%** through model improvement alone — a ~**4x increase in conversion**.
		- **Diversity of ads drives conversion**: If a user is mid-game in Candy Crush (happy, not looking for a new game), a gaming ad is wasted. An e-commerce ad for a product they'd actually want converts. More non-gaming ads = higher overall conversion.
		- E-commerce portion is now starting to accelerate; cost-per-ad is rising on the platform, which is pricing out lower-tier mobile gaming advertisers — evidence that e-commerce advertisers are willing to pay higher CPMs and are finding ROI.
		- Gaming base is well-entrenched (~\$300–400/share downside floor in Liu's view). E-commerce expansion is the call option with a wide range of outcomes.

	- ### Risks
		- **Apple**: Could attack the ecosystem by cracking down on fingerprinting / opening its own ad network. But incentive is weak: ~60% of App Store revenues come from gaming apps. Destroying the ad ecosystem = destroying the App Store's revenue base. Apple knows this.
		- **Facebook**: Meta exited the mobile ad network business; there have been noises about potentially re-entering. Could cause disruption for a few quarters if it re-entered at scale.
		- **Valuation**: At ~\$160B market cap / ~\$3.3B earnings, you're paying for e-commerce working. Gaming alone justifies a much lower price. The e-commerce thesis is still a call option, not a sure thing.

- ## Global E-Commerce Framework

	- ### Why Amazon Fails Globally
		- Amazon has been in Brazil for ~14 years, tried China, Singapore, and others — **failed in every market outside the US** (minor presence in Germany and UK).
		- Root cause: Amazon's model (logistics, trust, brand quality, 2-day shipping) is optimized for **high-GDP, high-income markets**.
		- In markets where GDP per capita is \$5K: price > delivery speed. Low AOV products (\$2 items) cannot justify Amazon-level shipping investment. Consumer priorities are entirely different.

	- ### Mobile vs. Desktop and E-Commerce Architecture
		- Countries that entered e-commerce first via desktop built different platforms than those that entered via mobile.
		- **Mobile-first markets**: users have cheap Android phones with limited storage → only 2–3 apps installed → super apps and multi-vertical apps dominate. Switching between apps is hard → one app gets most of the mindshare.
		- **Desktop markets**: easy tab-switching → specialized single-purpose apps win.
		- This structural difference makes US e-commerce learnings hard to export directly to emerging markets.

	- ### Secular Tailwind
		- There is no historical case where a country has gotten richer and e-commerce penetration has gone down. Countries going from 5% to 15%+ to 30%+ e-commerce penetration is the baseline scenario — the question is who captures the growth.

	- ### Fast-Follower Strategy (PDD Framework)
		- When market penetration is still low (~1–5%), incumbents have not yet captured the broad market → catching up with capital is possible.
		- Attack underserved segments first (rural, lower-income) rather than going head-to-head with entrenched incumbents on their home turf.
		- Build a base; then move up-market.
		- VC bias: investors living in tier-1 cities miss entire segments of markets that are served by fast-followers operating in demographics VCs don't interact with (Pinduoduo lesson).
