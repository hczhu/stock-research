- ## Memory Feed, Aug 21 – Sep 4 2026 — CXMT Breaks 10%, and What LTAs Actually Cost the Sellers
	- **Source**: TickerTick `$DRAM` AI feed, ~2 weeks to Sep 4 2026 — Counterpoint via Seoul Economic Daily / TechPowerUp, DIGITIMES, TrendForce, Omdia via ChosunBiz, Gartner, Bristlemoon Capital (SanDisk Investor Day analysis), MK News, Tech Times, Nikkei/Reuters
	- **Thesis**: Two things in this feed change the tracked view rather than confirm it. First, the majors' HBM pivot vacated the commodity tier and handed CXMT 10% of DRAM revenue **two years ahead of consensus** — the share loss is a direct cost of the margin win, not an unrelated risk. Second, the LTA regime that is supposed to de-cyclicalize memory can now be priced: contract floors sit roughly **a quarter below realized ASP**, and spot runs **4–5×** contract, so headline memory prices materially overstate what the majors actually collect.
- ## CXMT crosses 10% — the majors' own pivot created the opening
	- **Source**: Counterpoint Research Q2 2026 trackers, reported Sep 2–3
	- CXMT is the first Chinese maker to reach double-digit global **DRAM revenue** share. Counterpoint and UBS had both projected 10% would not arrive until **2028** — it is early by two years.
	- | Metric | Value |
	  |---|---|
	  | CXMT DRAM revenue share, Q2 2025 | 4% |
	  | CXMT DRAM revenue share, Q1 2026 | 8% |
	  | **CXMT DRAM revenue share, Q2 2026** | **10%** |
	  | Big 3 combined share | 94% → **87%** (first sub-90% in over a decade) |
	  | Total DRAM revenue, Q2 2026 | **+385% YoY** |
	  | CXMT stated next target | 15% |
	  | CXMT cost-per-bit disadvantage | ~30% |
	- **The mechanism is the finding.** Total DRAM revenue nearly quintupled in the same quarter the incumbents lost seven points of share. CXMT did not out-execute anyone — it absorbed the commodity demand Samsung, SK hynix and Micron walked away from to chase HBM and server DRAM. Record absolute profit and a structurally weaker position are simultaneously true.
	- Q2 2026 ranking per Counterpoint: Samsung 39%, SK hynix 26%, Micron 25% — Samsung retaking the lead as SK hynix fell from roughly 39% a year earlier. One widely circulated secondary account states SK hynix fell "39% → 25%"; treat the exact deltas as unconfirmed, the direction as clear. SK hynix still leads HBM at 50%; **CXMT does not appear in the HBM revenue ranking at all.**
	- Commodity competitiveness is no longer notional: CXMT DDR5 runs stably at **8,000 MT/s** and benchmarks within **0.86–1%** of SK hynix's latest M-die on productivity and gaming workloads. HBM3E is in early trial production with qualification underway at Alibaba and Cambricon.
	- The 30% cost gap is irrelevant at current prices and becomes the dominant margin factor the moment they normalize — which is the same statement as saying CXMT's share is most durable exactly while the shortage lasts.
	- **Legal overhang**: Korean prosecutors allege CXMT ran a written internal "Hefei Project" from August 2016 — spearheaded by Samsung's former DRAM development lead — to acquire Samsung's 18nm DRAM process including a **620-step recipe**, with phased engineer-poaching and tape-out goals. Samsung may escalate to the US ITC, which it has done before.
	- This is a different flank than [[2026-06-05-cxmt-korea-china-hbm-gap-narrows-three-years]] tracked. That memo watched HBM, where CXMT remains three generations behind and yield-constrained. The share broke in commodity DRAM instead. See also [[2026-06-03-china-memory-cxmt-ymtc-geopolitics]].
- ## What the LTA regime actually costs the sellers
	- **Source**: Bristlemoon Capital note on SanDisk's 2026 Investor Day (Aug 28); DIGITIMES on Samsung LTA coverage; Seoul Economic Daily on HBM export pricing
	- [[2026-06-03-memory-two-tier-market-and-ltas]] modeled the two-tier structure qualitatively. It can now be priced.
	- | Datapoint | Value |
	  |---|---|
	  | Samsung memory capacity under LTA | **~70%, running through 2031** |
	  | 36GB HBM3E spot vs LTA price | **4–5×** (spot ~\$2,100) |
	  | HBM average export price | **\$76.13**, +9.5% MoM — first print above \$70 |
	  | SanDisk NBM contracts | 8 customers, **\$94B** minimum revenue over 4 years at floor prices |
	  | Share of bits under NBM | ~50% of FY27, ~two-thirds of FY28 |
	- **Bristlemoon's derivation, which generalizes past SanDisk.** Working back from ~\$14B of FY27 contracted revenue against a \$38–39B run rate, the NBM floor price sits **26–28% below realized 4Q26 ASP**, implying a gross-margin floor of **78–79%** against 84.6% actual. SanDisk's CFO independently confirmed the NBM floor gross margin is "around 80%."
	- **The inconsistency they caught in management's own model.** The FY28–30 outlook attributes mid-high-teens revenue growth entirely to bit growth, i.e. flat ASP — but the ~80% average gross margin in the same model is only reachable if ASP falls **over 30%** from here. Both cannot hold. Their read is that the long-term model is investor marketing and the real outcome is materially better or materially worse.
	- Downside scenarios, for calibration: NBM floors hold and non-NBM ASP falls 50% → 78% GM, EPS \$218. NBMs breached and both fall 50% → 72% GM, EPS \$154. At 8× (one turn above the current 7× forward P/E) that is a \$1,200–1,700 "trough" price against \$1,485 today — **the bear case is close to already priced.**
	- Price sensitivity is violently asymmetric to small imbalances: a mid-single-digit % bit deficit drove NAND ASP up **~4× in a year**, and a MSD% surplus could in principle unwind it just as fast. Industry supply/demand forecasting at this sensitivity has error bars wider than the answer.
	- **The takeaway for the majors, not just SanDisk**: where spot is 4–5× contract and 70% of Samsung's capacity is contracted to 2031, reported spot and export prices overstate blended realized ASP by a wide margin. The floor is genuinely real — this cycle's downside is protected in a way prior cycles' were not — but the floor is a quarter below the prints being quoted.
	- Incremental NAND demand that is not cyclical: SanDisk models a **1 ZB installed base of KV-cache offload storage by 2030** against current industry capacity of ~1.2 ZB — a high-teens % of supply over four years. Bristlemoon calls that aggressive but agrees KV-cache offload plus persistent agentic memory is worth mid-high-teens annual bit demand. Consistent with [[2026-07-16-weka-kv-cache-nand-inference-memory-podcast]].
- ## The two most bearish signals came from the producers themselves
	- **Samsung is not adding wafers.** Omdia DRAM wafer capacity, via ChosunBiz — capex is going to node migration (1c nm for HBM4) and yield, not new wafers:
	- | Annual DRAM wafers | 2025 | 2026E | 2027E |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | Samsung | 7.695M | 8.175M (+6.2%) | 8.28M (**+1.3%**) |
	  | SK hynix | 6.06M | 6.66M (+9.9%) | 7.29M (+9.5%) |
	  | Samsung's capacity lead | 27.0% | — | **13.6%** |
	- **Samsung is returning capital instead.** The 2026 shareholder return is **₩90–110T (~\$65–80B)**, roughly **5× the prior record** of ₩20.3T set in 2020 and the largest ever by a Korean company. The stock had its worst day in three weeks on the announcement and dragged the whole complex down with it. A record return alongside +1.3% wafer growth is management saying it does not see attractive reinvestment at these prices.
	- **Inventory is building inside a shortage.** Samsung and SK hynix both ended H1 2026 holding sharply more inventory than six months earlier, with valuation reserves falling — an odd combination in a market that supposedly cannot make enough.
	- Where the profit actually sits: H1 corporate tax was Samsung ₩3.99T vs SK hynix **₩7.22T** — the smaller company paid ~1.8×. H1 operating profit was ~₩146.7T and ~₩98.2T respectively, 6–13× YoY.
- ## Nvidia is passing memory costs through, which extends pricing power into 2027
	- Nvidia reportedly raising **Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell server prices by >15%** in early 2027 (Bloomberg). The customer with the most leverage is choosing to re-price rather than push back — analysts see **HBM contract prices +50%+** in 2027, with UBS's bull case at **+79% ASP**. Reinforces [[2026-08-30-tsmc-across-the-board-price-increases-samsung-trigger]].
	- Gartner's revision frames the scale: 2026 semiconductor revenue **\$1.56T** against \$809B in 2025, with **memory at \$837.3B — ~54% of the total, passing non-memory for the first time**. DRAM +246.6%, NAND +371.9%. Gartner's April forecast was \$633.3B and 48%; a ~\$200B upward revision in four months.
- ## Capacity: who is actually adding it
	- **Micron** is roughly doubling HBM to **~100k wafers/month by end-2026** (adding up to 60k), skewed to 12-Hi HBM4 for Vera Rubin. Samsung and SK hynix sit at **150–200k wpm each** — still 3–4× Micron. Fiscal Q4 results Sep 30.
	- **YMTC** told IPO investors it targets becoming the **world's largest NAND supplier by end-2027**, overtaking Samsung and SK hynix; it already passed Kioxia to #3 by shipment volume in Q2. Parent CCSH filed a **¥33.3B (~\$4.9B)** STAR Market listing, now in first-round Shanghai Stock Exchange inquiry — the largest IPO in China's pipeline, from a company on the US entity list.
	- Bristlemoon's arithmetic is the right frame for YMTC: ~200kwpm today (~15% industry share), three new 100kwpm fabs planned with the first 50kwpm live in 2027. 300kwpm is a 20% increase in industry wafer capacity, but spread over five years it is only **~4%/year**. Enough to matter at the margin, not obviously enough to tip the market alone.
	- **Kioxia + SanDisk**: **¥5T (~\$31B)** through 2032, including a ¥1.8T (~\$11.3B) Kitakami K3 fab operational FY2029 — about 60% of their entire 25-year Japanese spend (\$50B), and contingent on Japanese government subsidies.
	- **SK hynix Indiana** broke ground Aug 27 (\$4B advanced packaging); HBM4E volume production targeted **Q3 2029**. CEO Kwak Noh-Jung expects the shortage to **persist through 2030**.
	- Both Korean makers are stepping up China NAND equipment spend through 2027 despite escalating US tool risk — SK hynix over ₩1T across Wuxi DRAM and Dalian NAND, adding ~30k wpm.
	- **Micron Taiwan supply risk**: ~10,000 union members across two unions, **~80% preliminary support for a strike**, vote possible in September, driven by the gap versus Samsung and SK hynix profit-sharing. Micron responded with record bonuses. A strike would tighten a market already short.
- ## Korea's non-market costs are becoming quantifiable
	- **KEPCO is asking Samsung and SK hynix to prepay ₩25T (~\$18.3B)** — five years of electricity bills, up front, to fund transmission build-out for the Yongin and Honam clusters and reduce the utility's bond issuance. No global precedent. Korean clusters need ~20 GW of new capacity. Structurally this is a large cash call engineered to stay off the utility's balance sheet; same family of arrangement as [[2026-08-24-wsj-usvyatsky-big-tech-off-balance-sheet-commitments]].
	- **US tariff pressure has a specific and severe shape**: Lutnick's proposal would tariff not only chips but **products containing them** — laptops, consoles, data center servers. Samsung and SK hynix would need **6.5× more US investment** to match TSMC, and their existing outlays skew to back-end packaging and non-memory. Neither produces DRAM on US soil. Against TSMC's \$265B and Micron's \$250B of the \$1.2T in commitments, the Korean makers are the exposed parties; a September announcement is expected.
	- **Yellow Envelope Act**: Labor Ministry guidance issued Sep 3 shields Honam construction from union bargaining but concedes that **worker transfers can still trigger legally valid strikes** — leaving the ramp phase of a ₩800T (~\$588B) project legally exposed, on top of power and water bottlenecks.
- ## Memory inflation has reached the end market — and Samsung is the only OEM hedged
	- Counterpoint sees global smartphone shipments falling **14.3% in 2026** on the memory shortage. Samsung is forecast **+0.8%** and to retake the #1 spot from Apple.
	- The reason is a deliberate counter-cyclical bet: Samsung plans **33.7M Galaxy A units from August through November — ~58% of ~58M total planned devices** — expanding the mid/low tier precisely when rivals cut it first, because low ASPs cannot absorb component inflation. Samsung can absorb it because it captures the memory margin on the other side of the trade. No other OEM has that hedge.
	- Price pass-through is now visible: Samsung raised Indian prices across the A17/A27/F17/A07 5G (A17 6/128GB from ₹24,499 to ₹27,999, +14%), following OPPO, Xiaomi and Nothing. Relevant to [[2026-06-24-apple-price-skimming-iphone-dram-passthrough]].
- ## Architecture roadmaps: the fight is moving past HBM capacity
	- **Samsung "CUBE"** 3D strategy — **HBM5 targets 2× HBM4E**; **zHBM targets 8×** with products **2029 or later**, thermals the stated blocker (stacking HBM directly atop the accelerator). Samsung has also filed a **zHBF** trademark, extending the fight into NAND-based AI memory.
	- **SK hynix custom HBM** places compute in the base die, claiming **up to 5.15× LLM inference improvement**. It is also evaluating **Intel Foundry as a second source for HBM4E base dies** — currently 100% TSMC. Negative read-through for TSMC's logic-base-die franchise, optionality for Intel.
	- **Samsung LPDDR5X-PIM**: mass production late 2026/early 2027, ~3× AI inference token throughput with 16 MAC blocks and 614 GB/s internal bandwidth, in the **same 561-ball package** — no PCB redesign, which is what makes it adoptable.
	- **SanDisk HBF** claims HBM-class bandwidth at **8–16× the capacity** at similar cost, targeting the inference tier between HBM and NVMe. Qualcomm's **HBC** (LPDDR stacked on the XPU) has both Samsung and SK hynix as named partners, first products in 2027.
	- Nvidia is pulling the HBM4 mix toward **8-high** rather than 12-high for thermal and supply-chain reasons, and Samsung is developing an 8-layer HBM4E for the custom **NVHBM** platform — where its in-house base-die, fab and packaging turnkey is a genuine structural advantage.
	- **Samsung is reportedly supplying the HBM4 for OpenAI's Jalapeño.** [[2026-08-25-semianalysis-jalapeno-specs-tco-vs-rubin]] established that Jalapeño's entire advantage over Rubin is HBM bandwidth per watt (22 vs 11.1 GB/s/W), not compute. Samsung sits on both sides of the OpenAI-versus-Nvidia trade.
- ## Smaller items with real read-through
	- **Substrates are the next bottleneck after memory.** H1 utilization hit 89% at Samsung Electro-Mechanics and 94% at LG Innotek; SEMCO's package solutions revenue rose 41% to ₩1.4966T with operating profit +237%, on ASPs up 18.7%.
	- **Netlist** is up 500%+ YTD on a Samsung patent settlement — Q2 revenue \$109.8M (+163% YoY), gross profit up 15× to \$22.9M, first profit in years. It has an ITC action against Samsung plus Broadcom, Google, Nvidia and Supermicro, and a new one against Micron, Supermicro, HPE and Lenovo covering DDR5-RDIMM and MRDIMM. An IP toll on the cycle that lands on the buyers as well as the makers.
	- **Solidigm**: a US asset manager pre-registered a Solidigm ETF with the SEC ahead of SK hynix's **Sep 4 disclosure deadline** on Nasdaq listing plans; SK hynix denies deciding on a reported ₩5T pre-IPO raise. Separately, China's SAMR conditions from the 2021 Intel NAND approval — reasonable pricing, capacity expansion, supporting a third entrant — **expire at end-2026**, unshackling the China eSSD business.
	- **SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won publicly floated joint production with Kioxia** in Japan, naming its NAND rival as a possible partner. A consolidation vector in a market where YMTC is targeting the top slot.
