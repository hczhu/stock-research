- ## Data-Center Builders Race to Sell Majority Stakes
	- **Source**: [The Wall Street Journal — “Data-Center Builders Are Racing to Offload Stakes Worth Billions”](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/data-center-builders-are-racing-to-offload-stakes-worth-billions-1a7d92f8), Anissa Gardizy, published 13 July 2026; user-supplied article text.
	- **Source-quality note**: The transaction processes are based primarily on unnamed people familiar with sale efforts. Reported valuations are prospective, processes are at different stages, and some may not produce a transaction. Completed-deal values and industry statistics provide firmer context than banker expectations for current sales.
	- **Thesis**: A wave of majority-stake sales is testing whether private capital can keep funding the AI data-center buildout at record valuations. Existing owners are monetizing platforms and recruiting deeper-pocketed partners because the next growth phase requires far more capital than the last. Strong outcomes would validate the private-market NAVs of public data-center REITs and infrastructure managers; weak bids, contingent pricing, or failed processes would show that development costs and community risk are rising faster than buyer appetite.
- ## Executive Takeaways
	- **Multiple developers are seeking majority-stake sales simultaneously**: Netrality, DataBank, Edged, EdgeCore Digital Infrastructure, and others are working with bankers on transactions potentially worth tens of billions of dollars.
	- **DataBank is the largest cited live process**: a majority stake could value the transaction at as much as $25B, although the article does not specify whether that refers to equity value, enterprise value, or the stake's purchase price.
	- **Capital intensity is forcing ownership turnover**: Jensen Huang estimates that one gigawatt of computing capacity using NVIDIA architecture could soon cost $80–100B. A developer can therefore outgrow the funding capacity of its original sponsor even while demand remains strong.
	- **Powered capacity—not buildings alone—drives value**: guaranteed electrical access is a major valuation factor because power, interconnection, and permitting increasingly determine whether a campus can deliver revenue.
	- **2025 established a record transaction backdrop**: the WSJ cites approximately $50B of data-center M&A, more than twice the prior year, based on S&P Global Market Intelligence.
	- **The buyer universe is concentrated**: many investors want exposure, but only a handful can write multibillion-dollar equity checks. A cluster of simultaneous sales can therefore create price competition among sellers rather than buyers.
	- **Deal structures are becoming more contingent**: rising costs and delays may lead buyers to tie consideration to construction, energization, leasing, or other completion milestones.
	- **Community acceptance is part of asset value**: local opposition over utility bills, noise, and AI can delay or cancel projects. A constructive relationship with communities increasingly functions like a permit or power right.
	- **Stake sales are not automatically a bearish exit signal**: sponsors commonly realize gains, return capital to fund investors, diversify risk, and bring in larger partners while retaining exposure. The signal depends on valuation, retained ownership, proceeds use, and contingent terms.

- ## Why Owners Are Selling Into Strong Demand
	- **Fund-life and realization pressure**
		- Private-equity and infrastructure funds eventually need liquidity to return capital and demonstrate realized performance.
		- A majority sale can crystallize gains while allowing the original sponsor to retain a minority interest or management role.
	- **Growth capital requirements**
		- Data-center businesses consume capital before producing rent: land, interconnection deposits, generation, substations, shells, cooling, and servers must be funded ahead of revenue.
		- At $80–100B per gigawatt for a complete NVIDIA-based compute deployment, ownership capacity matters as much as development expertise.
	- **Risk transfer**
		- Selling a majority stake transfers part of construction, leasing, financing, power, and residual-value risk to the new owner.
		- The original sponsor can redeploy proceeds into new campuses or return cash to investors rather than concentrating more capital in one platform.
	- **Valuation window**
		- Scarce powered-land portfolios and long-term hyperscaler demand can command premium values while AI infrastructure remains a favored allocation.
		- Owners have an incentive to test the market before supply, interest rates, local restrictions, or AI-demand expectations change.
	- **Interpretation**
		- A sale at a premium with meaningful retained ownership is consistent with bullish capital recycling.
		- A full exit, weak valuation, seller financing, large earnout, or failed auction would be more cautionary.
- ## Power Is the Real-Estate Scarcity Premium
	- A conventional building can be constructed where land and zoning permit. A large AI campus additionally requires:
		- Firm access to hundreds of megawatts or gigawatts.
		- Generation or utility supply and a credible energization date.
		- Transmission, substations, transformers, and switchgear.
		- Water or alternative cooling capacity.
		- Fiber routes and proximity to network backbones.
		- Permits and community acceptance.
	- Buyers therefore value **deliverable powered megawatts**, not announced megawatts or land banks.
	- A portfolio with contracted power and completed interconnection work can earn a scarcity premium because it shortens the time from capital deployment to tenant rent.
	- Power risk also explains milestone pricing: a buyer can pay part of the consideration only after a campus receives permits, reaches mechanical completion, energizes, or signs a tenant.
- ## M&A Market Structure
	- The market is shifting from individual asset trades toward whole-platform and majority-control transactions.
	- Platform value can exceed the sum of existing facilities because the buyer acquires:
		- Development staff and operating systems.
		- Customer relationships and master service agreements.
		- Land, power reservations, and interconnection positions.
		- A repeatable construction and procurement pipeline.
		- Financing access and credibility with utilities and municipalities.
	- The limiting factor is check size. Potential buyers include only the largest private-equity firms, infrastructure funds, pension plans, sovereign funds, insurers, and consortia.
	- Simultaneous auctions create a two-sided tension:
		- More assets give buyers choice and negotiating leverage.
		- Scarcity of quality powered portfolios can sustain premiums for the best assets.
	- The dispersion between winners and weaker platforms should widen based on power certainty, contracted tenancy, completion status, and community relationships.
- ## Deal Precedents
	- **Aligned Data Centers — approximately $40B**
		- A consortium led by [[$BLK]]'s Global Infrastructure Partners and MGX agreed to acquire Aligned in 2025.
		- The record value shows that consortium capital is required for the largest platforms and establishes a high headline benchmark for current sellers.
		- Closing risk still matters until financing, regulatory, and other conditions are satisfied.
	- **AirTrunk — approximately $16B**
		- [[$BX]]'s 2024 acquisition was the prior record, demonstrating how quickly the scale of platform deals expanded.
	- **QTS — approximately $10B**
		- [[$BX]] acquired QTS in 2021, before generative AI drove current capacity demand.
		- QTS illustrates the strategic advantage of owning an established powered-development platform before land, power, and construction costs repriced.
	- **Rowan Digital Infrastructure — approximately 49%**
		- [[$BX]] took a large minority stake in April 2026 rather than buying the entire business.
		- The structure preserves founder or sponsor participation while injecting capital and sharing the next development cycle's risk.
- ## Community Opposition as a Financial Variable
	- Local resistance is driven by concerns about higher utility bills, noise, water, tax incentives, visual impact, emissions, and the social consequences of AI.
	- Opposition can affect value through:
		- Longer permitting schedules and higher carrying costs.
		- Required utility, noise, water, or road mitigations.
		- Reduced campus size or power allocation.
		- Project cancellation and stranded land or equipment commitments.
		- Higher financing costs when completion dates become uncertain.
	- Community engagement therefore belongs in commercial due diligence, alongside leases and engineering.
	- Buyers should distinguish construction employment and tax revenue from permanent employment, ratepayer cost allocation, and the durability of negotiated community benefits.
