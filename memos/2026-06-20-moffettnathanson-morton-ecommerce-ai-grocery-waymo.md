- **Source**: Stratechery interview — Ben Thompson with Michael Morton (Senior Research Analyst, MoffettNathanson, e-commerce & marketplaces coverage), June 2026. Morton previously interviewed April 2024 and November 2025.

- ## The Unfalsifiable Bear Case

	- E-commerce / consumer internet multiples are down **~30% YTD** across Morton's coverage, driven by AI uncertainty rather than fundamental deterioration.
	- The bear case is uniquely difficult to kill because it can't be disproved with current data: (a) AI will automate all grocery orders, (b) AI agents will order Uber/DoorDash so no one opens the apps, (c) Shopify's stack will get vibe-coded. None of these is falsifiable today.
	- The pattern in markets: you worry about a risk when no one's talking about it; when everyone's talking about it, you often need to reassess and go the other way.
	- Stocks with unfalsifiable bear cases get multiple compression with "no floor" — investors can't be convinced there's a bottom, because you can't prove the negative.

- ## Shopify ([[$SHOP]])

	- ### Product Discovery Shift: Brand.com vs. Marketplaces
		- **Prior paradigm**: all product discovery roads led to Amazon — either directly or via Google Search → Amazon. Made it structurally hard for brand.com (Shopify's customer base) to build direct customer relationships.
		- **New data point**: MoffettNathanson measured traffic sent from AI platforms to marketplaces (Amazon, Wayfair, Chewy, eBay, Etsy) vs. brand.com (direct-to-consumer Shopify merchants).
		- In traditional Google Search: brand.com outgrew marketplaces by **~2x** (expected, as top Shopify merchants grow faster than Amazon).
		- In AI platforms (AI Mode, ChatGPT, etc.): brand.com is outpacing legacy marketplaces by **4–8x** in traffic growth.
		- Implication: AI product discovery is materially shifting share toward direct brand sites vs. marketplace aggregators — the opposite of what models told Morton when he asked them ("I favor Amazon, Walmart for trust, reviews, price, shipping"). Surprising outcome, had to change his mind.
		- Morton's thesis: this means Shopify GMV will "grow higher for longer than most people think."

	- ### Shopify Catalog
		- Tobi Lütke recognized the AI product discovery shift early and launched **Shopify Catalog** — a product feed that allows Shopify merchants to plug their inventory directly into AI platforms for discoverability.
		- Problem it solves: AI models don't like raw HTML scrapes for e-commerce (Google has "StoreBot" — a bot that places orders with fake credit cards to check delivery speeds and inventory; it's extremely hard to do this reliably).
		- Shopify is now offering Catalog access to **non-Shopify merchants** as well — expanding the competitive moat.
		- Shopify Catalog is another example of the puck moving in Shopify's favor in the AI era.

	- ### The Vibe-Coding Thesis Rebuttal
		- The "Shopify gets vibe-coded" bear case assumes merchants will rebuild their e-commerce stack from scratch using AI coding tools.
		- Reality check from a former Shopify employee who managed large enterprise relationships: the Shopify stack is effectively impossible to recreate at a lower cost even with unlimited resources and engineering talent, due to:
			- **Preferential fee structures** with Stripe that no individual merchant could negotiate independently.
			- **CAPI (Meta Conversions API) integration** — Tobi Lütke and Mark Zuckerberg worked on this deal personally; Shopify merchants get one-click CAPI integration, unavailable to standalone merchants.
			- A long list of partnership-level integrations across payments, shipping, tax, fulfillment, and marketing tools.
		- Even if vibe-coding were technically feasible, merchants don't want to own and maintain a custom stack — when something breaks across multi-channel selling (TikTok, Etsy, Amazon, Walmart Marketplace), all revenue is at risk with no support. "For the illusion of saving pennies."
		- Morton: "Shopify being vibe-coded is not one that keeps me up at night."

	- ### ChatGPT Checkout Failure — Why Shopify Wasn't to Blame
		- ChatGPT Checkout failed for multiple reasons: (a) ChatGPT needs Amazon more than Amazon needs ChatGPT; (b) never achieved critical mass; (c) consumers noticed incomplete inventory; (d) OpenAI was trying to do too many things at once even on the consumer side.
		- Bottlenecks were not on Shopify's side — Shopify had prepared well with the Catalog.

	- ### Shopify / Meta R-Squared
		- The R-squared between Shopify GMV and Meta revenue is "pretty much perfect" — Shopify's top line is tightly linked to Meta ad spending efficiency.
		- However, Morton views current Shopify weakness as separate from Meta's AI investment cycle — two different stories, both happening to compress at the same time.

- ## Referral vs. Distribution: The Core AI Framework

	- **The key distinction**: Is AI a referral channel (sends users to existing merchants/marketplaces with clear purchase intent, like a better Google Search) or a distributor (owns the customer relationship, makes e-commerce players into API calls)?
	- **6–9 months ago**: real fear that AI was becoming a distributor — some weaker consumer internet players ran into the arms of AI product offerings that risked making them pure API calls.
	- **Today**: distributor attempts have fallen short; AI looks much more like a **referral channel** — clearer intent, further down the funnel, higher conversion. This should be a de-risking event for e-commerce valuations, but hasn't played out in stock prices yet.
	- Crucial strategic observation: Google and Meta already learned that owning checkout/distribution doesn't work and is less profitable than advertising. They built advertising businesses — pure referral — where merchants owning the customer pay more for ads, creating a win-win. OpenAI Checkout getting "its butt kicked" was arguably the best thing that could happen to them.
	- **Traffic data update**: Traffic from AI platforms to e-commerce marketplaces (not brand.com, but Amazon/Wayfair etc.) growth rate has **slowed** since November 2025 (was "vertical, up and to the right"; now decelerating). Partly Amazon blocking AI discovery platforms, partly diversion to brand.com.

- ## UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol) / AI Commerce Standards

	- UCP is Google's e-commerce AI protocol — more generalizable than a purely Google product.
	- The bear case was that Google would use UCP to disrupt Shopify's payment processing. Reality: the protocol language **allows merchants to bring their own payment solution** — Google doesn't need to own payments.
	- The governing council now includes **Amazon, Meta, Stripe, Shopify**, and major merchants — the incumbent industry is co-designing the standard to protect their positions and move commerce to the next form factor.
	- Morton's view: "this is a really good thing for incumbent industry players" — Google (the apparent winner for consumer-facing AI) has every incentive to preserve its advertising business model, not disrupt it.

- ## Agent-Accessing-Apps Threat (Bear Case Rebuttal)

	- The fear: AI agents will directly use Uber/DoorDash/Instacart apps on behalf of users, cutting out the apps.
	- **Structural protection today**: Terms of service prevent unauthorized access and scraping. DoorDash, Uber, and Instacart have shut down third-party apps doing this on multiple occasions. Amazon is currently in legal proceedings with Perplexity over similar unauthorized access.
	- **Bigger picture**: if a Meta personal agent with hundreds of millions of users does this at scale, it becomes much harder to shut down — but consumer demand for this functionality is unclear. Morton's view: "I don't think there's friction in these types of things that consumers are looking to have solved."
	- Key insight from conference talks: "Silicon Valley needs to be constantly reminded that people don't want to be a lot more productive, they want to be entertained."

- ## Consumer Behavior: Non-Discretionary Automation Won't Work

	- **The milkman thesis**: In the early 1960s (pre-Walmart, smaller refrigerators, lower car ownership, maximum friction for grocery), only **30% of US milk consumption** was delivered — the most predictable, friction-heavy category. That share declined to zero in a straight line as car ownership and Walmart removed friction. If even the easiest possible category didn't achieve sustained automated delivery, it's hard to argue AI will.
	- **Amazon Subscribe & Save**: Amazon disclosed saving people ~$1 billion in 12 months; back-of-envelope based on assumed savings rates implies **only 1–3% of Amazon's 2023 GMV** was on Subscribe & Save. Despite years of availability and significant discounts, consumer adoption is minimal.
	- The key structural problem: the average household buys **250–300 items per year**. If you don't solve for all of them, you don't solve for anything — you just add another layer of complexity. No historical evidence consumers want non-discretionary goods showing up automatically.
	- **Where AI does help**: high-consideration, discretionary purchases — "I need a watch for surfing", "I need a specific fishing tool". Research and discovery for considered purchases, not replenishment of commodity goods.
	- CPCs will likely go **higher** as AI reduces clicks but increases intent and conversion — the offsetting mechanism that makes the Amazon on-site ads business more durable than feared.

- ## Ads and AI — Amazon ([[$AMZN]])

	- Morton changed his mind on Amazon's on-site ad risk: initially feared click decline would hurt CPC revenue; now less concerned.
	- **Evidence**: Mercado Libre was the first major marketplace to fully roll out LLMs in product search; they reported that higher conversion rates and CPCs are offsetting the decline in clicks. Caveat: Mercado Libre lacks a brand-building display ads business; Amazon has that additional layer.
	- Amazon has "implicit agreement" with consumers: consumers accept and click on Amazon ads in exchange for near-magical same-day/next-day delivery. Amazon is already rolling out **sponsored prompts** in its on-site AI search.
	- Rate of AI consumer adoption is slower than Morton expected, giving Amazon time to reposition.
	- **Etsy ([[$ETSY]])** — the most exposed name in coverage: by Morton's estimate, Etsy earns **>100% of EBIT from on-site ads** (i.e., merchandise business is roughly breakeven or loss; ads are the profit engine). With Shopify Catalog making brand.com more discoverable in AI, large Etsy merchants will increasingly ask why they pay a **~25% take rate** to Etsy when they could build a Shopify store and be discovered directly. Etsy has no fulfillment layer to protect itself.

- ## Grocery

	- ### Amazon's Grocery Reversal ([[$AMZN]])
		- Amazon had been middle-of-the-pack on grocery pricing for years; Walmart was the clear runaway leader.
		- Since November 2025: Amazon rolled out **same-day grocery** to **>2,000 US cities** and achieved **price parity with Walmart overnight** across a basket of consumables in 6 large suburban markets. Morton: "jaw-dropping."
		- How: Amazon quietly **put refrigerators into their fulfillment network** over time while no one was watching. Grocery grew **~40x YoY** (Amazon's own figure).
		- The limiting factor: Amazon reduced SKUs to achieve price competitiveness and speed. An average grocery store carries **40,000–50,000 SKUs**; Amazon's offering is still limited. Walmart learned this lesson the hard way — tried reducing SKUs, consumers revolted, had to expand again.

	- ### DoorDash ([[$DASH]]) — Grocery & Beyond
		- Morton rates DoorDash alongside Bezos/Amazon and Tobi/Shopify as one of the best operators in his coverage over the last 10–20 years.
		- **Origin story**: DoorDash started in suburban markets with larger-ticket orders when competitors were battling in cities over small orders. DoorDash was underfunded vs. peers, built a war chest, and now has **~2/3 of US restaurant delivery market share**.
		- DoorDash's approach to the restaurant business — mapping stairwells, front/back entrances, obsessive operational detail — is being directly applied to grocery.
		- **Grocery growth**: DoorDash growing **30–40%** in grocery, taking market share from Instacart and Uber Eats.
		- **DashMarts**: **100+ micro-warehouses** in cities allowing brands/merchants to store physical inventory for on-demand fulfillment.
		- **DashLink**: DoorDash rents warehouse space, stores online merchant packages, Dashers do last-mile delivery of **~70 packages per route** (similar to Amazon Flex / Walmart Spark). Morton: "building their own FBA 2.0."
		- **Speed inflection**: operators across the industry (Walmart, Ocado) are seeing an "explosion in conversion rate" when same-day/next-day is offered. Morton: "you wouldn't believe the conversion rates."
		- The mental model shift with same-day: once you believe something arrives in hours, the store becomes an absurd option. "FOMO ordering" — if I order now it's handled, if I don't I might forget.
		- DoorDash became the **#1 on-demand platform by order count** in the US in the past year.

- ## Autonomous Vehicles

	- ### Framework
		- Historical transportation paradigm: explosion of operators → overbuild → consolidation → price decline → volume explosion (railroads, airlines, autos all followed this pattern).
		- Consumer internet equivalent: there's almost always 1–2 dominant aggregators (Zillow in real estate, Spotify in music, YouTube in UGC, Netflix in streaming; Uber/Lyft in rideshare).
		- AV miles driven globally shows **Waymo + many Chinese operators** (who won't be allowed in the US) — if this were truly winner-take-all for Waymo, only one bar would exist.
		- Former Waymo engineers have spread across competitors (Zoox, etc.), per LinkedIn scraping.
		- Morton's 3–5-year view: multiple operators will exist, requiring demand aggregation (Uber-like) for high vehicle utilization → lower costs → higher volume.
		- Morton's 18–24-month view: **Waymo's game — they are so far ahead of everybody.**

	- ### Waymo
		- Near EBIT-per-mile positive in key markets: **high-20s trips/day** currently; breakeven estimated at **low-to-mid 30s trips/day**.
		- Per-car utilization is better than most realize; business model is better than the market appreciates.
		- Waymo fleet: originally ~3,700 Jaguar I-Pace vehicles (retrofit, proof-of-concept, expensive).
		- **Zeekr/Ojai van**: purpose-built AV platform — Chinese-manufactured. Despite Biden-era + Trump 100% tariffs on Chinese autos, Morton found via **bills of lading** (shipping manifests) that Waymo is importing **~300 Ojai vans/month**. That pace would **roughly double the Waymo fleet in 12 months**. Next platform is Hyundai Ioniq.
		- At this expansion pace, **mid-single-digit US rideshare market share** comes into view.
		- Co-CEO structure (Tekedra Mawakana and Dmitri Dolgov) produces mixed signals on strategy: "we don't want to change tires, we want to license" vs. actively opening 1P cities with a standalone app. Internal debate appears unresolved.

	- ### Waymo-Uber Relationship Breakdown ([[$UBER]])
		- The Uber-Waymo partnership appears to have broken down. Morton didn't fully anticipate how quickly this would happen.
		- Signs visible in hindsight: Uber announced Nuro/Lucid test city in **San Francisco** (Waymo's home turf); on earnings calls, Uber highlighted Waymo failures during SF power outages — unusual for a partner.
		- Bid-ask on economics: Uber wants to pay a take rate reflecting their demand-driving value; Waymo wants to earn more per trip and own the customer relationship.
		- Two models Waymo is testing: (1) **Lyft hybrid in Nashville** — Lyft fills non-peak demand; (2) **Uber model in Austin/Atlanta** — Uber rents vehicles and pays variable fee per trip, driving all demand.
		- Uber still has solid AV partnerships: Middle East, Europe, Wayve (UK), Nuro/Lucid (late 2026 US testing).
		- Market differentiation concern: Waymo customers view the Waymo experience as **highly differentiated** and worth opening a separate app — this puts Uber in a UPS/FedEx-to-Amazon-delivery position, only filling peak demand that Waymo can't serve.

	- ### Tesla ([[$TSLA]]) — The Wildcard
		- If Tesla truly delivers working FSD robotaxi at **~$30,000/vehicle**, it would be "game over for a lot of people."
		- Unique advantage: Tesla can produce vehicles at "biblical rates" vs. competitors, and at fraction of cost. OEMs produce millions of cars and won't dedicate a production line to 5,000-unit AV orders.
		- Morton: "a really terrifying fact" for all other AV operators — largely unknowable.
		- OEMs are now partnering with AV software companies (e.g., Wayve) to integrate into existing production vehicles — a path forward, but Waymo has a multi-year head start.

- ## Key Investment Conclusions (Morton's Current Views)

	- **Shopify** — Buy. AI product discovery is structurally favoring brand.com 4–8x vs. marketplaces. Shopify Catalog furthers the moat. Vibe-coding thesis is not credible to operators who know the actual stack complexity.
	- **DoorDash** — Bullish. Best operator in the space applying restaurant playbook to grocery. Building real physical infrastructure (DashMarts, DashLink) that creates durable competitive advantage in time-sensitive fulfillment.
	- **Amazon** — More constructive on grocery and on-site ads than previously. Same-day grocery at Walmart price parity is a major shift. Ad CPC offset from AI-driven higher conversion appears to be working (Mercado Libre evidence).
	- **Etsy** — Structurally exposed. >100% of EBIT from on-site ads, no fulfillment moat. AI-driven discoverability shift reduces merchants' dependence on Etsy's marketplace.
	- **Waymo (private/Google)** — 18–24-month runway of dominance; 3–5-year window before multiple operators emerge. Fleet doubling via Ojai imports. Near EBIT-per-mile positive. Tesla remains the large unknowable risk.
	- **Uber** — Strategically challenged by Waymo differentiation but still has fleet, international AV partnerships, and fills peak demand. Not going away.
