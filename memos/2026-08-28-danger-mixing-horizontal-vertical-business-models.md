- ## The Danger of Mixing Horizontal and Vertical Business Models
	- **Source**: User-provided excerpt from Stratechery, “Revisiting Roku,” revisiting Ben Thompson's analysis of Roku's origin inside Netflix.
	- **Thesis**: A horizontal platform must serve every participant, while a vertical business benefits from favoring its own product. Combining them can improve the product initially but eventually restrict distribution, alienate partners, and shrink the addressable market.

- ## The Strategic Conflict
	- Horizontal platforms win through broad reach and neutrality; vertical businesses win through control and integration.
	- Owning both creates pressure to preference the internal product, turning platform partners into competitors.
	- Self-preferencing can weaken participation and network effects even if it improves short-term economics.
	- Vertical integration is most useful in immature markets where critical complements do not yet exist. It becomes less attractive as the ecosystem matures.

- ## Netflix and Roku
	- Roku began as a Netflix set-top-box project when streaming hardware was immature, making integration useful for delivering a better early experience.
	- Netflix's long-term economics required distributing its high fixed-cost content across the largest possible global audience.
	- Keeping Roku inside Netflix would have made Netflix a device competitor to the television and streaming-hardware companies whose distribution it needed.
	- Spinning out Roku allowed Netflix to become ubiquitous while Roku became a neutral platform for competing streaming services.
	- Roku now monetizes third-party discovery and billing: subscriptions sold through its Channels business increased **19% year over year** in the cited second quarter, according to Antenna.

- ## Investor Diagnostic
	- Determine whether the platform or proprietary product represents the larger long-term profit pool.
	- Ask whether competitors can trust and benefit equally from the shared platform.
	- Watch for self-preferencing, partner defections, falling third-party participation, or rising capital intensity.
	- Be skeptical when a valuation assumes both ecosystem-wide platform reach and privileged economics for the company's own vertical product.
