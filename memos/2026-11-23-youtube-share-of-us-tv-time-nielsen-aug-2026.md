- ## YouTube is eating TV: 15.8% of US TV time, more than 2× Netflix (Nielsen, August 2026)
	- **Source**: App Economy Insights (@EconomyApp), X post and chart "Streaming in % of US TV Time," November 2026 (user-provided screenshot). The data is Nielsen's monthly Gauge (persons 2+). Values are as printed on the chart.
	- **Thesis**: YouTube's share of US TV time rose from **10.8% in January 2025 to 15.8% in August 2026**, a gain of 5 points (+46% relative).
		- **Pulling away from Netflix**: Netflix drifted down to **7.7%**, so YouTube now has **more than 2× Netflix's share**, up from 1.26× in January 2025.
		- **Every other platform is flat**: Disney's apps and Prime Video stayed within narrow ranges. YouTube is taking most of streaming's share gains of the TV screen.

- ## What the numbers show (memo calculations)
	- | Metric | Jan 2025 | Aug 2025 | Aug 2026 |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | YouTube ÷ Netflix | 1.26× | 1.51× | **2.05×** |
	  | YouTube share of all streaming time | 25.3% | 28.2% | **31.8%** |
	  | Streaming share of all US TV time | 42.7% | 46.5% | **49.7%**, close to half |
	- **Year over year (August 2025 to August 2026)**:
		- **YouTube +2.7 points**, or +21% relative.
		- **Netflix −1.0 point**.
		- Disney +0.2 points, Prime Video +0.3, and other streaming +1.0.
		- Total streaming gained 3.2 points, so YouTube took about **84%** of it.
	- **The August 2026 jump**: YouTube rose **1.6 points in one month**, from 14.2% to 15.8%, its largest monthly move in the series. Over the summer of 2025 it peaked in July and eased in August. One month can be seasonal noise, so later months will show whether the step is lasting.
	- **Netflix's pattern**: Netflix peaks around big releases and the holidays (9.0% in December 2025).
		- January to May 2026 were each slightly **above** the same month of 2025.
		- June to August 2026 fell **below** it. August was 7.7% vs. 8.7%, against the summer-2025 peak of 8.8%.

- ## Context in the repo
	- **A different Nielsen measure**: this chart is Nielsen's **platform** Gauge. The repo's earlier figure of about **12.5%** for late 2025 comes from Nielsen's **media distributor** rankings, where YouTube is also No. 1 ([[Google-GOOGL-thesis]] appendix). The February 2025 value here (11.6%) matches the record cited by MoffettNathanson ([[2026-11-19-moffettnathanson-youtube-king-of-all-media]]).
	- **More YouTube viewing is on the TV**: Tubular Labs puts YouTube's global viewing on TVs at about 44% in 2025, ahead of mobile ([[Google-GOOGL-thesis]] appendix).
	- **Monetization**: TV viewing is where YouTube competes for TV ad budgets and subscriptions (YouTube TV, Premium, NFL Sunday Ticket). MoffettNathanson values YouTube at up to \$550B on that basis ([[2026-11-19-moffettnathanson-youtube-king-of-all-media]]).
	- *Memo's reading*: share of time is not share of revenue. YouTube's ad load and revenue per viewing hour on TV remain below premium streaming and linear TV, so the viewing lead is ahead of monetization. That gap is the upside case.
