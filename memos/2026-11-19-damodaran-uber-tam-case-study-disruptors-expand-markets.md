- ## Case study: Damodaran's 2014 Uber TAM vs. what Uber became
	- **Source**: user-provided newsletter excerpt, November 2026 (author and publication not given), revisiting Aswath Damodaran's 2014 Uber valuation.
	- **Thesis**: Damodaran, an NYU Stern finance professor, argued in 2014 that Uber was overvalued because he sized its market as the existing taxi market. Uber instead expanded that market and added new ones, so its 2024 free cash flow came in at almost 10× his forecast. In the author's words, "Disruptive companies don't get X% of \$Y – they change what Y means and take a bunch of Z."

- ## Forecast vs. actual (2024)
	- | Metric | Damodaran's 2014 forecast for 2024 | Uber's actual 2024 | Actual ÷ forecast |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | Market size (TAM) | **\$183B** | Not one market: Uber created several | — |
	  | Uber's share | **10%**, about \$18.3B of bookings (memo calculation) | — | — |
	  | Mobility gross bookings | Within the \$18.3B | **\$83B** | About 4.5× (memo calculation) |
	  | Delivery gross bookings | Not in the TAM | **\$75B** | — |
	  | Freight gross bookings | Not in the TAM | **\$5B** | — |
	  | Total gross bookings | About \$18.3B (memo calculation) | About \$163B (memo calculation) | **About 9×** (memo calculation) |
	  | Free cash flow | **\$0.78B** | **\$6.9B** | **About 9×**, "almost 10×" |

- ## Why the TAM was wrong
	- **The taxi market grew**: Uber brought in new drivers, new riders and new kinds of trips that taxis never served.
	- **New markets were added**: restaurant delivery, freight, and later advertising, none of which belonged to the taxi TAM.
	- **The underlying error**: the author calls it the **lump-of-labour fallacy**, treating demand as a fixed pie. "Human needs are infinite and the pie can get not just get bigger, but get replaced by something completely different."
