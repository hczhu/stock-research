- ## Internet ads, from users' side: Instagram's targeting stands out, and retargeting persists because the numbers favor it
	- **Source**: User-provided Hacker News thread excerpts (commenters include mkehrt, HWR_14, WarmWash, pocksuppet, sublinear, CosmicShadow). These are first-hand anecdotes from a tech-savvy audience, not data. Related: [[META-2026-Q2]], [[2026-08-03-stratechery-meta-timing-problems-financial-tail]], [[2026-07-23-google-search-advertiser-extraction-claims-audit]].
	- **Thesis**: The thread contains three separate ideas about ads. First, **"dumb" ads persist because they work on the metric**: showing someone the blender they just bought is what the numbers reward. Second, **brand advertising works by repetition, not quality** ("Pantene, Pantene, Pantene"). Third, **Instagram is singled out, unprompted and repeatedly, as the one place where targeted ads feel useful**, especially for niche products from small brands. For Meta, that perception is the moat. The question raised in the thread is whether Meta keeps the surplus by raising ad prices.

- ## Why Instagram's ads stand out: the explanations in the thread
	- | Explanation | Who argued it | What it implies |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Better inference of taste (identifies "the social niche stereotype") | WarmWash, CosmicShadow | The advantage is in the model and ranking: durable if Meta keeps investing, as with GEM and its generative recommender |
	  | Product and format fit: display-worthy goods advertise well in a photo feed | sublinear | The advantage is in the format, so it carries over to any visual feed (TikTok, Pinterest) and isn't unique to Meta |
	  | Self-selected audience: being on Instagram is "the largest signal of all" | sublinear | The advantage is in the audience. It is cheap to target and less dependent on tracking, so more robust to privacy limits |
	  | Everyone else is bad at ads | CosmicShadow | The advantage is relative. It holds as long as rivals' ads stay poor, which the open web suggests they will |
	- These explanations aren't mutually exclusive. The Facebook comparison (same data, worse perceived ads) tells most against the "more data" explanation, and favors format and audience as the main reasons.
- ## What it means for Meta
	- **Users praising ads unprompted is rare, and Instagram gets that praise.** Several commenters, on a forum generally hostile to ads, describe Instagram ads as relevant or even "scary good." That fits Meta's reported numbers: GEM ranking drove **+15.7% conversions** on Facebook, and price per ad rose **+12%** with impressions up **+14%** in Q2 ([[META-2026-Q2]]).
	- **HWR_14's question is the real investment point**: "Why would Meta not raise their costs of advertising until it was the most efficient by the smallest margin?" In an auction, better targeting raises what advertisers are willing to bid, so Meta captures much of the efficiency gain through price per ad. Small businesses keep only the margin over their next-best channel. That makes Meta's ad revenue a toll on direct-to-consumer brand economics, and it's why rising price per ad and advertiser satisfaction can both hold for a while.
	- **Facebook vs Instagram** is the more surprising contrast: the same company and data, but a very different perception. If the gap comes from format (visual feed, Reels, interest-based follows) rather than data, Instagram's share of Meta's ad revenue mix is the thing to watch.
- ## Wider points about the ad market
	- **Optimizing metrics can lock in bad ads**: the Amazon story shows that last-touch attribution rewards ads shown to people who would buy anyway. Platforms that measure **incremental** lift, rather than attributed conversions, may show worse short-term numbers but deliver more real value to advertisers. Advertisers who shift to incrementality testing could reprice retargeting-heavy inventory downward.
	- **Brand and performance advertising both persist**: the P&G story is the brand-advertising model (repeated exposure creates pricing power), and the Instagram stories are the performance model (discovery leads to a purchase). Meta increasingly sells both on the same feed, and AI-generated creative makes repeated, varied exposure cheaper to produce.
	- **The open web loses on quality**: complaints about "sports gambling" and "800 singles" ads on news sites show why ad budgets keep concentrating in closed platforms with first-party data and controlled formats. See the counterargument below: these ads may be bad only for this audience.
- ## Counterargument: Hacker News commenters aren't average internet users
	- **Sample bias**: the people in this thread are tech-savvy, likely higher-income, often run ad blockers, and are unusually aware of tracking. Their sense of which ads are "good" or "dumb" reflects their own tastes, not those of the median user that ad revenue actually depends on.
	- **"Bad" ads may be well-targeted ads for someone else**: sports gambling, "800 singles in your area," and similar ads keep running because they **pay off** for their advertisers. In an auction, ads that don't convert stop getting bid on. These categories persist because a large audience responds to them. To an HN reader they look like targeting failures, but they may be accurate targeting of a different, larger audience that the commenter just isn't part of.
	- **What changes in the memo's reading**:
		- *Open-web quality*: "those are just shitty ads" says the ads don't suit this reader, not that the inventory has no value. Low-income, mass-market audiences on the open web can still be profitable for gambling, dating, sweepstakes and lending advertisers. The quality gap between the open web and closed platforms is smaller than this thread suggests.
		- *Instagram's praise*: Instagram may look exceptional to this group because its discovery ads for niche direct-to-consumer brands (wool socks, ceramics, \$1,000 furniture) happen to fit affluent, taste-driven buyers. For other users, Instagram also shows plenty of lower-quality ads. The thread shows that Meta serves **this** segment well, not that it serves everyone better than rivals.
		- *Facebook versus Instagram*: Facebook's older, broader audience may get ads that HN readers call "dumb" but that convert for that audience. That points to a different audience, not necessarily worse targeting.
	- **What still holds**: these points weaken claims about *perceived* quality, but not the economic logic. Meta still earns more as targeting raises conversion and price per ad (+12% in Q2). That shows up in reported metrics across all users, not just in anecdotes from a niche audience. The test is advertisers' measured return across segments, not how relevant users say the ads feel.
