- ## Internet ads, from users' side: Instagram's targeting stands out, and retargeting persists because the numbers favor it
	- **Source**: User-provided Hacker News thread excerpts (commenters include mkehrt, HWR_14, WarmWash, pocksuppet, sublinear, CosmicShadow). These are first-hand anecdotes from a tech-savvy audience, not data. Related: [[META-2026-Q2]], [[2026-08-03-stratechery-meta-timing-problems-financial-tail]], [[2026-07-23-google-search-advertiser-extraction-claims-audit]].
	- **Thesis**: The thread contains three separate ideas about ads. First, **"dumb" ads persist because they work on the metric**: showing someone the blender they just bought is what the numbers reward. Second, **brand advertising works by repetition, not quality** ("Pantene, Pantene, Pantene"). Third, **Instagram is singled out, unprompted and repeatedly, as the one place where targeted ads feel useful**, especially for niche products from small brands. For Meta, that perception is the moat. The question raised in the thread is whether Meta keeps the surplus by raising ad prices.

- ## What it means for Meta
	- **Users praising ads unprompted is rare, and Instagram gets that praise.** Several commenters, on a forum generally hostile to ads, describe Instagram ads as relevant or even "scary good." That fits Meta's reported numbers: GEM ranking drove **+15.7% conversions** on Facebook, and price per ad rose **+12%** with impressions up **+14%** in Q2 ([[META-2026-Q2]]).
	- **HWR_14's question is the real investment point**: "Why would Meta not raise their costs of advertising until it was the most efficient by the smallest margin?" In an auction, better targeting raises what advertisers are willing to bid, so Meta captures much of the efficiency gain through price per ad. Small businesses keep only the margin over their next-best channel. That makes Meta's ad revenue a toll on direct-to-consumer brand economics, and it's why rising price per ad and advertiser satisfaction can both hold for a while.
	- **Facebook vs Instagram** is the more surprising contrast: the same company and data, but a very different perception. If the gap comes from format (visual feed, Reels, interest-based follows) rather than data, Instagram's share of Meta's ad revenue mix is the thing to watch.
- ## Wider points about the ad market
	- **Optimizing metrics can lock in bad ads**: the Amazon story shows that last-touch attribution rewards ads shown to people who would buy anyway. Platforms that measure **incremental** lift, rather than attributed conversions, may show worse short-term numbers but deliver more real value to advertisers. Advertisers who shift to incrementality testing could reprice retargeting-heavy inventory downward.
	- **Brand and performance advertising both persist**: the P&G story is the brand-advertising model (repeated exposure creates pricing power), and the Instagram stories are the performance model (discovery leads to a purchase). Meta increasingly sells both on the same feed, and AI-generated creative makes repeated, varied exposure cheaper to produce.
	- **The open web loses on quality**: complaints about "sports gambling" and "800 singles" ads on news sites show why ad budgets keep concentrating in closed platforms with first-party data and controlled formats.
