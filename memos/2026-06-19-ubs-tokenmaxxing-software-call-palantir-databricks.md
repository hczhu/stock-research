- **Source**: UBS — US Software Sector Update Call "Token Maxxing," 19 Jun 2026. Speakers: Karl Keirstead & Roger Boyd (US Software Analysts); host Miya Liang (APAC).

- **Thesis ("Token Maxxing")**: Enterprise AI adoption ramped fast but **token/compute costs are now exceeding budgets**, forcing usage caps, guardrails, and cuts to other spend to fund AI. Risk: token demand growth flattens → "the first potential crack in the AI narrative." Most management teams frame the current optimization wave as a temporary "cleanup," not structural demand weakness.

- ## Company-Specific Data Points & News

	- **Palantir ([[$PLTR]])**
		- Saw a significant uptick in customers trying to cut token costs → launched a token optimization product called **"Evolve," reaching ~90% adoption in just 3 weeks**.
		- Evolve helps enterprises pick the right-sized model per task — when a heavy model (e.g., Claude Opus) isn't needed, route to a smaller/cheaper one (Haiku, GPT-Nano, or open-source).
		- **Real case study: helped a customer move from a heavier OpenAI model to a lighter "nano" version → ~97% token cost saving.** Optimization is very real and can sharply cut waste even while overall AI budgets keep growing.
		- **Key structural advantage: Palantir does NOT monetize on a token basis — it prices on an *outcome* basis.** So if a customer hits the same result with a cheaper model and lower token bill, Palantir isn't harmed (may even benefit). Being "outside the direct token path" is advantageous as the market shifts from usage-expansion to token-optimization.
		- Investor view: interest in the name as a **stronger data/infrastructure play relative to valuation**.
		- "Both can be true": overall AI spend can keep growing *while* waste is cut materially — enterprises are early in the adoption curve.
	- **Databricks (private)**
		- Flagged similar cost-control behavior — **customers actively pulling back and optimizing spend** (waste cleanup, not a demand collapse). Some (a UBS CTO) describe it as a "large speed bump."
		- CEO said customers are **now actively using Chinese models**.
		- **~15% of revenue now comes from AI pass-through.** Core growth ~65%; reported growth ~80% (pass-through inflates topline).
		- Pass-through is **very low gross margin** but highly accretive to reported growth. Databricks is more exposed than hyperscalers because it acts more like a **pure pass-through intermediary** (less value-add around the model layer).
	- **Cloudflare ([[$NET]])**
		- Clearer evidence of broad-based acceleration vs Akamai: traditional web-app performance accelerating, firewall/security improving, **developer platform growing triple digits** → blended business growing **~30%**.
		- Getting woven structurally into the AI/agentic-internet stack: code written via tools like Codex and deployed can default to hosting through Cloudflare's developer platform — more durable than renting scarce compute.
		- Post-analyst-day pushback was about the **50% long-term margin framework** (delicate for a premium-multiple stock), not about AI participation. Seen as beneficiary of coding-related traffic + developer platform + AI app deployment.
	- **Akamai ([[$AKAM]])**
		- Debate over why Akamai signed a **~\$2B compute deal with Anthropic** — durable AI participation or just near-term benefit from supply shortfalls? Limited visibility into structural benefit.
		- Viewed as **temporarily capitalizing on supply shortages**; has underperformed Cloudflare and DigitalOcean on growth.
	- **Microsoft / Azure ([[$MSFT]])**
		- UBS deploys Claude **via Microsoft Azure without a direct Anthropic relationship** — proof hyperscalers are the commercial interface between enterprises and AI labs.
		- Bedrock/Foundry/Vertex add tooling, orchestration, integration, security around models → **better gross margin than Databricks-style pass-through**; gateway role enables upsell/pull-through across the cloud stack.
	- **Accenture** — weakness seen as an early signal of IT-services budget being cut to fund AI.
	- **Salesforce / Workday / Adobe** — tough environment for seat-based/application SaaS. Adobe: "weak prints." Salesforce: 2H growth concerns keeping investors cautious.
	- **Snowflake & Datadog** — rallied strongly (narrow infra/data/cyber focus). **MongoDB** — potential lagging beneficiary.
	- **Cybersecurity names**: CrowdStrike & Palo Alto = main perceived beneficiaries of cyber tailwind. **Zscaler**: recent security issues a "bigger driver than COVID," but urgency plays out slower than market reaction implies. **Palo Alto**: customers assessing own risk/code/patching — a **6+ month process, not an immediate spending surge**. Newer interest: Akamai, Cloudflare, Okta.

- ## Core Themes

	- **1. Optimization phase underway (token waste cleanup)**: AI adoption only ~5–6 months in; token/compute costs running above enterprise budgets → usage caps on employees, spending guardrails, cutting other budgets. Risk = enterprise token spend flattens.
	- **2. Shift away from frontier models**: share shift from expensive frontier (OpenAI, Anthropic) toward smaller language models, cheaper closed models, and **Chinese open-source models** — now at *large* enterprises (not just AI-natives), some deployed **on-premise** to cut token spend. Negative for frontier labs (pricing/growth pressure); neutral-to-positive for hyperscalers (lower cost can drive higher volume).
	- **3. IT budget reallocation — 3 areas cut to fund AI**: (a) headcount growth (negative for seat-based SaaS, macro implications), (b) IT services spend (Accenture weakness, "worse before better"), (c) application software budgets (tough for Salesforce/Workday).
	- **4. Hyperscalers becoming the AI distribution layer**: AWS/Azure/Google Cloud/Oracle evolving from compute resellers to AI gateways. Low gross margin but accretive to revenue growth; strategically own the access layer. Not fully reflected in sentiment yet.
	- **5. Cyber budgets improving but slow to convert**: AI shifted from headwind → tailwind → now mixed. Budgets better, but 6–12 month sales cycles mean gradual uplift, "not weeks." Mythos announcement was the inflection.

- ## Frontier Lab (OpenAI / Anthropic) Pricing Risk

	- Too early to confirm clear pricing pressure, but risk building. WSJ suggested OpenAI may be trimming prices — unconfirmed whether it's a response to the Chinese/SLM mix shift. Concern is **gradual share shift** toward cheaper/open-source models rather than immediate demand collapse.
	- Two possible lab responses: (1) price cut, or (2) **proactively help customers optimize usage** (steer to lighter models) — analogous to **AWS in 2022–2024 helping customers cut cloud bills**; weighed on near-term growth but was right long-term. Helping customers manage bills may be the right move to penetrate enterprise at scale and build long-term trust ahead of IPOs.

- ## Investment Implications (UBS framing)

	- **Negative**: seat-based SaaS / application software (headcount + budget reallocation, very weak sentiment — <10% of investor conversations; "no strong bid").
	- **Mixed**: hyperscalers — fundamentals improving (multi-model platforms can route to cheaper models and capture share shifts) but not fully recognized; still net-negative for *sentiment* if the market believes token optimization is causing a broad AI-monetization slowdown.
	- **At risk**: frontier AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) — token pricing pressure + substitution.
	- **Best-positioned within the shift**: companies monetizing on **outcomes/orchestration/optimization rather than direct token pass-through** (Palantir the cleanest example); those on the direct token path (Databricks pass-through) are more exposed.
	- **Jevons-effect watch**: lower token cost could unlock more usage/adoption and offset revenue pressure (especially for hyperscalers); the open question is whether the cheaper-model shift cuts total revenue or just changes mix while expanding consumption.
	- **Key things to watch**: (short-term) Chinese open-source model (e.g., Qwen) adoption in regulated industries, frontier-lab pricing actions, hyperscaler add-on uptake (Bedrock/Vertex), Databricks resell contribution; (medium-term) whether lower token costs drive higher usage, hyperscaler revenue/margin impact, cyber deal timelines (6–12 mo) for CrowdStrike/Palo Alto/Zscaler/Okta.
