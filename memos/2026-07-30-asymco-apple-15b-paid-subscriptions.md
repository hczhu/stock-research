- ## Apple passes 1.5B paid subscriptions (Asymco chart)
	- **Source**: X post by Horace Dediu ([@asymco](https://x.com/asymco)), July 30, 2026, 14:26 — "Apple surpassed 1.5 billion paid subscriptions!" with his long-running "Subscriber and User Bases by Platform" chart (Jan 2009 → 2026). Figures OCR'd from the chart; mid-series labels overlap heavily, so only the clearly legible anchors are recorded below.
	- **Thesis**: Apple's paid-subscription base has crossed its own active-iPhone base — roughly 1.5B subscriptions against ~1.46B active iPhones and 2.5B active devices. The subscription count is now growing off a device install base that is itself still compounding, which is the structural argument for Services revenue durability. The critical caveat is definitional: Apple's disclosed "paid subscriptions" metric counts third-party App Store subscriptions alongside Apple's own, so this is a **billing-rail** metric, not a first-party Services subscriber count.

	- **Tesla FSD is the one accelerating small series**: roughly 3.7x growth from 0.4M to 1.48M across the plotted period, and the only sub-scale line with a visibly steepening slope. Peloton is the control case for a subscription business that stopped compounding.

- ## Ratios worth tracking over time
	- **Subscriptions per active device ≈ 0.60** (1,500 / 2,500). This is the single most monitorable number on the chart — it converts a raw count into a monetization-depth metric that can be tracked quarterly and compared against future device-base growth.
	- **Subscriptions now exceed active iPhones** (~1,500 vs ~1,460). The crossover matters because it means subscription growth is no longer bounded by iPhone unit growth — a device can carry multiple subscriptions, and non-iPhone devices (iPad, Mac, Watch) carry them too.
	- **Active devices ÷ active iPhones ≈ 1.71**, up steadily across the series — the multi-device attach rate that gives Services its second growth vector independent of smartphone replacement cycles.

- ## Definitional caution before using this number
	- Apple's reported "paid subscriptions" figure includes subscriptions purchased through the App Store for **third-party** apps, not only Apple's own services (iCloud+, Apple Music, TV+, Arcade, Fitness+). A large share of the 1.5B is therefore revenue Apple takes a **commission** on rather than revenue it owns end-to-end.
	- Practical consequence: the metric is highly sensitive to App Store commission regulation. Anything that reduces Apple's take rate or permits external purchase flows would leave the subscription count intact while cutting the revenue attached to it — so the count and the economics can diverge. Treat 1.5B as evidence of **platform billing scale**, not as a proxy for Apple-owned recurring revenue.
	- The chart mixes definitionally different series — paid subscriptions, active devices, monthly users, and subscriber counts are not like-for-like. It is useful for relative scale and slope, not for precise cross-company ratios.
