- ## AI Raises the Value of Live Rights, Attention, and Scarce Taste
	- **Source**: Ben Thompson, “A Conversation With Ari Emanuel About Entertainment and AI,” Stratechery, October 15, 2026. User-provided transcript of Thompson’s conversation with WME Group Executive Chairman and [[$TKO]] Executive Chair and CEO Ari Emanuel.
	- **Thesis**: AI should expand content supply and pressure digital intermediaries, but the resulting abundance increases the relative value of scarce assets: live sports rights, authenticated human connection, trusted distribution, exceptional taste, and negotiating talent. [[$TKO]] has the clearest public-market exposure because it owns league-level IP and monetizes the same events through media rights, sponsorship, site fees, premium hospitality, and tickets. [[$META]] is positioned to absorb advertising displaced from agent-disintermediated search and commerce, while search-led advertising and undifferentiated software face pressure.

- ## Advertising Moves Toward Surfaces Agents Cannot Bypass
	- Thompson’s core distinction is whether a human still chooses the commercial entry point. Search ads remain defensible while the user searches; autonomous agents threaten the model when they directly select products, reservations, or services.
	- That creates a potential shift away from “search-skim” advertising at Google and Amazon and toward surfaces that still generate desire: social feeds, entertainment, live sports, and events.
	- [[$META]] may benefit twice: a private, personalized ad predictor can improve relevance without moving personal context outside the user’s environment, while agents simultaneously weaken competing search and commerce ad inventory.
	- The loss of advertising can be short-to-medium-term inflationary. Advertising subsidizes retail, media, and subscription economics; if agentic cancellation and purchasing reduce that subsidy, providers may need higher direct prices.
	- Social graphs could become more valuable as AI removes ordinary reasons to contact other people for help. Thompson and Emanuel expect human connection and shared live experiences to become scarcer rather than obsolete.

- ## TKO and the Live-Event Monetization Stack
	- Emanuel described the operating playbook in layers:
		- Secure domestic and international broadcast rights.
		- Add sponsorship inventory.
		- Expand events geographically and collect site fees.
		- Sell premium tickets and hospitality while maintaining a broad general-audience experience.
	- UFC supplied the template; TKO then applied sponsorship, site-fee, premium-ticketing, and rights-negotiation capabilities to WWE. The same capabilities inform MARI’s expansion of art fairs, collectibles events, tennis, theater, food, and holiday experiences.
	- Sports are useful to both subscription and advertising-supported streaming because outcomes are unknown and consumption is time-sensitive. Emanuel expects enough bidders—including traditional media, Apple, YouTube, and free ad-supported streaming—to prevent a durable rights monopsony.
	- Owning a league is strategically distinct from owning a team: the league controls the competition and content supply, then sells rights across the whole system. Emanuel’s “four leagues” comparison is promotional and the transcript does not provide a sum-of-the-parts valuation, but it identifies the source of [[$TKO]]’s scarcity value.
	- MARI is best understood as a pro-AI complement rather than an anti-technology hedge. If AI increases leisure and atomizes digital life, repeatable live-event formats gain from both more available time and demand for physical community.

- ## AI Expands Content Supply but Concentrates Bargaining Power
	- AI can reduce set, makeup, visual-production, and iteration costs, enabling more content and more creators. Lower production cost alone is therefore not a studio moat; it may intensify competition for audience attention.
	- The scarce layer moves toward taste, recognizable talent, financing, conflict resolution, marketing, and distribution. Emanuel argues that agents retain value because they assemble these inputs and negotiate economics for unusually productive individuals.
	- AI may make the Hollywood talent model relevant to technology: exceptional researchers already command compensation in the millions, and scarce “barrel” talent may need representation across financing, employment, IP, and distribution.
	- Expertise changes the quality of AI output. Generic prompts produce average results, while a filmmaker who can specify a scene or shot uses the model as a high-bandwidth creative tool. This favors incumbents with differentiated taste and workflow knowledge, not merely access to the same model.

- ## Software and Media Economics Under Abundance
	- Thompson expects severe software pricing pressure, not the disappearance of software. AI makes custom tools credible and increases horizontal competition as productive companies expand into adjacent categories.
	- Maintenance and focus preserve a role for vendors: American businesses often prefer buying non-differentiating infrastructure rather than owning bespoke systems. The near-term bear case is margin compression and competition, especially for private-equity software vintages acquired near 2021 valuations.
	- Current AI use resembles a “glorified search” phase. Thompson argues that workflow transformation may require a generation accustomed to treating AI-native execution as normal, just as SaaS adoption required workers comfortable operating through web applications.
	- In entertainment, AI lowers the cost of making content while streaming has reduced the value of old syndication windows. Platforms may produce more, but hit-driven economics remain; cheaper supply does not guarantee consumer demand or restore legacy profit participation.

- ## Evidence Boundaries
	- The conversation is thesis-rich but data-light. Most figures are speaker recollections, analogies, or directional estimates rather than audited company disclosures.
	- MARI and WME Group are private, and the transcript gives no revenue, margin, leverage, acquisition-price, or return data. Their operating claims are useful mainly as a read-through to [[$TKO]] and the broader live-events category.
	- The [[$META]], [[$AMZN]], [[$GOOGL]], [[$SNAP]], and [[$NFLX]] implications are Thompson’s and Emanuel’s forecasts. The transcript does not quantify agent adoption, advertising migration, production-cost savings, or the timing of those changes.
