- ## Key Structural Insight

	- The US employs **59% of elite AI researchers** globally despite training only **24% domestically** — the gap is filled almost entirely by China-educated talent flowing to the US.
	- 72% of all researchers educated in China now work in US institutions. China is the world's largest producer of elite AI talent by a wide margin (38%), but retains only 11% at home.
	- The US AI advantage is not primarily a function of domestic educational output. It is a function of visa policy and institutional attractiveness that captures talent from elsewhere — especially China.

- ## Policy Implication

	- Any restriction on immigration from China — tightening student visas, OPT/H-1B limitations, security-based screening that slows or blocks Chinese nationals — **directly degrades US AI capacity**.
	- China's low retention rate (11%) reflects that the US and other Western institutions remain far more attractive for elite researchers. This dynamic has persisted despite China's massive increase in AI research output.
	- A US policy shift that slows this inflow would not primarily hurt Chinese researchers — it would hurt US AI labs, hyperscalers, and universities competing in frontier AI development.

- ## Investment Implications

	- **US AI labs and frontier model companies** (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta FAIR) are disproportionately dependent on China-born talent. A geopolitical escalation that restricts this pipeline hits them harder than their Chinese counterparts.
	- **Chinese AI companies** (ByteDance, Baidu, Zhipu, Moonshot, DeepSeek) face a structural disadvantage: they produce the researchers but lose 89% of the best ones. DeepSeek's competitive breakout suggests this gap may be closing, but the talent concentration data says US dominance remains intact for now.
	- The tracker validates the strategic logic of the Cursor/SpaceX acquisition: a world where 59% of elite AI researchers are US-employed gives US AI-adjacent software companies continued access to the best model capabilities, at least as long as immigration policy holds.
