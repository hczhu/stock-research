- ## Nestlé’s approach to AI in food manufacturing
	- **Source**: Aimee Look, [“Nestle Eyes Autonomous Factory Lines and Looks to Google, Microsoft as Partners”](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nestle-teams-up-with-microsoft-boston-dynamics-on-ai-in-factories-and-products-05bfb028), The Wall Street Journal, updated November 20, 2026 (supplied PDF, pp. 1–3).
	- Nestlé opened its Deep Tech Center in Orbe, Switzerland, on November 20, 2026. The center has facilities to prototype and test applications at pilot scale before wider deployment. Nestlé [described the center as a first for the food and nutrition industry when it announced the plan in 2025](https://www.nestle.com/media/pressreleases/allpressreleases/bio-tech-deep-tech-research-development). [Opening announcement](https://www.nestle.com/media/news/deep-tech-center-orbe-ai-robotics)

- ## Why food factories need to adapt to variable inputs
	- **Food ingredients vary more than automotive inputs.** Judith Arfsten, head of Nestlé’s Deep Tech Center, told the WSJ that a hotter-than-normal summer can materially change the natural raw materials a food line receives. In automotive manufacturing, the inputs are comparatively uniform.
	- **The goal is consistent output from inconsistent inputs.** Nestlé aims to use smart sensors to detect variations, AI to interpret them, and robotics to help adjust operations. The company is pursuing both consistent product quality and greater operating efficiency.
	- **Nestlé is starting with individual operations across its top ten manufacturing processes.** CTO Stefan Palzer says deployment must proceed category by category. Agentic AI coordinating an entire line is a longer-term objective, not a capability Nestlé says it has already deployed. Management expects human experts to retain a decision-making role.

- ## Technology partnerships and their status
	- | Partner | Status in the WSJ report | Role |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Microsoft and Boston Dynamics | Signed agreements | AI and engineering work for factory processes and products such as coffee machines |
	  | Google | In final discussions | Prospective partnership, not a signed deal in the report |
	  | Apple | Nestlé is using some of its technology | Spatial computing; no signed agreement described |
	  | IBM | Separate collaboration agreed in 2025 | AI for packaging innovation |
	- Orbe complements Nestlé’s Shenzhen hub, which identifies technologies in China that could be used in products globally.

- ## Consumer demand and product development
	- The WSJ links Nestlé’s AI effort to shifting consumer habits, including the effects of GLP-1 drugs and pressure on household budgets. Vontobel analyst Jean-Philippe Bertschy says AI can help with reformulation and product development, but whether those products drive sales depends on execution.
	- The report describes a development program, not a measured financial result: it gives no estimate of factory savings or expected sales uplift.
