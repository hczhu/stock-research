- ## Apple's memory problem: from price-setter to committed buyer
	- **Source**: Stratechery, Ben Thompson, "Memory Problems" section of his Apple FY2026 Q4 earnings update (quarter ended Sept 26, 2026; CEO John Ternus's first call, with COO Sabih Khan also new to it). Balance-sheet figures come from Apple's [FY2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000320193/000032019326000026/aapl-20260926.htm). Supplier attributions come from press reports, labeled as such. Related: [[AAPL-2026-Q2]], [[2026-07-04-horace-dediu-memory-shock-apple-supply-chain]], [[2026-08-28-stratechery-mac-refresh-memory-pricing-and-jalapeno]], [[2026-06-24-apple-price-skimming-iphone-dram-passthrough]].
	- **Thesis**: Memory is now hitting Apple on four fronts: margin already lost, more margin to lose, large purchase commitments, and memory still short. The fourth matters most. Apple's supply-chain edge was that it could **dictate price and flex volume while suppliers carried capacity and inventory**. In this market it has to forecast demand, commit cash up front to the big three memory vendors, and carry the inventory risk itself. The largest consumer memory buyer locking in supply at peak prices is also the clearest available signal that the consumer memory squeeze runs well into 2027.
- ## The four fronts
	- | Front | Evidence | Read |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | 1. Margin already lost | Memory cut gross margin by about **1 point** in the Sept quarter, even after the June Mac and iPad price increases | Third straight quarter where memory explains more than all of the sequential compression (see [[AAPL-2026-Q2]]) |
	  | 2. More margin to lose | December-quarter margin down another **1.5–2 points** despite September iPhone and October Mac/iPad increases; Ternus: memory pricing is "unprecedented" and "continuing to increase" beyond December | The carry-in inventory benefit is shrinking ("a decreasing benefit… over time"), so reported margins will catch up to spot-like costs |
	  | 3. Purchase commitments | Manufacturing purchase obligations **+\$33B** from June to September | Apple is locking in supply, and probably locking in today's high prices with it |
	  | 4. Still short | Khan: December-quarter constraints on **Mac and iPad, primarily driven by memory**, because demand beat forecasts | The binding constraint has moved from 2nm SoCs, which are easing, to memory |
- ## What the 10-K shows: Apple is stockpiling components
	- | Item | FY2025 (Sept 2025) | FY2026 (Sept 2026) |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Manufacturing purchase obligations | — | **\$89.7B**, of which **\$82.7B** due within 12 months (implies ~\$57B in June, given the +\$33B) |
	  | Component inventory | \$2.1B | **\$10.4B** (~5×) |
	  | Total inventory | \$5.7B | **\$14.5B** |
	  | Products gross margin | 36.8% | 39.3%: "product mix and tariff refunds, partially offset by higher costs, including memory" |
	- The component inventory jump is the physical counterpart to Thompson's argument. Apple historically ran near-zero inventory and pushed it onto suppliers. A fivefold rise in components on its own balance sheet means Apple now holds buffer stock it used to make vendors hold.
	- The 10-K's MD&A says Apple "expects these trends to intensify for memory and storage components." Its risk factors also flag impairment review of "inventory prepayments", which is the downside if prices fall before the commitments are used up.
	- **Caveat**: the \$33B is total manufacturing obligations, not memory alone. It also covers 2nm wafers and other components. Apple does not break out the memory share.
- ## Who the commitments go to
	- **DRAM: the big three.** Press reports put **Samsung at ~60%** of Apple's DRAM, with **SK hynix and Micron** splitting the rest. Korean reports say the new Samsung LPDDR5X contract is priced at **nearly double** the year-ago level ([MacTrast](https://www.mactrast.com/2025/12/apple-to-lean-on-samsung-a-bit-harder-as-ram-prices-go-crazy/), [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/apple-requested-13-million-10nm-lpddr5x-dram-chips-from-samsung-for-iphone-18/)). Caution: The Bell retracted one Samsung order report at Samsung's request.
	- **NAND**: a September report describes a **3–5 year long-term agreement with no price cap**. The counterparty is unnamed but widely reported to be Kioxia ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/apple-nand-memory-supply-deal-kioxia-2026)). Unconfirmed by either company.
	- **CXMT as leverage**: in February, Apple reportedly evaluated Chinese memory to pressure Samsung and SK hynix ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/02/26/news-apple-reportedly-eyes-chinese-memory-as-leverage-against-samsung-and-sk-hynix-amid-rising-costs/)). Committing large sums to the big three suggests that leverage did not change the price.
	- The structure fits the vendors' LTA regime: about **70% of Samsung's memory capacity is under LTAs through 2031** ([[2026-09-04-dram-feed-cxmt-share-break-and-lta-floor-arithmetic]]). Buyers who don't commit get the residual spot market. Apple choosing to commit is Apple accepting the vendors' terms.
- ## The consumer memory market: tight supply and weak demand at the same time
	- | Side | Data point | Source |
	  |---|---|---|
	  | Supply | Big three shifting wafers and leading-edge nodes to HBM and server DRAM; HBM4 uses **~3× the wafer area** of conventional DRAM per bit | [[2026-09-14-dram-feed-memory-supply-demand-tightness]] |
	  | Supply | Supplier inventory **<10 days** at Samsung and SK hynix (KB Securities, Sept) | same |
	  | Supply | CXMT filled the vacated commodity tier: **10%** DRAM revenue share in Q2 2026, two years ahead of forecasts; big three fell from 94% to 87% | [[2026-09-04-dram-feed-cxmt-share-break-and-lta-floor-arithmetic]] |
	  | Supply | New capacity is late: Micron's Taiwan fab adds >10% by late 2027; Samsung P5 targets late 2028 | [[2026-09-14-dram-feed-memory-supply-demand-tightness]] |
	  | Demand | 2026 smartphone shipments forecast **−13% to −14%**, the lowest since 2013; low-end retail prices would need +40–50% to hold margins | [[2026-07-20-xiaomi-raises-shipment-target-dram-demand-ceiling]] |
	  | Price | Mobile DRAM contracts **+8–13% QoQ** in Q3; TrendForce expects Q4 increases to "abate" into "high consolidation". PC DRAM Q4 forecast reportedly raised to **+3–8%** | TrendForce via [[2026-09-14-dram-feed-memory-supply-demand-tightness]]; [KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/trendforce-raises-q3-q4-dram-contract-price-forecasts-benefits-micron) |
	- **Why weak demand doesn't loosen supply**: per-quarter price increases are slowing because consumer affordability is binding, but prices are not falling. Vendors are withdrawing consumer capacity faster than handset and PC units decline, because an AI server pays more per wafer than any phone. Consumer supply is the residual after AI demand is served.
	- **Apple's position within that market**: Apple is the buyer best able to pay. It sells at premium prices, and its demand beat forecasts while the overall market shrinks. That is why its shortage is in **Mac and iPad**, the memory-heavy devices where AI-driven demand for unified memory is highest, not in iPhone.
	- **Knock-on effect for everyone else**: when the largest consumer buyer pre-commits volume, the uncommitted pool for Android and PC OEMs gets smaller. This favors Samsung's own handset business, which Counterpoint expects to retake #1 from Apple in 2026, and squeezes Xiaomi, OPPO/vivo, and Transsion at the low end.
- ## Why this conflicts with the services strategy
	- Thompson's longer arc: Apple claimed to be a services company in 2016, actually became one by 2019 when it stopped reporting units, and from 2022 **declined to raise prices through inflation**. Keeping hardware prices flat effectively cut real prices to grow the installed base that services monetize.
	- Memory broke that policy. Apple raised prices in June (Mac and iPad), September (iPhone), and October (Mac and iPad again), and margins are still falling. Each increase works against the installed-base growth the services business depends on. This is the cost the services strategy was built to avoid.
	- **The forecasting asymmetry is new for Apple**: forecast too low and Apple loses sales, as it is doing now on Mac and iPad. Forecast too high and Apple, not its suppliers, carries excess inventory bought at peak prices. Thompson made the same point earlier about 2nm SoCs; memory is a larger version of it because memory prices can fall sharply.
	- **The open question**: whether the commitments are pinned to today's prices or float with the market. A cap-free NAND LTA and doubled DRAM contract prices both suggest Apple is paying near the top. If consumer memory prices turn in 2027, as Xiaomi bet in July, Apple's December guidance pattern would reverse slowly, because higher-cost committed inventory would keep flowing through cost of goods.
