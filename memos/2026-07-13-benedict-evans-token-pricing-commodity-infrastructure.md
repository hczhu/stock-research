- **Source**: Benedict Evans, "Ways to think about token pricing," benedictevans.com, **July 9, 2026**. Subtitle: "AI is in a supply crunch today, but what happens when we come out of it?… Today, model labs can name their price, but [do they end up as] commodity infrastructure?" Companion to [[2026-07-13-emerging-market-for-intelligence-nber-llm-pricing]] (the NBER paper is the empirical version of this argument), [[2026-06-18-ai-bubble-debate-capital-cycle-cyclicality-podcast]], and [[2026-07-07-hyperscaler-capex-goldman-bofa-cy27-28e]].

- **Core argument (his words)**: Only two certainties about token prices — **we're in a supply crunch, and this is unstable**. The end-state question is whether foundation models get **sustainable pricing power, strategic leverage and value capture, or become low-margin commodity infrastructure**. His verdict: "**At the moment, I think every dynamic we can see points to the latter.**" And the kicker: "**every path to foundation models having market dominance… or anything else other than becoming commodity infrastructure, requires something to change.**"

- ## Why Today's Pricing Power Is Transitory
	- **Supply side**: **\$1T+ of data-center capex** is coming down the pipe (plus more semi capex behind it); inference efficiency improves very quickly; new models are far more (or far less!) efficient in token use.
	- **Demand side**: the market has been **capacity-constrained since 2022**, but the 1H26 crunch was driven by sudden product-market fit in **just one use case — software development — "actually a pretty small field."** If a consumer use case with **hundreds of millions of DAUs** hit PMF, "today's infrastructure couldn't support it at any price." We don't know the next use cases to scale, when, or their token needs.
	- **Unit economics are murky**: inference reportedly runs **40–50% gross margins** — but that includes server depreciation over an *unknown* asset life (5 years? 7?), and **excludes the cost of training the next model a couple of times a year**. Inference is a marginal-cost business; buyer-side **ROI is unproven** ("hard to sell to a CFO"), let alone for future use cases.
	- **Bottom-up modeling is futile**: modeling chips→TSMC delivery→data centers→power→price discipline→use cases is "like building a five-year broadband forecast in 1998" — pretty spreadsheet, too many unknowns. All you can say: **token price lands between sellers' marginal cost and buyers' ROI, and we don't know either.**
	- **No known physical limits**: unlike 1995 (we knew <100M PCs existed, telcos couldn't build FTTH overnight) or 2010, there's no theoretical understanding of why models work. "**Next month a new approach could cut inference compute needs by 90%, or double demand, or both.**"

- ## The Four Questions That Decide the End State
	- **1. How many will pay to be at the frontier (top-right of the intelligence-vs-cost-per-task curve)?** Some use cases already run fine on small/old/open models "for free" on-prem or on a phone; how much usage is handled by smaller, cheaper, "good enough," much more commoditized models? (The NBER data answers: **most** — 90th-percentile token intelligence sits well below the frontier; [[2026-07-13-emerging-market-for-intelligence-nber-llm-pricing]].)
	- **2. Does the frontier keep moving significantly?** The most basic science question — and does it move at a rate that outpaces efficiency/capacity gains?
	- **3. Convergence or divergence between frontier models?** "Everyone is using mostly the same science and mostly the same training data, and getting mostly the same results, and **we don't yet know of a network effect or any other winner-takes-all effect** that would let one company pull ahead, stay ahead, and do things others could not, in some sustainable way." Evidence for convergence: **in the last six months, Zuckerberg and Musk jumped from zero back onto the leaderboards** (also: 3 different #1 creators on OpenRouter in one year, per the NBER memo).
	- **4. How much of the high-end value is captured by the model itself?** Or is the model — no matter how good — still infrastructure you use to make the actual product (tooling, proprietary data, GTM, support)? "**Every SaaS company is a 'database wrapper.'**" The two poles: (a) "two or three giant minds run half of everything with massive pricing power," vs (b) "LLMs look like databases — millions of them… dozens of routers run real-time auctions across hundreds of **low-margin model-farms** and a **benchmark company takes a fee on every single one**." (Pole (b) is literally OpenRouter + Artificial Analysis today.)
	- None of these are binaries; they vary by use case. But every non-commodity outcome **requires something to change** from current dynamics.

- ## The Analogy Audit (his pattern-matching discipline)
	- **Fiber/dotcom — rejected**: fiber was built *ahead* of demand; AI compute is *behind* demand. More importantly, fiber was **fixed cost** (digging holes) while compute growth is **marginal cost** (buy more equipment).
	- **Mobile data — the fruitful one**: marginal-cost capacity, a demand surge ~15 years ago that overwhelmed capacity and forced pricing rebalance; selling bits ≈ selling tokens (an opaque marginal-cost unit that maps to no use case or value, eventually replaced by **bundles**). The punchline: **cellular traffic rose orders of magnitude into a \$1T-revenue industry with \$200B annual capex — "but the stocks have gone nowhere, and all the value was captured by other people further up the stack."** That is the core question for AI.
	- **Semiconductors — the partial counter**: Rock's Law (leading-edge fab cost doubles every 4 years) collapsed dozens of players to essentially one — TSMC. AI training cost escalation could do the same *even without network effects*. **But even TSMC — a de facto frontier monopoly with nice margins — doesn't capture much of the tech economy: net income last year \$53B, "less than half of Apple alone."** (Direct cross-check for [[TSMC-TSM-thesis]]: the moat is real *and* the value capture is bounded.)
	- **Altman's self-comparisons cut both ways**: OpenAI as **Windows** (high-margin, capital-light, network-effect monopoly) *and* as an **electricity utility** (natural monopoly selling a pure commodity) — "none of these have predictive value. You can't prove [the outcome] by arguing how much it's like [something else]."
	- Empirical lesson of all the analogies: something can be world-changing, expensive, and full of sophisticated science **and still land anywhere** — high or low margins, concentrated or not. "You can't hand-wave that away by talking about AGI and saying 'you don't understand exponentials!'"

- ## The Wildcards (deus ex machina ×2)
	- **Trump and China.** China is reportedly **considering regulating open source**; some people close to Trump have floated the same — and "**since Meta abandoned Llama, the US has no leading open model**." Export controls could expand and become systematic. Anthropic's (and sometimes OpenAI's) regulation pleas are seen by many as **regulatory capture** — either way, "we can't presume this will remain an entirely free market." (Rhymes with the US-gov gating of Fable/GPT-5.6 in [[2026-07-06-gpt56-sol-hn-cerebras-inference-economics]].)
	- Other things that could break the commodity path: network effects emerging; chatbots growing into products that don't need software wrappers; one lab out-executing all others on product dynamism ("Microsoft, Google, Facebook and Apple had to execute their way into a winning position *before* they had winner-takes-all effects").

- ## Investment Read-Through
	- **Bear case for standalone AI-lab economics (OpenAI IPO framing especially)**: the essay's conclusion — current dynamics point to falling prices, frontier models drifting toward commodity infrastructure, **value accruing to what's built on top** — is the strongest articulate version of the "labs = telcos" risk. It pairs with the NBER evidence (90%-cheaper open models, elasticity ~1, leadership churn, below-frontier usage) as theory + data of the same thesis.
	- **The mobile-data precedent is the scariest chart for lab investors**: trillion-dollar revenue, \$200B capex, flat stocks, value captured up-stack. Watch whether labs escape via **bundles/subscriptions** (the carriers' move — and effectively what Claude Code/ChatGPT Pro plans are) rather than per-token pricing.
	- **The TSMC precedent bounds even the best case**: if training-cost escalation (Rock's-Law-style) collapses the frontier to 1–2 labs, the winner still may earn TSMC-like — excellent but bounded — economics (\$53B < half of Apple), not platform economics, unless network effects appear.
	- **Bullish for the layers he says capture value**: application/agent companies on top ("hundreds of new companies that pick and choose models"), routers/aggregators (OpenRouter), benchmarks (Artificial Analysis), and — unchanged by any of this — the **supply chain beneath** (the \$1T+ capex flows to [[TSMC-TSM-thesis]] / [[DRAM-memory-ssd-index-thesis]] regardless of which lab wins).
	- **Key monitorables he implies**: (1) a second PMF use case at consumer scale (would re-tighten supply violently — bullish infra); (2) any evidence of a network effect at a lab; (3) open-source regulation in the US or China (would *create* the scarcity/moat that economics currently won't); (4) whether frontier stays contested (Meta/xAI re-entries suggest yes); (5) pricing-model shift from tokens to bundles/outcomes.
	- **Disconfirming evidence to track against his thesis**: closed-model price *rigidity* and the <30% open-source share (NBER) show differentiation is currently holding margins up; Anthropic's brand-centric substitution moat; and enterprise willingness to pay 9× more per token in high-stakes categories. Commoditization is the direction, but the pace is contested.
