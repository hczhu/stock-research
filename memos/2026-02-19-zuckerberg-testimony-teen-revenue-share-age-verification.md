- ## Zuckerberg's trial testimony: teens are 1% of revenue, and age limits are "very difficult" to enforce
	- **Source**: Bloomberg (Riley Griffin, Madlin Mekelburg), February 19, 2026, on Zuckerberg's testimony in the Los Angeles Superior Court social-media addiction trial. **Six months before** the state-AG agreement in [[2026-08-26-meta-state-ag-teen-safety-agreement]] — this is the litigation backdrop that explains why that settlement happened and how to price it.
	- **Thesis**: Two disclosures under oath do most of the work. **Teens are ~1% of Meta's revenue**, which bounds the commercial cost of every engagement restriction Meta later agreed to; and Meta's own chief executive conceded age enforcement is unreliable, which is both the liability exposure and the basis for Meta's campaign to push age verification onto **Apple and Google**.
- ## The number that sizes the August settlement
	- **Zuckerberg testified that teens account for just 1% of Meta's revenue**, almost entirely advertising: *"Most teens don't have that much disposable income… In terms of our business, I don't think it's a meaningful thing in the near term."* **Snap's Evan Spiegel said something similar on Snap's earnings call** the same month — independent corroboration from a company with far higher teen concentration.
	- **This fills the exact gap I flagged in the settlement memo**, which quantified everything except teen share of users, time, and revenue. Working it through against the figures in [[META-2026-Q2]] (Q2 expenses \$42B at a 31% operating margin implies roughly **\$243bn annualized revenue**):
	  | Derived quantity | Approximate value |
	  |------------------------------------------|--------------------|
	  | Teen-attributable revenue at 1% | **~\$2.4bn per year** |
	  | Q3'26 legal accrual (~\$10bn) | **~4.1 years** of total teen revenue |
	  | Annual non-contingent installment (~\$1.27bn) | **~52%** of annual teen revenue |
	- **The trade Meta made becomes legible**: pay roughly four years of teen revenue up front, accept restrictions on the 1%, and protect the other 99% from an open-ended jury-verdict tail. On those numbers the settlement is cheap insurance rather than a capitulation — which is the opposite of how the headline reads.
	- **Two caveats on the 1%.** It is **self-serving in exactly the way the case requires** — Meta's defense depends on rebutting the theory that it targeted minors for profit — though it was given under oath and corroborated by a competitor. And the qualifier *"in the near term"* is load-bearing: the plaintiff's documents argue the young demographic was seen as **key to long-term success**. Both can hold at once. **Low current monetization, high pipeline value** is the reconciliation, and it is the same framing I used in the settlement memo before this number was available.
- ## The age-enforcement admissions, and who Meta wants to pay for it
	- Zuckerberg called enforcement of Instagram's age limits **"very difficult"** and the under-13 problem **"challenging"**, conceding *"there are a set of people — potentially a meaningful number of people — that lie about their age."* Meta has deployed "proactive tools" to find and remove such accounts.
	- On the internal history: there were debates about the **"privacy sensitivity"** of requiring a date of birth, which Meta ultimately chose to collect. *"I think we got to the right place over time. I always wish we could have gotten there sooner."*
	- **The most damaging document surfaced** is an email from **Nick Clegg**, then Meta's top policy executive, stating that age limits were unenforced, making it **"difficult to claim we are doing all we can."** That is the category of internal admission that moves juries, and it is now public.
	- **The burden-shift is a consistent, years-long position**: Meta argues age verification should occur **before download**, making **Apple and Google** responsible via their app stores. Zuckerberg testified that verification is unreliable for children without a driver's license and that **phonemakers should bear more responsibility**. Bloomberg notes **Meta, Apple, and Google have all lobbied in various US states** to shape who ends up liable.
		- **This is the same argument the August settlement embeds as a continuing commitment**, so the position predates and survives the settlement. Combined with the App Store pressure in [[2026-08-19-stratechery-apple-app-store-fee-settlements-att-germany]], **age verification is an open second front on Apple's platform economics** — and one where Meta is an active lobbying adversary rather than a bystander.
- ## The litigation structure — why the settlement was rational
	- | Element | Detail |
	  |------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
	  | Venue and timing | Los Angeles Superior Court; trial began **Feb 9, 2026**, expected to run through **end of March** |
	  | Plaintiff | **Kaley G.M.**, 20, who had an Instagram account **at age 9**; sued over Instagram and YouTube |
	  | Plaintiff's counsel | **Mark Lanier**, with the Seattle-based **Social Media Victims Law Center** |
	  | Defendants at trial | **Meta and Google** |
	  | Already settled | **TikTok and Snap** reached **confidential settlements** shortly before trial |
	  | Stakes | A test case for **thousands of other lawsuits**; defendants face **billions in potential damages** if early juries side against plaintiffs |
	- **The pre-trial settlements by TikTok and Snap are the informative signal.** Two defendants priced this risk as not worth trying and paid to exit before a verdict; Meta and Google chose to litigate. That tells you both how the defense bar values the exposure and that Meta believed it had a defensible position — and it makes Meta's **subsequent** decision to settle with 52 AGs read as a considered change in posture rather than a reflex.
	- Evidence themes the plaintiff put before the jury: internal emails, slides, and messages spanning years indicating Meta viewed the young demographic as central to long-term platform success, with **granular segmentation from "tweens" through older teens**, alongside employee concerns about child-safety policy. Lanier tied the period when the plaintiff had an account at nine to a company push to **increase time spent**.
- ## Background that compounds the exposure
	- **2021 whistleblower documents**: employees were aware Instagram could negatively affect teens, especially girls — and separately that **teen usage of Facebook was declining**, prompting internal strategizing about how to "optimize" for young people. Meta has since made attracting young adults to Facebook a focus by **surfacing more content from outside a user's network**, the TikTok-popularized approach.
	- **FTC antitrust trial documents** showed Instagram's automated systems **recommended that child "groomers" connect with minors** — the single worst fact in the public record on this topic.
	- **January 2024**: Zuckerberg stood and apologized to families at a congressional hearing on youth safety.
	- The remediation timeline Meta points to — **Teen Accounts** in late 2024, **PG-13 default content settings** for under-18s in October, restrictions on live streaming for younger teens — is the direct predecessor of the settlement's control set. **Read the August agreement as the terminal step of a four-year sequence**, not a discrete event.
