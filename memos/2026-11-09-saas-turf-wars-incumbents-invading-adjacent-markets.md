- ## SaaS turf wars: mature software platforms are growing by invading each other's core markets
	- **Source**: ServiceNow Q3 2026 investor presentation slide, "Trailing Twelve Month Net New ACV Contribution Across Workflows and Products" (user-provided image), plus CRM details from the Q2 2026 call in [[NOW-2026-Q2]]. Atlassian JSM evidence comes from [[TEAM-2026-Q1]] and [[TEAM-2026-Q2]] (Atlassian fiscal Q3 and Q4 FY26, i.e. calendar Q1 and Q2 2026). Related: [[2026-10-27-servicenow-repvue-sales-sentiment-and-retention]], [[2026-10-28-databricks-ali-ghodsi-future-of-firm]], [[2026-03-28-us-saas-median-growth-rate-by-quarter-2019-2025-estimated]], [[2026-03-28-saas-ten-moats-destroyed-weakened-intact-stronger]].
	- **Thesis**: SaaS leaders' home markets are maturing, and AI is pressuring seat-based pricing. The easiest new growth is a neighbor's installed base, so the big platforms are now selling directly into each other's core categories. Their pitch is the same: one platform, one data model and one set of AI agents in place of another vendor. For investors, category leadership counts for less than it did, because each leader's core market is now someone else's expansion target.
- ## Example 1: ServiceNow is taking share in Salesforce's CRM market
	- **What the slide shows**: the share of ServiceNow's trailing-twelve-month net new ACV coming from each workflow group.
		- | Workflow group | Q3-25 | Q4-25 | Q1-26 | Q2-26 | Q3-26 | Change |
		  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
		  | Technology Workflows (ITSM, ITOM, SecOps, Armis…) | 52% | 50% | 49% | 48% | 48% | −4 pts |
		  | CRM and Industry & Core Business Workflows | 28% | 29% | 30% | 32% | 34% | **+6 pts** |
		  | Creator Workflows (App Engine, Workflow Data Fabric…) | 19% | 21% | 21% | 20% | 18% | −1 pt |
		- The CRM group's share has risen every quarter, and it now brings in more than a third of new business. IT, ServiceNow's home market, still contributes the largest share but has fallen below half.
		- **What the share gain implies**: the CRM group's share rose from 28% to 34% (~1.2×), so its new ACV grew about 20% faster than the company total. Illustration: if total trailing-twelve-month new ACV grew 20%, CRM and Core grew about 46% and Technology about 11%. The slide doesn't give the total growth, so these are only illustrative.
	- **Evidence from the Q2 call** ([[NOW-2026-Q2]]):
		- CRM reached about **\$2B in ACV**. Net new ACV sped up both year over year and quarter over quarter.
		- Sales CRM average deal size **doubled** year over year. ServiceNow is now selling sales software, not just customer service.
		- A partner completed a **full replacement of a major incumbent CRM deployment in two months**. The incumbent wasn't named, but the obvious candidate is Salesforce.
		- A large airline runs **5M customer-service voice calls a year** through ServiceNow voice AI agents. That competes directly with Salesforce Service Cloud and Agentforce.
		- Quote-to-cash: a telecom infrastructure customer expects to triple quote volume with no extra headcount.
	- **ServiceNow's pitch**: the company that runs a customer's IT tickets already holds the records of its employees, assets and workflows. Customer service is another workflow on the same platform, so customers can consolidate onto it and run AI agents there.
	- **Caveats: this slide overstates the CRM story**:
		- **The group is broader than CRM.** It also includes HR Service Delivery, Workplace Service Delivery, Legal, Source-to-Pay and **Moveworks**, the acquired employee AI assistant. Some of the share gain is acquired, and some of it is HR or procurement, not Salesforce's market.
		- **The Technology group has an acquisition too** (Armis), and its share still fell, which makes the CRM group's rise more notable.
		- **Industry products are spread across groups.** Net new ACV from industry products appears in several workflow groups, though mostly in CRM and Industry. Some of the gain may reflect industry bundles that include IT.
		- **The figures aren't comparable with older slides.** The footnote says earlier presentations split net new ACV by product, so the series can't be extended further back.
		- **These are shares, not dollar amounts.** A falling Technology share can still mean IT new ACV is growing.
		- **No figures from Salesforce's side.** The slide shows ServiceNow gaining, not Salesforce losing. Salesforce's Service Cloud growth or churn to ServiceNow would confirm the shift.
	- **The fight goes both ways** (my background, not in the source): Salesforce has launched IT service management products under Agentforce aimed at ServiceNow's core ITSM market. Each company is selling into the other's installed base.
- ## Example 2: Atlassian's Jira Service Management is attacking ServiceNow's ITSM base
	- **The target**: ITSM is the core of ServiceNow's Technology Workflows, the group that still brings in **48%** of its net new ACV in the slide above. Example 1 shows ServiceNow moving out of IT. Example 2 shows a competitor moving into it.
	- **Evidence from Atlassian's results**:
		- **Service Collection** (JSM + Customer Service Management + Assets + Rovo) passed **\$1B ARR** in calendar Q1 2026, growing **more than 30%** a year, with **65,000 customers**, including half the Fortune 500 ([[TEAM-2026-Q1]]).
		- The same quarter was Atlassian's **largest ever for competitive displacements from "a major ITSM provider."** Atlassian didn't name it. ServiceNow is the likely one, but that is an inference.
		- In calendar Q2, **Service Collection revenue growth accelerated**. Agentic automations rose **nearly 3× in six months** as customers deployed agents beyond IT into HR, legal, finance and marketing ([[TEAM-2026-Q2]]).
		- Field example: Ace Hardware rolled JSM out to **5,200 stores**, replacing a legacy system, with 42K jobs a day ([[2026-07-04-atlassian-news-roundup-pricing-rovo-competition]]).
	- **How Atlassian attacks**: it starts from the developers and IT teams already in Jira. JSM ties service desk tickets to the engineering work that resolves them. My reading, not from the earnings notes: lower list prices and self-serve purchase make it easy to try, and the price gap with ServiceNow is a big part of the pitch. Atlassian then sells upmarket with enterprise contracts, and set records in \$1M+ deals in calendar Q2.
	- **Both companies are expanding the same way**: JSM is also moving outside IT. Atlassian has launched a Customer Service Management app on the JSM foundation and is pushing agents into HR, legal and finance. That is the same expansion ServiceNow is making in its CRM and Core Business group. ServiceNow, in turn, is going downmarket with a product-led conversational service desk aimed at the "Fortune 500,000" ([[NOW-2026-Q2]]), which is Atlassian's home market.
	- **Caveats**:
		- **No direct share data**: Atlassian discloses no JSM-only ARR or win rate against ServiceNow. Service Collection includes CSM and Assets, and growth includes migrations of existing Data Center customers to Cloud.
		- **ServiceNow shows no damage yet**: its Technology share fell from 52% to 48%, but that is a mix effect alongside fast CRM growth. In Q2, ITOM was in 18 of the top 20 deals. Atlassian's displacements are most likely mid-market or departmental for now, not ServiceNow's global enterprise accounts.
		- **ServiceNow's enterprise moat is still deep**: its CMDB, IT operations and security products (Armis, Veza) and governance layer are hard to match. JSM competes most directly on the service desk itself.
- ## The pattern across both examples
	- | Attacker → target | Attacker's home base | Target category | Entry wedge | Evidence |
	  |---|---|---|---|---|
	  | ServiceNow → Salesforce | IT workflows (ITSM) | CRM, customer service, sales | Same platform and data model; AI voice and service agents | CRM group 28% → 34% of new ACV; ~\$2B CRM ACV; sales CRM deal size 2× |
	  | Atlassian → ServiceNow | Developer and team collaboration (Jira) | ITSM, service desk | Jira-connected tickets; low price; self-serve, then enterprise | Service Collection >\$1B ARR, >30% growth; record ITSM displacements |
	  | Salesforce → ServiceNow | CRM | ITSM | Agentforce IT service | Product launch only (background, not in source) |
	- Salesforce and ServiceNow are attacking each other's core, and Atlassian is attacking ServiceNow's from below. **Every leader is defending its core while attacking a neighbor's.** Attackers bring a cheaper price or a platform bundle, and defenders respond by bundling more themselves.
- ## Why SaaS platforms are moving into each other's markets now
	- **Slower growth at home**: median SaaS growth has slowed sharply since 2021 ([[2026-03-28-us-saas-median-growth-rate-by-quarter-2019-2025-estimated]]). A leader with high share in its own category can find more growth in a neighbor's.
	- **AI is weakening seat pricing**: when agents do the work, seat counts can shrink. ServiceNow says 50% of new business is already not seat-based ([[NOW-2026-Q2]]). Vendors make up for this by selling more workflows into each account.
	- **Replacing software is cheaper**: AI-assisted migration and agent-built workflows shorten replacement projects, as in the two-month CRM replacement. That lowers switching costs that used to protect incumbents.
	- **Consolidation sells well to customers**: CIOs cutting their vendor count favor the platform that already holds the data. Eighteen of ServiceNow's top 20 Q2 deals included at least eight products.
	- **Everyone wants to be the place AI agents work**: whoever owns the system that holds the records and lets agents act on them gets the AI budget. Databricks is entering the same contest from the data side ([[2026-10-28-databricks-ali-ghodsi-future-of-firm]]).
- ## What it means for the stocks
	- **Winners**: platforms that already hold a customer's records and can cross-sell into adjacent categories. ServiceNow's rising CRM share and Atlassian's Service Collection are the measurable cases.
	- **Losers**: companies whose core category is a neighbor's expansion target and that have little room to expand themselves. ServiceNow is attacker and target at once; its growth depends on winning more in CRM than it loses at the low end of ITSM. More competition shows up as price discounting, longer sales cycles and higher sales and marketing spend.
	- **What to check in each company's results**: cross-category deal wins and replacements, mix disclosures like this slide, gross retention in the core product, and comments about competitive pricing.
