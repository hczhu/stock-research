- **Source**: McKinsey & Company Growth, Marketing & Sales Practice, “From campaigns to continuous growth: AI capabilities shaping marketing,” June 2026. Authors: Eli Stein, Jamie Wilkie, Julien Boudet, Kelsey Robinson and Lalit Bhagia, with Emily Scofield. User-provided nine-page PDF.
- **Thesis**: AI is pushing marketing from periodic campaigns toward an always-on growth system that continuously senses demand, generates content, personalizes offers, markets to consumer agents and reallocates spend. Creative production and campaign execution should become cheaper and more abundant, shifting strategic value toward first-party data, identity, machine-readable product knowledge, closed-loop measurement and cross-platform orchestration.

- ## How Consumers Use AI Search
	- | Purchase stage and task | Share among surveyed AI-search users |
	  |---|---|
	  | Learn broadly about a category, brand, product or service | 73% |
	  | Plan a trip or special occasion | 53% |
	  | Explain features or technical specifications | 60% |
	  | Generate personalized recommendations | 57% |
	  | Compare specific products or services | 61% |
	  | Summarize product or feature reviews | 60% |
	- Survey: McKinsey AI Discovery Survey, August 2025; n=1,927 users of ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, Google AI Overview or Perplexity.
	- Discovery, consideration and decision are converging inside a single conversational interface. An assistant can filter irrelevant promotions, assess seller policies, choose a merchant and complete a transaction.
	- The report alternates between “nearly half” and “more than half” of consumers using AI for purchase decisions. The direction is clear, but the precise overall penetration should not be inferred from the task-level exhibit.

- ## The Five-Pillar AI Marketing System
	- | Pillar | Campaign-era model | AI-first model | Scarce enabling asset |
	  |---|---|---|---|
	  | Continuous insights | Periodic surveys and static segments | Always-on signals, synthetic audiences, sentiment scraping and real-time idea testing | Governed customer data and feedback loops |
	  | Scaled creativity | Manual content with limited A/B tests | Agents score, generate, deploy and learn from on-brand content continuously | Brand rules, proprietary assets and performance data |
	  | Hyperpersonalization | CRM segments and propensity models | Dynamic one-to-one messages, offers and experiences | Identity, decision engines and offer management |
	  | Marketing to AI agents | Optimize for human search and channels | Optimize discoverability, trust and transactions across AI-native journeys | Structured product knowledge and credibility signals |
	  | Always-on orchestration | Backward-looking mix models and episodic budgets | Real-time media optimization and cross-platform allocation | Unified measurement, governance and execution APIs |

- ## Continuous Insights and Synthetic Customers
	- Digital twins or synthetic personas can simulate customer groups and test responses to pricing, products and campaigns before live deployment.
	- Continuous-insight systems combine structured and unstructured first- and third-party signals rather than relying on infrequent surveys.
	- The near-term benefit is faster, cheaper hypothesis testing; the limitation is that simulated answers can reproduce model assumptions rather than true customer behavior.
	- Real-world experimentation and sales outcomes must remain the calibration layer for synthetic research.
	- McKinsey proposes a “Customer Wayfinder” role that combines AI-generated insight with cultural understanding and strategic judgment.

- ## Creative Abundance Changes Industry Economics
	- Creative is the most mature gen-AI marketing use case among the senior marketers surveyed.
	- Content factories can produce many localized and personalized variants, monitor trends and continuously adapt assets to performance.
	- As production cost falls, the competitive bottleneck moves away from the ability to create an ad toward:
		- Owning differentiated customer and conversion data.
		- Maintaining a coherent brand across massive content volume.
		- Securing distribution and attention.
		- Testing variants quickly enough to identify genuine lift.
		- Proving incremental sales rather than generating more assets.
	- External creative and production spending faces direct pressure; strategic concepting, brand systems and outcome measurement become more valuable than manual versioning.

- ## Hyperpersonalization Becomes a Closed-Loop System
	- AI personalization moves from static customer segments to continuously updated next-best actions for each individual.
	- The required stack includes accessible customer data, identity resolution, real-time decision engines, reinforcement learning and an offer-management system that works across channels.
	- The reported 5%–8% revenue uplift and up-to-30% cost-to-serve reduction make personalization one of the clearest areas where growth and efficiency can occur together.
	- Privacy, regulatory rules and inaccurate personalization become operating constraints, which is why McKinsey assigns oversight to a “Hyperpersonalization Architect.”

- ## Marketing to Machines
	- Agentic commerce changes the optimization target from winning a click to being selected by a recommendation system.
	- Brands need a machine-readable knowledge engine containing detailed product specifications, current availability and policies, verified reviews, expert input and direct answers to customer questions.
	- Generative-engine optimization should differ from traditional SEO:
		- Trust and citation quality matter alongside relevance.
		- Product feeds and policies must stay current enough for an agent to transact.
		- Merchant reliability, delivery speed and returns can affect recommendation rank.
		- Content must be interpretable by multiple AI platforms, not only a single search crawler.
	- Marketers in the report rate agentic commerce as their least-ready capability, suggesting that spending should follow consumer behavior with a lag.

- ## Always-On Orchestration Replaces Campaign Cycles
	- The full-funnel system continuously analyzes performance, generates campaign ideas and briefs, identifies audiences, launches across channels and improves live campaigns.
	- Humans define objectives, constraints and which signals matter; agents handle repeated decisioning and execution.
	- A 30% marketing-ROI improvement and the reduction of execution time to 10%–15% imply meaningful labor and agency-spend pressure if the gains reach the P&L.
	- Cross-platform orchestration can reduce loyalty to any single buying interface, but platforms with proprietary conversion data and closed-loop optimization retain structural leverage.

- ## Conditions Required to Capture Value
	- **Rewire complete workflows**: automating isolated tasks produces local efficiency without changing end-to-end economics.
	- **Create a unified data and identity layer**: agents need consistent customer, product and intent identifiers across systems.
	- **Measure business outcomes**: leads, share of wallet, acquisition cost, retention, revenue and external spend matter more than content volume or AI usage.
	- **Build feedback into every workflow**: each interaction should improve later decisions, content and targeting.
	- **Reallocate saved capacity**: productivity gains need explicit headcount, vendor-spend or reinvestment decisions to become financial value.
	- **Establish governance and decision rights**: testing standards, human approval boundaries and accountability are necessary for autonomous allocation and messaging.
	- **Develop hybrid talent**: skills development was the number-one adoption barrier in the senior-marketer survey.

- ## Predictions and Trends
	- Marketing planning will shift from quarterly campaign calendars toward continuously operating growth systems.
	- Content volume will rise faster than marketing budgets, causing unit creative prices and manual production fees to decline.
	- First-party data, identity, product catalogs and conversion feedback will become more valuable as generative models commoditize copy and images.
	- AI assistants will increasingly filter marketing messages before consumers see them, reducing the value of generic outbound campaigns.
	- Brands will allocate more budget to structured knowledge, verified evidence and merchant-service quality because agents incorporate trust and fulfillment into recommendations.
	- Ad buying will become more automated and cross-platform, increasing demand for neutral measurement and orchestration while pressuring manual media operations.
	- Marketing headcount and agency rosters will become smaller, with remaining roles concentrated in strategy, system design, brand standards and exception handling.
	- Retail-media networks and commerce platforms should benefit because purchase data provides the strongest closed-loop training and measurement signal.
	- CRM and data-cloud platforms will compete to become the identity and decision layer for one-to-one journeys.
	- AI value reporting will move from adoption metrics toward realized revenue, acquisition-cost, retention and external-spend outcomes.

- ## Evidence Quality and Open Questions
	- Several outcome estimates are based on McKinsey client experience rather than controlled studies or disclosed company financials.
	- The senior marketing-executive survey has only 26 respondents, limiting the precision of maturity and roadmap conclusions.
	- Case studies are anonymized, so baseline economics, implementation cost and durability cannot be independently assessed.
	- Productivity metrics do not reveal whether savings reached operating profit or were reinvested.
	- Sixfold organic-traffic growth can be easier from a small base and does not disclose conversion or revenue magnitude.
	- Agentic commerce may shift discovery faster than transactions if permissions, payments, returns and consumer trust develop slowly.
	- The thesis is strengthened by disclosed revenue lift, lower acquisition costs and durable external-spend reductions after full implementation costs.
	- It is weakened if content volume rises without incremental conversion, or if customers resist autonomous personalization and purchasing.
