- ## What Is MEMS (微机电系统)?

	- MEMS stands for **Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems** (微机电系统, literally "micro-electromechanical system"). It refers to devices that integrate **mechanical structures** (beams, membranes, gears, levers, nozzles) with **electrical circuits** on the same silicon chip, at the microscale (microns to millimeters).
	- Unlike a pure logic chip that only moves electrons, a MEMS chip moves **physical matter** — it can sense acceleration, pressure, sound waves, or rotation, and convert them to electrical signals, or vice versa (e.g., an inkjet nozzle that fires a droplet on command).
	- MEMS are fabricated using the same photolithography processes as regular semiconductors (often at mature nodes: 180nm, 250nm), which makes them extremely cheap to mass-produce — billions of units per year.
	- The key insight is that **silicon is not just a conductor/insulator — it is also an excellent mechanical material**: stiff, nearly perfectly elastic (no fatigue), and etchable to micron precision. This makes silicon ideal for building miniaturized springs, membranes, and resonators that also carry electrical signals.

- ## Applications by Sector

	- ### Consumer Electronics
		- Every smartphone contains a **MEMS gyroscope** — used for optical image stabilization (OIS) to prevent lens shake, step counting (pedometer), navigation assistance, and gaming orientation sensing.
		- **~5.5 billion MEMS microphones** are produced globally each year, with total production value of **$1.3 billion**. MEMS mics have replaced electret condenser mics in virtually all smartphones, earbuds, and smart speakers due to smaller size, lower power, and digital output compatibility.

	- ### Printing & Display
		- Inkjet printers use MEMS **cantilever beams** (悬臂梁) as the thermal/piezoelectric actuators that fire ink droplets. **~1.5 billion cantilever beams** are produced globally per year for inkjet applications.
		- **MEMS-based digital projection** (Texas Instruments' DMD — Digital Micromirror Device) has captured **>50% of the front-projector market** and **>85% of digital cinema projection**. Each DMD chip contains millions of microscopic mirrors (each ~16 microns wide) that tilt ±12° at up to 5,000 times/second to modulate light — a canonical MEMS success story.

	- ### Healthcare & Biotech
		- **Continuous glucose monitoring** (CGM) — electrochemical MEMS sensors implanted subcutaneously for real-time blood sugar tracking.
		- **Artificial retinas** — MEMS electrode arrays stimulate remaining retinal cells.
		- **Lab-on-a-chip** (片上实验室) — integrates sample prep, reaction, and detection on a single chip; used in point-of-care diagnostics.
		- **Microfluidics** (微流控) — microscale channels that manipulate tiny volumes of fluid for assays.
		- **Neural electrode arrays** — MEMS probes for brain-machine interfaces.
		- **HIV detection, forensic DNA testing, blood pressure sensors** — all enabled by MEMS miniaturization of what were previously bench-scale instruments.

	- ### Automotive
		- **Airbag accelerometers** (加速计) — the MEMS accelerometer in airbag ECUs detects crash deceleration in <1ms and triggers deployment. First mass-market MEMS application (1990s).
		- **Tire pressure monitoring** (TPMS, 胎压检测) — MEMS pressure sensors inside each tire wirelessly report pressure to the dashboard.
		- **Electronic Stability Control** (ESP, 电子稳定性控制) — uses MEMS yaw-rate gyroscopes and accelerometers to detect skidding and selectively apply brakes.

- ## Investment Relevance
	- MEMS are a **high-volume, mature-node** semiconductor category — production volumes in the billions per device type, but low ASP (often cents to low dollars). Unlike logic chips, MEMS don't benefit from leading-edge nodes; the value is in process specialization and mechanical design IP.
	- Key MEMS producers: **STMicroelectronics** (dominant in consumer motion sensors), **Bosch Sensortec** (automotive + consumer), **Texas Instruments** (DMD), **Knowles** (MEMS microphones), **InvenSense** (now TDK) (gyroscopes/accelerometers).
	- The automotive MEMS market is a durable, growing segment: ADAS, electrification, and autonomous driving increase sensor count per vehicle substantially.
